Fuao on Nangan and Baisha on Beigan are useful ferry access nodes, but they
should not be collapsed into one generic “Matsu ferry terminal” label. The
Maritime and Port Bureau's passenger-ship records name the Nangan Fuao
Passenger Transport Building and the Beigan Baisha Port Affairs Building as
the published station and shore-connection locations for passenger vessels.[^motc-matsu-vessel][^motc-matsu-island-vessel]

The official Small Three Links schedule groups the cross-strait services into
Fuao–Fuzhou Langqi and Baisha–Fuzhou Huangqi, alongside the two Kinmen route
families.[^motc-matsu-schedule] The same page publishes approximate crossing
times of about 90 minutes for Fuao–Langqi and about 30 minutes for
Baisha–Huangqi.[^motc-matsu-schedule] Those are route-family durations, not a
promise that every dated sailing, vessel assignment or immigration process
takes exactly that long.[^motc-matsu-schedule]

The Bureau's operating page was checked on 6 September 2026 and names the two
Matsu Small Three Links routes as distinct passenger services.[^motc-matsu-operations]
It also lists the domestic Matsu island routes and the vessels associated with
the two cross-strait families in separate table contexts.[^motc-matsu-operations]
The guide therefore keeps cross-strait and domestic island services as
different route families even when they share a port building.[^motc-matsu-operations]

There is an explicit published ground-access relationship for Fuao, but it is
more limited than a confirmed airport-to-ferry interchange. The Maritime and
Port Bureau's Matsu-office page says passengers arriving by air can take a bus
from Nangan Airport to Fuao Village stop and walk about five minutes to the
New Administrative Passenger Transport Building.[^motc-matsu-office] The same
page describes a passenger arriving at Fuao pier as walking about three minutes
to that building.[^motc-matsu-office] This establishes an official access
chain through a named bus stop and building; it does not establish a timed
transfer, exact bus stop ID, step-free path, or a guaranteed connection to a
particular sailing.[^motc-matsu-office]

The vessel records show why terminal and vessel evidence should remain joined
explicitly. Ji Shun No. 10 is recorded on the Baisha–Huangqi route with a
published capacity of 149 passengers and vessel-level accessibility fields
including wheelchair seating, an accessible toilet, staff assistance and a
ramped shore facility.[^motc-matsu-vessel] Hong Shun No. 6 is separately
recorded on the Nangan–Beigan island service and names the same Fuao and
Baisha buildings as the service locations.[^motc-matsu-island-vessel] A vessel
accessibility field is not automatically a statement about the whole port
building or every sailing.[^motc-matsu-vessel]

## Join boundary

This page records three evidence levels: vessel-to-port association from the
Maritime and Port Bureau vessel records; an official airport/bus-stop/access-
building chain for Fuao; and route-family membership from the official Small
Three Links schedule.[^motc-matsu-vessel][^motc-matsu-office][^motc-matsu-schedule]
It does not promote a generic map proximity, a port name match, or a shared
island into a confirmed interchange. Current fares, check-in deadlines,
security and immigration timings, port accessibility beyond the cited vessel
records, and a complete terminal-facility inventory remain TBC.[^motc-matsu-schedule][^motc-matsu-vessel]
