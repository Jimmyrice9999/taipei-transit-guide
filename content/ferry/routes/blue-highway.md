The Blue Highway is a set of scheduled and chartered passenger-boat services on
the Tamsui and Keelung rivers, not one route with one timetable[^ntpc-blue-highway-permits].
The current New Taipei permit sheet covers the 2025–2026 operating period and
lists six approved operators, while the city’s 2025 notice identifies the legal
piers and warns passengers to use inspected, permitted services[^ntpc-blue-highway-operators][^ntpc-blue-highway-permits].

## The fixed routes

The following is the current official fare-sheet snapshot retrieved 23 August
2026[^ntpc-blue-highway-fares].

| Route | Published operation | Published fare | Operator |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tamsui Ferry Pier–Bali Ferry Pier | Weekdays 07:00–19:00; weekends 07:00–20:00; about 10–30 minutes on the water | Full NT$40; concession NT$20; bicycle NT$25; motorcycle up to 150cc NT$40 | Shun Feng[^ntpc-blue-highway-fares] |
| Tamsui Passenger Pier–Fisherman’s Wharf | Weekends 10:00–19:00; about one-hour headway; weather and operating conditions apply | Full NT$100; concession NT$50 | Shun Feng; Taipei Cruise also lists group-reservation service[^ntpc-blue-highway-fares][^ntpc-blue-highway-operators] |
| Zhongxiao Pier–Dadaocheng–Guandu–Zhuwei–Tamsui Passenger Pier | One one-way sailing at 15:00 on Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays | Full NT$300; concession NT$150 | Chang Hong[^ntpc-blue-highway-fares] |
| Dadaocheng–Tamsui Passenger Pier | Saturdays at 10:00 and 13:30; two round trips per listed departure; tide and advance reservation apply | Full NT$400; concession NT$200 | Haloha[^ntpc-blue-highway-fares] |

The same New Taipei department published two different current Tamsui–Bali
headway figures: the 20 March 2026 operator sheet says approximately 15–20
minutes, while the 18 May 2026 fare sheet says approximately 15–30 minutes
[^ntpc-blue-highway-operators][^ntpc-blue-highway-fares].
The two official values are retained because the documents may be describing
different operating conditions; this guide does not choose one as the single
truth[^ntpc-blue-highway-operators][^ntpc-blue-highway-fares].

Tamsui Passenger Pier is about 700 metres or 10–15 minutes from MRT Tamsui
Station Exit 3 according to the current New Taipei route sheet, which also
lists the local bus stop and route access[^ntpc-blue-highway-fares].
The same sheet places Zhongxiao Pier about 1.3 kilometres from MRT Taipei Bridge
and identifies the nearby Tianhou Temple bus stop as another access point[^ntpc-blue-highway-fares].

## Operators and vessels

The current permit sheet names Shun Feng’s fixed Tamsui–Bali and Tamsui–Fisherman’s
Wharf services and lists the vessels 順風66, 順風68, 順風122, 順風126, 順風128,
順風162, 順風166, 順風168 and 順風186, alongside charter operation[^ntpc-blue-highway-operators].

It names Haloha’s Dadaocheng–Tamsui fixed service and lists 飛魚3, 飛魚7,
花博3 and 藍天1, with charter operation also available[^ntpc-blue-highway-operators].

Chang Hong’s fixed Zhongxiao–Tamsui route uses 長弘1, 長弘2, 長弘6 and 長弘12,
and the operator sheet also records charter service[^ntpc-blue-highway-operators].

Boatyacht, Goddess Yacht and Taipei Cruise appear in the same current permit
list as charter or reservation services, with the listed vessels 舶克1、舶克2、
舶克6; 女神哈莉婭、阿加芙、佳樂婗; and 台航3、台航7、台航8 respectively[^ntpc-blue-highway-operators].
The permit sheet gives the current operator contacts, but a Taipei Public
Transport Office FAQ gives a different current Haloha phone number, so the
contact conflict is left visible rather than silently harmonised[^ntpc-blue-highway-operators][^taipei-pto-ferry-faq].

## Piers and access

New Taipei’s 2025 official notice identifies Tamsui Passenger Pier, Tamsui Ferry
Pier, Bali Ferry Pier, Tamsui Zhuwei Pier, Bali Longxing Pier, Zhongxiao Pier
and Huajiang Pier as legal Blue Highway piers[^ntpc-blue-highway-permits].
The notice’s legal-pier list is not a promise that every operator or sailing
calls at every pier; the route-specific sheet above is the controlling source
for the fixed services described here[^ntpc-blue-highway-permits][^ntpc-blue-highway-fares].

## EasyCard and fare boundaries

EasyCard’s current transport FAQ treats ferry travel as a transport mode that
can use its one-time advance arrangement, but names Chang Hong’s Taipei Blue
Highway service as an exception to that arrangement[^easycard-ferry-faq].
That is an acceptance rule with an operator-specific caveat, not proof that
every boat accepts every EasyCard product at every pier[^easycard-ferry-faq].

Taipei Cruise’s current EasyCard page describes validators at the pier and
lists single full fares of NT$20–50 for its own Fisherman’s Wharf, Tamsui Old
Street and Bali Left Bank services[^taipei-cruise-easycard].
That page also says welfare, senior, disability and companion subsidy points do
not apply to those Taipei Cruise fares, so passengers need a sufficient stored
balance or another accepted payment method[^taipei-cruise-easycard].

The TPASS operating notice names MRT and light rail, municipal buses, the
specified TRA segment, listed coach routes and YouBike as its coverage
categories, but does not name Blue Highway ferries[^tpass-megacity-notice].
This guide therefore treats the published ferry fares as separate from TPASS
until an operator or government source explicitly says otherwise[^tpass-megacity-notice].

## Weather, tides and seasonal operation

The Taipei Public Transport Office says holiday fixed departures are published
through its Blue Waterway timetable and warns that weekday services can be
cancelled when the tide is too low, so passengers should contact the operator
before travelling[^taipei-pto-ferry-faq].
New Taipei’s 9 July 2026 typhoon notice suspended all Tamsui River passenger
boats, including the Tamsui–Bali service, and made resumption conditional on
conditions and a later official announcement[^ntpc-weather-stop].
The practical rule is therefore date-sensitive: check the operator and the
official timetable on the day, especially for tide-dependent or sightseeing
services[^taipei-pto-ferry-faq][^ntpc-weather-stop].

## Danjiang Bridge river tours

New Taipei announced special Danjiang Bridge river tours beginning 16 May 2026,
with weekend operation, an onboard guide and a city subsidy that reduces the
published fare for eligible riders[^ntpc-danjiang-bridge].

Route A runs from the Tamsui Phase 2 passenger-pier floating platform to the
Danjiang Bridge and back in about 50 minutes, with three Saturday/Sunday
departures at 12:30, 13:20 and 14:10; the full fare is NT$300 and the
concession fare is NT$100[^ntpc-danjiang-tour].

Route B links Zhongxiao or Dadaocheng with the Danjiang Bridge and Tamsui Phase
2 pier in about 90 minutes, with a published 10:30 outbound and 15:10 return
on Saturdays and Sundays[^ntpc-danjiang-tour].

The Danjiang Bridge sheet gives a discounted NT$240 full fare or NT$80
concession fare for passengers showing a three-day tourism-bus receipt, and
identifies Haloha as the operator contact for the tour[^ntpc-danjiang-tour].

## What remains date-sensitive

The published fares and headways above are a 23 August 2026 snapshot, not a
guarantee that a boat will sail on a particular tide or weather day[^ntpc-blue-highway-fares][^taipei-pto-ferry-faq].
The current official material does not provide one consolidated live table of
all charter prices, vessel capacities, same-day cancellations and payment
methods, so those fields remain TBC until the relevant operator publishes them
for the specific sailing[^ntpc-blue-highway-operators][^taipei-pto-ferry-faq].
