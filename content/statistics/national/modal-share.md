MOTC's Department of Statistics runs a recurring National Travel Survey
(民眾日常使用運具狀況調查) measuring how people in Taiwan actually travel on
a typical day. The most recent wave surveyed 1 September to 31 December
2024 by telephone (landline and mobile), reaching an effective sample of
34,236 people aged 15 and over.[^motc-travel-survey-2024]

## The 2024 figures

By share of trip-legs, motorcycles and scooters remain the largest single
mode at 43.7%, down from 45.8% in the 2022 survey.[^motc-travel-survey-2024]
Private cars are second at 26.6%, up from 25.0%.[^motc-travel-survey-2024]
Walking holds essentially steady at 9.9% (was 10.0%).[^motc-travel-survey-2024]

The notable shift is in rail. Combined MRT, THSR and TRA travel reached a
6.6% mode share in 2024, up from 5.5% in 2022 — and the survey's own summary
states this is the first time rail's share has exceeded bus, which held
steady at 6.0% across both surveys: 「『軌道運輸』市占率6.6%則首次超越汽車客運」.[^motc-travel-survey-2024]
Bicycles fell slightly to 2.7% (from 3.0%) and taxis to 2.5% (from
2.7%).[^motc-travel-survey-2024]

## The aggregate picture, and a recovery that is not complete

Grouped into broader categories, public transit's overall trip-leg share
rose to 15.2% in 2024 from 14.3% in 2022 — a real year-on-year gain the
survey states directly — but the same report notes this remains below the
16.0% recorded in 2020, before pandemic-era travel patterns
depressed transit use: 「113年我國運具次數之公共運輸市占率為15.2%...較111年調查結果之14.3%，增加0.9個百分點...惟仍未達109年之16.0%水準」.[^motc-travel-survey-2024]
Private motorised transport (cars, scooters and other private vehicles)
accounted for 72.0% of trips in 2024, essentially flat against 72.3% in
2022.[^motc-travel-survey-2024]

## A national figure that hides a large regional split

The national public-transit share obscures wide variation between cities
and counties. Taipei City posts by far the highest transit share at 38.3%,
followed by Keelung (33.4%) and New Taipei (29.0%).[^motc-travel-survey-2024]
At the other end, Nantou (3.3%) and Changhua (3.4%) post the lowest
transit shares in the country — meaning a resident of Taipei is roughly
eleven times more likely to travel by public transit on a given day than a
resident of Nantou, per this same survey wave.[^motc-travel-survey-2024]
The survey also records how many people leave home at all on a typical
surveyed weekday, which itself rose to 76.1% in 2024 from 73.4% in
2022.[^motc-travel-survey-2024]

## What remains TBC

This page reports mode share by share of trip-legs, which is what the
survey itself leads with; a national aggregate ridership figure summing
absolute passenger counts across every mode and operator was not found in
this survey and would need to be separately reconstructed from
operator-by-operator annual reports, most of which this site already covers
on their own pages.
