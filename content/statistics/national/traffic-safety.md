Taiwan's road-safety statistics come from at least three different
counting systems, and they do not agree with each other for the same year —
a gap serious enough that MOTC issued a public statement in February 2026
specifically to address it.[^motc-clarification-2026]

## Three tracks, three names

**A1** counts deaths within 24 hours of a crash — described by MOTC as
valued for its immediacy in supporting rapid policy response.[^motc-clarification-2026]
**A30** counts deaths within 30 days, the figure most often quoted as the
headline annual road-death total: 2,950 for 2024 and 3,023 for
2023.[^tadd-2024][^udn-death-comparison] A third series comes from the
Ministry of Health and Welfare's own cause-of-death registry, which MOTC
describes as professionally assessed and aligned with international
standards.[^motc-clarification-2026] MOTC's own explanation for why these
diverge is direct: 「上述三個死亡數據的落差，係源於統計定義與資料來源之本質差異」 —
the gap stems from fundamental differences in statistical definitions and
data sources — and the ministry states it has not changed its counting
method, choosing instead to publish all of them side by side rather than
collapse them into one number.[^motc-clarification-2026]

## The 2024 figures, and a conflict this page does not resolve

For 2024, the A30 total is well established at 2,950 deaths, alongside
393,000 recorded accidents and 524,000 injured, from an NGO republication of
the official MOTC/police statistics.[^tadd-2024] The Ministry of Health and
Welfare's own official statistical release states a 2024 "運輸事故" (transport
accident) death count of 2,932, down 136 people (4.4%) from
2023.[^mohw-2024-causes-of-death]

A separate news comparison, published to illustrate the growing gap between
MOTC and MOHW figures, cites a different MOHW-attributed value for the same
year: 2,426.[^udn-death-comparison] That comparison piece uses the same
Chinese term, 運輸事故死亡人數, for a number 506 lower than the one on
MOHW's own official page for the identical year. This page does not resolve
which of the two MOHW-attributed figures is correct, or whether they
measure genuinely different populations — for instance, MOHW's own page may
report all-mode transport-accident deaths including rail, air and water,
while the news comparison's figure may be a road-traffic-only subset drawn
from a more granular MOHW release this research did not locate and fetch.
Both values are published here with their sources rather than one being
silently preferred.

The same news comparison gives the 2024 MOTC-MOHW gap as 524 people, or
17.7%.[^udn-death-comparison] That arithmetic is only consistent with the
2,426 MOHW-attributed figure (2,950 − 2,426 = 524), not with MOHW's own
official 2,932 figure (2,950 − 2,932 = 18, a 0.6% gap) — which is itself
evidence that the "2,426" comparison figure and the "2,932" figure on MOHW's
own page are measuring two different things, even though both are
attributed to the same ministry.

## Beyond the death count

MOHW's own 2024 release also gives the wider context: all-cause deaths in
Taiwan totalled 201,383, down 2.0% from 2023, with accidental injury of all
kinds (a broader category than transport accidents specifically) ranking
7th among the top-10 causes of death at 6,924.[^mohw-2024-causes-of-death]

## What remains TBC

A full-year A1 (24-hour) national total for 2024 from a primary MOTC or
police page was not located during this research — only a partial figure
for January 2024 was found. The specific MOHW data table that would resolve
the 2,932-versus-2,426 conflict above, and any MOTC statement quantifying
what share of A30-recorded deaths are later reclassified as natural causes
on follow-up (a claim reported in secondary coverage but not independently
verified against a primary MOTC statement here), both remain TBC.
