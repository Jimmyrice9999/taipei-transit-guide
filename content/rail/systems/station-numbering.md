Every station on the Wenhu Line has two numbers[^dorts-wenhu]. One is on the
signs. The other is on the drawings, and almost nobody outside the project has
seen it.

The diagram above puts them side by side. Above the rule, the two orders run in
opposite directions and the connecting lines cross. Below it, they agree — and
the numbers are not even in the same series.

## The operating code

BR01 to BR24, south to north, the codes on every platform sign, map and
train announcement[^dorts-wenhu]. They arrived late. The line opened in
1996[^dorts-wenhu]; the line number and colour were assigned on 11 October 2014
and the two-letter station code in October 2016[^zh-wenhu]. **For its first
eighteen years this railway had a name and no code**[^zh-wenhu].

## The engineering number

DORTS lists a second number against every station in its own project
table[^dorts-wenhu]: the 工程編號, the number the station carried while it was
being built.

On the Muzha section it runs **north to south**, the reverse of the operating
code:

| Operating | Station | Engineering |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BR01 | Taipei Zoo | `BR13` |
| BR02 | Muzha | `BR12` |
| BR03 | Wanfang Community | `BR11` |
| BR04 | Wanfang Hospital | `BR10` |
| BR05 | Xinhai | `BR9` |
| BR06 | Linguang | `BR8` |
| BR07 | Liuzhangli | `BR7` |
| BR08 | Technology Building | `BR6` |
| BR09 | Daan | `BR5` |
| BR10 | Zhongxiao Fuxing | `BR4` |
| BR11 | Nanjing Fuxing | `BR3` |
| BR12 | Zhongshan Junior High School | `BR2` |
| BR13 | Songshan Airport | `BR1` |

Thirteen stations, and the two numbers sum to fourteen at every one of
them[^dorts-wenhu].

## The third series

The Neihu stations do not continue the BR series. They start again at **B1**:

| Operating | Station | Engineering |
| --- | --- | --- |

| BR15 | Jiannan Rd. | `B2` |
| BR16 | Xihu | `B3` |
| BR17 | Gangqian | `B4` |
| BR18 | Wende | `B5` |
| BR19 | Neihu | `B6` |
| BR20 | Dahu Park | `B7` |
| BR21 | Huzhou | `B8` |
| BR22 | Donghu | `B9` |
| BR23 | Nangang Software Park | `B10` |
| BR24 | Taipei Nangang Exhibition Center | `B11` |

Eleven stations, running the same way as the operating codes, in a series of
their own. That is what a separate design and contract package looks like from
the outside.

## The station that does not fit

Look at the boundary. The Neihu section is BR13 to BR24 — twelve stations. The
B series has eleven. **BR13 Songshan Airport is a Neihu-section station carrying
a Muzha-section number**[^dorts-wenhu].

The chronology says why it might be. Songshan Airport was not in the original
scheme: MOTC directed a study on serving the airport directly in February 2000,
the Executive Yuan agreed in principle on 1 August 2001, and the funding was
approved on 4 January 2002[^dorts-wenhu]. By then the Neihu section's
B series had been allocated to the stations that already existed on paper.

A station added to a project after its numbers were handed out has to go
somewhere. This one went into the other series, at its head.

**That last step is this site reading the table, not DORTS explaining it.** The
addition date is the builder's; the inference about the numbering is ours, and
it is offered as a reading rather than a fact. What the table shows without
interpretation is that the number is in the wrong series for the section the
station is in.

## Why any of it matters

Taipei's network was not designed and then built. It was built project by
project, each with its own budget, contractor, systems supplier and drawing set,
and then operated as one railway afterwards. Almost every oddity on this site
traces back to that — two incompatible fleets on one line, a depot in the wrong
district for its name, station numbers that jump 29 places on the
Zhonghe–Xinlu Line.

The numbering is the cleanest evidence of it, because it is the seam left
visible in the paperwork. The signs were made to be read by passengers on a
finished railway. The drawings were made by two teams, working outward from
their own starting points, years apart.
