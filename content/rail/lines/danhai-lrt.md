Danhai is the line the network map's legend quietly implies does not exist:
rail running down the middle of a street, sharing the road rather than owning
a reserved corridor of its own. It serves the reclaimed new town north of
Tamsui, feeding [Tamsui Station](/rail/lines/tamsui-xinyi-line/) at its
southern end rather than duplicating it, and it runs on a domestically
designed and built tram that this line put into service before any other in
Taiwan[^ntmc-train]. It is operated by
[New Taipei Metro](/rail/operators/ntmc/).

## Why light rail, not an extension of the metro

New Taipei's Rapid Transit Engineering Bureau — then still the Taipei City
agency, before New Taipei's own DORTS existed — completed a feasibility study
in November 1992 that considered extending the existing heavy-rail Tamsui
Line north from Tamsui Station, and rejected it in favour of a medium-capacity
system branching from Hongshulin instead[^zh-danhai]. The project then stalled:
Taipei DORTS submitted the medium-capacity proposal to the Ministry of
Transportation and Communications (MOTC) in 1999, and MOTC declined to approve
construction in 2002, citing budget priorities and recommending denser bus
feeder service in the meantime rather than committing the money[^zh-danhai].
Construction of what eventually became the Danhai LRT did not begin until
November 2014, more than two decades after the original study — DORTS's own
Green Mountain Line project record dates the main-line works' start to that
month[^dorts-green]. The 1992 study and the 2002 decision themselves rest on
a Chinese Wikipedia account read in full[^zh-danhai]; no DORTS or MOTC
primary document describing either in these terms was independently located,
so that earlier history is published here as secondary, not upgraded to
primary.

## What has opened

Two lines share the V prefix. The **Green Mountain Line** (綠山線, V01
Hongshulin to V11 Kanding) is 7.34 km with 11 stations, and DORTS's own
project record gives its opening date as 24 December 2018[^dorts-green] — but
Chinese Wikipedia's account instead splits this into two dates, an opening
(通車) on 23 December and the start of trial operation (試營運) on 24
December[^zh-danhai]. Both readings are published here rather than one being
quietly preferred; DORTS's own field may record trial-operation status rather
than a ceremonial opening a day earlier, but neither source states that
explicitly.

The **Blue Sea Line phase 1** (藍海線第一期, branching off the Green Mountain
Line at V09 to reach V28 Taipei University of Marine Technology, V27 Shalun
and V26 Tamsui Fisherman's Wharf) added 2.21 km and 3 stations,
opening 15 November 2020[^dorts-blue1]. Both lines were funded and approved
as one combined project — DORTS's own pages for each state an identical total
of NT$12.81 billion and identical approval dates[^dorts-green][^dorts-blue1]
— and together they make up the **14-station, 9.55 km network now
operating**[^wedid-phase1].

![The canopy-covered platform at Danhai Xinshi Zhen Station, empty in the rain](/images/danhai-lrt/station-800.webp "Danhai Xinshi Zhen Station, on the new town side of the line. — 梁立豪, CC BY-SA 4.0, Wikimedia Commons")

A **Blue Sea Line phase 2** is still in design: DORTS's own project page gives
its length as 3.32 km, its budget as NT$6.98 billion, and its six stations by
code — `V21` through `V25`, plus `V26A` — running from a new station behind
Tamsui MRT Station, along the riverside to Guanchao Plaza, back onto
Zhongzheng Road, and out to rejoin the existing line at Fisherman's
Wharf[^dorts-blue2]. (These codes are not yet in this site's station data,
which covers only what is built and operating, so they render as plain text
here rather than as badges.) That is a direct answer to what the gap between
**V11 and V26** is for: it is reserved for exactly these six phase-2
stations. Chinese Wikipedia's own article on the Blue Sea Line gives a
different phase 2 length, 4.44 km[^zh-bluesea] — a real disagreement with
DORTS's own figure that this page does not resolve. What `V12`–`V20` are
reserved for is not established by any source found; the depot's Chinese
Wikipedia article mentions two further
lines under discussion, 三芝線 (Sanzhih) and 八里線 (Bali), that might
eventually draw on part of that range, but nothing found states this as
settled, and that article discloses on its own page that it lists no
references at all[^zh-depot]. Once the Executive Yuan approves the revised
phase 2 plan, DORTS states completion is expected five years
later[^dorts-blue2]; a separate DORTS tracking page shows a preliminary
utility-relocation contract for the phase already under way, awarded April
2026 and due to finish January 2027[^dorts-blue2-progress].

**No source found in this research gives one total figure for the fully
built-out network.** A "13.99 km, 20 stations, 1 depot" total circulates
across travel-blog aggregators, but it does not appear in Chinese Wikipedia's
own infobox and does not fall out of a clean sum of any of the primary figures
collected here — DORTS's own phase 1 (9.55 km) plus phase 2 (3.32 km) figures
add to 12.87 km, not 13.99. That total is marked TBC rather than published.

## Rolling stock — 行武者號

NTMC's own fleet page states plainly: **the first domestically manufactured
metro-family vehicle in Taiwan was the Danhai LRT train**[^ntmc-train]. It
was built by Taiwan Rolling Stock Co. in partnership with Germany's Voith
Engineering Services, with design, manufacture and testing all completed in
Taiwan[^pansci]. NTMC's spec table — which, tellingly, presents Danhai and the
later Ankeng LRT fleet under one shared "light rail train" specification
rather than two separate ones — gives: 5 cars per train, 265 passengers (62
seated, 203 standing), overall dimensions 34,450 × 2,650 × 3,750 mm, steel
wheel on steel rail, a 100% low-floor cabin at 350 mm above rail level,
stainless and weathering steel construction, a minimum curve radius of 25 m,
a maximum gradient of 70‰, electric/hydraulic/track braking, and 750 V DC
power taken either from the overhead catenary or from an onboard lithium
battery, giving 70 km/h under wire and 50 km/h off it[^ntmc-train]. Phase 1's
fleet is 15 trainsets, out of a combined 30-trainset order NTMC also placed
for Ankeng[^ntmc-train][^ntmc-train-intro]. A Taiwanese science-media account
gives a "domestication rate" of 21.5% for the Danhai order, rising to 42% for
Ankeng's, and a stated 500 kN crash-resistance design figure — neither
independently corroborated against a manufacturer or NTMC primary
source[^pansci].

![The empty interior of a Danhai LRT tram, seats still wrapped in protective plastic ahead of entry into service](/images/danhai-lrt/interior-800.webp "A Danhai LRT tram's interior, seating still under delivery wrap. — Jason110234, CC BY-SA 4.0, Wikimedia Commons")

The name **行武者號** was chosen by public vote in 2015 from three candidates —
智多星, 行武者 and 海精靈 — with 行武者 winning; the livery was originally
designed yellow and changed to water-blue after the designer visited Tamsui,
according to Chinese Wikipedia's rolling-stock article[^zh-rollingstock]. That
detail was not independently corroborated against a primary NTMC or
manufacturer announcement.

## The Kaohsiung years, 2017–2020

For its first three years of revenue service, Danhai LRT was not operated by
the company that now runs it[^zh-danhai]. New Taipei Metro Corporation was
itself only incorporated on 26 January 2018 — after the Green Mountain
Line's construction was already well under way — and for the line's first
three years of operation, maintenance and day-to-day running were
subcontracted to
**Kaohsiung Rapid Transit Corporation**, the only company in Taiwan with
prior light rail operating experience, working under China Steel Corporation's
turnkey construction contract; NTMC's role in that period was administration,
finance, fares and the line's accounts[^zh-danhai]. Operation transferred to
NTMC directly once that three-year term ended in 2020, consistent with NTMC's
own current pages making no reference to Kaohsiung Rapid Transit
Corporation[^ntmc-train]. This account rests on a Chinese Wikipedia synthesis
of 2017 news coverage (TVBS and newtalk.tw); the original news articles could
not be fetched directly in this research (their web.archive.org snapshots
would not load), so the arrangement's exact contract value and the mechanics
of the 2020 handover remain unestablished beyond this secondary account.

## Public art and the route stamps

The Green Mountain Line's public art is a collaboration with illustrator
**幾米 (Jimmy Liao)**, based on his picture book 《閉上眼睛一下下》 ("Close
Your Eyes for a Moment"). NTMC's own page describes it as covering all 11
Green Mountain stations and as 幾米's first formal large-scale public art
commission, put together over "more than two years"[^ntmc-art]. The artist's
own site gives a slightly larger scope — 11 stations plus two bus shelters,
13 installations in total — and a longer timeline, from a 2015 invitation to
2018 completion, which it describes as "nearly four years," not two-plus; it
also notes the project won the 2020 National Excellence Construction Award in
the Best Environmental Culture category[^jimmyspa]. Both accounts are
published rather than reconciled. In keeping with this site's policy on
copyrighted artwork, this page states the facts of the collaboration without
describing what the installations depict.

Danhai also carries what Chinese Wikipedia describes as one of only two
surviving railway *route* stamps (路線章, as distinct from ordinary station
stamps) left anywhere in Taiwan, now kept at V01 Hongshulin — a claim read in
full on the page but attached to no footnote there, so it is published here
as an unattributed secondary claim rather than a well-sourced fact[^zh-danhai].

## Depot

**淡海機廠 (Danhai Depot)** sits beside V11 Kanding, at the end of the Green
Mountain Line, and houses the system's operations control centre, train
stabling and maintenance[^ltn-depot]. It was not ready when the Green Mountain
Line opened: a contemporaneous news report quotes a DORTS deputy director
attributing the delay to a land-acquisition dispute — the site was originally
private farmland whose owners disagreed on terms — and states the depot was
not expected to be finished until August 2019, about eight months after
passenger service began[^ltn-depot]. A figure of roughly 5.38 hectares, with
capacity for 16 trains, circulates widely for the depot's size, but the only
source located for it is a Chinese Wikipedia article that discloses on its
own page that it carries no references at all; that figure is not carried
onto this page's spec table as a result.

## Fares, bicycles and TPASS

A single Danhai LRT fare starts at NT$20 for journeys of 5 km or less, rising
to a maximum NT$25 for the full 7.3 km Green Mountain Line on its
own[^ntmc-fare-green]. Once Blue Sea Line phase 1 began charging, the
full-system maximum single fare, Hongshulin to Fisherman's Wharf, became
NT$30[^ntmc-fare-blue]. Electronic stored-value cards get an 8折 (20%)
discount off the base fare[^ntmc-fare-green]. A same-day, unlimited-ride
one-day pass costs NT$50[^ntmc-oneday]. The line is covered by the 基北北桃
1200 TPASS monthly pass, alongside the rest of New Taipei Metro, Taipei
Metro, Taoyuan Metro and regional buses[^ntmc-tpass].

Bicycles have been carried since 4 March 2019, at a flat NT$50 single fare
covering rider and bicycle together regardless of distance, with space for
four bicycles per train in designated zones in cars 2 and 4 (two per zone);
bicycles must be non-motorised and no larger than 180 × 120 × 70 cm, and
folding bicycles ride free without a separate ticket[^ntmc-bike].
