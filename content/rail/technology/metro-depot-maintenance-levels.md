## The short answer

Taipei's depot levels are capability labels. They describe how much inspection,
disassembly, repair and overhaul a facility is equipped to perform; they are
not a ranking of which depot is more important to passengers.[^inspection-rule][^dorts-depot-table]
The legal rule divides periodic vehicle inspection into five levels, then
requires the operator to define the detailed items and cycles for each vehicle
type and submit them for approval.[^inspection-rule]

The useful distinction is between Level 3 and Level 5. Level 3 is the point at
which the planning table adds wheel reprofiling and annual route-train
maintenance to the lower-level work.[^dorts-depot-table] Level 5 adds
network-wide inspection and maintenance, emergency repair, spares, major
overhaul and staff training.[^dorts-depot-table] A Level 3 depot is therefore
not a storage yard, and a Level 5 label does not by itself publish the current
allocation of every trainset.

## What each level means

| Level | DORTS planning capability |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Train storage, interior cleaning and the pre-departure safety check.[^dorts-depot-table] |
| 2 | Level 1 work, exterior cleaning and the monthly inspection task.[^dorts-depot-table] |
| 3 | Level 2 work, wheel reprofiling and annual route-train maintenance.[^dorts-depot-table] |
| 4 | Level 3 work, route-train emergency repair, spares, non-routine inspection and the three-year inspection task.[^dorts-depot-table] |
| 5 | Level 4 work, network-wide regular inspection and maintenance, emergency repair, spares, major overhaul and training.[^dorts-depot-table] |

The legal wording describes the same progression at the vehicle rather than
the building level. Level 3 uses partial disassembly for inspection,
adjustment, calibration and testing of propulsion, transmission, running,
braking and instrument equipment.[^inspection-rule] Level 4 applies disassembly
to specified parts of major systems.[^inspection-rule] Level 5 thoroughly checks
general components and overhauls important components.[^inspection-rule] Higher
levels include the lower-level inspection work, and a vehicle receiving Level 4
or higher work must undergo a test run under the rule's conditions.[^inspection-rule]

## Main versus secondary

DORTS's planning text gives the practical boundary: a main maintenance depot is
a complete base that can perform train overhaul and has the test track and
maintenance equipment needed for that role, while a secondary maintenance depot
handles work short of train overhaul and prepares special track-maintenance
vehicles.[^dorts-depot-planning] This is why the label matters when reading a
depot page: “secondary” does not mean “storage only”, and “main” does not mean
that every train in the system is currently based there.[^dorts-depot-planning]

For medium-capacity lines, DORTS's official planning presentation groups Muzha
and North Depot as secondary depots in the Level 2–4 range, and Neihu, South and
Jincheng as Level 5 main depots.[^dorts-depot-presentation] For high-capacity
lines, the same presentation lists Zhonghe at Level 1; Xindian, Nangang and
Xinzhuang at Level 3; Tucheng and Luzhou at Level 4; and Beitou at Level 5.[^dorts-depot-presentation]
These are the classifications in that DORTS planning record, not a current
unit-by-unit maintenance roster.

## Why “quasi-Level-5” stays qualified

DORTS's FAQ assigns Xinzhuang Level 3 work and says Luzhou handles
“quasi-Level-5” work, supports the Xinyi Line and has a test track.[^dorts-depot-faq]
The public record does not define which Level 5 capability the “quasi-” prefix
qualifies, so this page keeps the published wording rather than silently
promoting it to a full Level 5 claim.[^dorts-depot-faq] DORTS separately calls
South Depot a Level-5 full-function depot and describes the equipment needed for
its highest-level maintenance, alongside storage and dispatch for 64 Circular
Line trainsets.[^dorts-depot-faq]

The remaining boundary is important: the public rule delegates detailed
inspection items and cycles to each operator.[^inspection-rule] A depot grade
therefore explains facility capability, but it does not establish a current
calendar, an active fleet allocation, or which particular overhaul jobs are
being performed there today.[^inspection-rule][^dorts-depot-planning]

## What remains open

The opened primary sources do not provide one current maintenance schedule for
each fleet, a complete current depot responsibility matrix, or a definition of
Luzhou's quasi-Level-5 qualification.[^inspection-rule][^dorts-depot-faq] Those
remain TBC until an operator-approved schedule, current work-allocation record,
or design/acceptance document makes them explicit.
