Gauge is a property of a running system, not a synonym for capacity, power
collection or automation. The official records checked here describe three
different boundaries: Taipei's high-capacity steel-wheel railway, the
rubber-tyred Wenhu guideway and New Taipei's steel-wheel light rail.[^dorts-system-boundary][^dorts-val-guideway][^ntmc-lrt-trains]

## The 1,435 mm steel-wheel boundary

DORTS's track-engineering record specifies 1,435 +/- 1.5 mm for the basic
steel-wheel track plan of the Taipei high-capacity system.[^dorts-gauge-spec] A
separate DORTS system description states that the high-capacity metro systems
uniformly use 1,435 mm steel-wheel and steel-rail track.[^dorts-system-boundary]
This is the useful network-level rule for the high-capacity corridors; it is not
evidence that every other Taipei-region railway shares the same running
geometry.[^dorts-system-boundary]

The Circular Line belongs to the steel-wheel side of the medium-capacity
boundary. DORTS describes it as a steel-wheel, steel-rail, fully automatic
medium-capacity system.[^dorts-circular-system] The checked whole-line answer
supplies no numeric gauge, so the system classification is kept separate from
an unquoted 1,435 mm line figure.[^dorts-circular-system]

## Wenhu: guideway, not an ordinary steel-rail gauge

The original Muzha/Wenhu running system is described as rubber-tyred. DORTS
identifies the VAL256 as a medium-capacity rubber-wheel vehicle and describes a
concrete running surface with guide rails designed specifically for the
rubber-wheel system.[^dorts-val-guideway] The later Wenhu integration record
says the Neihu vehicles and the replacement CITYFLO 650 control system had to
remain compatible with the original Muzha system.[^dorts-wenhu-integration]

That is enough to identify Wenhu's guideway family, but not to assign it a
conventional steel-wheel gauge. The checked descriptions give the guideway and
compatibility requirements rather than a two-steel-rail measurement.[^dorts-val-guideway][^dorts-wenhu-integration] The correct entry is therefore
“rubber-tyred VAL-derived guideway”; a numeric gauge for Wenhu remains TBC
rather than being copied from the high-capacity specification.

## New Taipei light rail

New Taipei Metro's current train page says the Danhai and Ankeng light-rail
fleets use the same vehicle design, with 15 trains assigned to each line, and
describes the vehicles as steel-wheel and steel-rail.[^ntmc-lrt-trains] A 2025
DORTS energy-storage paper gives the vehicle-parameter table for this shared
light-rail design and lists a 1,435 mm gauge.[^dorts-lrt-ess]

The 1,435 mm value is published here as a vehicle-design parameter for the
common Danhai/Ankeng train set.[^dorts-lrt-ess][^ntmc-lrt-trains] It should not be expanded into a claim about
every light-rail construction tolerance, turnout or future extension without
an as-built infrastructure register.[^dorts-lrt-ess][^ntmc-lrt-trains]

## What remains open

The sources checked for this subject do not provide one current,
line-by-line infrastructure gauge register covering high-capacity metro,
Circular, Wenhu, Sanying, Danhai, Ankeng, the Taoyuan Airport MRT and the
Taoyuan Green Line.[^dorts-gauge-spec][^dorts-system-boundary][^dorts-val-guideway][^dorts-circular-system][^dorts-lrt-ess][^ntmc-lrt-trains] The page therefore reports exact values only where the
source states what is being measured, and leaves the remaining line-level
infrastructure fields TBC.

This distinction matters when comparing the network with TRA. TRA's own
route-history terminology uses 1,067 mm as its railway example while defining
1,435 mm as standard gauge; a metro source's 1,435 mm statement cannot be used
to rewrite the railway operator's current or historical segment record.[^tra-history]
