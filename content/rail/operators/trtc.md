## Founding and legal basis

The operator's own corporate timeline records a preparatory office
established 18 December 1990 and formal incorporation on 27 July
1994.[^trtc-timeline] A government company-registry open-data record for
TRTC's business number independently gives the same 27 July 1994
setup date.[^gcis-trtc-registry] The first chairman and general manager's
recorded term begins 29 July 1994, two days after incorporation, which is
internally consistent with that date.[^trtc-leadership]

TRTC's own organisational-structure page states its board and supervisors
are established under Taiwan's general Company Act, not a bespoke transit
statute, and that the board is convened by the chairman with a chief
secretary's office and an internal audit office
supporting it.[^trtc-org-structure] The company registry independently
confirms TRTC is registered with the Department of Commerce
Development.[^gcis-trtc-registry] Whether a specific transit statute
required or authorised Taipei City to found TRTC as a company, as opposed
to this being a discretionary Company Act incorporation, is not established
by the sources checked here and remains TBC.

## Capital and ownership

The company registry gives registered and paid-in capital of NT$10 billion,
1,000,000,000 shares at NT$10 par value, as most recently
amended.[^gcis-trtc-registry] That figure is a snapshot of the registry's
current record rather than a constant since 1994; no capital-increase event
appears on the operator's own timeline to explain any earlier
figure.[^gcis-trtc-registry][^trtc-timeline] TRTC is widely described as
majority-owned by the Taipei City Government with the Ministry of
Transportation and Communications and the New Taipei City Government
holding minority stakes, but the sources checked here do not establish
exact current percentages attributable to a primary source with
confidence, so specific ownership shares remain TBC.

## Overview

Taipei Rapid Transit Corporation (TRTC) is the operating company behind the Taipei Metro. Its own network material describes train formations and service characteristics, while the line pages in this guide keep government construction records separate from the operator's operating record.[^trtc-network] That distinction matters: a company can publish how a railway runs without being the agency that selected the alignment, let the civil contracts or accepted the works.

## Operations and fleet responsibility

TRTC's Wenhu Line introduction describes two pairs coupled as a four-car train, an 80 km/h maximum and approximately 420 passengers at the stated standing-density assumption.[^trtc-network] Those figures belong to that line's published vehicle description; they should not be copied across the high-capacity fleet, whose car lengths, formations and refurbishment histories differ. The rolling-stock pages therefore name their own contracts and technical sources rather than treating “Taipei Metro” as one uniform vehicle family.[^trtc-network]

The operator's vehicle-plant page assigns rolling stock, depot equipment and track-maintenance vehicles by line responsibility. It associates Tamsui–Xinyi work with Vehicle Plant One and Beitou workshop management, for example.[^trtc-duties] That is a maintenance-organisation statement, not a current overnight-stabling inventory: the cited page does not establish how many sets are parked at each depot on a given night.[^trtc-duties] The gap is left explicit rather than inferred from the line map.

## Service patterns and governance boundary

TRTC's published Bannan Line pattern gives a 06:00–24:00 service window, overlapping peak patterns and different one-way run times for the two operating patterns.[^trtc-bannan] The page is useful precisely because it states service as an operator responsibility; it does not turn those Bannan values into a claim about every TRTC line.[^trtc-bannan]

Construction, project approvals and civil-package history remain with the relevant Taipei or New Taipei rapid-transit engineering bureau.[^trtc-network] The operator inherits and runs those assets, and the source family here does not support a single consolidated TRTC procurement history for every line.[^trtc-network][^trtc-duties] Where a contract, incident or opening date is line-specific, it is cited on that line or vehicle page rather than attributed to the corporation in the abstract.[^trtc-network][^trtc-duties]

## Business scope beyond train operations

TRTC's own organisational chart names an Asset Development Division and a
Commercial Development Division as standing first-level departments
alongside its operations and engineering divisions, which the company
itself treats property and commercial development as a core
business.[^trtc-org-structure] The operator's timeline records taking over
operation of the Taipei Arena (臺北小巨蛋) on 1 September 2008, and
establishing a dedicated recreation-business subsidiary, 北捷遊憩事業股份
有限公司, on 1 July 2025.[^trtc-timeline] The Taipei Arena is a separate,
earlier and much smaller venue than the later-built Taipei Dome (臺北大巨蛋);
the two should not be confused.[^trtc-timeline] TRTC's own timeline also
records the Maokong Gondola entering revenue service on 4 July
2007.[^trtc-timeline] No source checked here breaks out non-fare revenue
(property, advertising, retail) as a share of TRTC's total income; that
figure remains TBC.

## Leadership

TRTC's own succession pages name the current chairman as 趙紹廉, in office
since 17 January 2023, and the current general manager as 黃清信, in office
since 5 August 2021, out of full recorded lists of 14 chairmen and 9
general managers since incorporation.[^trtc-leadership]
