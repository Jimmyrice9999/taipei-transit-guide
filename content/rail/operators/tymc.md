## Overview

Taoyuan Metro Corporation (TYMC) is the operator designated for the Airport MRT. Its own “About” page says the company was formed by the three city governments and that Taoyuan City Government was designated by the Ministry of Transportation and Communications to run the railway.[^tymc-about] This ownership and operating arrangement is distinct from the project history: the company runs the line, while the builder and approval milestones belong to the national and local agencies recorded in the project sources.

## Formation and project chronology

TYMC's milestone page records Ministry designation of the High Speed Rail Bureau as builder on 28 October 2003, central approval of the construction plan on 9 March 2004 and a groundbreaking ceremony on 26 June 2006.[^tymc-history] It dates the company's own establishment to 6 July 2010.[^tymc-history] The sequence shows why a company-establishment date cannot be used as the project start date: civil planning and construction decisions predate the operating corporation by years.

The operator's preparation period was operational work, not just a corporate formation step: TYMC says it joined construction testing, took over stations progressively, trained staff on equipment and station layouts, rehearsed operations, revised operating rules and helped contractors improve and maintain equipment.[^tymc-history-2026] Its own chronology then places the operating licence on 25 January 2017, trial operation on 2 February, formal A1–A21 service on 2 March, and the later extension to A22 on 31 July 2023.[^tymc-history-2026]

## Service patterns

The current timetable page distinguishes three stopping patterns. The express calls at A1, A3, A8, A12 and A13; a peak-only enhanced express adds A18 and A21; commuter trains call at every station.[^tymc-timetable] These are stopping-pattern definitions, not a promise that every departure uses the same pattern or that TDX's route objects can encode skip-stop service. The Airport MRT page therefore keeps station geometry and express semantics as separate evidence layers.[^tymc-timetable]

## Ownership and structure

TYMC's own "About" page states the ownership arrangement directly: 「由機場捷運線沿途所經過的桃園、新北、臺北等三地巿政府共同投資設立」— formed by joint investment from the Taoyuan, New Taipei and Taipei city governments along the Airport MRT route.[^tymc-about] Specific shareholding percentages for the three cities circulate widely in secondary and aggregator sources, commonly attributed to a MOTC transportation yearbook; that yearbook page could not be read directly for this entry (it returned as an unextractable compressed PDF), so those percentages are not published here as confirmed.[^tymc-about]

The company's own organizational-structure page names 13 primary divisions: Operations (運務處), Systems (系統處), Facilities (設施處), Safety and Security (工安處), Planning (企劃處), Business Development (事業發展處), General Affairs (總務處), Human Resources (人力資源處), Finance (財務處), an Accounting Office (會計室), a Legal Affairs Office (法務室), a Public Affairs Office (公共事務室), and an Audit Office (稽核室).[^tymc-org] Operations alone is further divided into eight secondary units, including separate control centres for the Airport Line and the Green Line, a vehicle operations centre, two station operations centres, and a ticketing centre.[^tymc-org]

The same current organisation page separates technical responsibility as well: Systems maintains trains, signalling and communications through a vehicle plant and electronics plant, while Facilities covers power, station mechanical/electrical/environmental systems, lifts, escalators, platform doors, track and civil infrastructure through specialist units.[^tymc-org-2026] That division is useful context for the project record's distinction between the Airport MRT's operating corporation and the agencies responsible for constructing future routes; it does not establish a Green Line fleet, timetable or opening date.

The operator's own chairman page names two people rather than a single current officeholder: 李憲明 (Li Hsien-ming), listed as interim/acting chairman, with a master's in architecture from National Cheng Kung University and a prior career as Taoyuan Deputy Mayor and City Government Secretary-General; and 沈志藏 (Shen Zhicang), with a master's in transportation from National Chiao Tung University and prior senior roles including president and vice-president at Taipei Metro (TRTC).[^tymc-chairman] The page itself does not state which is the currently serving chairman as opposed to a preceding one, so both are published here rather than one being picked.[^tymc-chairman] A fuller leadership succession list circulates in secondary sources but was not independently confirmed against a primary registry or company page for this entry and is not published here.

## Governance boundary and gaps

TYMC's operator pages establish who runs the Airport MRT and how its current stopping patterns are described.[^tymc-about][^tymc-timetable] They do not, in the sources read for this entry, provide a complete current shareholder table with percentages, a total staff count, a depot-by-depot train allocation or a single company-wide headway table. Those fields remain TBC. Line construction packages, extension contracts, fare tables and incidents are cited on the Airport MRT page and should not be reconstructed from the operator's short corporate history.[^tymc-history]
