## Overview

Xinzhuang Depot, 新莊機廠, is the Zhonghe–Xinlu Line's secondary depot, beside
O21 Huilong and spanning the New Taipei–Taoyuan boundary.[^dorts-planning][^dorts-line]
DORTS classifies it at level three and lists storage, inspection, maintenance,
washing, and storage for line power and track-maintenance equipment among its
functions.[^dorts-line]

TRTC groups Xinzhuang with Luzhou and Xindian workshops under Vehicle Plant
Three.[^trtc-duties] That unit maintains trains for two trunk lines and two
branches, but the page is not a depot-level roster. Current fleet allocation at
Xinzhuang therefore remains TBC.

## Losheng Sanatorium

The depot occupies part of the historic Losheng Sanatorium site. The Ministry
of Culture's heritage record says the institution opened in 1930 as Taiwan's
first public Hansen's-disease prevention and isolation facility.[^culture-losheng]
Its compound combined hospital, residential, work, religious and community
functions, reflecting the coercive isolation policy then used for Hansen's
disease.[^culture-losheng]

The same official history says the government selected Losheng as the depot
site in 1994, while new hospital construction and depot planning proceeded
together.[^culture-losheng] Metro work began in 2002; preservation and resident
rights disputes then interrupted and reshaped the project.[^culture-losheng]
This page does not reduce that history to a generic “heritage delay”: it changed
the depot's physical footprint and completion sequence.[^dorts-line]

## Redesign and site area

DORTS says the original site was approximately 17.4 hectares and was reduced to
13.9 hectares to accommodate the Losheng preservation plan.[^dorts-line] Another
paragraph on the same route page describes the earlier depot at approximately
17.5 hectares, while the depot-equipment table gives 17.2 hectares without
dating or defining that design stage.[^dorts-line][^dorts-equipment-table]
The 17.4/17.5 values are original-layout approximations and 13.9 is explicitly
post-redesign; the unexplained 17.2 value remains an unresolved intermediate or
different-boundary measurement.

The Ministry of Culture records the 30 May 2007 preservation decision as 39
buildings retained in place and six demolished.[^culture-losheng] Its overview
says nine buildings were to be rebuilt, while its longer history says ten were
to be relocated/reassembled or rebuilt.[^culture-losheng] That internal
nine-versus-ten conflict is published rather than silently corrected.

## Facilities and capacity

The pre-reduction DORTS description lists a train wash, entry and exit tracks,
maintenance and civil/track workshops, a test track, stabling, perimeter road,
car park, electrical facilities, administration/training and a wastewater
plant.[^dorts-line] A later equipment table says “no” test track.[^dorts-equipment-table]
The records may describe different layouts, but the opened source does not say
when the test-track provision changed; both statements are retained as a
design-stage conflict.

The planning inventory gives storage space for 16 trainsets, while the equipment
table lists 17 stabling-road positions.[^dorts-capacity][^dorts-equipment-table]
CK379A supplied fourteen categories of main maintenance equipment.[^dorts-equipment-list]

## Delayed activation

DORTS explicitly says preservation-driven facility rearrangement prevented the
depot from being completed when the Xinzhuang main line opened.[^dorts-line]
The line reached Huilong on 29 June 2013, but the depot did not undergo
preliminary inspection until 25 October 2020 or final inspection until 23
December 2020.[^dorts-line] It entered operation on 4 January 2021, more than
seven years after trains reached the terminus.[^dorts-line]

DORTS calls it the only initial/final inspection case without an associated
route section.[^dorts-line] Exact civil contract completion and current
equipment allocation remain TBC; the dated inspections and activation are the
official commissioning milestones recovered in this search.
