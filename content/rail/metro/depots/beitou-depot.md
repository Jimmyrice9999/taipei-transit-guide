## Overview

Beitou Depot, 北投機廠, is the Tamsui–Xinyi Line's main depot, on the south side
of Zhongyang North Road Section 3 in Beitou District.[^dorts-planning] DORTS
classifies the steel-wheel facility at level five, the highest grade shown in
its operating-depot inventory.[^dorts-capacity] A separate DORTS history calls
it the network's largest maintenance depot.[^dorts-history]

TRTC's current organisation places Beitou workshop management in Vehicle Plant
One. That unit maintains Tamsui–Xinyi trains, depot equipment and
track-maintenance vehicles.[^trtc-duties] The operator does not publish a
current model-by-model stabling roster on that page, so present fleet allocation
remains TBC rather than being inferred from the line's rolling-stock history.

## Construction history

Two official dates describe different starts. DORTS says CT107, the Beitou
Depot earthworks contract, was signed and began construction on 19 January
1988; it calls this the earliest construction contract on the Tamsui Line.[^dorts-line]
A later DORTS history dates the depot's formal groundbreaking to 22 July
1988.[^dorts-history] The January contract commencement and July ceremony are
therefore retained as separate milestones, not forced into a single start date.

## Site and ground conditions

The depot occupies a long strip on the Guandu alluvial plain. DORTS describes a
site about 350 metres north–south and 1,720 metres east–west, bounded by Guizikeng
Creek to the east and Zhongyang North Road to the north.[^dorts-geotechnical]
Before filling, most of it was farmland with ponds and a small number of one-
and two-storey houses; the surface fell from northeast to southwest.[^dorts-ground]

That ground dictated the earthworks. Beneath the depot, highly compressible,
water-rich clay thickens from about 15 metres on the north side to 50 metres on
the south.[^dorts-geotechnical][^dorts-ground] DORTS says the site had to be
raised for flood protection and later recorded maximum settlement of about
1.46 metres in the western part of fill area seven, with differential movement
damaging or impairing power, communications, fire, drainage and track
facilities.[^dorts-geotechnical]

## Area and capacity

Official figures disagree unless their labels are preserved. The geotechnical
manual twice describes approximately 40 hectares, while the network-planning
inventory and depot-equipment table publish 41 hectares.[^dorts-geotechnical][^dorts-ground][^dorts-capacity][^dorts-equipment-table]
No opened source reconciles the one-hectare difference, so both values are
reported; rounding or boundary choice is possible but not established.

Capacity has a second, more explicit difference. The planning inventory gives
33 trainsets of 儲車空間—storage space—while the equipment table gives 30
駐車軌列數, stabling-road positions.[^dorts-capacity][^dorts-equipment-table]
Those are differently named measures and are not converted into one generic
capacity figure.

## Maintenance equipment

DORTS lists a larger package family here than at the other initial-network
depots. CT309A supplied 28 categories of main maintenance equipment; CT309B
four dual electric/battery locomotives; CT309C twelve work cars; and CT309D 65
road–rail and auxiliary-equipment items.[^dorts-equipment-list] CT309E, CT309F,
CT309G and CT501A covered small tools, a driving simulator, maintenance test
instruments and track-maintenance machinery, with seven further kinds of
special track vehicle funded through TRTC.[^dorts-equipment-list]

The equipment table also records a test track.[^dorts-equipment-table] These
procurement records establish what the depot was equipped to do, but not which
original machines remain in service or which current train model occupies each
road. Those live asset details remain TBC.
