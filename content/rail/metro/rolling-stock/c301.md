## Overview

The 301 type was the first electric fleet bought for Taipei's high-capacity
metro.[^dorts-first-generation] Contract CT301 supplied 132 cars for the original Tamsui Line, and
DORTS's detailed history counts them as 22 fixed six-car trains.[^dorts-first-generation][^dorts-tamsui]
Passenger service began when the first Tamsui section opened on 28 March
1997.[^dorts-tamsui]

The contracting record is more precise than the usual “Kawasaki-built” label.
DORTS names the contractor as Union Rail Car Partnership (URC); a later DORTS
article describes URC as the United States subsidiary of Kawasaki Heavy
Industries.[^dorts-first-generation][^dorts-no48] No corporate filing defining
that relationship was found in this search, so the contractor and DORTS's
description are retained as two separate statements.

## Procurement and specification

The train contract formally began on 20 December 1989 at a stated value of
NT$4.5 billion.[^dorts-first-generation] Its first tender failed: the original
specification called for all six cars to be motored, which DORTS's retrospective
says made the offer too expensive.[^dorts-first-generation] The specification
was changed to 4M2T — four motor cars and two trailers — arranged as two
three-car DM1–T–M2 units and operated as a fixed six-car train at both peak and
off-peak times.[^dorts-first-generation]

That decision sat beside two other foundational choices. DORTS selected AC
induction motors after comparing them with DC traction, citing efficiency,
maintenance and reliability, and selected stainless steel rather than aluminium
after considering corrosion and welding in Taiwan's climate.[^dorts-first-generation]
The published design load was 60 seated and 310 standing passengers per car,
with a maximum operating speed of 80 km/h.[^dorts-first-generation]

## Design

The original electrical system collected 750 V DC from a bottom-contact third
rail and combined regenerative electrical braking with pneumatic friction
braking.[^dorts-first-generation] Its bolsterless two-axle bogies used conical
rubber primary suspension and air-spring secondary suspension, while ATP and
ATO supervised train operation.[^dorts-first-generation]

The stainless bodysides incline inward above the waist, a profile DORTS says
both fitted the tunnel envelope and left passengers a step back from a door
that opened unexpectedly.[^dorts-no48] Each 1,500 mm exterior-sliding door used
pneumatic drive, and each car had two independent split air-conditioning sets,
with condensers below and evaporators above the ceiling.[^dorts-first-generation][^dorts-no48]
The driving end used an FRP face panel and an anti-climber intended to resist
cars riding over one another in a collision.[^dorts-no48]

## Manufacture, delivery and testing

Final design review finished on 31 August 1992, followed by a prototype-train
factory ceremony in the United States that September.[^dorts-first-generation]
Prototype cars EMU 01/02 reached [Beitou Depot](/rail/metro/depots/beitou-depot/)
on 1 December 1992; production deliveries followed in March, May and August
1993, and all 132 cars had reached Beitou by November 1995.[^dorts-first-generation]

Testing lasted from December 1992 until May 1996.[^dorts-first-generation]
DORTS lists fourteen prototype-test groups covering coasting, abnormal
operation, wheel slip and slide, heat rise, regenerative power, clearances,
functions, noise, vibration, bogie stability, ride comfort, communications,
train-control simulation and electromagnetic interference.[^dorts-first-generation]
The operator completed the production-car tests and joint inspections in
November 1996 before passenger opening the following March.[^dorts-first-generation][^dorts-tamsui]

## Renewal

The fleet's systems have been renewed in stages rather than in one documented
mid-life package. By 2012, TRTC had renewed communications and passenger
information on all 22 trains, adding three-colour LED displays, wider viewing
angles, destination, door-side and time information, and line-colour frames on
the exterior destination displays.[^trtc-2012]

TRTC's 2014 report records preventive work on important traction circuit boards
and improvements to door guides; the 2015 report records nine overhauled trains
and traction and air-conditioning reset work.[^trtc-2014][^trtc-2015] A DORTS
engineering article defines the central traction change as replacement of GTO
inverter modules with IGBT modules to reduce energy use.[^dorts-energy] None of
the fetched primary pages gives a single completion date or names both the
original and replacement traction suppliers, so those details remain TBC.

## Fleet-count conflict

The detailed DORTS history and TRTC's 2012 report both give 22 trains, while
the fixed formation and total of 132 cars independently produce the same
arithmetic: 22 × 6 = 132.[^dorts-first-generation][^trtc-2012] DORTS's later
no. 48 article instead prints `132 輛 (21 列)` — 132 cars and 21 trains.[^dorts-no48]
That parenthetical conflicts with both the other official records and its own
six-car context; it is published here as an unresolved source error rather than
quietly discarded.
