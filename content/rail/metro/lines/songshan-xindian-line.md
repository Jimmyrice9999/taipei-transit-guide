The green line is not one railway but three, built twenty-three years apart
and now running as a single line: the Xindian Line (1991–1999), the
Xiaonanmen Line (2000), and the Songshan Line
(2006–2014)[^dorts-xindian][^dorts-xiaonanmen][^dorts-songshan]. It is underground for its entire
length[^dorts-xindian], and it carries one of the network's two short
branches — a single stop to Xiaobitan, worked by dedicated three-car trains
that climb from underground running to an elevated platform built inside the
line's own depot[^dorts-xindian][^dorts-xiaobitan-faq].

![The green-signed main entrance to Xindian Station, at the foot of forested hills](/images/songshan-xindian-line/station-800.webp "Xindian Station, the line's southern terminus, at the foot of the hills. — Foxy1219, CC BY-SA 4.0, Wikimedia Commons")

## Three projects, not one

DORTS's own project pages describe three separate builds that today read as
one line on the map. The **Xindian Line** project began construction on
1 March 1991 at NTU Hospital station and ran south 10.5 km to Xindian, laid
out as 11 stations[^dorts-xindian]. It opened in two pieces: NTU Hospital to
Guting on 24 December 1998, together with the Zhonghe Line, and Guting to
Xindian on 11 November 1999[^dorts-xindian]. The **Xiaonanmen Line**
project — 1.6 km, one station, Ximen to CKS Memorial Hall — was designed and
built alongside the Nangang Line specifically to give Bannan Line (blue
line) trains a route to Beitou Depot for maintenance; it opened on
31 August 2000, sixteen years before its passenger service formally became
part of this line[^dorts-xiaonanmen]. The **Songshan Line** project — 8.5 km,
8 underground stations including Ximen — was approved in 1997, financed in
2004, its earliest civil contract signed 29 March 2006, and it opened
15 November 2014, at which point the whole route from Songshan to Xindian
began running as one connected line[^dorts-songshan].

Add the three station counts and subtract the one Xindian Line station that
does not operate as part of this line (see below) and the total is 19 —
G01 to G19 — plus the Xiaobitan branch's G03A, for 20 stations in
all[^dorts-xindian][^dorts-xiaonanmen][^dorts-songshan][^tdx].

### Redesigned from part-elevated to fully underground before it opened

The original 70.3 km first-phase network, including this line, was approved
by the Executive Yuan on 4 April 1986 with a combined budget of
NT$444.4 billion[^dorts-xindian]. As approved, the alignment was **part
underground and part elevated**. DORTS's own record gives the reasons the
route was changed: the width of the streets it runs under, and the
environmental and visual impact an elevated structure would have had along
that alignment[^dorts-xindian]. The Executive Yuan agreed to build the whole
line underground on 20 May 1987[^dorts-xindian] — before a single station on
this line had opened.

### The line was numbered "3" for two years before it became "G"

TRTC's station-numbering scheme did not exist when the Songshan Line opened
in 2014. zh.wikipedia records that in October 2016 the company began
phasing in station numbers built from a line-colour letter plus a station
sequence number, and this line's earlier numeral, "3," was replaced with
"G"[^zh-songshan-xindian]. The date "3" was first assigned to the line is
not established from any source fetched for this page — TBC.

## The Xiaobitan branch

The branch itself is short — DORTS's own project record gives it as
approximately 1.5 km[^dorts-xindian], while zh.wikipedia and Taiwan's TDX
open-data platform both put it closer to 1.9 km[^zh-xiaobitan][^tdx] (see
"How long the line is," below). What makes it structurally unusual is that
it drops from underground running to grade at Qizhang, then climbs to an
elevated platform built inside Xindian Depot itself[^dorts-xindian].

DORTS's own planning FAQ explains why the branch cannot simply be folded into
main-line service. The Xiaobitan platform is long enough for three cars only,
so a full six-car main-line train arriving there could not open its rear
three cars' doors — and there is no way to disable just those doors on
demand, which the bureau describes as a fall risk[^dorts-xiaobitan-faq]. The
single-track section between Qizhang and Xiaobitan has only one platform
face in each direction, and the branch also has to accommodate trains moving
in and out of Xindian Depot[^dorts-xiaobitan-faq]. DORTS's conclusion is that
the branch is best kept as a dedicated three-car operation rather than
merged with the main line[^dorts-xiaobitan-faq].

That fleet is three three-car sets — nine cars in total — procured under
contract CH321A specifically for this branch[^dorts-fleet-faq].
zh.wikipedia names two of the three sets, 397 and 398, built by Kawasaki as
part of the C371 series-3 family, and describes them as genuine three-car
trains rather than a shortened six-car formation: three cars form one set,
and one set forms one train[^zh-xiaobitan]. The third set's number is not
confirmed by any source found for this page — TBC.

![The doors of a C371 train car, a green destination sign overhead reading G Xindian, next stop Nanjing Sanmin](/images/songshan-xindian-line/interior-800.webp "A C371 car's doors, signed for Xindian on the Songshan–Xindian Line, stopped at Nanjing Sanmin. — Solomon203, CC BY-SA 4.0, Wikimedia Commons")

### Why the branch exists

The branch was approved by the Executive Yuan on 17 September
1999[^dorts-xindian]. zh.wikipedia attributes the decision to lobbying by
nearby residents and by representatives at various levels of government, and
records the first documented planning reference as a 1999 Taipei County
government notice — not an earlier date[^zh-xiaobitan]. A News Lens feature
examining the branch's low ridership (about 4,400 boardings and alightings a
day) identifies 中央新村, a neighbourhood originally built to house retired
National Assembly representatives, as overlapping the area the branch was
built to serve, and frames the decision as representatives' pressure
succeeding over planners' objections[^thenewslens-xiaobitan]. A stronger
version of this account, naming a specific DORTS director and a construction
cost, could not be confirmed — the source carrying it returned a server
error on every attempt to read it in full, and this page does not repeat
what it could not verify.

## NTU Hospital: a station on this line's own construction project that isn't on this line

DORTS's Xindian Line record states, in the same sentence that gives the
project's route and length, that the project's own eleventh station — NTU
Hospital — "operates as part of the Tamsui-Xinyi Line"[^dorts-xindian]. That
is: the physical alignment this line's builder constructed runs from NTU
Hospital south to Xindian, but the northernmost of its eleven stations does
not carry this line's own trains today.

No source found for this page explains the mechanism beyond that one
sentence. The most consistent reading of the route geography is that the
NTU Hospital–CKS Memorial Hall section is shared trunk track that the
Tamsui-Xinyi Line has always used, while this line's own route reaches CKS
Memorial Hall from the north instead, via the separate Xiaonanmen Line
project[^dorts-xiaonanmen]. That reading is this page's inference from the
primary source and the network's layout, not a sentence DORTS states outright,
and it should be read as such.

## Beimen and the historic gate

G13 Beimen station takes its name from its position beside the historic
Taipei city gate[^zh-beimen]. Separately, DORTS's own record for the Bannan
Line states that its Nangang Line construction project had to tunnel beneath
the same gate — a first-grade historic monument — and names it as one of
that line's higher-difficulty sections[^dorts-bannan]. No source found for
this page states that the Songshan-Xindian Line's own tunnel near Beimen
carried a comparable engineering difficulty; what is sourced is the station's
naming and an archaeological display at platform level[^zh-beimen], not a
construction claim about this line's own bore. TBC.

## The end of cross-colour running

For sixteen years, Xindian Line trains and Tamsui Line trains ran through as
one service, and the pattern changed repeatedly as the wider network grew
around it[^zh-tamsui-xinyi] — a service so familiar that a retrospective
piece written years later remembers commuters at Taipei Main Station having
to watch the platform display closely to tell which of the two destinations
their train was heading to[^nownews-tamsui-xindian]:

| Date | Pattern |
| --- | --- |
| 24 Dec 1998 | Tamsui – Nanshijiao[^zh-tamsui-xinyi] |
| 11 Nov 1999 | Tamsui – Xindian, and Beitou – Nanshijiao[^zh-tamsui-xinyi] |
| 30 Sept 2012 | Beitou – Nanshijiao ends; Beitou – Taipower Building begins[^zh-tamsui-xinyi] |
| 24 Nov 2013 | Beitou – Taipower Building becomes Beitou – Xiangshan, crossed with Tamsui – Xindian[^zh-tamsui-xinyi] |
| 15 Nov 2014 | Songshan Line opens; Tamsui – Xindian through-running ends[^zh-tamsui-xinyi] |

On the night of 14 November 2014, the day before the Songshan section opened, a
closing ceremony was held at Beimen station and the last Tamsui–Xindian and
Xiaonanmen Line trains departed at midnight[^zh-songshan-xindian]. From
15 November, the former Tamsui Line settled into its current
Tamsui–Xiangshan and Beitou–Daan pattern, and this line ran on its own
colour for the first time[^zh-tamsui-xinyi].

| 1.94 km | The Xiaobitan branch alone, separate from the above | TDX[^tdx] |

The 18.77 km and 20.6 km figures are close, and the roughly 1.8 km gap
between them is plausibly the NTU Hospital–CKS Memorial Hall stretch, which
DORTS's 10.5 km Xindian Line figure includes but which does not operate as
part of this line[^dorts-xindian] — but that segment's own length was not
found anywhere in this research, so the arithmetic is not confirmed. The
21.3 km zh.wikipedia figure does not resolve cleanly against either of the
others from what was found[^zh-songshan-xindian]. All four are published
here rather than picked between.

The Xiaobitan branch has the same problem in miniature: DORTS's own figure is
about 1.5 km[^dorts-xindian], while both zh.wikipedia and TDX put it at about
1.9 km[^zh-xiaobitan][^tdx] — a 25–30% gap between a primary source and two
independent secondary/data sources that agree closely with each other.

## Service pattern today

TRTC runs three named workings on this line: the full Songshan–Xindian
service, a shorter Songshan–Taipower Building working, and the Qizhang–
Xiaobitan branch[^trtc-headways-g]. On the shared trunk, weekday peak
headways run about 4–6 minutes, off-peak about 6–8 minutes, and about
12 minutes after 23:00; the Xiaobitan branch runs every 12–20 minutes
throughout the day, with last trains at 23:57 from Xiaobitan and 00:09 from
Qizhang[^trtc-headways-g]. End to end, Songshan–Xindian takes about
37 minutes and Songshan–Taipower Building about 23[^trtc-headways-g].
