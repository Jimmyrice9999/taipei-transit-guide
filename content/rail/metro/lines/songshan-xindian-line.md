## Overview

The Songshan–Xindian Line is a single operating railway assembled from three
construction projects: the Xindian Line, the Xiaonanmen Line and the Songshan
Line. DORTS records their different approval, construction and opening dates,
while the 15 November 2014 Songshan opening created the continuous
Songshan–Xindian service seen on the map today.[^dorts-xindian][^dorts-xiaonanmen][^dorts-songshan][^ey-songshan-opening]

The main line is underground from Songshan to Xindian. Its short Xiaobitan
branch diverges at Qizhang, changes from underground to grade and then rises to
an elevated platform inside Xindian Depot; dedicated three-car trains serve
that platform.[^dorts-xindian][^dorts-xiaobitan-faq] The current TDX route data
lists G01 through G19 on the main route and G03A on the branch, twenty stations
in the operating sequence.[^tdx]

## Route and alignment

The Xindian project runs south from NTU Hospital through Guting and CKS
Memorial Hall to Xindian. DORTS describes an approximately 10.5 km project with
eleven stations, but explicitly says NTU Hospital operates as part of the
Tamsui–Xinyi Line; the physical project and the present green-line service
therefore have different northern boundaries.[^dorts-xindian]

The 1.6 km Xiaonanmen project links Ximen to CKS Memorial Hall.[^dorts-xiaonanmen] It was designed
and built with the Nangang project to provide Bannan Line trains access to
maintenance facilities, then became part of Songshan–Xindian passenger service
when the Songshan section opened.[^dorts-xiaonanmen]

The Songshan project runs from Ximen through Beimen, Zhongshan, Songjiang
Nanjing, Nanjing Fuxing, Taipei Arena and Nanjing Sanmin to Songshan. DORTS's
project page calls it approximately 8.5 km, eight underground stations including
Ximen, and fully underground; the 2014 technical article uses the earlier
working labels G13, G14, G16, G17, G18, G19, `G21` and `G22` for that same sequence.[^dorts-songshan][^dorts-songshan-planning]

## Planning and approvals

The first 70.3 km Taipei rapid-transit network, including the future Xindian
corridor, received Executive Yuan approval on 4 April 1986.[^dorts-xindian] The approved form
was partly elevated and partly underground; DORTS attributes the later change
to narrow streets and the environmental and visual impact of elevated
structures, and records Executive Yuan agreement to an entirely underground
line on 20 May 1987.[^dorts-xindian]

The Songshan planning record traces a 1993 network review, a 1994 corridor
report, route-plan approval in 1997, financial-plan approval in 2004, civil
construction beginning in 2006 and opening in 2014.[^dorts-songshan-planning]
The DORTS project page gives the more precise approval date of 21 November
1997, the financial approval date of 2 December 2004 and the first civil
contract signing on 29 March 2006.[^dorts-songshan]

Xindian construction began at NTU Hospital on 1 March 1991.[^dorts-xindian] The northern
section to Guting opened on 24 December 1998; Guting to Xindian opened on
11 November 1999.[^dorts-xindian] The Xiaobitan branch received Executive Yuan approval on 17
September 1999, and the Xiaonanmen project opened on 31 August 2000.[^dorts-xindian][^dorts-xiaonanmen]

## Construction history

The Songshan civil works were split into CG590A, CG590B and CG590C. DORTS's
milestone article identifies CG590A as beginning on 18 December 2006 with Maeda
as contractor, CG590B as beginning on 27 August 2006 with Rongmin and Okumura
in a joint venture, and CG590C as beginning on 15 August 2006 with Daxin.[^dorts-songshan-milestone]
The route project page lists the same three-contract structure and places the
earliest contract signing on 29 March 2006.[^dorts-songshan]

The technical-document record says Xindian packages CH218, CH221, CH222 and
CH224 were put to international tender where domestic technical capacity was
insufficient, with other packages using domestic firms and foreign technical
cooperation.[^dorts-xindian-construction] This procurement history matters
because the Xindian and Songshan projects were delivered in different eras and
under different package structures, even though today’s passenger map presents
one green route.[^dorts-xindian-construction][^dorts-songshan-milestone]

## Engineering and construction

The original 70.3 km first-phase network, including this line, was approved
by the Executive Yuan on 4 April 1986 with a combined budget of
NT$444.4 billion[^dorts-xindian]. As approved, the alignment was **part
underground and part elevated**. DORTS's own record gives the reasons the
route was changed: the width of the streets it runs under, and the
environmental and visual impact an elevated structure would have had along
that alignment[^dorts-xindian]. The Executive Yuan agreed to build the whole
line underground on 20 May 1987[^dorts-xindian] — before a single station on
this line had opened.

The Songshan alignment had to pass beneath active TRA and HSR corridors,
existing roads, deep foundations and operating metro infrastructure. DORTS
describes bored tunnels beneath the Beimen overpass and railway tunnel, a
minimum clearance requirement, cut-and-cover work at some stations, difficult
crossings near Zhongshan and Songshan, and a tail track because a new depot site
was not available.[^dorts-songshan-planning][^dorts-songshan-milestone]

The milestone article records station boxes roughly 20–32 m deep.[^dorts-songshan-milestone] It describes
soft clay and old foundations beneath Songshan station, narrow Bade Road, piles
near the former Asiaworld site, and tunnelling 16.65 m below an operating
Wenhu-line station. At Songjiang Nanjing, ten evacuation passages were manually
constructed between tunnels inside an operating station, with grouting at more
than 20 m depth.[^dorts-songshan-milestone]

Beimen required a heritage procedure as well as a tunnel design. The DORTS
heritage paper says contracts required an immediate stop when suspected relics
appeared, followed by cultural-property review and expert assessment. It records
Qing-period remains, drainage and foundations in the station area, and a 4.8 m
overlap between the station and the 1909 Taipei Workshop; the workshop was moved
about 30 m southeast after reinforcement and design-build changes.[^dorts-beimen-heritage]

The power system was designed as part of the same engineering response.[^dorts-songshan-power] DORTS
describes 161 kV and 22 kV gas-insulated equipment, optical-fibre temperature
monitoring for tunnel cables, stray-current monitoring and high-impedance
ground-fault warning. The Songshan main substation was placed underground at
Taipei Arena to protect the surrounding urban view.[^dorts-songshan-power]

On the earlier Xindian works, the CH218 geotechnical article records face
pressure and water-pressure monitoring during shield tunnelling. Adjusting the
face pressure and applying backfill and secondary grouting reduced observed
surface settlement from 54 mm to about 25 mm, with quality checks continuing
through the work.[^dorts-xindian-geotech]

## Branch and depot

The branch itself is short — DORTS's own project record gives it as
approximately 1.5 km[^dorts-xindian], while zh.wikipedia and Taiwan's TDX
open-data platform both put it closer to 1.9 km[^zh-xiaobitan][^tdx] (see
"Length records and conflicts," below).[^dorts-xindian][^zh-xiaobitan][^tdx] What makes it structurally unusual is that
it drops from underground running to grade at Qizhang, then climbs to an
elevated platform built inside Xindian Depot itself[^dorts-xindian].

DORTS's own planning FAQ explains why the branch cannot simply be folded into
main-line service. The Xiaobitan platform is long enough for three cars only,
so a full six-car main-line train arriving there could not open its rear
three cars' doors — and there is no way to disable just those doors on
demand, which the bureau describes as a fall risk[^dorts-xiaobitan-faq]. The
single-track section between Qizhang and Xiaobitan has only one platform
face in each direction, and the branch also has to accommodate trains moving
in and out of Xindian Depot[^dorts-xiaobitan-faq]. DORTS's conclusion is that
the branch is best kept as a dedicated three-car operation rather than
merged with the main line[^dorts-xiaobitan-faq].

## Rolling stock

DORTS's procurement FAQ lists three three-car sets — nine cars in total — under
contract CH321A specifically for this branch[^dorts-fleet-faq].
zh.wikipedia names two of the three sets, 397 and 398, built by Kawasaki as
part of the C371 series-3 family, and describes them as genuine three-car
trains rather than a shortened six-car formation: three cars form one set,
and one set forms one train[^zh-xiaobitan]. The third set's number is not
confirmed by the cited secondary account — TBC.

DORTS's same procurement table lists ten six-car sets, sixty cars, under
Songshan contract CG391.[^dorts-fleet-faq] These are procurement quantities
tied to projects, not a present-day dispatch roster: DORTS explains that
high-capacity batches are intended to be usable across the network, so current
line allocation cannot be inferred safely from the old contract names alone.[^dorts-fleet-faq]

### Planning and catchment

The branch was approved by the Executive Yuan on 17 September
1999[^dorts-xindian]. zh.wikipedia attributes the decision to lobbying by
nearby residents and by representatives at various levels of government, and
records the first documented planning reference as a 1999 Taipei County
government notice — not an earlier date[^zh-xiaobitan]. A News Lens feature
examining the branch's low ridership (about 4,400 boardings and alightings a
day) identifies 中央新村, a neighbourhood originally built to house retired
National Assembly representatives, as overlapping the area the branch was
built to serve, and frames the decision as representatives' pressure
succeeding over planners' objections[^thenewslens-xiaobitan]. The named-director
and construction-cost version is not included because the underlying page was
not readable in full.[^thenewslens-xiaobitan]

## Stations and interchange

The current TDX sequence is G01 Xindian, G02 Xindian District Office, G03
Qizhang, G04 Dapinglin, G05 Jingmei, G06 Wanlong, G07 Gongguan, G08 Taipower
Building, G09 Guting, G10 CKS Memorial Hall, G11 Xiaonanmen, G12 Ximen, G13
Beimen, G14 Zhongshan, G15 Songjiang Nanjing, G16 Nanjing Fuxing, G17 Taipei
Arena, G18 Nanjing Sanmin and G19 Songshan, with G03A Xiaobitan as the branch
station.[^tdx] This current numbering should not be confused with the 2014
technical article's working labels, in which Ximen appears as G13 and Songshan
as `G22`.[^dorts-songshan-planning][^dorts-songshan-milestone]

The line interchanges with the Tamsui–Xinyi Line at Guting and CKS Memorial
Hall, with the Bannan Line at Ximen and Guting, with the Wenhu Line at Nanjing
Fuxing, with the Airport MRT at Beimen, and with TRA at Songshan. DORTS's
Songshan planning article describes six interchange stations in the project
section and specifically identifies Beimen's airport connection and Songshan's
railway connection.[^dorts-songshan-planning]

The NTU Hospital anomaly is a project-boundary issue rather than a missing
station in the built network. DORTS counts it among the Xindian project's
eleven stations but states that it operates as part of the Tamsui–Xinyi Line;
TDX's current green-line sequence starts with G01 Xindian.[^dorts-xindian][^tdx]

## History, naming and public art

Beimen takes its name from the historic north gate. The DORTS heritage paper
records the station-area archaeological work, Qing-period remains, and the
railway heritage buildings that shaped the final station treatment.[^dorts-beimen-heritage]
The current code is G13; the 2014 technical article's contemporary working
label for Beimen was G14, so its labels are historical identifiers rather than
current badges.[^tdx][^dorts-songshan-planning]

The Songshan technical article records public-art commissions at Zhongshan,
Nanjing Fuxing, Taipei Arena, Nanjing Sanmin and Songshan, including the
crystalline work 晶透, the platform work 流線月台 and works associated with the
historic urban setting.[^dorts-songshan-milestone]

TRTC records that the earlier numeral "3" was replaced by the colour-letter
code G in October 2016.[^zh-songshan-xindian] The exact date on which the numeral 3 was first assigned
to this line remains TBC.[^zh-songshan-xindian]

### Cross-colour service history

For sixteen years, Xindian Line trains and Tamsui Line trains ran through as
one service while the wider network grew around them.[^dorts-songshan-planning] DORTS's operating-pattern
article records the 1998 Zhonghe–Tamsui opening, the 1999 Tamsui–Xindian and
Beitou–Nanshijiao pattern, later Beitou–Taipower Building and Xiangshan changes,
and the 2014 separation into independent coloured lines.[^dorts-songshan-planning]

On the night of 14 November 2014, the day before the Songshan section opened, a
closing ceremony was held at Beimen.[^ey-songshan-opening] The last Tamsui–Xindian and Xiaonanmen
trains departed at midnight. From 15 November, the former through service was
replaced by Songshan–Xindian and Tamsui–Xiangshan operations.[^ey-songshan-opening][^dorts-songshan-planning]

## Operations

TRTC runs three named workings on this line: the full Songshan–Xindian
service, a shorter Songshan–Taipower Building working, and the Qizhang–
Xiaobitan branch[^trtc-headways-g]. On the shared trunk, weekday peak
headways run about 4–6 minutes, off-peak about 6–8 minutes, and about
12 minutes after 23:00; the Xiaobitan branch runs every 12–20 minutes
throughout the day, with last trains at 23:57 from Xiaobitan and 00:09 from
Qizhang[^trtc-headways-g]. End to end, Songshan–Xindian takes about
37 minutes and Songshan–Taipower Building about 23[^trtc-headways-g].

TRTC's separate peak clarification gives a more granular picture: approximately
three-minute Songshan–Taipower Building service, with some six-minute gaps on
the shared section because Xiaobitan trains are inserted through shared Qizhang
infrastructure.[^trtc-peak-clarification] The two official pages describe
different levels of the operating plan and are both retained.

## Incidents and ridership history

The Songshan technical article reports roughly 400,000 passenger trips per day
in the 2014–2015 opening period.[^dorts-songshan-planning] Within the same
network, the article gives about 1.98
million daily trips in 2014 and 3.028 million in 2015. These are the article's
historical network and line measures.[^dorts-songshan-planning]

On 13 September 2016 an engineering vehicle derailed near Xiaonanmen.[^dorts-songshan-planning] The
response used single-track bidirectional working between Xiaonanmen and
Zhongshan for 11 hours 39 minutes, shortened service intervals on the remaining
segments and a bus bridge while the obstruction was cleared.[^dorts-songshan-planning]

The incident illustrates why the line's history includes operating interfaces:
the Xiaonanmen segment was first built for Bannan Line depot access, then became
a passenger section of the green line, and later formed the centre of a
disruption-management plan.[^dorts-xiaonanmen][^dorts-songshan-planning]
