## Overview

The Sanying Line is New Taipei's first metro of its own: the operator describes
it as the city's first autonomously led medium-capacity system, rather than a
line delivered for New Taipei by Taipei's DORTS.[^ntmetro-trains] It opened on
**30 June 2026** with 12 elevated stations over 14.29 km, linking the Bannan
Line at Dingpu to Sanxia and Yingge and to the TRA main line at Yingge
station.[^ntpc-dorts-route][^ntmetro-opening]

That opening followed a feasibility approval on 3 September 2012, comprehensive-
planning approval on 2 June 2015, advance works from October 2014, and main works
from July 2016.[^ntpc-dorts-route] The line completed stability testing on
7 April 2026, preliminary inspection on 26 April, the Ministry of
Transportation's final inspection on 7 June and its operating permit on
23 June.[^ntpc-dorts-route][^motc-inspection]

## Route and alignment

From LB01 beside the Bannan Line's Dingpu terminus, the viaduct follows
Zhongyang Road Section 4, crosses Freeway 3 and reaches Jieshou Road in Sanxia.
It turns onto the Hengxi riverside road and the new Longpu Road approach to the
Taipei University district, passing the National Academy for Educational
Research before following Sanshu, Guoqing and Fuxing roads.[^ntpc-dorts-route]

The alignment then climbs across Freeway 3 and the Dahan River into Yingge,
follows the Yingge River, crosses Wenhua Road and the TRA main line, and bends
north past Park No. 3. A second crossing of the main line brings it to Yingtao
Road and the LB12 terminus at Fude 1st Road; tail tracks there preserve an
interface for the Bade extension.[^ntpc-dorts-route]

This is not a simple road-median railway. The city's early-works account lists
crossings of Freeway 3, the TRA railway, the Dahan, Yingge, Sanxia and Hengxi
waterways.[^ntpc-early-works] The 2018 engineering report measured 13,452 m of
bridge structures: 6,450 m of precast or cast-in-place prestressed U-girder,
2,052 m of prestressed box girder, 4,375 m of steel box girder and 575 m of
special arch structures.[^ntpc-technical-report]

## Planning and approvals

The project moved while its approved programme kept changing. The 2015 plan
release said the National Development Council had completed its review, that
basic design and project management were already proceeding in parallel, and
that a construction access road beside Freeway 3 would let the viaduct works
start promptly.[^ntpc-ndc-2015] The Executive Yuan approved the comprehensive
plan that June; a first revised plan was not approved until October 2023, with
an end-2025 completion target and project period through 2027.[^ntpc-dorts-route]

The live builder record lists four submissions of a second revised plan between
February 2025 and July 2026.[^ntpc-dorts-route] It nevertheless records the
railway opening in June 2026 while that revision remained under review — a useful
distinction between the passenger railway being complete and the wider funded
project, including land and close-out work, reaching its administrative end.

### The rejected underground alternative

The elevated scheme was contested in Tucheng. At an August 2016 public meeting,
residents opposed an elevated medium-capacity line on Zhongyang Road and asked
for the underground, high-capacity Bannan Line to continue from Dingpu to LB02,
with Sanying beginning there.[^ntpc-elevated-case] The road was planned as 30 m
wide but some sections then provided only 18–20 m.[^ntpc-elevated-case]

New Taipei DORTS said the underground station and crossover would still require
demolition, place shield tunnels beneath houses, and meet a Dingpu station built
without an extension interface.[^ntpc-elevated-case] Taipei DORTS additionally
identified operating disruption, land acquisition, demolition, poor financial
performance and changes to an approved project; New Taipei therefore retained
the elevated scheme.[^ntpc-elevated-case] Those are the agencies' stated reasons,
not an independent comparison of the alternatives.

## Engineering and construction

### Ground, foundations and tight curves

The basic alignment was shaped to limit land acquisition and demolition, which
produced a **50 m minimum curve radius** and a **4.535% maximum gradient**.[^ntpc-technical-report]
The engineering report describes Tucheng largely as the Dahan River's right-bank
hills and alluvial plain, then a sequence of river terraces toward Yingge.
Mountain sections expose sandstone and shale formations; the newer terraces and
alluvium carry gravel, sand and silt over rock, with groundwater commonly
3.4–13.7 m below ground.[^ntpc-technical-report]

The designers preferred well foundations where ground and groundwater allowed,
especially on 18–20 m roads where a smaller work area reduced traffic impact.[^ntpc-technical-report]
Pile foundations were retained for difficult dewatering or conflicts with major
buried utilities.[^ntpc-technical-report] The report's comparison put a typical
well foundation at about 20 m² and 21 days against about 57 m² and 30 days for a
pile-cap alternative; those are design comparisons, not whole-project averages.[^ntpc-technical-report]

### Bridges, BIM and the single design-build contract

For ordinary spans under 35 m the project made the prestressed U-girder its
default.[^ntpc-technical-report] Its low structural depth, continuous appearance and ability to carry a
90-degree curve were the stated reasons; the technical report expected roughly
800 m of U-girder erection work.[^ntpc-technical-report] At the Sanxia River the
metro bridge was planned parallel to two road bridges with a 6 m clear gap and a
180 m arch span. Across the Dahan River the report shows a 520 m crossing with a
100 m deep-channel zone and a flying-bird arch concept.[^ntpc-technical-report]

The civil, water-and-environmental, railway systems and track works were procured
together as one large design-build contract using a best-value award.[^ntpc-technical-report]
The report says 22 firms returned 502 comments in the first consultation, ten
returned 235 in the second, and 17 returned 431 during public review before
three teams ultimately tendered.[^ntpc-technical-report] The city also combined
a three-dimensional terrain model with BIM to simulate stations, bridges,
construction space and later operations — its first such metro application in
New Taipei.[^ntpc-early-works]

## Stations and interchanges

All 12 passenger stations are elevated.[^motc-inspection] LB01 connects with the
Bannan Line at Dingpu, while LB08 connects the metro with TRA Yingge station;
the opening notice presents those two transfers as the route into the Taipei
metro network and the conventional railway.[^ntmetro-opening]

The technical report's design-stage station programme used two standard forms.
Road-median stations were about 58 m long and 21 m wide, while constrained sites
used a side-platform form intended to reduce the bulk over the road.[^ntpc-technical-report]
It also required station entrances to include level-access or lift provision and
parking for motorcycles and bicycles.[^ntpc-technical-report]

Public art was planned as a line-wide system rather than a late collection of
objects. By July 2026 the bureau described three themed trains — Hakka, New
Taipei City Art Museum and Indigenous — alongside station works tied to local
culture.[^ntpc-first-month] The Indigenous train uses Amis clothing and weaving
motifs, an Amis *'alofo* bag, and Atayal and Paiwan characters; the bureau says
these trains are dispatched randomly rather than to a published timetable.[^ntpc-first-month]

## Rolling stock

Hitachi Rail designed the steel-wheel, steel-rail trains and they were
manufactured in Japan.[^ntmetro-trains] The fleet is **29 two-car trains**, not
29 cars. Each aluminium train is 35 m long, 2.6 m wide and 3.8 m high, carries
330 people — 50 seated and 280 standing — and uses electric and hydraulic
braking.[^ntmetro-trains]

The operator publishes an **80 km/h maximum operating speed**, 750 V DC third-
rail supply and communications-based train control.[^ntmetro-trains] The 2018
functional specification required CBTC, a system headway of 90 seconds or less
and GoA 4 unattended operation, but specified a design maximum speed of at least
70 km/h.[^ntpc-technical-report] Those figures describe different things: the
earlier minimum performance requirement and the delivered fleet's maximum
operating speed.

## Operations

Opening service was advertised at roughly six-minute peak and eight-minute off-
peak headways, with about 30 minutes end to end.[^ntmetro-opening] The free-trial
period ran through 31 August 2026: stored-value-card users tapping in and out
paid nothing.[^ntmetro-opening] Hours began at 10:00–20:00, expanded to
08:00–22:00 from 1 August and to 06:00–24:00 from 16 August.[^ntpc-august-hours][^ntmetro-fare-notice]

The published normal fare band is NT$20–35 by distance, but the operator says
the actual charging date will be announced separately.[^ntmetro-opening][^ntmetro-fare-notice]
Ending the free trial therefore does not, by itself, establish when fares began.

By 25 July the bureau counted more than **750,000 journeys**, an average of
53,000 on holidays, and 72,000 station exits on 4 July during the museum's
fireworks and light show.[^ntpc-first-month] For that event it says trains ran
at a 2.2-minute interval.[^ntpc-first-month] That demonstrated a special-event
service, not the normal opening headway and not proof that the full line can
sustain the 2018 design-stage 2.2-minute pattern in every operating condition.

## Bade extension

The approved extension continues 4.03 km from LB12 with three stations and a
published budget of NT$16.058 billion.[^ntpc-dorts-bade] `LB12a` and `LB13` are
planned elevated; the line then descends beneath the south side of Danan Forest
Park to underground `LB14`, built together with Taoyuan Green Line station
`G04`.[^ntpc-dorts-bade]

The builder records feasibility approval on 13 November 2019, environmental-
assessment acceptance on 2 November 2023 and Executive Yuan approval of the
comprehensive plan on **1 August 2025**.[^ntpc-dorts-bade] Its header gives an
expected completion in **2034**.[^ntpc-dorts-bade] The same live page still
labels the introductory alignment 「本計畫尚未核定」 — “the project remains
unapproved.”[^ntpc-dorts-bade] That sentence conflicts with the approval
timeline on the page and is published here as stale official copy, not silently
resolved.

## Where the sources disagree

**Project cost.** Three official values survive. The October 2014 early-works
release gave **NT$55.022 billion**.[^ntpc-early-works] The April 2015 NDC-stage
release and August 2018 engineering report gave **NT$50.5 billion**; the report
says value engineering used shorter trains, tighter headways, shorter stations,
well foundations and U-girders to reduce the earlier NT$55 billion proposal.[^ntpc-ndc-2015][^ntpc-technical-report]
The live builder page now gives **NT$50.200 billion**.[^ntpc-dorts-route] These
are dated project estimates or current programme figures, not interchangeable
measurements of final expenditure.

**Journey and peak interval.** In 2014 the city forecast an end-to-end journey
of no more than **23 minutes** and a **three-minute** peak interval.[^ntpc-early-works]
Opening service was about **30 minutes** and **six minutes**.[^ntmetro-opening]
The engineering report's mature design pattern put LB01–LB08 at 2.2 minutes but
LB08–LB12 at 4.4 minutes.[^ntpc-technical-report] The sources describe an early
forecast, initial operations and a design-year branching pattern respectively;
none is substituted for another.

**Bade approval.** The extension page's dated record says the Executive Yuan
approved the comprehensive plan on 1 August 2025, while its introduction says
the project has not been approved.[^ntpc-dorts-bade] The page gives no update
date for the stale sentence, so its provenance cannot be reconstructed.
