At 07:58 on 3 April 2024 a magnitude 7.2 earthquake off Shoufeng in Hualien
County destroyed the pot bearings between the steel box girders and the piers on
the Circular Line's viaduct through Zhonghe and along Jingping Road, shoving
eleven girders out of position, deforming the track, damaging 139 items of
equipment, and closing the railway between Banqiao and Zhonghe[^cy-114]. No
train was on the damaged section when it happened[^cy-114].

Eight months later the line reopened. Between those two dates the Control Yuan —
Taiwan's audit and censure branch — opened an investigation on its own motion,
and its report, case 114交調0005, reviewed on 11 March 2025 and published on 17
June 2025 by commissioners 趙永清 and 葉宜津, is the fullest published account of
what happened and why[^cy-114]. It is the source for most of this page.

## What the investigation established

Read the report carefully and the first thing to notice is what is *not* a
finding[^cy-114]. Its terms of reference recite the allegation it was asked to look at —
eleven suspected displacements, a repair initially costed at over NT$400
million, at least a year to fix[^cy-114]. Those are the reported claims the
Control Yuan set out to test, not its conclusions, and they are quoted here as
such.

### The mechanism

The report's first finding is a construction fault, stated without hedging: the
contractor, BES Engineering (中華工程), **did not build to the
drawings**[^cy-114]. It
milled through the grade-adjustment plate (調坡板) that sits between the girder
and the bearing, leaving void space above the locating pin; under the earthquake
the pin jumped upward into that void, its base left the interface between the
upper and lower plates of the pot bearing, and it lost its ability to resist
shear[^cy-114].

The numbers behind that sentence are the heart of the case[^cy-114]:

| Dimension | Depth |
| --- | --- |
| Milled recess, as shown on the contractor's own shop drawing | 5 mm[^cy-114] |
| Milled recess, as measured on site by the investigation | 41 mm[^cy-114] |
| Void left above the pin | 36 mm[^cy-114] |
| Height of the pin itself | 40 mm[^cy-114] |

36 mm of void plus the 5 mm of engagement the drawing required is 41 mm — the
report's own arithmetic — and 41 mm is more than enough to swallow a pin only 40
mm tall[^cy-114]. The pins were found by the New Taipei City Civil Engineers
Association, which jacked the damaged bridges up and discovered them lodged in
the upper plate of the bearing or lying on the pier cap where they had
fallen[^cy-114].

The section was built under contract CF651B, part of the CF650 section works,
and the structures carried a warranty running to 12 March 2026[^cy-114] — which
is why the timing of the claim against the contractor matters.

### The shaking was smaller than the design case

This is the part most likely to be misread. The viaducts were designed to the
Ministry of Transportation and Communications' 2006 railway bridge seismic
design code, at a Taipei basin design ground motion of 0.24 g, with an
importance factor of 1.2 because metro bridges are classed as important
structures — so the minimum design seismic force is 0.288 g, which on the
Central Weather Administration's 2020 intensity scale falls in the
6-weak band[^cy-114]. The "designed for 6-weak" formulation is the report's own
arithmetic on 0.24 g × 1.2 followed by a lookup on the 2020 scale, not a design
document that says 6-weak[^cy-114].

What the instruments recorded near the damaged bridges was
smaller[^cy-114]:

| Station | North–south peak ground acceleration |
| --- | --- |
| TAP026, 頂溪國小 | 224.60 gal[^cy-114] |
| TAP032, 積穗國小 | 206.77 gal[^cy-114] |
| TAP115, 安和國小 | 223.17 gal[^cy-114] |

At 積穗國小 the peak ground velocity reached 32.16 cm/s, an intensity of
5-strong, and the peak horizontal acceleration measured there was 0.229 g —
below the 0.288 g of the 475-year return-period design
earthquake[^cy-114]. New Taipei DORTS said the same thing three weeks after the
earthquake: intensity 5-strong at the nearest recording station[^ntpc-dorts-damage].

The comparison the report itself draws is between seismic microzones rather than
between metro lines. The adjacent zones — the DF111 contract range covering
Wenshan and Xindian, and the DF113 range covering Sanchong, Xinzhuang and
Banqiao — recorded far lower ground motion in the same earthquake and suffered
**no bridge damage at all**[^cy-114]. In the DF112 range covering Zhonghe and
Yonghe, horizontal peak ground acceleration was around twice its neighbours' and
horizontal peak ground velocity around fifteen times[^cy-114].

Which produces the single most quotable line in the document: 「也就是說，輸入比
規範要求小20%的地震，卻得到比預期大50%的力量，顯示出反應譜分析不能反映當地地震反應
的實況」 — an input earthquake 20% smaller than the code requires produced forces
50% larger than expected, showing that response-spectrum analysis does not
reflect the local seismic response[^cy-114].

### The design, and what nobody checked

The report's second finding is against the design. The structure is described in
it as **Taiwan's first stacked viaduct** — 國內史無前例的疊式高架橋 — carried on
branching piers through climbing and descending sections, and the finding is
that no higher design standard was adopted and no displacement-limiting devices
were added to provide a further line of defence, and that this unprecedented
structural form went through **no external structural review**[^cy-114].

Note the word: viaduct, not station. Nothing found in the research behind this
page describes stacked *stations*.

The structural engineers' report the investigation relied on sets out the
hierarchy plainly: displacement-limiting devices assist the bearings and resist
seismic force, and fall-prevention equipment or sufficient seat length prevents
the span dropping — the second and third lines of defence respectively for a
bridge bearing system in an earthquake[^cy-114]. The line was built with neither
of the two backups.

Three further design failures are recorded[^cy-114]:

- The detailed design consultant, CECI (世曦), **double-deducted dead load** when
  calculating the bearings' uplift design force, and so underestimated the
  required uplift resistance[^cy-114].
- The pot-bearing detail drawing **contradicts itself about the pins**[^cy-114].
  Note 1 in its lower-left corner gives every pin a height of 40 mm with
  diameters of 120, 140, 150, 160, 200, 210, 220 and 280 mm; the table on the
  same drawing gives every pin a diameter of 100 mm with heights of 15, 20 and 25
  mm; and neither set matches the pins actually found in the damaged
  bearings[^cy-114]. The report's own word for this is 令人費解 — baffling.
- **Nobody senior signed that drawing**[^cy-114]. Only the handling officer at
  Taipei DORTS's third civil works office stamped it; neither the design unit
  responsible for reviewing it nor DORTS as the approving authority signed at
  all, which the report finds is a failure to carry out the review and approval
  the contract's specification chapter on pot bearings required[^cy-114].

888 pot bearings were supplied to the works in total, sampled in six batches
against the production schedule and tested at National Taiwan University of
Science and Technology's structural mechanics laboratory[^cy-114].

### The plate that nobody in Taiwan inspects

The third finding is the one most easily missed, and it is the reason the fault
survived construction. The grade-adjustment plate is a secondary component
between the girder and the bearing; it is designed by the contractor, assembled
in the fabrication shop, and concealed once the girder is lifted into place — so
it was **not an inspection hold point**[^cy-114].

The investigation then checked whether it is one anywhere else, and found that it
is not: not on the Directorate General of Highways' viaducts, not on the
Hualien–Taitung railway works, not on the Tamkang Bridge, not on the Ankeng LRT,
not on the Sanying Line, not on the Taichung Metro, not on the Wenhu Line, not on
the second phase of the Wanda Line, and not in the Public Construction
Commission's inspection checkpoints for steel bridge erection[^cy-114]. Taipei
DORTS's remedy, dated 13 December 2024 — the day after the line reopened — was to
add a pot-bearing inspection flow chart and a sampling standard[^cy-114].

### The seismometer that was told not to report

The fourth finding concerns an instrument that was installed, worked, and was
disconnected from anyone who could act on it. Zhongyuan station has triaxial
accelerometer-type seismometers at both ends of its platform level[^cy-114]. On 28 August
2019 Taipei DORTS's electrical and mechanical systems engineering office wrote to
Taipei Rapid Transit Corporation that the seismometer detection signals need not
be relayed to the control centre — and from the line's opening in 2020 to the
date of the report, the Circular Line control centre held **no seismic record
from that instrument at all**[^cy-114].

The 2019 letter identifies the instruments by the codes `Y9` and `Y13`, which are
not the codes the stations at those positions carry today; this page does not
attempt to reconcile the two numbering schemes, and the station the report names
is Zhongyuan[^cy-114].

The report also states as fact that when Taipei City Government handed operations
to New Taipei City Government on 23 May 2023, New Taipei Metro took the equipment
as it stood, and received training on the anemometer only — nothing on the
seismic instrumentation[^cy-114].

### The code itself

The sixth finding is against the ministry. Through years of revision, including
the substantially updated 2021 edition of the railway bridge seismic design code,
MOTC has never addressed **transverse** displacement: the rules still deal only
with the longitudinal, running direction[^cy-114]. The line failed sideways.

### Who was actually corrected

This is worth stating precisely, because the operator of the line and the builder
of it are different cities and the two are easily transposed. The formal 糾正 — a
correction, the Control Yuan's censure of an agency — was proposed against
**Taipei City Government**, on findings one and three: the construction fault and
the supervision gap[^cy-114]. Taipei DORTS built the line and supervised its own
construction[^cy-114].

New Taipei City Government and MOTC received 函請檢討改進 — a request to review
and improve, and report back — not a correction[^cy-114]. Findings two and four
went to Taipei City for review, finding four also to New Taipei City, and
findings five and six to MOTC[^cy-114].

## What happened on the day, and over the following eight months

**3 April 2024.** The whole line was suspended[^ntmetro-0403]. The Danhai LRT was
unaffected and the Ankeng LRT was back to normal by 16:30 on a 15-minute
headway[^ntmetro-0403]. From 17:00 the Circular Line ran **single-track
bidirectional between Banqiao and New Taipei Industrial Park** on a 16-minute
headway, and free shuttle buses ran between New Taipei Industrial Park and
Dapinglin from 06:00 to 24:00 — every 10 to 15 minutes at peak, 20 to 30
off-peak — calling at every Circular Line station except Shisizhang, which was
served instead from the 新店民權路工業區 bus stop[^ntmetro-0403].

**24 April 2024.** New Taipei DORTS published the severity split: of the eleven
girder displacements, **seven were severe, between 20 and 92 cm**, and four were
between 3.5 and 6 cm[^ntpc-dorts-damage]. Recovery was initially assessed at over
a year[^ntpc-dorts-damage]. The largest single displacement, 92 cm, is the figure
the Control Yuan also carries, attributed to New Taipei DORTS's
briefing[^cy-114].

**12 December 2024, 12:00.** Full service resumed[^ntmetro-reopening]. The repair
inventory the operator published: nine spans of steel box girder repositioned,
139 items damaged, 1,438 track baseplates replaced, 1,050 m of track restored,
41,500 m of cable replaced, and headways back to 5–7 minutes at peak and 8–10
off-peak[^ntmetro-reopening]. The bearings were replaced wholesale with optimised
pot bearings, and permanent fall-prevention and anti-uplift devices were added as
a second line of defence — the protection the design had
lacked[^ntmetro-reopening]. No source read for this page gives a count of
bearings replaced.

The Control Yuan's own summary of the repair: 250 days of emergency work against
an original estimate of one year and two months, finished six months early, with
123 hours of total testing before service resumed — whole-line and section tests,
and ten simulation drills[^cy-114].

## The claim against the contractor, and the contractor's answer

On 3 January 2025 New Taipei City Government announced that the two city
governments would jointly sue BES Engineering for **NT$1.927 billion**, on the
basis that the contractor had not built to the approved drawings; the same
release carries the 40 mm pin, the 5 mm drawing and the 41 mm as-built account of
the mechanism[^ntpc-claim]. The suit was filed on 27 March 2025 at the Taipei
District Court[^cna-filing].

BES has not conceded. It says it 「均依核定施工圖說嚴格執行」 — that it strictly
executed the approved construction drawings — and that the milled-through plate
and the bearing failure are **not** the main cause of the damage, which it says
needs further examination from the direction of structural design
strength[^pts-bes].

The outcome of the litigation is not established here, and neither is whether any
prosecution followed the Control Yuan's request that New Taipei DORTS pursue
liability for public-danger offences[^cy-114].

## Where the sources disagree

### How many displacements, and how many spans

| Figure | Source | What it appears to count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **11** displaced steel box girders | Control Yuan[^cy-114]; New Taipei DORTS[^ntpc-dorts-damage] | Locations where a girder moved |
| **9** spans of steel box girder repositioned | New Taipei Metro's reopening release[^ntmetro-reopening]; CNA[^cna-filing] | Spans that had to be jacked back into place |

Eleven displacement locations across nine spans is arithmetically possible and is
the obvious reading. **No source found in the research behind this page says so**,
so this page does not assert it.

### How far the damage ran

The primary source is inconsistent with itself, and that is worth publishing
rather than smoothing over. The Control Yuan's terms of reference and its opening
paragraph put the damage between Banxin and Qiaohe and along the Jingping Road
section; the conclusion of its first finding gives the range as Banxin to Jingan;
and its own breakdown, quoting New Taipei DORTS, places **five displacements
between Qiaohe and Zhongyuan, five between Zhongyuan and Banxin, and one near
Jingan** — five plus five plus one, spanning Y15 down to Y11, not Y15 to
Y13[^cy-114]. Jingping and Jingan are adjacent stations, and the report uses both
names for the outlying eleventh location[^cy-114].

The service suspension was wider than the damage in either reading: Banqiao to
Zhonghe could not operate[^cy-114].

### Whose fault it was

Four positions, and they are not reconciled[^cy-114][^ntpc-claim][^pts-bes]:

- **The contractor did not build to the drawings**, and milling through the
  grade-adjustment plate is the cause — Control Yuan's first finding, and New
  Taipei City Government's basis for the claim[^cy-114][^ntpc-claim].
- **The contractor denies it**, says it built strictly to the approved drawings,
  and says the plate and the bearings are not the main cause[^pts-bes].
- **The design was at fault** — no displacement-limiting devices, dead load
  double-deducted in the uplift calculation, drawings neither reviewed nor
  approved, no external structural review of an unprecedented
  structure[^cy-114].
- **The supervision was at fault** — a concealed component with no inspection
  hold point[^cy-114].

The Control Yuan apportions responsibility to contractor, designer, supervisor
**and** the ministry's own code[^cy-114]. The contractor rejects the
first[^pts-bes]. The litigation filed in March 2025 has not been resolved on this
page.

## The line itself

The Circular Line is a **steel-wheel, steel-rail, fully automatic driverless
medium-capacity system** — not straddle-beam and not rubber-tyred[^dorts-faq].
The section in service is 15.4 km with 14 stations, thirteen elevated and one
underground, and one depot[^dorts-faq]. It opened on 31 January
2020[^dorts-phase1].

The second revised plan for Phase 1 was approved by the Executive Yuan on 6 May
2015 at a total cost of **NT$69.973 billion**, against an initial financial plan
of NT$40.118 billion[^dorts-phase1] — an increase of roughly 74%, which is
arithmetic on those two published figures rather than a figure DORTS states. New
Taipei DORTS publishes the same total[^ntpc-dorts-line].

The depot's published name is **South Depot** (南機廠), in the Shisizhang
agricultural zone in Xindian: about 14.33 hectares of site, of which about 12.19
hectares are laid out as depot[^dorts-phase1].

The trains were built by **Hitachi Rail Italy**[^ntpc-hitachi]. The bureau published an article
specifically to explain why the winning bidder was reported as AnsaldoBreda and
the trains arrived branded Hitachi: Hitachi bought AnsaldoBreda in 2015 and
renamed it[^ntpc-hitachi]. That article gives no fleet size, formation or
technical specification, and none is asserted here.

![The empty yellow-and-grey interior of a Circular Line carriage, straps and grab poles overhead](/images/circular-line/interior-800.webp "The interior of a Circular Line train, photographed by the bureau that built the line. — Department of Rapid Transit Systems, New Taipei City Government, public domain, Wikimedia Commons")

The line runs unbranched from Y07 to Y20 in one sequence, and the operator's own
data records it as not a branch[^tdx]. It meets five other lines[^tdx]:

| Interchange | To | On site | Transfer time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Y07 Dapinglin | Songshan–Xindian Line | yes | 3 min[^tdx] |
| Y11 Jingan | Zhonghe–Xinlu Line | yes | 6 min[^tdx] |
| Y16 Banqiao | Bannan Line | no | 11 min[^tdx] |
| Y17 Xinpu Minsheng | Bannan Line | no | 9 min[^tdx] |
| Y18 Touqianzhuang | Zhonghe–Xinlu Line | yes | 6 min[^tdx] |

End to end takes 34 minutes in either direction[^tdx].

## The rest of the ring

Three further sections are planned or under construction, and Taipei DORTS
publishes dimensions for all of them[^dorts-faq]:

| Section | Length | Stations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phase 1 (in service) | 15.4 km | 14, plus one depot[^dorts-faq] |
| North ring | 14.93 km | 12, plus one underground depot[^dorts-faq] |
| South ring | 5.73 km | 6, all underground, not counting Dapinglin[^dorts-faq] |
| East ring | about 13.25 km | 10, all underground, plus one underground depot[^dorts-faq] |

The South and North rings together run 20.66 km, of which 8.21 km lie inside New
Taipei City, with 18 stations of which 7 are in New Taipei, at a cost of
**NT$179.898 billion** and a stated completion year of 2031[^ntpc-dorts-rings].
Section contracts started as follows: CF680C on 1 March 2022, CF680B on 18 March
2023, CF670A on 13 June 2023, CF680A on 5 October 2023, and CF690A, B and C in
March 2025[^ntpc-dorts-rings].

The East Ring's basic design was completed on 18 October 2021 and approved on 29
March 2023[^dorts-east-ring]. Its project page carries **no cost figure and no
completion year**, and neither is asserted here.

## Ridership

In 2025 the Circular Line carried over **22.04 million** passengers, which New
Taipei Metro describes as the main engine of its network's
growth[^ntmetro-ridership]. The operator's whole network passed 30.09 million for
the year, against 21.35 million in 2024 — growth of more than
40%[^ntmetro-ridership]. That comparison is only meaningful with the reason
attached: 2024 is the year this line lost its Zhonghe section from 3 April to 12
December[^ntmetro-0403][^ntmetro-reopening].

A figure of 30.11 million for the same network total appears in search summaries
of that release; the fetched text says 30.09 million, and that is what this page
publishes[^ntmetro-ridership].
