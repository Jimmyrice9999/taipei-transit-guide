## An early adopter, by its own account

TRTC and its engineering bureau, DORTS, describe Taipei Metro as the first
mass-transit system in Taiwan to adopt accessibility-facility concepts, since
evolved into what the operator calls "universal design" — serving not just
disabled riders but anyone travelling with heavy luggage, a stroller, or
simply older age.[^dorts-accessibility-design] Neither DORTS page checked
for this research cites a specific law or design standard by number, despite
being the operator's own dedicated accessibility-design pages — recorded
here as an absence found by checking directly, not an oversight in the
search.

Every TRTC station has a lowered accessible ticket machine, an accessible
fare gate, and elevators fitted with braille control panels, voice
announcements, a secondary control panel and side handrails, alongside
anti-slip stair-edge strips.[^trtc-instation-facilities] Twenty-nine
stations — selected for higher visually-impaired ridership or longer
branch-line headways — carry additional priority seating for visually
impaired passengers near the platform's first car.[^trtc-instation-facilities]
On board, every priority seat system-wide is painted dark blue to stand out
from ordinary seating.[^trtc-train-facilities] Wheelchair space placement
differs by line: on the four heavy lines it sits in the first and last cars
by the driver's end, with staff positioned to assist; on the Wenhu Line,
every car carries its own wheelchair space, and riders may board from any
car, though the end cars are recommended as less
crowded.[^trtc-train-facilities]

## A real platform-gap problem, and the guide-dog policy

TRTC is not fully level-boarding. A documented minimum gap of 75mm exists
between platform and train on straight sections, growing larger on curved
platforms, where a rigid train body cannot conform to the curve — and
TRTC has stated it could not add further rubber edge strips to close
it.[^freeguider-platform-gap] A specific incident in February 2020 saw a
powered wheelchair's front wheel become stuck in the gap at Dapinglin
Station, requiring other passengers to help free it — a concrete
illustration of why staff assistance is built into the first-car boarding
procedure described above.[^freeguider-platform-gap] Guide dogs, hearing
dogs and mobility-assistance dogs are exempted from TRTC's general
animal-carriage rules, reinforced by both public campaign material and a
2025 dog-friendly train event with the Taipei mayor; the formal legal text
of this exemption could not be independently confirmed for TRTC specifically
in this research, since its passenger-regulations PDF resisted text
extraction — though NTMC's own formal passenger guidelines, successfully
read, state an identical-in-spirit exemption for guide, hearing and
mobility-assistance dogs.[^ntmc-passenger-rules]

## A finding this page won't soften

In October 2023, Taipei City's own building-management department inspected
81 TRTC accessibility facilities and found every single one failed
inspection — most commonly for stairway handrails that weren't level, were
the wrong height, had unprotected ends, or were incorrectly mounted, plus
32 elevators missing a lower-position call button and non-compliant tactile
warning signage.[^ltn-81-facilities-failed] TRTC's then general manager
committed to improvements starting the following January, and a city
advisory panel set an end-of-2023 deadline — no source found confirms
whether that deadline was met.[^ltn-81-facilities-failed]

Separately, the Control Yuan issued a formal corrective measure against
TRTC in February 2023 after finding the company had fined an autistic
passenger over an alleged spitting incident on the Tamsui-Xinyi Line
without ever reviewing the CCTV footage that would have cleared him — footage
TRTC only checked after the Control Yuan's own investigators requested it,
at which point the fine was reversed.[^cy-trtc-correction][^ltn-trtc-correction]
The Control Yuan's report specifically criticised TRTC's subsequent public
statements as factually wrong in a way that reinforced negative
misconceptions about disabled people.[^cy-trtc-correction] TRTC committed to
staff training in response.[^ltn-trtc-correction] Most recently, disability
advocacy groups staged an accessible-route protest at Taipei Main Station in
December 2025, naming Taipei Metro alongside TRA and THSR and describing a
ground-floor elevator that reaches only the platform level, leaving a single
stair-mounted lift platform — with a 6–10 minute wait — as the only way to
the basement.[^rti-accessibility-protest]

## NTMC: two systems, two different accessibility profiles

New Taipei Metro's Circular Line and Danhai LRT are not accessibility
equivalents of each other, by design. The Circular Line has an accessible
restroom at every station, wheelchair space in the first and last cars, and
30 of its 98 seats per train — 30.6% — designated priority
seating.[^ntmc-accessibility-hub] Danhai LRT has accessible restrooms at
only three of its stations (紅樹林, 淡水漁人碼頭, 十四張), wheelchair spaces
in cars two and four of every train, and 12 of its 62 seats per train —
19.4% — as priority seating.[^ntmc-accessibility-hub] Both figures are
published as reported, not averaged into one "NTMC" number. Ankeng LRT has
no accessibility detail documented separately from the general "light rail"
language covering Danhai, and Sanying Line — NTMC's newest — has no
accessibility content on the operator's own hub page at all as of this
research, a gap this page states plainly rather than filling with an
assumption.

## Remaining gaps

No source found gives TRTC a network-wide accessible-elevator coverage
percentage or a completion year, despite the "one accessible route per
station" design principle being clearly documented at the individual-station
level. TRTC's formal legal text on disability accommodations, including the
precise guide-dog exemption clause, could not be read in this research pass
— its source PDF resisted extraction. Whether the city's improvement
deadline for the failed facilities was met is not established. Sanying Line
accessibility is entirely undocumented on NTMC's own accessibility hub page.
