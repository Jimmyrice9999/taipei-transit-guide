## Two depots, one construction-era snapshot

The Airport MRT was built with exactly two maintenance depots — Qingpu
Depot (青埔機廠) and Luzhu Depot (蘆竹機廠) — as part of a 22-station,
51.03&nbsp;km line.[^hsr-bureau-2013-annual-report] The clearest primary
account of what each depot actually contains comes not from an operating-era
source but from the High Speed Rail Engineering Bureau's own 2013 annual
report, read here as a construction-progress snapshot rather than a
description of the system as it runs today.

**Qingpu Depot** was, by the report's account, the more complete and more
central of the two. Its administrative building was finished and received
its occupancy permit in January 2012, well ahead of the line's eventual
opening.[^hsr-bureau-2013-annual-report] As of the 2013 report, Qingpu
hosted the system's main maintenance shop, a stabling yard, a locomotive
repair shop, a sewage treatment plant, a car-wash shop, a main substation
and a staff cafeteria — and, notably, the operations control centre
(行控中心) itself.[^hsr-bureau-2013-annual-report] Civil-engineering
progress on the depot stood at 98.06% by the end of that
year.[^hsr-bureau-2013-annual-report] All 123 EMU carriages that make up
the delivered fleet — 11 express and 17 commuter trainsets — arrived at
Qingpu Depot, the last of them logged in the report's own project
chronology.[^hsr-bureau-2013-annual-report]

**Luzhu Depot** trailed Qingpu on every measure the report
gives.[^hsr-bureau-2013-annual-report] Its 2013
building list comprised a main maintenance shop, a traction-power
substation, separate stabling yards for express and commuter trains, a
car-wash shop, and a track-and-fixed-facilities maintenance shop, with
civil-engineering progress at 82.36% by year-end — noticeably behind
Qingpu's 98.06%.[^hsr-bureau-2013-annual-report] Depot-specific maintenance
equipment told the same story: 69.50% complete at Qingpu against 32.79% at
Luzhu.[^hsr-bureau-2013-annual-report] Signalling installation followed
the same pattern — Luzhu Depot was, like A1 station, still at the
layout-and-cabling stage while the A21–A2 station section's signalling was
already complete, a detail this project's separate signalling research
already records.[^hsr-bureau-2013-annual-report]

## Qingpu Depot doubles as headquarters

TYMC's own registered headquarters sits on the Qingpu Depot site, at 251
Linghang North Road Section 4, Dayuan District — the company relocated
there on 19 July 2014, according to a company-registry-sourced Wikipedia
article.[^zhwiki-tymc-company] This is consistent with, though not
independently re-confirmed against, the 2013 primary report's own placement
of the administrative building and operations control centre at the same
site; TYMC's own website returned no working page on the URLs tried this
session, so the headquarters address itself still rests on the secondary
source alone.

## Remaining gaps

Neither depot's site area in hectares has been found in any source read for
this page — not the 2013 primary construction report, and not TYMC's own
site, which could not be reached. Which of the two depots each trainset is
permanently allocated to in current operation, rather than where the fleet
was first delivered during 2013 commissioning, is TBC — Qingpu's role as
the delivery point does not by itself establish today's operating
allocation. Present-day stabling capacity and maintenance-tier
classification for either depot are also TBC; the 2013 report describes
construction progress, not operating capacity. The Taoyuan Green Line's own
depot allocation is a separate, already-recorded gap and is out of scope
here.
