## Two fleets, and an operator page that names neither's builder

The Taoyuan Airport MRT runs two distinct train types. The operator's own
current page describes an express (直達車) configuration of 5 cars — 4
passenger cars plus 1 luggage car, about 102 m total — and a commuter
(普通車) configuration of 4 all-passenger cars, about 82 m total, with
longitudinal seating, 188 seats and a crush-load capacity of roughly 1,000
passengers including standing.[^tymc-train-introduction] That page does not
name a manufacturer, fleet size, or maximum speed for either
type — a real gap in what the operator itself currently publishes, not an
oversight of this page.

## A seat count two primaries agree on, and Wikipedia doesn't

Taiwan Rolling Stock Co., a manufacturer, states on its own product page
that it built commuter trainsets numbered 102 through 117 — 64 cars —
delivered December 2012.[^trsc-commuter-spec] Its own per-car seat
breakdown (44, 50, 50, 44) sums to exactly 188 — matching the operator's own
whole-train figure precisely. Zh.wikipedia's composition table for the same
fleet gives a different middle-car figure (47 instead of 50), summing to
185 instead — a discrepancy this page treats as a likely transcription
error in the secondary source, since two independently read primary
sources agree with each other exactly and the secondary source's own table
does not even match its own article's stated total elsewhere.[^zhwiki-1000-series]
This page does not silently correct Wikipedia's number; it publishes 188 as
the sourced figure and records the conflict.

Contemporaneous national news from just after the line's 2017 opening
independently corroborates an 11-trainset express fleet, and describes a
seat-removal modification then underway — 28 seats taken out across the
train to add luggage space.[^ltn-express-luggage-seats]

## What secondary sources add, and where they're not confirmed

Zh.wikipedia's model-specific articles give further detail not verified
above: the express fleet (2000-series) as 11 trainsets, 55 cars, all-powered,
855 passengers per set, delivered from August 2011;[^zhwiki-2000-series] the
commuter fleet (1000-series) as 20 trainsets, 80 cars, built 2011–2012 and
again 2015, split between Kawasaki Heavy Industries (sets 101 and 118–120,
with 121–124 reserved but not built) and Taiwan Rolling Stock (sets
102–117).[^zhwiki-1000-series] Both fleets are stated to have entered
service 2 March 2017, matching this line's already-established opening
date. Taiwan Rolling Stock's own role is independently confirmed above;
**Kawasaki's role — the entire express fleet, plus part of the commuter
fleet — rests on Wikipedia alone.** No Kawasaki corporate source, TYMC
primary page, or Railway Bureau document naming Kawasaki was found.

## Remaining gaps

No manufacturer is named anywhere on TYMC's own current public-facing page
for either fleet. Kawasaki's role is unconfirmed by any primary source.
Whether the four reserved commuter trainset numbers have since been built
is TBC. No combined total fleet size is stated by any primary source; this
page does not compute one from the two separately-sourced counts.
