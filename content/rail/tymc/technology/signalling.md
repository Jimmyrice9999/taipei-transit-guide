## A subcontractor named, a decade later

This project's existing research already established that Marubeni, the
contractor for the Airport MRT's electromechanical works, illegally
subcontracted the signalling system — which its own contract required it to
design — to an unnamed British firm, and had part of its performance bond
withheld as a result. A 2017 wire report, read in full for this page, names
that firm directly: Invensys, with roughly NT$1.9 billion of the bond
withheld over the violation.[^cna-marubeni-signalling-dispute]

MOTC's own press release for the extension line's A22 opening confirms, in
its own words, that the Airport MRT runs on two distinct signalling-system
generations — the original line and the newer extension — and that trains
carry dual onboard signalling equipment to bridge them, describing high
technical difficulty integrating the two systems and a correspondingly long
testing schedule.[^motc-a22-opening] MOTC does not itself name either
signalling product.

## What this page does not claim

Several more specific technical labels appear in Wikipedia's infobox for
this line — "Siemens Trainguard MT," "DTG-R SCS," "Rail9000" — and an
automation grade of GoA2 for the original line, rising to GoA4 (commuter
trains only) on the extension.[^zhwiki-airport-mrt] None of these is
published here as established. The GoA2/GoA4 figure in particular carries
no citation whatsoever within the Wikipedia article that states
it,[^zhwiki-airport-mrt] and no primary TYMC or MOTC source found anywhere
describes any driverless or unattended capability on this line — every
source on this line's rolling stock describes conventionally crewed trains
with driving cabs, which is inconsistent with GoA4 (full unattended
operation). This page publishes automation grade as TBC rather than repeat
a plausible-sounding but unsupported number. The three named signalling
products each trace only to Wikipedia's infobox, whose own citations point
to a government-report repository (`report.nat.gov.tw`) that this page
could not reach and treats as unverified.[^zhwiki-airport-mrt]

## Remaining gaps

The line's automation grade and its signalling system's actual product name
are both TBC. Whether Marubeni's illegally-subcontracted Invensys system is
the same equipment later labelled "Siemens Trainguard MT" in Wikipedia
infoboxes — Siemens acquired Invensys Rail in 2013 — is not stated by any
source found. Who actually led the 2006 ME01 electromechanical contract is
disputed between sources: Marubeni, per Chinese-language coverage, versus
Hitachi, per a 2006 Hitachi press release cited by English Wikipedia that
this page could not independently read.
