## 2025: a record year, from the operator's own numbers

THSR carried 82.07 million passengers in 2025, up 4.88% on 2024's 78.25
million, across 59,606 train services.[^thsr-2025-annual-report] Average
daily ridership reached 225,000, more than 10,000 higher than 2024's
214,000.[^thsr-2025-annual-report] Passenger-kilometres reached 13,748
million against 19,203 million seat-kilometres, giving a loading factor of
71.59%, down slightly from 71.73% in 2024.[^thsr-2025-annual-report]
Punctuality — arrival within five minutes of schedule — was 99.31% in 2025,
down 0.19 percentage points from 2024's 99.50%, which the operator
attributes to natural-disaster impacts; excluding natural-disaster delays,
the reliability rate was 100%.[^thsr-2025-annual-report] From 1 July 2025,
weekly train services rose from 1,103 to 1,128, an increase of 1,877 runs
over the year compared with 2024.[^thsr-2025-annual-report]

## The COVID recovery, year by year

THSR's own five-year Key Operating Statistics table gives a complete,
primary-sourced account of the pandemic's impact and recovery:[^thsr-2025-annual-report]

| Year | Ridership | Loading factor | Punctuality (within 5 minutes) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 43.46 million | 49.88% | 99.00%[^thsr-2025-annual-report] |
| 2022 | 54.16 million | 53.31% | 99.47%[^thsr-2025-annual-report] |
| 2023 | 73.09 million | 70.67% | 99.58%[^thsr-2025-annual-report] |
| 2024 | 78.25 million | 71.73% | 99.50%[^thsr-2025-annual-report] |
| 2025 | 82.07 million | 71.59% | 99.31%[^thsr-2025-annual-report] |

The five-year average punctuality over this period was 99.38%, per the same
table.[^thsr-2025-annual-report] Pre-pandemic, 2019 ridership had reached
67.41 million with an average daily figure of roughly 184,000 — more than
four times 2021's trough.[^thsrc-600m-release] By late 2022, THSR's own
chairman was quoted putting daily ridership at roughly 140,000, around 90%
of the pre-pandemic level, on the way back to the 2025 figures
above.[^ltn-thsr-2022-recovery]

## Cumulative ridership and single-day records

THSR passed 500 million cumulative passengers on 23 July 2018 and 600
million on 17 January 2020, about eighteen months later.[^thsrc-600m-release]
Growth continued through the pandemic recovery: cumulative ridership passed
900 million in December 2024, and had reached more than 984 million by 31
December 2025.[^thsr-2025-annual-report] The system's all-time single-day
record — 338,000 passengers, on 1 October 2023, the final day of that year's
Mid-Autumn Festival travel period — surpassed the previous record of 318,250
set on the equivalent day in 2019.[^cna-thsr-record-day]

## Fleet and future capacity

Each 700T trainset seats 977 passengers — 911 in standard class, 66 in
business class.[^thsr-2025-annual-report] THSRC signed a contract on 18 May
2023 with the Hitachi Toshiba Supreme Consortium (HTSC) for 12
new-generation N700ST trainsets (144 carriages), expected to begin arriving
in Taiwan in August 2026 and enter commercial service in the second half of
2027.[^thsr-2025-annual-report]

## An error caught, and a gap left open

An early, unverified pass at summarising a Wikipedia account of THSR's
ridership history produced a claim that the system carried its "one
billionth passenger" 265 days after opening, on 26 September 2007 — a claim
not established by any source this project could confirm. Re-checking the
same source's specific footnote text suggested a correction, itself equally
unverified: the milestone it actually describes, 265 days after opening, is
10 million cumulative passengers, not one billion — a difference of two
orders of magnitude. That correction is itself sourced only to an AI tool's
paraphrase of a Wikipedia page, not to a directly read primary or the page's
own raw text, so neither figure is published here as established; the
milestone stays TBC. The broader lesson
holds regardless of the exact number: an intermediate summarisation step
introduced an error a full page-read would not have. THSR's cumulative
ridership at its 100-million (2010) and 200-million (2012) milestones is
separately unconfirmed and also TBC.

## Remaining gaps

Exact 2020 annual ridership is TBC — two close but non-identical secondary
figures exist (approximately 57.2 million and 57.24 million), and no
primary source fetched this run extends the operator's own statistics table
back that far. Whether cumulative ridership has now passed 1 billion is not
established by any source read in full, though the trajectory from the
984-million/31-December-2025 figure makes it plausible within months of
that date.
