## One named mechanism, five stations — and three more outside it

A single, named government mechanism covers five of THSR's twelve stations
— Taoyuan, Hsinchu, Taichung, Chiayi and Tainan — under which the Ministry
of the Interior led zone expropriation in partnership with the Railway
Bureau and each station's county government, covering 1,507 hectares of
planned area (1,386 expropriated) at a cost of NT$60.534
billion.[^motc-yearbook-districts] This figure rests on a government
yearbook this research could not directly render — the underlying PDF
resisted extraction in this environment — so it is reported here from a
search-engine synthesis that reproduced consistent text across two separate
yearbook editions, not from a direct read.

Miaoli, Changhua and Yunlin — all opened later, in December 2015 — have
their own special districts, but no source found in this research
establishes that they sit under the same five-station mechanism, or states
plainly who does designate them. This is reported as a gap, not resolved by
assumption.

## Success and stagnation, in the same programme

THSR's own twentieth-anniversary framing draws a sharp contrast. At Qingpu,
next to Taoyuan Station, roughly 70% of the local population is now under
45, with new commercial floor space added between 2017 and 2025 equal to
about 80% of the area's entire prior office stock.[^cna-thsr-20th] At
Zhubei, next to Hsinchu Station, the under-45 population grew 43.8% — the
only one of the twelve station areas to show positive growth in that age
band — alongside a fourfold rise in local output value.[^cna-thsr-20th]
THSR's chairman described this as a shift from "movement" to "relocation" to
"liveability."[^cna-thsr-20th]

Changhua tells a different story. THSR's own twentieth-anniversary figures
rank it last of the twelve stations by absolute ridership.[^cna-thsr-20th]
Since 2014, the county has offered 66.34 hectares of buildable land for sale
around the station; 36.76 hectares remained unsold as of the most recent
figures read for this page, and the county's own description is blunt:
development has fallen short of expectations.[^ltn-changhua-land] Miaoli
tells a related story — two county-owned industrial parcels went to bid 42
times without a single successful sale — though the county was still
actively pitching the site to prospective tenants, including Nvidia, as
recently as October 2025.[^cna-miaoli-nvidia]

A genuine statistical trap sits inside this same anniversary
report:[^cna-thsr-20th] Miaoli, Yunlin and Changhua post the *highest*
ridership-growth multiples of all twelve stations. Both things are true at
once — the fastest percentage
growth, and the lowest absolute ridership — because an extremely small
opening-period base makes any subsequent traffic look like a large
multiple. Neither figure should be read as contradicting the other.

## Building-level development, on public land near the station

THSR's clearest example of literal joint building development sits at
Taichung, where the Railway Bureau tendered the largest station-area
development site in Taiwan — roughly 48,085 ping directly facing the
station, awarded in August 2021 on a 70-year superficies right with a
four-year royalty holiday, for a project combining a department store,
international conference centre, hotel and cinema at the point where THSR,
TRA and the Taichung Metro Green Line converge.[^cna-taichung-tender] This
is structurally different from the building-integrated joint development
used by Taiwan's metro systems: it is a superficies-right tender on
government-held land adjacent to the station, not a single structure split
between the transit operator and a private co-developer.

Zuoying, Taiwan's only THSR/TRA/Kaohsiung Metro three-rail interchange, is
a second confirmed case: the city government's "左營高鐵科技之心" project
rezones roughly 2.4 hectares of entirely public land west of the station,
for more than 70,000 ping of mixed commercial, office and transfer-hub
space, explicitly invoking transit-oriented development and citing TSMC's
move into nearby Nanzi as a demand driver.[^kcg-finance-zuoying]

## Nangang and Banqiao: different origins, not the same story

Nangang's redevelopment sits inside Taipei City's "東區門戶" (East District
Gateway) plan, with the station's own skywalk — now grown from 1.3km to
3.56km — designed to connect it to the Nangang Exhibition Center and the
Taipei Music Center; no source read for this page uses the specific term
"sub-centre" for this framing. Banqiao's special zone predates THSR
entirely — it began under Taipei County's own late-1980s and 1990s
planning, driven by the old Banqiao station's inability to handle growing
ridership on limited land, before THSR's route had even been
finalised.[^zhwiki-banqiao-district] THSR simply became one of four rail
systems that later converged there, rather than the reason the district
exists.

## Remaining gaps

Exactly how many of the twelve stations have a dedicated area district, and
under which of at least two distinct mechanisms, is not established to one
clean figure — roughly eight is a reasonable characterisation, but the
governance path for Miaoli, Changhua and Yunlin specifically is
unconfirmed. A reported joint-development tender at Chiayi, structurally
similar to Taichung's, is TBC — it rests only on unfetched search results.
No government report was found that applies the
term "ghost station" to any of the twelve stations themselves, as distinct
from underdeveloped land nearby — that distinction is deliberate, not an
oversight.
