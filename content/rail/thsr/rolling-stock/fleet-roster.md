## The fleet, and a manufacturer split the shorthand collapses

THSR's current fleet is 34 700T trainsets — the original 30, plus 4 more
ordered in November 2008 and delivered 2012–2015 — each a 12-car set seating
977 passengers (911 standard, 66 business) per the operator's own current
car-facilities page.[^zhwiki-700t][^thsr-car-facilities] The carbody itself
was built by three Japanese manufacturers — Kawasaki Heavy Industries,
Nippon Sharyo and Hitachi — a fact often summarised as "Kawasaki, Nippon
Sharyo, Hitachi and Toshiba built the 700T." That shorthand conflates two
different scopes of work: Toshiba did not build cars, it designed the
traction and auxiliary power system, with Mitsubishi Electric fabricating
much of the actual equipment — 220 traction motors, 21 main transformers and
45 traction converters for the first batch alone.[^zhwiki-700t] Commercial
service began 5 January 2007, at a commercial operating speed of 300 km/h
against a test/contractual maximum of 315 km/h.[^zhwiki-700t]

## A new generation, arriving now

THSRC signed a contract with the Hitachi Toshiba Supreme Consortium on 18
May 2023 for 12 new-generation trainsets — 144 carriages — based on the
Shinkansen N700S.[^thsr-n700st-contract] Named N700ST, the new fleet extends
rather than breaks from the 700T's orange-and-black livery, with a
brighter white nose section where the two colour curves
meet.[^thsr-n700st-naming] The first unit arrived at Kaohsiung Port on 15
August 2026; the fleet is planned to enter service in two phases, six sets
in 2027 and six more in 2028, lifting the total fleet to 46 trainsets and
peak-hour capacity by roughly 25% once complete.[^cna-n700st-schedule] Each
N700ST set will carry six wheelchair spaces rather than the 700T's four,
with fixed wheelchair devices and an enlarged nursing room.[^thsr-n700st-naming]

Both fleets are serviced at Yanchao Main Depot in Kaohsiung — not, as an
early hypothesis for this research assumed, somewhere near Taipei. THSRC's
own language describes Yanchao as its "core base for bogie overhaul, major
overhaul and new-train assembly," and the operator broke ground on an
11,500+ m² expansion there in March 2026 specifically to handle the two
fleet types together during the N700ST transition.[^thsr-yanchao-expansion]

## A seating-capacity conflict that stays open

THSR's own current car-facilities page states 977 seats per
trainset.[^thsr-car-facilities] A different figure, 989, traces back through
Wikipedia's own citation trail to an archived MOTC Railway Bureau document
this research could not independently fetch to check its date or
context.[^zhwiki-700t] No source found explains why the two numbers differ —
a guess that some standard-class seating was later converted to luggage
space is not established and not published as fact here. Both figures are
recorded; neither is picked.

## The 2010 derailment

On the morning of 4 March 2010, a magnitude-6.4 earthquake centred in
Jiaxian, Kaohsiung, caused a northbound THSR train to derail near Tainan's
Xinshi District — the system's first passenger-train
derailment.[^cy-jiaxian-derailment] Reporting citing the incident's technical
detail puts the train at roughly 298 km/h when four wheelsets deviated from
the rail by 15–20cm and dragged along the trackbed for about 2.7
km.[^nownews-jiaxian-technical] One account attributes the train staying
upright to its eddy-current brake pad slipping onto the rail and being held
there magnetically, rather than the wheelset rolling free.[^gvm-jiaxian-mechanism]
Passenger counts for the incident vary by scope rather than by
disagreement: one figure of 300 appears to describe those aboard the
derailed train itself, while figures of "over 2,000" (the Control Yuan's own
investigation report) and 2,400 (NOWnews) appear to describe passengers
stranded system-wide, across six trains halted that day.[^cy-jiaxian-derailment][^nownews-jiaxian-technical]
The Control Yuan's own investigation, approved three months later, found its
main fault not in the derailment mechanism but in the length of the
subsequent evacuation.[^cy-jiaxian-derailment]
