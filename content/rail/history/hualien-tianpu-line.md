## An old-station segment in the operator's register

Taiwan Railways' full construction-history table contains a Hualien–Tianpu
entry of 2.4 km.[^tra-construction-history] In the same historical record, the
operator says that Hualien–Tianpu was dismantled in ROC year 71, or 1982.[^tra-construction-history]
The table is valuable because it names the segment and supplies a route-length
field, but it does not provide a former-station list, a timetable or a dated
legal closure instrument for this entry.[^tra-construction-history]

The segment belonged to the older Hualien railway geography rather than the
current TDX line identity. Hualien County's account describes the old Hualien
station as the northern hub of the East Line's 762 mm narrow-gauge railway and
says that the East Line widening completed in 1982 removed the railway between
Tianpu and the old station.[^hualien-rail-park] The same account names Meilun,
Xincun, old Hualien and Tianpu among the stations retired in that transition.[^hualien-rail-park]

## The 1982 transition

The move was part of a larger change in how Hualien connected to the national
railway. The Hualien government account says that, after the North-Link
Railway was completed in 1980, the city had both a 1,067 mm North-Link Hualien
station and a 762 mm narrow-gauge old Hualien station while the East Line
widening was unfinished.[^hualien-rail-park] A separate municipal page dates the
completion of the widening and the transfer of old-station business to the new
Hualien station to 27 June 1982.[^hualien-old-station]

Those dates should not be compressed into a single invented event. The operator
publishes 1982 as the dismantling year, while the municipal page supplies a
specific station-transfer date within that year.[^tra-construction-history][^hualien-old-station]
The sources support a year-level closure and a dated operational transition,
but they do not say that every rail, crossing or building was removed on 27
June.[^tra-construction-history][^hualien-old-station]

## A route remembered in the street plan

The Hualien County Cultural Affairs Bureau identifies today's Fucheng Road as
the former old-station railway and says that rails were progressively removed
and the corridor paved after the railway offices moved.[^hualien-water-tower]
The bureau also records the former station water tower and reservoir as a
surviving railway structure.[^hualien-water-tower] This is a heritage footprint,
not evidence of a preserved operating track.[^hualien-water-tower]

The municipal cultural history places the old station in a broader port-line
network. It gives 1.1 km for the former coastal line and 4.0 km for the 1939
old Hualien Port line, while separately describing the old-station–Tianpu
connection.[^hualien-port-history] Those figures describe other segments and
are not substitutes for TRA's 2.4 km Hualien–Tianpu entry.[^tra-construction-history][^hualien-port-history]
The same page says the old station site became the Hualien Rail Cultural Park
in 2002, turning the former railway headquarters and surrounding structures into
a cultural landscape after the station's closure.[^hualien-port-history]

## What remains TBC

The fetched sources do not establish a complete historical station sequence,
the exact legal closure instrument, a segment-by-segment removal schedule, or a
gauge conversion date for every phase of Hualien–Tianpu.[^tra-construction-history][^hualien-rail-park][^hualien-old-station]
They support the 2.4 km operator entry, the 1982 dismantling year, the 27 June
station-transfer milestone and the surviving heritage footprint; the missing
fields remain TBC until an operator register, widening completion file, gazette
or right-of-way inventory is fetched and read.[^tra-construction-history][^hualien-water-tower]
