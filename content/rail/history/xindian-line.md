## A railway with several names

The former railway from Wanhua to Xindian was called the Xindian Line, or
Wanxin Railway (萬新鐵路), in later local memory.[^dorts-xindian-history][^xindian-office-bitang] Taiwan Railways’ construction-history table records the route as 10.4 km and says its constructor was Taipei Railway Company.[^tra-construction-history] New Taipei’s Department of Rapid Transit Systems gives 25 March 1921 as the full opening date and records a 1,067 mm gauge.[^dorts-xindian-history]

The line began as an industrial route serving coal, timber and other goods, then
also carried commuters and visitors to Bitan.[^dorts-xindian-history][^xindian-office-bitang] The Xindian District Office describes a single-track line with fourteen stations and four daily round trips.[^xindian-office-bitang] These are descriptions of the service at the source’s stated historical scale, not a present timetable or a complete former-station register.[^xindian-office-bitang]

## Purchase, withdrawal and dismantling

The operator’s table records that the railway was purchased in 1949 and
dismantled in 1965.[^tra-construction-history] New Taipei DORTS gives a more
specific operating milestone—20 March 1965—and identifies the line as the first
TRA branch to stop operating.[^dorts-xindian-history] The district-office page
also describes the postwar transfer to Taiwan Railways and retirement in ROC
year 54, or 1965.[^xindian-office-bitang]

The exact final-service date is not settled here. A full National Taiwan
University alumni feature places the last passenger and freight movements on
24 and 25 March 1965, while the municipal DORTS account gives 20 March.[^ntu-wanxin-history][^dorts-xindian-history] The operator’s construction table confirms the year and dismantling, but not a last-train time or a legal closure instrument.[^tra-construction-history]

## From railway corridor to road and metro

The sources do not use one route-length figure. The operator table gives 10.4
km, the Xindian District Office gives 10.5 km, and a Taipei City Government
account gives 10.7 km.[^tra-construction-history][^xindian-office-bitang][^taipei-jingmei-history] The Taipei account also distinguishes fourteen stations in
the Japanese-era record from ten after retrocession.[^taipei-jingmei-history]
Those figures are retained as source-specific records rather than averaged into
a new length or station count.

New Taipei DORTS says the track was converted into ordinary roads in 1968 and
identifies today’s Tingzhou, Roosevelt and Beixin roads with the former route.[^dorts-xindian-history] Taipei’s account likewise describes road construction on
the dismantled alignment and says that much of the modern Xindian MRT route
south of Guting almost overlaps the old railway.[^taipei-jingmei-history][^dorts-xindian-history] “Overlaps” is the safe description: the modern metro is not
silently treated as the same historical TRA route.

## Records still missing

The checked primary records do not provide a complete former-station register,
a dated legal closure instrument, a full track-removal chronology or a surveyed
boundary showing which road and metro sections reused railway land.[^tra-construction-history][^dorts-xindian-history][^taipei-jingmei-history] Exact last-service times remain TBC because the
full sources disagree or give only a year.[^dorts-xindian-history][^ntu-wanxin-history]
