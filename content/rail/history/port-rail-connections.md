## Kaohsiung: a century-old line, closed for two projects at once

Kaohsiung's harbour line ran from Kaohsiung Port Station to Kaohsiung
Station via Qianzhen Yard, split operationally into a First Harbour Line of
roughly 13.471 km and a Second Harbour Line of roughly 8.767 km, later
truncated to 5.18 km serving China Steel Corp.[^zhwiki-kaohsiung-harbour-line]
Its final train departed Qianzhen Yard at 16:48 on 28 September 2018 — a date
and time independently corroborated by two separate news outlets that this
research read in full, not just as headlines.[^ltn-kaohsiung-harbour-line]
Both attribute the closure to the Kaohsiung Railway Underground Project;
Wikipedia's own timeline additionally credits the Circular Light Rail's
Phase 2 construction, which needed the corridor once the underground project
left the harbour line disconnected from the trunk network — the two
accounts are not necessarily in conflict, but neither source states both
reasons together, so both are recorded here rather than merged into one
narrative.[^zhwiki-kaohsiung-harbour-line] Kaohsiung Port Station itself had
already been decommissioned on 16 December 2008 and is now preserved as the
舊打狗驛故事館 museum.[^zhwiki-kaohsiung-port-station] Kaohsiung's Circular
Light Rail directly reuses parts of the former harbour-line corridor — the
city's own Mass Rapid Transit Bureau names specific segments along Kaisyuan
and Chenggong roads as following the old TRA right-of-way — though this
research did not independently confirm the light rail's total route length
or station count against that same primary page.[^mtbu-kaohsiung-lrt-route]
No freight rail remains at Kaohsiung Port today.

## Keelung: gone in three stages

Keelung's harbour line dates, per an unverified Wikipedia infobox figure, to
1891.[^zhwiki-keelung-harbour-line] Its closure came in stages: partial
closure in December 2003, yard demolition in 2011 tied to a station
relocation project, and the removal of the last remaining stub — serving
Pier West 4 — on 29 June 2015, coinciding with the new Keelung Station's
opening.[^zhwiki-keelung-harbour-line] That final date rests on an archived
Public Television Service article that Wikipedia quotes but that this
research could not independently fetch, since the fetch tool used could not
reach web.archive.org — it is reported here as Wikipedia's citation, not as
independently verified.[^zhwiki-keelung-harbour-line] No active freight rail
connection to Keelung port survives today.

## Taichung: the one port line still running, on a disputed birth date

Taichung Port Station remains an active TRA station today, with a
freight-consignment desk still listed on the operator's own current station
page.[^tra-taichung-port-station] The line itself is 7.8 km, but its origin
date is genuinely disputed between two different TRA-adjacent sources: a
Wikipedia article, uncited for this specific figure, gives an opening or
operator-transfer date of 31 October 1976, while a TRA construction-history
PDF — read directly for a separate piece of this project's research — lists
the same line as built in 1973 and put into operation in 1974, and the
current TRA HTML construction-history page omits the line
entirely.[^zhwiki-taichung-port-line][^tra-construction-history-pdf] Both
dates are published here without resolving which is correct. TRA's own 2024
social-media statement puts the line's annual freight volume at 202,000
tonnes — mostly wheat moved to flour mills at Fugang and Hsinchu, at roughly
three round trips per day on the port line itself — within a system-wide TRA
freight total of 6.81 million tonnes that year.[^tra-facebook-freight] A
separate, uncited claim on Wikipedia that this wheat traffic ended in June
2025 and was replaced by irregular energy-storage-container shipments from
November 2025 could not be corroborated against any primary or independently
written source in repeated searches, and is not published here as
established.

## Records still missing

Keelung's 1891 opening date and its 2003/2011 partial-closure milestones
rest on citations this research could not open, and remain unverified.
Kaohsiung's exact 1 October 2018 formal abolition date, cited by Wikipedia
to two offline print sources (a book and a railfan-magazine issue), is
likewise unverified. No modal-split or rail-share-of-cargo figure was found
published by TIPC or TRA for any of the three ports. Taichung Port Line's
construction date is not established — the TRA construction-history PDF and
an uncited Wikipedia figure disagree by three years — and the 2025
commodity-shift claim is not established by any source found either.
