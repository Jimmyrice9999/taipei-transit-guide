## What the TRA route-history table actually says

Taiwan Railways' full construction-history table names Taipei–Danshui as a
21.2 km route.[^tra-construction-history] The same table places its construction
in 1900, operation start in 1901 and the beginning of dismantling on 8 May
1989.[^tra-construction-history] Those fields describe the operator's historical
route record; the dismantling date is not silently rewritten as the last day of
passenger service.[^tra-construction-history]

The National Archives' full article supplies a different kind of chronology. It
dates the line's opening to 25 August 1901, describes an initial role carrying
materials for railway construction, and says the line later became a major
passenger corridor that operated until 1988.[^archives-tamsui-history] The article
also frames the line's five historical sidings as part of its evolution, but its
detailed research PDF timed out in this retrieval and is not used for a siding
inventory.[^archives-tamsui-history]

## From TRA railway to MRT corridor

The transition was a conversion of railway geography into a new rapid-transit
system. DORTS's builder page says the current Tamsui route runs south from
Tamsui along the original Beidan railway right-of-way before continuing through
elevated and underground sections.[^dorts-tamsui] The same page describes the
current route as approximately 23.8 km including the approximately 1.2 km
Beitou–Xinbeitou branch, with 22 stations and one main depot.[^dorts-tamsui]

The Control Yuan places the former Tamsui Line and Xinbeitou branch in its table
of old TRA tracks converted into MRT systems.[^control-yuan-old-rail] That is a
route-level reuse classification, not proof that every present track, station
shell or boundary follows the former railway exactly.[^control-yuan-old-rail][^dorts-tamsui]

DORTS's technical schedule records a separate MRT measurement: 22.8 km, a
1988–1997 construction period and a 25 December 1997 opening date.[^dorts-schedule]
The current route page's approximately 23.8 km and the schedule's 22.8 km are
therefore published beside the TRA table's 21.2 km, with their apparent scopes
left visible rather than averaged.[^tra-construction-history][^dorts-tamsui][^dorts-schedule]

## What survives from the railway era

The old Xinbeitou station makes the conversion physical and visible. Taipei's
cultural-affairs page says the station entered history when the TRA Tamsui Line
stopped in 1988, was dismantled and moved to the Taiwan Folk Village in
Changhua, then returned through a 2016 reconstruction and reopened in Taipei in
2017.[^taipei-newbeitou] This is a heritage-station chronology, not a complete
record of the former branch's track removal.[^taipei-newbeitou]

The surviving evidence supports a phased account: a 1901 TRA opening, an
operating era ending in the 1988 boundary reported by the Archives, physical
dismantling beginning in May 1989, and MRT construction and opening work from
1988 to 1997.[^archives-tamsui-history][^tra-construction-history][^dorts-schedule]
The exact last train date, legal closure instrument, former station sequence,
gauge chronology and boundary between reused right-of-way and rebuilt
infrastructure remain TBC.[^tra-construction-history][^archives-tamsui-history][^dorts-tamsui]
