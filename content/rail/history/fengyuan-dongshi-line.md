## A TRA branch built for the mountain economy

Taiwan Railways’ construction-history table records the Fengyuan–Dongshi route
as 14.1 km, constructed in 1958 and put into operation in 1959.[^tra-construction-history]
The line is also called the Dongshi Line, Dongshi branch or Dongfeng Railway in
the local historical record.[^taichung-dongfeng-retrospective] The Forestry
Bureau’s 1991 chronology says the branch had been built to move materials for
Dasyueshan Forestry Company.[^forest-bureau-1991]

A Taichung municipal retrospective describes the route as running between
Fengyuan and Dongshi, with Meizi, Shigang and Puzikou named between the two
endpoints.[^taichung-dongfeng-retrospective] That is a retrospective stop
sequence, not a complete period station register; the latter remains open until
an operator or archival register is found.

## The last train and the operator’s withdrawal date

The operator’s chronology records a precise administrative milestone: on 1
September 1991, all Dongshi Line stations stopped operating and were withdrawn.[^tra-timeline]
The municipal retrospective dates the last train to 31 August 1991.[^taichung-dongshi-retrospective]
These statements are compatible as different boundaries—last public train and
the following day’s operator withdrawal—so neither is replaced by the other.

The operator’s construction-history table gives the broader year-and-month
record, “stopped operating in September 1991.”[^tra-construction-history] The
Forestry Bureau likewise records the branch as suspended in September 1991 and
describes the proposed next use of part of the former corridor as an 11 km green
corridor park.[^forest-bureau-1991]

## From railway right-of-way to bikeway

The current Taichung tourism page describes the 13.6 km Dongfeng Bikeway as a
cycle path rebuilt from an abandoned railway, with former platforms, rails and
the Dongshi tunnel still visible along the route.[^taichung-tourism-dongfeng]
That current-path length is not substituted for the operator’s 14.1 km railway
length: the sources describe different assets and scopes.[^tra-construction-history][^taichung-tourism-dongfeng]

The 1991 Forestry Bureau entry records an earlier plan for an 11 km section of
the former line to become a green-corridor environmental park.[^forest-bureau-1991]
The later 13.6 km tourism figure and the earlier 11 km plan are therefore kept
as separate planning and present-use measurements, not presented as one
continuous conversion milestone.[^forest-bureau-1991][^taichung-tourism-dongfeng]

## Records still missing

The checked primary sources do not provide a complete former-station register,
a dated legal closure instrument, a track-removal chronology or a surveyed
boundary showing which parts of the present bikeway retain the historical
right-of-way.[^tra-construction-history][^tra-timeline][^forest-bureau-1991][^taichung-tourism-dongfeng]
The municipal stop list and the current bikeway description are useful
orientation, but they do not settle those archival questions.[^taichung-dongshi-retrospective][^taichung-tourism-dongfeng]
