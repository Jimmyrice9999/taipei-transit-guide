## A double-track segment, not a separate current route

Taiwan Railways' full construction-history table lists Houtong–Sandiaoling as
row 42, a 2.5 km segment marked as double-track.[^tra-construction-history] The
aligned year fields give 1967 for construction and 1968 for operation start,
with the explanation “Extended to be double-tracked.”[^tra-construction-history]
This is a historical construction entry: it does not by itself define a
separate current timetable line or a present-day route identity.[^tra-construction-history][^tra-bike-route]

The table's place name is written 侯硐─三貂嶺, while current operator pages
identify the western station as 7350 猴硐 and the eastern station as 7330
三貂嶺.[^tra-construction-history][^tra-station-houtong][^tra-station-sandiaoling]
The spelling change in the current station page is kept visible rather than
silently treating the two Chinese forms as different locations.[^tra-construction-history][^tra-station-houtong]

## The stations still sit inside the operating corridor

The current Houtong station page shows trains toward Sandiaoling and lists the
current route status as normal at the time it was fetched.[^tra-station-houtong]
The current Sandiaoling page shows trains toward Houtong and also displays
services toward Mudan and toward the Pingxi branch.[^tra-station-sandiaoling]
Those pages establish present station adjacency and service direction, not the
historic date on which the second track was commissioned.[^tra-station-houtong][^tra-station-sandiaoling]

TRA's current bicycle-train station map places Houtong and Sandiaoling in the
New Taipei station group and also lists Sandiaoling with the Pingxi/Shen'ao
group.[^tra-bike-route] That grouping is an operating and passenger-information
view, not evidence that the 2.5 km construction entry was a separate branch.
The historical segment and the current service group therefore remain two
layers of the record.[^tra-construction-history][^tra-bike-route]

## A wider modern engineering problem

The operator's full safety report discusses a larger Houtong–Shuangxi corridor,
not just the 2.5 km construction-table segment.[^tra-safety-report] It counts
16 curve bottlenecks with radii of 300–400 metres and describes a proposed
cutoff beginning west of Houtong station and connecting toward the north end
of Wufen Tunnel.[^tra-safety-report] Those details are useful context for why
the Houtong–Sandiaoling area remains an engineering subject, but they do not
change the historical table's 2.5 km value or prove that the proposed work was
built.[^tra-safety-report][^tra-construction-history]

The safety report's larger study boundary also prevents a false comparison:
its Houtong–Shuangxi scope includes more railway than the row 42
Houtong–Sandiaoling entry.[^tra-safety-report][^tra-construction-history] The
two values are retained as different scopes rather than added, averaged or
substituted.[^tra-safety-report][^tra-construction-history]

## Records still missing

The checked operator records do not provide the exact day and month of the
1967–1968 work, a contract or completion certificate for the second track, a
track-by-track station plan, or a surveyed centreline proving the historical
2.5 km boundary.[^tra-construction-history][^tra-safety-report] They also do
not establish that the modern Houtong–Shuangxi cutoff proposal was completed.
Those remain TBC rather than being inferred from current trains or from the
existence of a double-track entry in the historical table.[^tra-safety-report][^tra-station-houtong][^tra-station-sandiaoling]
