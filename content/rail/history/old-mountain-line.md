## Bypassed because it could not be widened

The original Western Trunk Line alignment between Sanyi and Fengyuan via
Houlong, now known as the Old Mountain Line (舊山線), was completed in
1908.[^motc-tourism-old-mountain-line] Zh.wikipedia's account, citing the 1911
official railway history, breaks that completion into stages — Miaoli–Sanyi
open by 7 October 1903, Sanyi–Houlong by 20 February 1908 and Houlong–Fengyuan
by 20 April 1908 — a level of detail this project has not independently
verified.[^zhwiki-old-mountain-line] Shengxing Station, on the old alignment,
sits at 402.326 m, TRA's highest station, and is corroborated by every source
checked.[^motc-tourism-old-mountain-line] Zh.wikipedia separately dates the
station's own opening, as a signal station rather than a full station, to 1
April 1907, with an upgrade to full-station status on 1 April
1930.[^zhwiki-shengxing-station]

Rather than double-track the existing route, TRA built an entirely new,
straighter alignment about 4 km shorter.[^culture-memory-bank-old-new-line] A
Ministry of Culture-hosted, Miaoli County-curated account describes the old
route as "the most treacherous and difficult to traverse on the entire
[Western Trunk] line" — steep grades, sharp curves, and repeated hills and
rivers — which made widening it in place impractical.[^culture-memory-bank-old-new-line] The last train on the old
line, Chu-Kuang Express No. 29, passed Shengxing Station on the night of 23
September 1998; the new alignment took over the following morning, 24
September 1998.[^culture-memory-bank-closure-reopening]

## A line already scarred by earthquakes

The old alignment's best-known landmark, Longteng Bridge (龍騰斷橋, also
known as 魚藤坪斷橋), was destroyed in the 21 April 1935 Hsinchu–Taichung
earthquake — centred on Kuandao Mountain — rather than repaired: the Railway
Bureau built a replacement bridge roughly 80 m to the west, completed 15 July
1938, and left the ruined original standing as a
landmark.[^twh-boch-old-mountain-line] That ruin was damaged again in the 1999
earthquake.[^twh-boch-old-mountain-line] The old alignment as a whole is
described by the Tourism Administration as running through 8 tunnels, 3
bridges and 4 stations, though other secondary accounts give different tunnel
and bridge counts for the line's original, pre-1935 configuration; this page
does not attempt to reconcile the two, as no source read for it does
either.[^motc-tourism-old-mountain-line]

## Heritage status and today's rail bike

Two of the 1938-era replacement iron bridges — 內社川鐵橋 (Neishechuan Iron
Bridge) and the Yutengping replacement span — were designated Miaoli County
monuments, approved by the county's Tangible Cultural Assets Review Committee
on 10 March 2019 and announced the next day, making them the corridor's fourth
and fifth protected heritage sites after Shengxing Station, Longteng Broken
Bridge and Taichung's Taian Station.[^einfo-two-bridges-monument] This page
does not state the designation dates for Shengxing Station or Longteng Bridge
themselves as established facts: they are reported by several secondary
accounts, but the Ministry of Culture's own heritage database could not be
read to confirm them directly (see Remaining gaps).

The old alignment briefly returned to scheduled service on 5 June 2010, with
a steam-hauled heritage train running Sanyi to the former Taian
station.[^culture-memory-bank-closure-reopening] Miaoli County Government has
since redeveloped the corridor as 舊山線鐵道自行車 (Old Mountain Line Rail
Bike), which the Tourism Administration describes as Taiwan's first rail-bike
attraction, run under a "dynamic preservation" model rather than as a TRA
passenger service.[^motc-tourism-old-mountain-line] Sources disagree on
exactly when the Rail Bike itself began operating, and this page does not
pick between them.

## Remaining gaps

The Ministry of Culture's National Cultural Heritage Network — the primary
record for every heritage-designation date on this corridor — is a
JavaScript-rendered site that returned only its static shell on every
case-record fetch attempted, so its content remains unverified here. No
designation date for Shengxing Station or Longteng Bridge is therefore
established on this page; each is not established beyond the secondary
reporting cited above. TRA's own first-person account of the 1998 bypass
decision was not found; the reasoning given on this page comes from a Miaoli
County-curated Ministry of Culture page, not the operator. Longteng Bridge's
original construction year, the exact Rail Bike launch year, and whether TRA
retains any operating role on the corridor today are all TBC.
