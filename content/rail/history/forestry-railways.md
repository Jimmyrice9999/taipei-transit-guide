## Three systems, one purpose

太平山, 八仙山 and the connecting 羅東 line together moved logged timber out
of Taiwan's mountains for roughly six decades under both Japanese and
Republic of China administration, forming — with Alishan — what Taiwanese
forestry history describes as the three great state-run logging operations
of the era.[^zhwiki-baxianshan]

## Taipingshan: 1915 to 1982, then a 3 km tourist line

Logging at Taipingshan began in 1915, following a 1914 survey
expedition.[^tps-forest-history] Operations shifted in 1937 to "New
Taipingshan," a further area upstream on the Lanyang River, where logging
continued until 1982, when the operator formally ended direct logging
under a shift in forestry policy.[^tps-forest-history][^luodong-park-primary]
The network's own operator describes 16 named lines totalling more than 100
km across the system's whole operating life, while a separate account citing
a specialist book instead gives a narrower figure — six mountain lines
totalling 31.15 km plus one 36.40 km lowland line, summing to 68.01
km.[^tps-bengbeng][^zhwiki-taipingshan-railway] These figures are not
reconciled here: the 16-line total may count every spur ever built across
decades of operation, including lines predating the 1937 move, while the
smaller figure may describe only lines active at one particular point — no
source found explains the gap. The gauge is given as 762 mm, though only by
a secondary source; no fetched primary page states the figure
directly.[^zhwiki-taipingshan-railway]

One stretch survives as a tourist operation. The 茂興線 (Maoxing Line),
roughly 20.9 km, began carrying timber in 1937 and stopped in 1979; a 3 km
section from Taipingshan to Maoxing was restored in 1985 and, after
certification under Taiwan's mechanical-amusement-facility regulations,
formally reopened to the public as the "蹦蹦車" (bengbeng car) in November
1991, using six diesel locomotives built in 1988 and entering service in
1991.[^tps-bengbeng] A second short remnant, the 見晴線 (Jianqing Line), was
converted into the 見晴懷古步道 walking trail; the operator's own page
describes the trail but does not give the year of conversion.

## Baxianshan: dismantled, nothing but photographs

Construction of Baxianshan's forest railway began in 1917, was halted by
local resistance, and resumed in 1922 under continued colonial pressure; the
system reached its final form by 1940 after a second construction phase
following a 1938 shift to new logging grounds.[^recreation-baxianshan] Two
figures for the year logging ended come from two different official pages on
the same forest.gov.tw domain: one gives 1963, describing 48 years of
operation from the 1915 start; the other, citing a park guidebook, gives
1965.[^baxianshan-primary] Both are published here as a conflict rather than
resolved. Total route length is given only by a secondary source as roughly
58.8 to 60+ km across three named segments, though a separate secondary
account uses a different three-way segmentation for what may be the same
alignment, and the two do not obviously correspond.[^zhwiki-baxianshan] No
source found gives Baxianshan's gauge. Unlike Taipingshan, nothing of
Baxianshan's historical railway survives on the ground — the operator's own
page states the entire system was dismantled after logging ended, leaving
only old photographs, with the recreation area's present-day "mountain
train" display built as a reconstruction rather than an original
survival.[^recreation-baxianshan]

## The Luodong connector

The 羅東森林鐵路 carried Taipingshan's logs from the mountain terminus at
土場 down to storage ponds and mills at Luodong, built in stages: the 19.36
km Tuchang–Tiansongbi mountain segment was completed in 1921, and a further
3.07 km municipal donation segment, from 歪仔歪 to 竹林, in
1924.[^luodong-park-primary] The mountain section was damaged by typhoon in
September 1978, and the whole line was formally closed in August 1979, with
production shifted to logging roads and the track subsequently ordered
removed.[^luodong-park-primary] A secondary source independently
corroborates the closure timing and adds declining ridership and
flood-vulnerable riverside construction as contributing factors not stated
on the primary page.[^zhwiki-luodong-railway] The former mill site was
registered as a cultural landscape under the Cultural Heritage Preservation
Act in 2012 and now operates as the Luodong Forestry Culture
Park.[^luodong-park-primary]

## Records still missing

Baxianshan's gauge is not established by any source found. Its
logging-closure year is not established either — 1963 and 1965 are two
different values from two different pages of the same government
department, contradicting each other. Its recreation-area opening year is
likewise not established between the same department's own conflicting
1986 and 1988 pages. Taipingshan's true network-length total is not
established — 100-plus km across 16 lines and 68.01 km across seven
lines are two different published counts. The year the 見晴線 trail
conversion took place is unverified.
No cultural-heritage registration for Taipingshan's surviving Maoxing Line
infrastructure or diesel locomotives was found in this search — recorded as
not found, not confirmed absent.
