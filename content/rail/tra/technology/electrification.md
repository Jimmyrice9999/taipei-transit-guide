Taiwan Railways did not electrify its network in one step. The operator's
achievement record dates the start of the electrification programme to ROC 63
and the full electrification of the Western Main Line to 26 June in ROC 68.[^tra-electrification-history]
The same record divides the western work into three phases rather than treating
the Western Main Line as a single undated project.[^tra-electrification-history]

## The western programme

Phase 1 was Keelung–Zhunan, with an operating length of 125.7 km, and opened
electrically on 24 February in ROC 67.[^tra-electrification-history] Phase 2
covered Zhunan–Changhua: the operator lists 91.2 km on the Coast Line and 89.3
km on the Mountain Line, with the opening dated 25 October in ROC 67.[^tra-electrification-history]
Phase 3 was Changhua–Kaohsiung; the operator says it was complete in June of
ROC 68, two months earlier than planned.[^tra-electrification-history]

The regional chronology continued after the western trunk. The operator
records catenary activation between Badou and Luodong in ROC 89 and completion
of North Link electrification in ROC 92.[^tra-electrification-history] In the
south, the Kaohsiung–Fengshan section was energised for test locomotives in ROC
84, Fengshan–Pingtung was energised and activated in ROC 85, and a Jiuqutang
substation was added in ROC 87.[^tra-electrification-history]

Two short branches have their own dates in the same record. The 6.52 km Shalun
branch connected Zhongzhou with the high-speed rail station; its catenary was
completed in ROC 99 and the branch opened on 2 January in ROC 100.[^tra-electrification-history]
The Liujia work included a new Hsinchu–Zhuzhong link and an electrified Liujia
branch described by the operator as 11 km; the new Neiwan service opened on 11
November in ROC 100.[^tra-electrification-history]

The operator also records the Hualien–Taitung electrification project as a
155.46 km programme covering 30 stations, approved in ROC 97 and started in ROC
98.[^tra-electrification-history] That is a project record and date boundary;
it is not used here as proof of a current segment-by-segment commissioning
register.[^tra-electrification-history]

## What “25 kV” describes

A 2014 Taiwan Railway Journal study describes the ordinary-speed railway power
chain as a Taipower primary substation delivering 69 kV or 161 kV, three-phase
60 Hz power to a railway substation, where transformers reduce it to 25 kV,
single-phase 60 Hz power for the catenary.[^tra-electrification-paper] The
paper describes the catenary system as a combination of overhead contact wire,
feeders, return circuits, warning signs and protective equipment.[^tra-electrification-paper]
This is the study's technical model of the system family, not a claim that the
paper supplies today's equipment inventory.[^tra-electrification-paper]

The operator's achievement page supplies a separate control-room boundary. It
records centralised power dispatch beginning on 18 December in ROC 90, using
SCADA to monitor and control the electrified-line power equipment, including
161 kV and 69 kV transmission, substations and 25 kV catenary switching and
supply.[^tra-electrification-history] A central dispatch description explains
who supervises the system; it does not identify every feeder, isolation point
or current fallback mode.[^tra-electrification-history]

## Aggregate totals and the open register

The operator's 2023 report gives 1,065 km as the network total, made up of 551.2
km of western main-line routes, 423.3 km of eastern main-line routes and 90.5
km of branches.[^tra-network-report] It separately gives 997.7 km as
electrified and 67.3 km as non-electrified, with 241 stations at the end of
2023.[^tra-network-report] These are dated network aggregates, so they are not
silently converted into a current line-by-line catenary map.[^tra-network-report]

The sources used for this record do not provide a current route-by-route
electrification status table, neutral-section map, substation and feeder
inventory, or dated commissioning and renewal register.[^tra-electrification-history][^tra-electrification-paper][^tra-network-report]
