Three distinct systems answer to "TRA signalling," and this page keeps them
separate because the sources do: a completed 2007 train-protection rollout, a
funded but only recently launched dispatch-control upgrade, and an unproven
in-house research programme that has not entered service.

## ATP: completed in 2007, still the backbone

TRA phased out an earlier Ericsson-developed ATS/ATW system as its current
Automatic Train Protection (ATP) system came online; the operator's own
achievement record dates full-line ATP on-board system testing and activation
to August ROC96 (2007), stating that from that point its signalling and
train-safety-protection system reached ERTMS/ETCS Level 1
standard.[^tra-achievement-page] The same record dates dual-redundant
train-occupancy detection — track circuit plus axle counter — to December of
the same year, and traces earlier milestones back to the first electrical
signals in ROC39 (1950) and the first electronic interlocking, at Shulin
yard, in March ROC86 (1997).[^tra-achievement-page]

The 2001 ATP procurement contract went to Bombardier; reliability-verification
testing later recorded 178 faults, and TRA lost a resulting court case,
ordered to repay roughly NT$64.8 million in penalty deductions plus
interest.[^ltn-bombardier-atp-dispute] An investigative account puts TRA's
cumulative signalling and train-protection spending at roughly NT$3–4 billion
over nearly 30 years, with the 2007 ATP system itself costing NT$3.9
billion.[^twreporter-atp-history]

## What the 2018 Puyuma accident changed

The Taiwan Transportation Safety Board's own investigation into the 2018
Puyuma derailment found that ATP had been illegally isolated and the train
was travelling over the applicable speed, attributing part of the root cause
to TRA's own failure to enforce its existing rules against unauthorised ATP
isolation; the report issued 27 recommendations, though the published
announcement does not itemise which specifically concern ATP or signalling
design as distinct from management and procedure.[^ttsb-puyuma-report]

In response, TRA built an "ATP isolation speed-limit system" that forces a
speed cap even when ATP itself is switched off. Sources disagree on both its
budget and its cap: one report gives a 60 km/h limit and a NT$245 million
budget covering 52 systems across 18 Puyuma and 8 Taroko
trainsets;[^ltn-atp-isolation-speedlimit] another gives a NT$270 million
budget.[^twreporter-atp-history] A separate, earlier report describes an
initial ministerial directive for a 65 km/h cap that explicitly did not rule
out going lower — consistent with, but not confirmed as, the same rollout.
Post-accident operating rules also require drivers to check ATP at both cab
ends before departure and require a faulty-ATP train be swapped rather than
dispatched.

## CTC 3.0: the current dispatch-control programme

Separately from train-borne ATP, TRA's dispatch-side Central Traffic Control
system controls route-setting from the operations centre. The Ministry of
Transportation and Communications' own press release states that TRA formally
launched its "third-generation Central Traffic Control System" (CTC 3.0) on
29 April 2026, with Japan Signal Company and Taiwanese partners 亞力電機 and
華電聯網, targeting completion in ROC118 (2029) and describing the existing
system as over 20 years old and no longer meeting modern dispatch
needs.[^motc-ctc3-launch] The new system's stated scope includes intelligent
automatic route-setting driven by schedule, train priority, route occupation
and power-supply status, integration of slope, rockfall, level-crossing and
platform-monitoring alerts, and a geographically separate backup control
centre at the Fugang base in Taoyuan.[^motc-ctc3-launch]

The primary release does not state a budget figure; secondary press
independently gives NT$3.95 billion.[^ettoday-ctc3-budget] Sources also disagree on when the
predecessor system it replaces first entered service: a 2023 wire report
gives 2001 and notes the original manufacturer, Japan Signal, no longer
produces spare parts for it;[^cna-ctc-2023] a 2026 report gives ROC93 (2004).
Both figures are published here rather than one being chosen.

## Train Control 4.0: unproven, and a separate thing

After the Puyuma accident, TRA also launched a domestically engineered
research programme — variously called 行控4.0 or 列控4.0 — aimed at active
train control with functional elements associated with ETCS Level 2 and CBTC,
rather than purchasing an existing foreign system.[^cna-liekong4-2024] This is
not the same programme as CTC 3.0 above. Reported budget figures conflict: a
National Science and Technology Council subsidy is given as NT$60.67
million in one set of reports[^cna-liekong4-2024] and NT$69.46 million in an
earlier one,[^ettoday-liekong4-2021] while a separately scoped NT$56.4 million
contract went to the winning bidder, selected by public tender among three
teams.[^ltn-liekong4-contractor]

The programme completed a "proof of concept" acceptance in December 2022 but
had not progressed further by an April 2024 legislative hearing, at which the
Taiwan Transportation Safety Board's chairman called the system "fundamentally
unusable" and a legislator alleged the winning contractor's claimed reference
project in Jacksonville, Florida had been denied by the transit authority
there.[^cna-liekong4-2024] Whether the programme remains active, paused or
discontinued as of 2026 is TBC — a claimed December 2024 discontinuation
decision could not be independently confirmed from any source read for this
page.
