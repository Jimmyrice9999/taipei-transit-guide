## A national bridge code whose numbers this page could not read

MOTC's Railway Bridge Seismic Design Code was first promulgated 15 July 1999
and has been revised three times since, most recently 30 November 2021; it
applies to general railway bridges of 150 m span or less, with special
structures needing separate treatment.[^motc-bridge-seismic-code] The
regulation index page, fetched in full, confirms the code sets three
horizontal seismic design force levels — Level I, II and III — by site
ground classification. It does not itself give the numeric thresholds for
each level, and this page could not extract text from either MOTC's own copy
of the code or a civil-engineers'-association mirror to check them; the
commonly assumed 30/475/2,500-year return-period pattern used in Taiwan's
building and highway-bridge codes is not stated here as established for the
railway code, since no source read for this page confirms it.[^motc-bridge-seismic-code]
A Taipei Metro engineering publication's bibliography cites the code's second
revision with a December 2006 date, one month earlier than MOTC's own 9
January 2007 dating of the same revision; both are published rather than one
being picked.[^dorts-code-citation]

## An operating rule the operating regulation doesn't contain

News coverage of several 2026 earthquakes consistently attributes a
graduated speed-restriction rule to "TRA regulations": at intensity 4, the
first train after a quake runs at 30 km/h and later trains at 60 km/h to the
next station pending inspection; at intensity 3, the first train runs at 60
km/h and, absent an abnormality, later trains resume normal speed.[^ltn-quake-speed-rule]
The rule's application in real events is corroborated by news reports naming
specific sections run under it during a real 2026 event.[^ltn-quake-speed-rule]
But the national 鐵路行車規則
(Railway Operating Rules) regulation, read in full, does not contain this
rule — it requires only that operators install natural-hazard detection
facilities, and does so explicitly for high-speed rail, not
TRA.[^law-moj-operating-rules] The speed rule's actual written source is most
likely an internal TRA operating manual not published online; this page does
not claim to have located it.

Separately, TRA operates an AI-based slope rockfall-warning system, reported
as installed on the North Link Line from late 2022 and credited with helping
a train avoid a rockfall zone during the 2024 Hualien earthquake — a general
hazard-detection system, not a seismic-specific one.[^cna-rockfall-warning-system]
The Central Weather
Administration's own FAQ page names automatic transport-system control
during earthquakes only as a future capability, and does not name TRA or
THSR as a current implementer; whether TRA runs any automatic,
CWA-triggered train-stop system, as opposed to the manual speed rule above,
is TBC.[^cwa-eew-faq] CWA and TRA signed a disaster-resilience cooperation
MOU in September 2025, explicitly citing the 2024 earthquake's aftermath as
motivation.[^cna-cwa-tra-mou]

## The 2024 Hualien earthquake and its aftermath

The earthquake struck 3 April 2024, offshore of Shoufeng, Hualien; TRA
suspended Yilan–Fenglin service the same day after rockfall and rail
buckling at multiple points, targeting single-track restoration overnight
and full double-track restoration by noon the following day, while adding
extra inter-city services, substituting road shuttles, and waiving
cancellation fees for affected tickets.[^cna-0403-restoration] That initial
restoration did not hold: the North Link Line's Heren–Chongde section
suffered three further closures over the following months — a 21 June
mudslide that derailed a train, a 4 July boulder collapse that severed
rails, and extensive debris flows during Typhoon Gaemi in late
July.[^cna-north-link-2024] TRA's chairman said the volume of remaining
unstable material on the mountainside could not be estimated, and TRA opened
a realignment feasibility study.[^cna-north-link-2024]

Three realignment options were proposed for the Chongde–Heren section — a
mountain-side tunnel, optimising the existing route, or a coastal-side
reroute — but MOTC's preliminary review returned the proposal for further
engineering study, with a cited geologist flagging debris-flow risk zones
and high lateral rock pressure as obstacles to the tunnel
option.[^pts-realignment-rejected] With realignment stalled, TRA advanced an
interim, roughly NT$7.799 billion Yilan-Hualien-Taitung and South Link
railway safety-improvement plan instead, covering vulnerable sections with
slope stabilisation, cut-and-cover tunnels and box culverts; the National
Development Council approved the plan in principle, with construction
reportedly targeted for 2026 and completion by 2031.[^udn-north-link-safety-plan]
