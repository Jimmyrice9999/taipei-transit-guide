## What corporatisation actually changed

TRA became a corporatised state enterprise — Taiwan Railway Corporation
(臺灣鐵路股份有限公司), replacing the former government agency — on 1
January 2024.[^tra-first-year-report] The transition split TRA's land
holdings into two distinct legal tracks, per its own establishment
act.[^zhwiki-tra-establishment-act] Assets the new company actually operates
on — stations, tracks, yards — transferred to TRA Corp itself, under a mix
of capitalised investment, lease and superficies-right arrangements.
Everything else — "surplus" land inherited from the old Taiwan Railway
Administration — moved into a separate fund administered by the Ministry
of Transportation and Communications, set up specifically to retire the old
agency's debt, with its own development and disposal rules and a
floor-area-transfer provision for any fund-held asset later designated a
monument or historic building.[^zhwiki-tra-establishment-act] TRA Corp's
own first-year report independently confirms this fund is a live,
functioning accounting boundary: its subsidiary-business revenue is
reported explicitly "excluding asset-disposal gains and debt-repayment-fund
transfers," a phrase that only makes sense if money is genuinely moving
into a separate pool.[^tra-first-year-report]

Both the Railway Act and TRA Corp's own establishment act give the company
fast-track access to urban-plan rezoning around its
projects.[^zhwiki-railway-act][^zhwiki-tra-establishment-act] Land
development itself runs through an internal division, 資產開發處 (Asset
Development Division), with four regional branch offices covering the
island north to south — not a separate real-estate
subsidiary.[^tra-tender-page]

## Six deals, six real figures

TRA Corp's first-year report names its executed land-development cases from
that year individually, not as an aggregate figure.[^tra-first-year-report]
Two are lease renewals under the
ROT (reconstruct-operate-transfer) model: Banqiao Station's retail
concession, renewed with 冠誠公司 across March–May 2024 for an estimated
NT$159 million a year, and Xinzuoying Station's, renewed with 冠友公司 that
May–June for an estimated NT$13.18 million a
year.[^tra-first-year-report] Three are urban-renewal or MRT-adjacent
development cases with construction or tender already under way: the
Nangang Marshalling Yard urban-renewal project, whose main construction
began 18 June 2024 with an estimated returned property value near NT$70.8
billion; a Pingtung Station urban-renewal tender announced that October,
estimated at roughly NT$1.76 billion; and TRA land participating in
Kaohsiung's Gangshan RK1 MRT development case, tendered that June at an
estimated NT$770 million — a fraction of Nangang's scale, a useful reminder
that "TRA land development" spans wildly different project
sizes.[^tra-first-year-report] A sixth item is really three signed cases
grouped together: the Taipei Twin Towers development, the Nangang
Marshalling Yard project above, and a separate Nangang Commercial-3
public urban-renewal case, all projected to complete progressively before
2033, with a combined projected benefit exceeding NT$30
billion.[^tra-first-year-report]

Four more sites — in Keelung, Taipei's Andong Street, Taichung's Fuxing
Road, and Kaohsiung's Zili Road — were still in pre-tender planning as of
the report's writing, with consultants engaged for legal, market and
financial groundwork ahead of board and ministry
approval.[^tra-first-year-report] Beyond named individual cases, TRA Corp
states it is prioritising development at ten stations and surrounding
areas through 2024–2025: Kaohsiung, Fengshan, Qidu, Taichung, Pingtung,
Yuanlin and Sankuaicuo stations, plus named streets in Hualien and
Taipei.[^tra-first-year-report]

## What this adds up to

Asset development brought in NT$3.781 billion in 2024, 17% of TRA Corp's
total revenue that year — behind only passenger service (77%) and ahead of
freight (2%) and lunchbox/merchandise sales (4%).[^tra-first-year-report]
That figure was up 8.5% on 2023 once disposal gains and fund transfers are
excluded.[^tra-first-year-report] Development methods TRA Corp names as new
additions in its first year include joint construction development (合建
開發), superficies rights (設定地上權), participation in hazardous/old
building reconstruction, and what it calls "MRT-style development" (捷運
開發) — the last a direct echo of the mechanism Taipei's own metro operators
use.[^tra-first-year-report]

## What this page could not confirm

Whether TRA has ever built a literal shared structure — a station combined
with an independently-owned private tower in one building, the way TRTC
and NTMC's joint developments work — could not be confirmed anywhere in
this research; a promising Taichung lead (a station-integrated commercial
development branded "橘町三丁目") could not be verified against a working
source. Taipei Station's long-running ground-floor retail concession —
operated by Breeze Group from 2007 and due to pass to Shin Kong Mitsukoshi
in 2026 — is a different mechanism again: TRA retains the building outright
and contracts out its operation, rather than sharing ownership of a
co-built structure.[^zhwiki-taipei-station] This is not independently
verified against a primary TRA source and should be treated as a lead, not
an established fact.
