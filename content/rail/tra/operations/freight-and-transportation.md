The June 2026 statistical report is an aggregate transportation record, not a station-by-station freight operating plan.[^tra-monthly-2026-06] Its contents list separate tables for freight volume, freight tonnage by goods, ton-kilometres by goods, transportation status, train counts, train kilometres and car kilometres.[^tra-monthly-2026-06]

## Freight volume and work

The report records 296,529 tonnes of freight in June 2026, with a daily average of 9,884 tonnes.[^tra-monthly-2026-06] It records 2,039,101 tonnes for January through June 2026, and says that the June monthly total was 37.78% lower than June 2025 while the cumulative total was 33.83% lower than the same period in 2025.[^tra-monthly-2026-06]

Freight work in June was 15,001,347 tonne-kilometres, or an average of 500,045 tonne-kilometres per day.[^tra-monthly-2026-06] The report gives an average freight journey of 50.6 km for June and 52.2 km for January through June.[^tra-monthly-2026-06]

The June freight total included 119,893 tonnes of cement and 106,345 tonnes of limestone in the report's goods table.[^tra-monthly-2026-06] Those commodity rows are components of the report's aggregate freight table and are not treated here as a complete origin-and-destination account.[^tra-monthly-2026-06]

## Revenue and operating measures

June freight revenue was NT$28,561,095, with a daily average of NT$952,037.[^tra-monthly-2026-06] The January–June freight-revenue total was NT$206,212,960, and the report records a 12.53% year-on-year decrease for that cumulative measure.[^tra-monthly-2026-06]

The report records 1,360,940 freight-car kilometres in June, an average of 45,365 per day, and freight-car utilisation of 42.67%.[^tra-monthly-2026-06] It records 100.00% freight-train punctuality for June and 41.81% freight-car utilisation for January through June.[^tra-monthly-2026-06]

The report attributes the month-on-month freight-tonnage decrease to reductions in limestone and special goods, the year-on-year decrease to reductions in limestone, aggregate and cement, and the cumulative decrease to reductions in cement, aggregate and limestone.[^tra-monthly-2026-06]

## A 74-year decline

TRA's own 2025 statistical yearbook carries an unbroken annual freight-tonnage
series back to 1951.[^tra-yearbook-2025] Volume peaked in 1976 at 17,000,403
tonnes, stayed near that level through the mid-1990s (16,480,600 tonnes in
1996), then fell steadily: 12,371,444 tonnes in 2001, 10,661,020 in 2011, and
5,193,219 in 2025 — roughly a 70% fall from the 1976 peak.[^tra-yearbook-2025]
The freight-car fleet fell over the same span in a similar proportion, from
5,655 cars in 1971 to 1,428 at the end of 2025.[^tra-yearbook-2025] This gives
the June 2026 report's own year-on-year decline (already reported above) a
much longer context: freight has been TRA's shrinking business for half a
century, not a recent development.[^tra-monthly-2026-06][^tra-yearbook-2025]

TRA discontinued less-than-carload (零擔) freight service on 1 January 1992,
a change visible in the yearbook's own tonnage series (a non-zero L.C.L.
column through 1991, then none) and stated directly in its tariff-history
table.[^tra-yearbook-2025] TRA's own reporting-form methodology classifies
freight by raw-material and processed-goods category and is compiled from
its own freight data warehouse before formal reporting to the Ministry of
Transportation and Communications' Statistics
Department.[^tra-freight-form-methodology]

## Named customers and route length

The yearbook's rolling-stock tables carry a specific, three-times-repeated
footnote: TRA's diesel-electric locomotive count includes six
self-supplied units belonging to Taiwan Cement Corporation
(台泥).[^tra-yearbook-2025] That is the only specific freight customer this
page can name from a primary source, and only as a locomotive-fleet
footnote, not a route or contract description.[^tra-yearbook-2025]

TRA's network has a single freight-only route segment, the Hualien Port
Line (Beipu–Hualien Port), given as 7.2 km in the yearbook's route-length
classification table and 7.4 km in its separate line-and-track length
table — a genuine internal conflict within the same primary
document.[^tra-yearbook-2025] One table's own footnote states its "average
operating mileage" is calculated by dividing operating-extended
kilometres by the year's operating days, which could explain a fractional
difference from a fixed nominal length, but the yearbook does not itself
draw that connection, so both figures are published rather than
reconciled.[^tra-yearbook-2025]

The same yearbook's station-level freight table shows the North Link Line
(北迴線) carrying by far the largest freight tonnage of any line in 2025 —
4,670,163 tonnes loaded, 2,039,935 tonnes unloaded — while every individual
station listed under that line shows zero.[^tra-yearbook-2025] No source
checked for this page explains how that line-total freight is generated if
no constituent station shows any; it is reported as an unresolved gap, not
attributed to the Hualien Port Line branch or any other explanation.[^tra-yearbook-2025]

## Freight's share of TRA's revenue

TRA's own 2025 operating-revenue table gives freight revenue as 1.21% of
total revenue (including non-operating income), against 53.90% for
passenger revenue.[^tra-yearbook-2025] Measured only against operating
revenue (passenger, freight and other, excluding non-operating income),
freight is 1.26% — a figure this page calculates directly from the
table's own raw NT$ amounts, not itself printed in the source, and
reported alongside the source's own 1.21% figure rather than in place of
it, since the two use different denominators.[^tra-yearbook-2025]

Freight revenue rose 6.65% in 2025 even as freight tonnage fell 4.35% in
the same year, a genuine pattern rather than a contradiction: a freight
tariff increase took effect 23 June 2025.[^tra-yearbook-2025] No source
checked states explicitly that the tariff change caused the revenue rise;
the two facts are reported side by side, not asserted as
cause-and-effect.[^tra-yearbook-2025]

## Boundary of this record

These figures describe the report's month, cumulative period and aggregate categories, so they should not be read as a current list of freight trains, terminals or wagon assignments.[^tra-monthly-2026-06] The opened report does not establish station-to-station freight origins and destinations, an active freight-terminal inventory, a current wagon-by-commodity roster, or current freight timetable and working-day rules.[^tra-monthly-2026-06]

Those gaps remain TBC until the operator publishes a joined freight movement dataset, a current terminal register, a wagon and commodity assignment record, or a dated freight operating plan that answers them directly.[^tra-monthly-2026-06]
