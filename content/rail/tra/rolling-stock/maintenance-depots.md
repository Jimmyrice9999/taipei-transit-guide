## Two tiers, not one list of depots

TRA maintains its rolling stock across two legally distinct tiers, and
treating them as one flat list of "depots" — as the fleet-roster page's
group-level stabling assignments (Qidu, Taipei and Hsinchu for EMU900;
Hsinchu, Changhua and Chiayi for EMU800) might suggest — misreads the
structure. TRA's Organization Act authorises workshops (機廠) to be
established under their own separate law, apart from TRA's general branch
structure.[^law-moj-organization-act] The general branch-organisation law
instead names seven operating sections (機務段: Qidu, Taipei, Hsinchu,
Changhua, Chiayi, Kaohsiung, Hualien, plus three sub-sections at Shulin,
Yilan and Taitung) and four inspection sections (檢車段: Qidu, Taipei,
Changhua, Kaohsiung, plus one at Kaohsiung Port), with duty wording limited
to inspection and upkeep rather than comprehensive overhaul.[^law-moj-branch-organization]
Rolling-stock inspection itself is governed by a separate national
regulation setting four maintenance levels, Level 1 through Level 4 — a
different framework from the five-level facility-grading scale this project
has already documented for Taipei Metro depots, which grades a depot's
capability rather than a per-vehicle inspection depth.[^law-moj-inspection-rules]

## Three workshops do the heavy work

TRA's approved 2024–2027 rolling-stock maintenance plan names exactly three
current workshops performing Level 3 and Level 4 overhaul: Fugang (富岡),
Chaozhou (潮州) and Hualien (花蓮), and states that Level 3/4 maintenance for
TRA's core fleet is reserved to these three rather than distributed across
the operating sections.[^tra-maintenance-plan] The plan gives each
workshop's planned annual maintenance volume for 2024 through 2027: Fugang
438/413/445/446 vehicles, Chaozhou 286/514/504/504, and Hualien
86/89/109/109.[^tra-maintenance-plan]

The same plan explains why Chaozhou's role expanded: TRA's newer long-formation
EMU900 (520 cars, 52 groups, fully delivered by the end of 2023) and EMU3000
(600 cars, 50 groups, delivered to the 42nd group by the same date) were
planned for periodic Level 3/4 maintenance at Chaozhou from 2024, because
Fugang and Hualien were already at full maintenance capacity for the
existing fleet.[^tra-maintenance-plan] A separate operator policy page gives
the Chaozhou base's Phase II expansion — a comprehensive workshop, an
EMU-dedicated maintenance facility, a preparation workshop, materials
storage and a test-line inspection shed — a total investment of NT$12.148
billion, approved 27 September 2021, with a project period running to
September 2030 under revision.[^tra-chaozhou-phase2] As of the maintenance
plan's own snapshot at the end of 2023, Phase II's pre-construction and
equipment contracts were awarded and partly underway, with the main-works
and equipment contracts at detailed design following a December 2023
basic-design approval.[^tra-maintenance-plan] A government release confirms
a commissioning ceremony at Chaozhou attended by the then-Premier, who is
quoted remarking that the old name "Kaohsiung Workshop" no longer matched
reality once the facility had moved — though the exact ceremony date is a
conflict between two dated accounts this page does not resolve.[^eysc-chaozhou-opening]

Hualien Workshop's own operating bylaw divides its work across three
internal workshops by rolling-stock subsystem: engines, transmissions and
braking for diesel railcars and locomotives; bogies, brakes, couplers and
bodywork; and electrical, HVAC and interior systems.[^law-moj-hualien-workshop]
Fugang's history, per news coverage not independently confirmed against a
TRA primary release, traces to a 1935 Japanese-era Taipei railway works,
relocated to Yangmei, Taoyuan in 2013 and renamed Fugang Workshop on 28 June
2022; it is reported to currently maintain roughly 1,400 vehicles across
heritage steam locomotives, diesel-electric and electric locomotives, and
EMUs.[^cna-fugang-rename]

## Bylaws that predate the fleets they'd need to describe

Both Fugang's and Chaozhou's own operating bylaws, as filed in the national
law database, still carry their pre-rename names — Taipei Workshop and
Kaohsiung Workshop respectively — and were each last amended in
2017,[^law-moj-fugang-bylaw][^law-moj-chaozhou-bylaw] before the 2022
renaming and, for Fugang, before even its 2013 relocation was reflected in
the bylaw's title. This page does not read either bylaw as a current
description of EMU900 or EMU3000 maintenance scope, since both predate those
fleets' arrival; that current scope comes instead from the 2024 maintenance
plan above.[^tra-maintenance-plan]

## Remaining gaps

Chaozhou's Phase I relocation project — its budget, exact dates and site
area — rests only on unconfirmed reporting; this page's own attempt to
locate the operator's Phase I project page directly returned an unrelated
project and a server error. The Chaozhou commissioning date is an unresolved
conflict between two dated accounts. Both Fugang's and Chaozhou's site
areas are reported identically at roughly 52 hectares by unverified search
summaries, which may be coincidence or a conflation between the two sites; a
verified site area for either is not established by any source read for this
page. What distinguishes the
"A" and "B" sub-grades used within the maintenance plan's own Level 3 and
Level 4 schedules is not defined in any source read for this page.
