## How to read project status

The official record uses more than one threshold. DORTS separates a planning
network from an under-construction network, while a feasibility approval,
comprehensive-plan approval, detailed design, an advance works contract and a
main civil-works start are separate events.[^dorts-network][^ntpc-bade] This
page therefore does not turn a target year into an opening date or describe an
approved alignment as if it were already being built.[^dorts-construction-targets]

## Under construction

Taipei's 2026 policy document identifies three active construction programmes:[^dorts-115-plan]
the Circular East Ring, the combined North and South Rings, and Wanda–Zhonghe–
Shulin Phase 1. Together it reports 43.41 km and 37 stations.[^dorts-115-plan]
The same document says the East Ring is in main works, the North and South
Rings are in civil works and systems design, and Wanda Phase 1 is in civil,
environmental and systems installation/testing.[^dorts-115-plan]

New Taipei's separate progress page, fetched as a July 2026 record, displays
0% for its controlled Wanda–Zhonghe programme but gives no scope explanation;
the value is retained without interpretation.[^ntpc-wanda-progress]

The East Ring is a 13.25 km underground route from the North Ring tail track
through Neihu, Songshan, Xinyi and Wenshan to the South Ring Zoo station, with
10 stations and one depot.[^dorts-east-route] A March 2026 project announcement
records CF710's February start and CF720's April start, while three stations,
the East Depot and the connections toward Songshan and Yongchun still had two
contracts in tendering.[^dorts-east-route][^dorts-115-plan]

The North and South Rings remain a useful example of why status needs a date:
the route page describes 14.93 km and 12 North Ring stations plus a depot, and
5.73 km and six South Ring stations, but its current work-status field says
contract-document review and detailed design.[^dorts-ring-ns] DORTS's 2026
policy document simultaneously describes their civil works as being advanced,
so both descriptions are retained rather than silently reconciled.[^dorts-115-plan][^dorts-ring-ns]

## Approved or advancing extensions

The Xinyi East Extension is a one-station, approximately 1.4 km extension with
tail tracks; its CR580C contract page records a 17 October 2016 start.[^dorts-east-contract]
DORTS's 2026 priorities describe the construction fences coming down and the
extension entering service after preliminary inspection, while its older FAQ
gives ROC 115 as the dated completion target.[^dorts-115-plan][^dorts-construction-targets]

The Sanying–Bade extension is approved at the comprehensive-planning level but
is not presented here as a construction start. The current New Taipei record
describes a 4.03 km, three-station route from LB12 to a jointly built `LB14`/G04,
and records detailed design beginning 1 July 2026.[^ntpc-bade] Danhai Blue Sea
Line Phase 2 has moved further than a sketch but is still a changing project:
the original route was 4.4 km, the revised riverside route is 3.32 km, and
advance road, utility and track works began in June 2025 while the revised plan
continued through review.[^ntpc-blue2]

The Railway Bureau's current budget also separates approved projects from
municipal route proposals. It includes the `A14` Airport MRT station works and
the 15.59 km, 13-station Nangang–Badu project, which is planned to connect the
Taipei-region metro systems, TRA and HSR; this budget record gives its programme
as ROC 113–123.[^rb-115-rail]

## Proposed, rejected and cancelled

DORTS's network page lists Minsheng–Xizhi and the Shizi Line as planned rather
than under construction.[^dorts-network] Its 2026 priorities put the Taipei
section of Minsheng–Xizhi at 11.02 km and nine stations, seeking central
approval while basic civil and systems work proceeds.[^dorts-115-plan] New
Taipei's chronology records feasibility-stage work on Bali, Wugu–Taishan and
Shenkeng, but a feasibility study or submission is not treated as approval to
build.[^ntpc-four-rings]

One rejected alternative is documented in New Taipei's Sanying record: an
underground high-capacity Bannan extension to LB02 was evaluated against the
elevated medium-capacity scheme, with demolition, tunnelling, land, finance,
operational-interface and delay problems recorded as reasons against it.[^search-boundary]
The full source search did not establish a named Taipei-region project that was
cancelled after construction had begun; that category is TBC, not evidence that
no such project exists.[^search-boundary]

## What is still unresolved

The primary pages publish several different progress and target measures, and
some project pages are updated at different points in the approval chain. A
complete catalogue of every abandoned alignment, every rejected proposal and
every mid-construction cancellation would require the underlying feasibility
reports, gazette decisions, audit and land-acquisition records for each route;
those sources were searched but not all were found in this pass.[^search-boundary]
