## What TOD means here

The Taipei-region primary record uses land development and joint development as
the operative terms, rather than treating every station-area building as a
generic TOD project.[^dorts-framework] The mechanism combines a station,
entrances, depot or other rail facility with offices, shops, housing or public
space, and the development record is therefore about land rights and interfaces
as much as about architecture.[^dorts-framework][^ntpc-framework] The page uses
TOD as a reader-facing label, but keeps the official categories and site names
in the tables below.[^dorts-framework]

## How value and risk are allocated

Taipei's FAQ says the authority and landowners each receive half of the actual
design incentive floor area created under the applicable MRT and planning rules,
unless a different agreement or law applies.[^dorts-framework] Its separate
value-conversion explanation subtracts the authority's incentive-volume value
from the building's total value and treats the investor's assessed construction,
tax and expected-profit contribution as a conversion value, rather than
publishing a single investor revenue percentage.[^dorts-framework] Taipei also
states that rent, sale and operating income from returned property can support
MRT construction finance.[^dorts-framework]

New Taipei publishes a different sequence: after planning designation and
negotiated acquisition, the authority selects an investor, then proceeds through
design, permits, construction and rights allocation before transferring property
according to the agreed allocation.[^ntpc-framework] Its value rule starts with
the completed building's total value, subtracts the authority's allocated value,
and uses the balance to calculate landowner and investor values.[^ntpc-framework]
The public record therefore supports a process comparison, not a single
Taipei-wide revenue-share ratio.[^dorts-framework][^ntpc-framework]

## Completed and operating cases

The DORTS technical journal records Daan Forest Park J2 as a 2,994 m² private
site with 34,644.77 m² of non-MRT floor area, 62 homes, and an occupancy permit
in ROC 99; its station entrance is integrated into the building.[^dorts-daan-cases]
The same case records an estimated NT$56.77 million saving in land-acquisition
cost at the stated valuation basis, which is a case-specific estimate rather
than a general return.[^dorts-daan-cases] The adjacent Daan J3 case has an 890 m²
site, 12,650.72 m² of non-MRT floor area, 31 commercial units and an occupancy
permit in ROC 101; its station entrance is also built into the development.[^dorts-daan-cases]
These cases link to [Daan Park (R06)](/rail/metro/stations/r06/) and
[Daan (R05)](/rail/metro/stations/r05/), while the source-era labels J2 and J3
remain the development identifiers.[^dorts-daan-cases]

The Daan Forest Park design book shows why completion cannot be inferred from a
drawn station plan: its basic design proposed 23,499.96 m², while the investor's
building-permit design showed 34,644.77 m² and a different building height and
use mix.[^dorts-daan-design] The source also makes the station entrance a
programme constraint, with completion targeted before the Xinyi Line opening so
that passenger access and evacuation would not be delayed.[^dorts-daan-design]

Zhongshan has at least two separately numbered development cases. The J1 record
gives a 1,468.58 m² site, 18,228.12 m² of planned non-MRT floor area, a retail,
office and residential mix, and 95.40% construction progress at the end of July
2017; it does not settle a final completion date in that page.[^dorts-zhongshan-case]
The J2 design record instead gives a 1,681 m² site and 24,070.77 m² total floor
area, including 1,164.14 m² of MRT facilities, with exit 6 integrated into the
building.[^dorts-zhongshan-design] The current registry links the station to
[Zhongshan (R11)](/rail/metro/stations/r11/) and [Zhongshan (G14)](/rail/metro/stations/g14/); the source's G16 label is an engineering-era label,
not a current station code.[^dorts-zhongshan-design]

The current DORTS rental table shows the returned-property side of this system,
not just the construction side. It lists, for example, one `BR4` Zhongxiao Fuxing
shopping-mall property at 2,036.38 ping and NT$3,869 per ping per month, and a
G02 Xindian District Office development with 25 office units listed and 25
leased.[^dorts-rental-inventory] The table also records a range of areas and
rents for Daan, Daan Park, Xinpu, Guting and other sites, but it is not a
complete construction history or a developer register.[^dorts-rental-inventory]

## Current and advancing portfolio

Taipei's public-land register supplies contract dates and named private
investors for newer cases while leaving many area, completion and revenue fields
to their individual project records.[^taipei-land-bid] Its current list includes
the [Xinpu adjacent site (BL08)](/rail/metro/stations/bl08/) signed with 旺泰建設
and 忠泰長虹建設 on ROC 110-07-05, the [Chiang Kai-Shek Memorial Hall site
R08](/rail/metro/stations/r08/) and [G10](/rail/metro/stations/g10/) signed with
愛山林建設 and 宏匯 on ROC
114-05-22, and future-labelled `Y26` and `Y21` Circular Line development cases
with named investors.[^taipei-land-bid] The contract list is evidence of an
investor agreement, not evidence that construction or occupancy has finished.[^taipei-land-bid]

DORTS's current land-development office page shows that its work now spans
investor selection, design review, contract management, construction-cost
assessment, rights allocation and post-handover defects; it names current work
at `Y26B`, `Y37`, `Y21B`, `Y20B`, `Y23B`, CKS Memorial Hall, Xinpu and Xindian
District Office sites.[^dorts-org] Those future Circular labels cannot be
silently converted into current station pages because the committed station
registry does not yet contain every planned code.[^search-boundary]

## New Taipei scale and public return

New Taipei's Circular Line integrity platform identifies ten planned land-
development stations and says four—Xiulang Bridge, Jingping, Zhonghe and
Zhongyuan—had investors performing contracts when the page was fetched.[^ntpc-circular-integrity]
For the six sites placed on the integrity platform, the same official page
publishes an estimated NT$7.287 billion contribution to self-financing funds,
NT$176.072 billion in total building value, NT$60.883 billion in investment and
NT$67.937 billion in public benefit including public landowners.[^ntpc-circular-integrity]
These are the authority's estimates for that portfolio, not realized revenue.
The source also says the Circular Line itself completed and entered service on
31 January 2020, while the ten development sites remained a separate land-
development programme.[^ntpc-circular-integrity]

New Taipei's current financial account says its authority had 37 development
cases, had selected investors for 13, and had completed two when the article was
updated; it describes returned-property rents and sales as a principal source
for repaying self-financing debt.[^ntpc-revenue] A separate Sanying depot news
record gives a 14.1-hectare depot-and-LB05 site, says about 80% of agreeing
landowners had chosen participation or retained development rights, and leaves
the investor-selection stage ahead.[^ntpc-sanying-depot] It is therefore an
advancing land-rights case, not a completed building.[^ntpc-sanying-depot]

## Meihe City and the legal dispute

Meihe City is the region's essential caution against treating joint development
as an uncomplicated financing success. New Taipei's official 2014 account says
the review lasted more than 15 years, reduced an original 26-building design to
13 buildings, and required greater setbacks and public space after urban-design
and environmental review.[^ntpc-meihe-news] A later New Taipei government
research report instead describes 16 towers, 2,220 units, about 400,600 m² of
floor area, and Rich Development as the selected private developer.[^ntpc-meihe-research]
Those are conflicting project descriptions from different official records; the
site publishes both rather than choosing one.[^ntpc-meihe-news][^ntpc-meihe-research]

The legal issue is not just a design disagreement. Judicial Interpretation No.
743 states that land expropriated under MRT Law Article 6 cannot be used in the
same plan for Article 7 joint development, and that transfer to a third party
requires clear legal authority.[^judicial-743] The interpretation records the
underlying Xindian depot case, the different statutory purposes of expropriation
and joint development, and the disagreement among the Control Yuan, executive
agencies and courts before the interpretation.[^judicial-743] Meihe City is
therefore published here as a legal and governance conflict as well as a
large-scale station-area project.[^judicial-743]

## What the inventory still cannot say

The fetched primary material does not provide one reconciled table containing
every site's developer, site area, total floor area, completion date, realized
revenue share and dispute history.[^search-boundary] It provides detailed case
records, current rental inventory, contract lists, portfolio estimates and legal
records in separate families.[^dorts-daan-cases][^dorts-rental-inventory][^taipei-land-bid][^ntpc-circular-integrity]
The missing values stay TBC until the project contract, occupancy record,
rights-allocation decision or audited revenue statement is fetched for that
specific site.[^search-boundary]
