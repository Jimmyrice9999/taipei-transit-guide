## Corridor and purpose

The Taichung Airport MRT Orange Line is planned as an airport, city and Tun District connection.[^tcrt-orange] The bureau links the project to demand on Zhongqing Road, Guoguang Road and Zhongxing Road, and to airport, Shuinan, airport-park and redevelopment plans.[^tcrt-orange] The project is therefore presented as a transport corridor with both airport access and cross-city development roles, not as an operating route.[^tcrt-orange]

The current route page starts the plan at Taichung International Airport, runs through Daya, the Shuinan Economic and Trade Park, Yizhong, Taichung Station, National Chung Hsing University and the Taichung Software Park, and ends near the Legislative Yuan's Wufeng site.[^tcrt-orange] The 5 August 2026 release describes the same route at district level through Daya, Xitun, Beitun, North, East, South, Dali and Wufeng.[^tcrt-orange-aug] These descriptions identify a planning corridor; they are not a final station inventory.[^tcrt-orange]

## Published measurements and the rounded value

The current project page gives approximately 29.23 km, 26 stations, 15 elevated stations and 11 underground stations.[^tcrt-orange] The MOTC 112 Transport Yearbook gives 29.2 km for the same 26-station and 15-elevated/11-underground scope.[^motc-112-yearbook] Both values are retained because the sources do not explain whether 29.2 km is rounding or an earlier measurement.[^tcrt-orange][^motc-112-yearbook]

The August 2026 bureau release gives an estimated total cost of approximately NT$1,602 hundred-million, or NT$160.2 billion.[^tcrt-orange-aug] This is an estimate attached to a feasibility-approved project, not a construction contract value.[^tcrt-orange-aug] The same release says the city will review route and station details during comprehensive planning in response to local suggestions.[^tcrt-orange-aug]

## Feasibility approval and pre-construction work

The official chronology records local meetings in June 2020, the first submission to the Transport Ministry in April 2021, a Railway Bureau site inspection in November 2021 and an initial review in December 2021.[^tcrt-orange] Revised submissions and ministry reviews continued through 2022 and 2023.[^tcrt-orange] The current page records principle agreement at the ministry committee meeting on 2 July 2024.[^tcrt-orange]

The report then moved into a longer approval sequence: the ministry forwarded it to the Executive Yuan in July 2025, requested supplementary public-private-participation evaluation material, and re-forwarded it for Executive Yuan review in February 2026.[^tcrt-orange] The current page records Executive Yuan feasibility approval on 18 August 2026.[^tcrt-orange] The whole-network page repeats that approval and distinguishes the Orange Line from its separate Sea Line extension, which remains in the medium-term feasibility group.[^tcrt-network]

The 25 May 2026 release shows that the city began preparatory public reading for comprehensive planning, environmental assessment, land-use conversion and basic-design technical services before the final feasibility approval.[^tcrt-orange-may] The 15 July procurement page records the response to that public reading, but not a main construction award.[^tcrt-orange-procurement] The August release describes the later sequence as land acquisition, basic design, detailed design and construction, with a preliminary construction period of about 12 years.[^tcrt-orange-aug] No opening date follows from that estimate.[^tcrt-orange-aug]

## Forecasts are not current ridership

The feasibility-study demand chapter explicitly treats ridership as a scenario result.[^traffic-orange-forecast] It models conservative, basic and TOD cases, with different trip-rate and station-area development assumptions.[^traffic-orange-forecast] In the report's ROC 114 opening-year scenarios, forecast daily route ridership ranges from 102,474 to 120,236; in the ROC 130 target-year scenarios, it ranges from 153,390 to 185,280.[^traffic-orange-forecast] These are model outputs made under stated assumptions, not observed passenger counts.[^traffic-orange-forecast]

The forecast identifies the O9/B9–O10 segment as the maximum-load section in the scenario table.[^traffic-orange-forecast] The report also models interchange demand at O9/B9 with Taichung Station and the Blue Line, and at O4/G7 with the Green Line.[^traffic-orange-forecast] Those labels belong to the feasibility model and should not be treated as a published operating station-code registry.[^traffic-orange-forecast]

## Station data boundary

The live TDX TMRT response checked for this page contains the operating Taichung Green Line records, not an Orange Line project or operating dataset.[^tdx-tmrt] The official Orange pages provide aggregate route length and station counts but no complete bilingual station-name list, coordinates, exits, accessibility facilities, timetable or actual ridership record.[^tcrt-orange][^tcrt-orange-aug][^tdx-tmrt] This page therefore records the approved project without manufacturing 26 station pages from an aggregate feasibility scope.[^tcrt-orange]

The opening date, construction start, programme-wide progress and current ridership remain TBC.[^tcrt-orange][^tcrt-orange-aug][^tdx-tmrt] A future approved comprehensive plan, procurement award, machine-readable geometry and TDX publication would establish those fields.[^tcrt-network][^tdx-tmrt]
