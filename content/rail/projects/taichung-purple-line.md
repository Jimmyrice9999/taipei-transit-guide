## Purpose and alignment

The Taichung Metro Purple Line is a feasibility-stage project for the city’s eastern Tun District. The Taichung Rapid Transit Systems Bureau frames it as a response to population, industrial and travel growth in Dali, Wufeng and Taiping, with goals that include commuting, school travel, tourism, economic activity and more balanced development.[^tcrt-purple]

Its preliminary route begins at the Green Line’s Daqing Station, runs through Dali and Taiping, then turns north toward Beitun and Green Line extension G1 to form a loop.[^tcrt-purple] The January 2026 release adds the intended interchange pattern: G1 at the north, the Blue Line Taiping extension in Taiping, Orange Line near Defang South Road, and TRA plus the Green Line at Daqing.[^tcrt-purple-jan] These are planned network relationships, not operating transfers.[^tcrt-purple][^tcrt-purple-jan]

## Published scope and conflicts

The current project page gives a preliminary length of approximately 16.31 km, 16 stations and an elevated form.[^tcrt-purple] The July 2026 release describes the same preliminary project as approximately 16.3 km, with 16 elevated stations and a medium-capacity metro system.[^tcrt-purple-july] Both values are retained because the sources do not explain whether the 0.01 km difference is rounding or a small planning revision.[^tcrt-purple][^tcrt-purple-july]

The July release gives a total estimated cost of NT$690.87 billion, with estimated central support of NT$292.04 billion and a local share of NT$398.83 billion.[^tcrt-purple-july] These are estimates attached to a feasibility-stage project, not a construction contract value or a final approved funding split.[^tcrt-purple-july]

## Feasibility review

The project’s public chronology starts with local explanation meetings in October 2021 and an initial submission to the Transport Ministry on 20 June 2022.[^tcrt-purple] Written comments followed in September 2022, a revised report in May 2023 and further ministry comments in May 2023.[^tcrt-purple] On 26 March 2024, the city split the prior combined Tun District feasibility work into the Purple Line and the Blue Line Taiping extension, then submitted both projects together.[^tcrt-purple]

The Railway Bureau held a site inspection in May 2024 and an initial review in June.[^tcrt-purple] The city submitted revised reports in January 2025, July 2025 and January 2026 after further central comments.[^tcrt-purple] The January release says the city had completed a revision after the October 2025 comments and resubmitted it for central review.[^tcrt-purple-jan] The July release likewise describes the study as resubmitted in January and under central review.[^tcrt-purple-july]

The current project page records another Railway Bureau response on 27 July 2026 and says the city is now revising the report before resubmission.[^tcrt-purple] The whole-network page correspondingly places Purple in the medium-term feasibility-study group, alongside the Blue Line Taiping extension, rather than presenting it as an approved construction programme.[^tcrt-network] The status is therefore current central review with another revision in progress, not a construction start.[^tcrt-purple][^tcrt-network]

## Station data boundary

The live TMRT TDX response checked for this release contains current Green Line records, not a Purple Line project or operating dataset.[^tdx-tmrt] The official Purple Line pages give an aggregate count of 16 stations and the elevated guideway form, but do not provide a complete bilingual station-name list, coordinates, exits, accessibility facilities, operating timetable or ridership record.[^tcrt-purple][^tcrt-purple-jan][^tcrt-purple-july] This page records the feasibility project without manufacturing 16 station stubs from a preliminary route.[^tcrt-purple]

There is no published opening date or construction progress percentage in the primary pages read for this unit.[^tcrt-purple][^tcrt-network] Those values remain TBC until the feasibility study is approved and later design, procurement or operating records establish them.
