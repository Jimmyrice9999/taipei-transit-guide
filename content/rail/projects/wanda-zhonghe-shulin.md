## One corridor, two phases

The Wanda–Zhonghe–Shulin project is a medium-capacity metro corridor planned in two construction phases.[^ntpc-phase1-route] Phase 1 starts at Chiang Kai-shek Memorial Hall, follows the southern Taipei and Yonghe–Zhonghe corridor, and ends at Juguang beside the depot branch.[^ntpc-phase1-route] Its published scope is approximately 9.5 km including an approximately 700 m depot branch, nine underground stations and one depot.[^ntpc-phase1-route] The New Taipei portion is 5.7 km and includes LG05, LG06, LG07, LG08 and LG08A.[^ntpc-phase1-route]

The planning-stage station page lists LG01 Chiang Kai-shek Memorial Hall, LG02 Taipei Botanical Garden, LG03 Xiaan, LG04 Jiala, LG05 Yonghe Yongping Elementary School, LG06 Zhonghe, LG07 Liancheng Jinhe, LG08 Zhonghe Senior High School and LG08A Juguang.[^ntpc-phase1-stations] The same page identifies LG06 as a transfer to the Circular Line and warns that these codes are planning identifiers that may be renumbered when the operating company takes over.[^ntpc-phase1-stations]

Phase 2 is the Tucheng–Shulin section. It continues from the Phase 1 route along Jincheng Road, rises near Mingde Road, crosses Chenglin Bridge and the Dahan River, then follows Shulin streets to the Xinzhuang Line Huilong connection.[^ntpc-phase2-route] The phase is approximately 13.3 km with 13 stations: two underground and 11 elevated.[^ntpc-phase2-route]

## Approval and programme record

Phase 1’s environmental statement was accepted in 2008 and its comprehensive plan was approved in 2010.[^ntpc-phase1-route] A second revision approved in 2018 set the cost at NT$74.178 billion and moved the planned completion to the end of ROC year 116.[^ntpc-phase1-route] The current Phase 1 page records a further revision sent to the Executive Yuan for review on 18 May 2026, with a revised NT$84.801 billion total, a December ROC 116 completion adjustment and a programme period extending to December ROC 118.[^ntpc-phase1-route] Those figures are published as a dated planning record; the later revision is not presented as an approved replacement before the source says it is approved.[^ntpc-phase1-route]

The Phase 2 route page records its financial plan approval in 2018 and a first revision in 2025.[^ntpc-phase2-route] Its design history includes urban-plan changes, DQ125 and DQ126 detailed-design contracts and later civil and systems packages.[^ntpc-phase2-route] The New Taipei government’s project record says basic design was completed and identifies CQ880A, CQ880B and CQ890 as construction sections.[^ntpc-phase2-wedid]

## Construction packages

The New Taipei government’s Phase 1 record says that four civil packages were under construction: CQ850 in Yonghe, CQ861 for the LG06–Y11 underground connection, CQ860 from LG06 to LG08 and along Tucheng’s Jincheng Road, and CQ870 for the Jincheng depot and LG08A.[^ntpc-phase1-wedid] The record illustrates why a route-level progress percentage cannot replace package-level reporting: it describes road surfacing, an interchange passage and station enamel-panel installation while the separate progress page reports 0% for its controlled programme.[^ntpc-phase1-wedid][^ntpc-phase1-progress]

Phase 2’s current progress page lists CQ880A, CQ880B and CQ890 as construction projects.[^ntpc-phase2-progress] It gives CQ880A a 1 March 2024 start, CQ890 an 11 November 2022 start and CQ880B a 29 December 2025 start, with all three completion dates marked as under plan adjustment.[^ntpc-phase2-progress] The same page gives contract values of NT$11,597,832,043 for CQ880A, NT$18,000,358,242 for CQ890 and NT$15.99 billion for CQ880B.[^ntpc-phase2-progress]

## Engineering and interfaces

The Taipei DORTS package record describes CQ880A as approximately 2.84 km with underground LG09 and LG10, including cut-and-cover and shield-tunnel work.[^ntpc-phase2-technical] CQ880B covers approximately 3.82 km, including elevated LG11, LG12 and LG13, a route embankment and the crossing toward the Dahan River.[^ntpc-phase2-technical] CQ890 covers approximately 6.6 km and eight elevated stations, including stacked and side-platform arrangements across the Shulin section.[^ntpc-phase2-technical]

The engineering interfaces are unusually dense. The technical record lists major utility relocation, a crossing above operating TRA, a passage below operating HSR, stacked elevated construction on Zhonghua Road and a double-deck elevated structure shared with the Taoyuan Brown Line.[^ntpc-phase2-technical] The page also identifies LG18 as the only off-road island-platform station in that package and LG21 as a shared transfer station for the Zhonghe–Xinlu Line and planned Taoyuan Brown Line.[^ntpc-phase2-technical]

## LG11 and the limits of the current record

LG11 is the phase’s documented public-design dispute. The station Q&A records concerns over the adjusted route, the use of park and parking land, and the distances between the track, homes and Tucheng Elementary School.[^lg11-qa] The bureau says the current siting followed a combined assessment of alignment, roads, land, station spacing, interchange, service area, local development and residential and commercial activity, and calls it the best feasible choice.[^lg11-qa] The Q&A also records repeated meetings in 2020 and early 2021 with the New Taipei bureau, Taipei bureau, elected representatives and residents.[^lg11-qa]

The latest progress pages should not be read as a single comparable construction percentage. The Phase 1 page reports 0% through the end of ROC year 115 July, while the New Taipei government record lists four packages as under construction.[^ntpc-phase1-progress][^ntpc-phase1-wedid] The Phase 2 page likewise reports 0% while listing three active packages with award and start dates.[^ntpc-phase2-progress] No fetched primary page supplies a final opening date for the complete corridor, a final station-exit and accessibility inventory, or observed ridership; those fields remain TBC.[^ntpc-phase1-stations][^ntpc-phase1-progress][^ntpc-phase2-progress]
