## Purpose and alignment

The Taichung Metro Blue Line is the city’s second metro project. The Taichung Rapid Transit Systems Bureau describes it as an east–west route following Taiwan Boulevard, linking the port, Shalu, the civic centre, Taichung Station and the new Jianguo Market. It is intended to cross the operating Green Line and connect with TRA’s mountain and coast lines, so its value is in the interchange network as much as in the line itself.[^tcrt-blue]

The current official project summary gives a planned length of 24.78 km, 20 stations and one depot.[^tcrt-blue] Eight stations are planned elevated and 12 underground.[^tcrt-blue] These are project-level figures.[^tcrt-blue] The route-section contracts are shorter pieces of the same approved scheme and should not be added together and presented as the complete line.[^tcrt-blue]

## Approvals and design

The feasibility study was approved by the Executive Yuan on 3 October 2018.[^tcrt-blue] The transport ministry approved the comprehensive-plan report on 20 February 2023, followed by Executive Yuan approval on 29 January 2024.[^tcrt-blue] The environmental-impact review passed on 7 September 2022.[^tcrt-blue] Basic design was submitted in May 2024 and approved by the Public Construction Commission on 29 October 2024 after a September site review and deliberation.[^tcrt-blue]

The bureau records the core electromechanical system, depot and main-substation package BM01 as awarded on 2 April 2025, contracted on 14 May and marked by a commencement ceremony on 26 June 2025.[^tcrt-blue] It also records the three detailed-design and supervision packages BD01, BD02 and BD03 as awarded in May 2025.[^tcrt-blue] Those milestones establish an approved project moving through implementation; they do not establish a passenger opening date.[^tcrt-blue]

## Route construction

BC11 is the first route section to reach contract signing.[^tcrt-bc11] The bureau’s 31 July 2026 release says it was awarded on 14 July to Yili Construction for NT$11.95758 billion, with a 1,950-calendar-day term from notice to commence.[^tcrt-bc11] The 2.49 km elevated section runs from the port-side Lingang Road area through B01, B02 and B03 near Wuqi and Tong Hospital.[^tcrt-bc11]

BC12 followed in August 2026.[^tcrt-bc12] The bureau’s 11 August release records an award of NT$22.63460 billion to Far Eastern Construction.[^tcrt-bc12] The package covers 7.86 km from the B03 tail track toward the underground transition near Xitun Road, including five elevated stations, B04–B08, and power, signalling and communications work.[^tcrt-bc12] The procurement ledger shows why the status must be dated: its page, last modified on 21 July, still listed BC12’s second tender as the latest event, while the later news release records the award.[^tcrt-procurement][^tcrt-bc12]

## Station data boundary

The live TDX TMRT dataset checked for this release returns the current Green Line records, not Blue Line project or operating records.[^tdx-tmrt] The official Blue Line page gives a total station count and guideway split, and the route procurement pages identify B01–B08, but the checked primary pages do not provide a complete station-name, coordinate, exit, accessibility, timetable, ridership or opening-date dataset.[^tcrt-blue][^tcrt-procurement][^tdx-tmrt] This page therefore records the project and its packages without manufacturing 20 station stubs. A later approved design table, GIS/TDX response or operator record can support station pages when it exists.

The opening date and one programme-wide physical progress percentage remain TBC. The current primary record establishes approvals, contracts and package scope, but not a verified passenger-service date or a single progress measure that can be compared across all packages.[^tcrt-blue][^tcrt-bc11][^tcrt-bc12]
