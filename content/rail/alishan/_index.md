The Alishan Forest Railway is a working heritage railway, not a scenic line invented after the forest industry ended. The operator dates its opening to 1912 and describes a main line of about 72 km with a network that once grew to 21 branches.[^afr-technology] Its current public timetable runs from Chiayi to Alishan, while the forest-park branches continue the railway beyond the main-line terminus toward Shenmu, Zhaoping and Zhushan.[^afr-main-timetable][^afr-branch-timetable]

The railway is unusually legible as a system because the operator publishes route technology and station information together. Its gauge is 762 mm, and the line climbs from Chiayi at 30 m above sea level to Alishan at 2,216 m before the Zhushan branch reaches 2,451 m.[^afr-technology][^afr-home] The line is non-electrified and its engineering vocabulary is a response to tight mountain land, not a decorative theme: the official heritage record identifies spiral, horseshoe, Ω/U-turn and Z-shaped alignments alongside the special Shay geared locomotive.[^boch-potential]

The line should be read in two layers. The 16-stop main line is the current intercity-style forest-railway service between Chiayi and Alishan, with a timetable effective from 10 January 2025.[^afr-main-timetable] The branch timetable is a separate operating pattern inside the Alishan National Forest Recreation Area: Shenmu and Zhaoping have frequent short workings, while the Zhushan service is an observation train whose departure is announced the day before because it is tied to sunrise.[^afr-branch-timetable] Duigaoyue is a named platform and passing location on the Zhushan alignment in the operator's station overview, but the branch timetable does not establish it as a separately scheduled passenger stop.[^afr-home][^afr-branch-timetable]

This distinction also explains why station counts vary between pages. The current operator FAQ enumerates 16 main-line stations and four named forest-park points, while the heritage potential-site description uses an older landscape boundary and a different list of 16 named stations.[^afr-faq][^boch-potential] The guide keeps the inventories side by side in the station data rather than treating a heritage boundary as a current timetable. The older operation-mileage page adds another source view: its matrix omits Luma Chan and the branch points, even though the current operator overview and timetable include Luma Chan and the park services.[^afr-mileage][^afr-home][^afr-main-timetable]

The railway's most famous engineering is concentrated in the climb. At Duli Shan, the track circles the mountain in a three-turn spiral; at Shuisheliao, the Ω-shaped alignment turns the train through 180 degrees; and beyond Pingzhena the railway uses Z-shaped reversals because the mountain shelf is too narrow for a normal curve.[^boch-potential][^afr-home] The operator describes the Shay design in the same practical terms: vertical cylinders, bevel gears and flexible drive connections let a short locomotive negotiate tight curves, tunnels and steep grades while relying on adhesion rather than a rack rail.[^afr-shay][^boch-potential]

The rolling stock records are a roster of families rather than a set of thin vehicle biographies. The operator identifies early 13-ton Shay locomotives, eight 18-ton two-cylinder locomotives bought between 1910 and 1913, and twelve 28-ton three-cylinder locomotives bought between 1912 and 1917.[^afr-shay] Its current steam-locomotive page says Nos. 25 and 31 were repaired and returned to running after conversion from coal to diesel firing, with the repairs completed in 2006 and 2004 respectively.[^afr-shay-running] The operator's FAQ separately places No. 26 in static display and says the surviving display and occasional-running locomotives can be seen at the Chiayi and Fenqihu facilities or on the mountain.[^afr-faq] Diesel power is the everyday roster: the operator's table lists active DL25–30, DL31–34, DL38, DL39–43 and DL45–51 groups, alongside retired early Mitsubishi and O&K groups.[^afr-diesel]

Typhoon damage is part of the railway's operating history. Typhoon Morakot damaged the system in 2009, and Typhoon Dujuan caused two collapses totalling 55 m in the old No. 42 tunnel between Shizilu and Pingzhena in 2015.[^forest-2024-open][^afr-42] The replacement 1,106 m tunnel began construction on 8 January 2021 and was completed on 29 December 2023, according to the railway's project record.[^afr-42] That is an infrastructure milestone; the scheduled passenger reopening was separately announced for 6 July 2024 after Railway Bureau inspection, with four daily main-line services in the opening timetable.[^forest-2024-open][^afr-main-timetable] The distinction matters because “completed” and “open to passengers” are not the same date.

The line is a Taiwanese World Heritage potential site, not an inscribed UNESCO World Heritage property. The Ministry of Culture's heritage page presents the railway and its surrounding cultural landscape as a potential site, describes its 762 mm non-electrified mountain railway, and gives a 71.34 km potential-site scope across Chiayi and Nantou.[^boch-potential] The same record compares the route's industrial settlements, forest ecology and mountain engineering with the values usually used to assess railway heritage.[^boch-potential] In 2019, the government recreation history records the railway's registration as an important cultural landscape, which is a domestic heritage status distinct from UNESCO inscription.[^recreation-history]

This system page therefore links to a sourced line record and one station-inventory page. It does not create twenty short articles whose only content would be a name and elevation: the official sources provide a strong route-level history and a useful station table, but not a uniform 300-word engineering or accessibility record for every stop.[^afr-home][^afr-faq]
