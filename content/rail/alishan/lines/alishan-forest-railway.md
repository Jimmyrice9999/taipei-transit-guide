The Alishan Forest Railway is built around a constraint that ordinary railway descriptions often hide: it climbs a high mountain on 762 mm track without a rack rail.[^afr-technology][^boch-potential] The operator dates the railway's opening to 1912 and describes a main line of approximately 72 km, while the current station overview measures the Chiayi–Alishan run to 71.6 km.[^afr-technology][^afr-home] The Ministry of Culture's potential-site page uses 71.34 km for a wider cultural-landscape boundary, so the line does not have one source-independent “length” to substitute for the others.[^boch-potential]

The published main-line timetable is effective from 10 January 2025 and shows two daily trains up the mountain and two down, with the longer up train leaving Chiayi at 10:00 and reaching Alishan at 14:56 after a scheduled Fenqihu stop.[^afr-main-timetable] The 16 main-line names are Chiayi, Beimen, Luma Chan, Zhuqi, Muliliao, Zhangnaoliao, Duli Shan, Liyuanliao, Jiaoliping, Shuisheliao, Fenqihu, Duolin, Shizilu, Pingzhena, Erwanping and Alishan.[^afr-main-timetable][^afr-faq] The current FAQ fetched on 5 September 2026 nevertheless says the railway has not fully resumed and only runs from Chiayi to Shizilu, while also describing a Chiayi–Fenqihu train and bus connection.[^afr-faq-current-2026] The timetable is therefore a dated service record and the current operating extent remains an official-source conflict, not a claim that every listed station is currently reachable.

The mountain route is a sequence of controlled reversals and long curves. The official heritage description places a three-turn spiral at Duli Shan, a large horseshoe or 180-degree curve between Zhuqi and Zhangnaoliao, and Z-shaped reversals farther up the mountain where there is not enough shelf to turn a train normally.[^boch-potential] The operator's own overview adds that the railway passes eight tunnels on the Duli Shan climb and that the Ω-shaped track at Shuisheliao turns the train through 180 degrees.[^afr-home] The published maximum gradient is 6.25%, and the source describes trains as adhesion-worked rather than rack-assisted.[^boch-potential]

Alishan is also a junction rather than a simple terminal. The operator identifies the forest-park branches as Shenmu, Zhaoping and Zhushan, with frequent short workings on the first two and a sunrise-dependent departure notice for Zhushan.[^afr-branch-timetable] Duigaoyue is a named platform and passing location on the Zhushan alignment; its presence is confirmed by the operator's station overview, but the current branch timetable does not establish a scheduled boarding stop there.[^afr-home][^afr-branch-timetable] The Mianyue Line is a separate historical case: it was built as a timber route, later reopened as a tourist railway, and closed after the 1999 earthquake damaged its trackbed and tunnels and later repairs continued to fail.[^afr-branch-timetable]

The rolling-stock story follows the railway's changing work. The operator records 18-ton two-cylinder Shay locomotives bought in eight units between 1910 and 1913 and twelve 28-ton three-cylinder locomotives bought between 1912 and 1917; it says these LIMA-built machines were selected for the line's steep, curved mountain route.[^afr-shay] The official English locomotive page says Nos. 25 and 31 were repaired and returned to running, with coal bunkers converted to generator rooms, air compressors added and coal replaced by diesel fuel; it dates the repairs to 2006 and 2004 respectively.[^afr-shay-running] That is why “steam locomotive” needs qualification here: the boiler-and-cylinder form survives, but the two operating examples described by the operator are diesel-fired after conversion.[^afr-shay-running]

The operator's FAQ gives a different roster perspective for No. 26, identifying it as a 1912 28-ton Shay and placing it in static display, while saying that 28- and 18-ton examples are displayed at the Chiayi and Fenqihu facilities and one locomotive may run irregularly in the mountains.[^afr-faq] The diesel table is the better source for the everyday fleet: it identifies active DL25–30, DL31–34, DL38, DL39–43 and DL45–51 groups, with retired early Mitsubishi and O&K groups kept in the same record so that “current” is not mistaken for “ever acquired.”[^afr-diesel]

The line's recent service history is inseparable from typhoon and landslide work. The Forestry and Nature Conservation Agency says Typhoon Morakot and Typhoon Dujuan damaged the railway and that the collapse of No. 42 left the line interrupted for 15 years before the 6 July 2024 full-line opening.[^forest-2024-open] The railway office's completion notice records two collapses totalling 55 m, a new 1,106 m tunnel begun on 8 January 2021, and construction challenges including water inflow, material transport and pandemic labour shortages.[^afr-42] A government recreation chronology gives 29 December 2023 as the date the last-mile construction was completed, while the passenger announcement gives 6 July 2024 as the date scheduled trains again ran from Chiayi to Alishan.[^recreation-history][^forest-2024-open] These are different milestones and are published as such. The current FAQ's contrary service-status wording is kept visible rather than silently treating the 2024 reopening notice as a current guarantee.[^afr-faq-current-2026]

The 2024 reopening did not end weather-related operational risk.[^afr-krathon] An official October 2024 notice says the railway suspended the Chiayi–Alishan main line and the Shenmu, Zhaoping and Zhushan branches while Typhoon Krathon was in the warning period, then resumed normal service on 5 October after a route inspection found no damage.[^afr-krathon] That notice is evidence for a specific typhoon closure and recovery, not a general operating guarantee; later interruptions remain TBC unless the operator's dated notices are checked.

The heritage case is similarly specific. The Ministry of Culture presents the Alishan Forestry and Railway Cultural Landscape as a Taiwan World Heritage potential site and describes the 762 mm railway, its mountain methods, forest ecology and associated settlements.[^boch-potential] The site is not presented here as a UNESCO-inscribed property. A separate government chronology records its domestic registration as an important cultural landscape in 2019, which is a national cultural-heritage status rather than UNESCO inscription.[^recreation-history] The route, station inventory, rolling-stock roster and closure history therefore belong together, but each claim stays tied to the primary record that actually supports it.
