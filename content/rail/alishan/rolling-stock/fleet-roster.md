## From timber railway to museum piece

The Alishan Forest Railway's Shay geared steam locomotives were procured in
three waves under Japanese rule: an undated first unit ordered from Lima
(USA) by the original timber concessionaire, eight 18-ton locomotives
between 1910 and 1913, and twelve 28-ton locomotives between 1912 and
1917.[^afr-shay] Of the fourteen that survive in the current roster, seven
sit on static display and two are out of service awaiting
repair.[^afr-steam-table]

The operator's own pages disagree on how many still run. The Shay-specific
history page credits Chiayi Depot's own repair workshop — which fabricates
replacement parts by hand, including boilers and steam engines, since no
factory supply exists anymore — with keeping exactly one locomotive, No.
26, in dynamic operating condition.[^afr-shay] A separate, fuller roster
table published by the same office lists four locomotives as
operational — Nos. 21, 25, 26 and 31 — noting that two of them (25 and 31)
were converted to diesel fuel when they returned to service in
2007.[^afr-steam-table] Both figures are published here, unresolved: it is
possible the narrower page describes something more specific, such as
coal-fired operation on original equipment, but neither page states that
distinction itself.

The two locomotives converted to diesel fuel don't even have agreed
completion dates. This page's own Chinese-language source dates both No.
25's and No. 31's return to service and diesel conversion to the same
year, 2007.[^afr-shay] The [system overview](/rail/alishan/) instead cites
an English-language mirror of a different operator page for 2006 (No. 25)
and 2004 (No. 31) — two different years, from what is nominally the same
operator. Both are published; neither is corrected to match the other.

## A fleet that grew by accident of arithmetic

Diesel locomotives began arriving in 1953, starting with a Mitsubishi
batch, and have been added in seven distinct groups since, spanning
Mitsubishi, West German O&K, and Japan Sharyo builds through
2006–07.[^afr-diesel] The operator's own summary line describes this as six
batches, even though the page's own content details seven — an
inconsistency published as found. One of the older diesels, DL-32, was
destroyed in the depot fire described below, per a secondary
source.[^afr-shay] No locomotive class named "DL-14" appears anywhere in
the operator's own diesel-fleet page.

## What's arriving now, and one claim that doesn't hold up

The railway's next-generation train, 森里號 EVI, is built by Taiwan Rolling
Stock Co.; its first unit — one locomotive and five coaches — reached
Chiayi Depot on 29 August 2025, and after trials is planned to enter
Chiayi–Alishan main-line service in 2026, as part of a total order of five
locomotives and twenty-six coaches.[^afr-senli-press] Each set seats 97,
including two accessible seats, against the existing fleet's 108, trading
some capacity for upgraded suspension, automatic doors, passenger
information displays and a cleaner-emissions engine.[^afr-senli-press]

The railway fully reopened on 6 July 2024 after extended restoration work,
with the inaugural train's 85 seats selling out in under ten
minutes.[^cna-reopening-2024] A separately-branded train called 福森號
(Formosensis) is real — the operator's own site links to a themed tour
product under that name — but a Wikipedia claim that it launched the same
day as the reopening, described as a "cypress-wood train," cites the CNA
article above as its source. That article, read directly, contains no
mention of the train at all. This is a checked citation failure, not
published as fact: 福森號's actual launch date and construction remain
unconfirmed here.

## Where the fleet is kept, and where it once caught fire

Three depots serve the railway: Chiayi Depot Park, the historic 1912
北門修理工場 and the site of a serious fire on 23 August 1993 that burned
wooden workshop buildings and destroyed some of the railway's older
locomotives;[^afr-chiayi-depot] Fenqihu Depot, a former water-and-coal
exchange point that stopped operational use around 1985 and is now a
static exhibition space; and Alishan Depot, rebuilt with a steel frame in
1997, which today prepares the Zhushan sunrise-line trains for their
regular 3am departures.

## Remaining gaps

Whether one or four Shay locomotives can currently run is unresolved
between the operator's own pages. No current public schedule for steam
excursions was found. 福森號's actual launch date, materials and capacity
are unconfirmed. Whether the Zhushan, Shenmu and Zhaoping branch lines use
any dedicated rolling stock beyond a shared 1969 diesel batch is not
established.
