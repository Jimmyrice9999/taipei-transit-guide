This inventory separates three things that are easy to merge by accident: a dated main-line timetable, the operator's named forest-park points, and the older heritage landscape list. The timetable names 16 main-line stations from Chiayi to Alishan, including Luma Chan, which the operator says resumed stopping in 2019.[^afr-main-timetable][^afr-faq] A current FAQ fetched on 5 September 2026 says the railway has not fully resumed and only runs from Chiayi to Shizilu, while also describing a Chiayi–Fenqihu train and bus connection; the table below therefore remains a published-timetable inventory, not a claim that every row is currently reachable.[^afr-faq-current-2026] The operator's overview then adds Shenmu, Zhaoping, Duigaoyue and Zhushan around the Alishan terminus.[^afr-home] The branch timetable confirms scheduled service to Shenmu, Zhaoping and Zhushan, but describes Duigaoyue as a platform and passing point rather than a separate scheduled stop.[^afr-branch-timetable][^afr-home]

The station positions and elevations in the table come from the operator's current overview, while the main-line service status and sequence come from its published timetable.[^afr-home][^afr-main-timetable] The operator's separate mileage matrix was updated on 31 March 2016 and omits Luma Chan from its station columns, so its values are retained as a dated source view rather than used to erase the newer station record.[^afr-mileage][^afr-home]

Chiayi is the lowland interchange: the operator describes it as the railway's connection to the Western Trunk Line and notes the former three-railway arrangement of TRA, sugar railway and forest railway.[^afr-home] Beimen is the timber-built gateway, estimated by the operator to date from 1910–1912 and built with Alishan red cypress.[^afr-home] Zhuqi is the end of the flat section and the start of the climb; the government station history also records its historic triangle track for turning locomotives when Zhuqi and Erwanping were the two ends of the early mountain operation.[^recreation-history]

The middle stations show why a station list is not just a sequence of names. Duli Shan is the point where the railway climbs in a three-turn spiral, while Shuisheliao's Ω-shaped track turns the train through 180 degrees.[^afr-home][^boch-potential] Fenqihu is the principal intermediate station with five tracks and a long history as a mountain settlement; its current station landscape was redesigned in 2021 with local planting and permeable paving.[^station-fenqihu] Pingzhena is a reversing station, after which the line enters the famous Z-shaped ascent toward the high mountain section.[^afr-home]

Alishan is the current main-line terminus and the origin of the forest-park branches. The present station was built as a new terminus when tourism outgrew Zhaoping, opened in 1981, closed after the 1999 earthquake, and reopened on its rebuilt site in September 2007.[^station-alishan] Zhaoping is the old terminus, first built in 1914 and replaced by a two-level building opened in 2013 that combines railway functions with exhibition and local cultural uses.[^station-zhaoping] Shenmu was historically the third passing point, became known for the giant red cypress beside the track, and received a 2022 repair that added waiting space, weather protection, an accessible ramp and a restored passing track.[^station-shenmu]

The highest point in the current inventory is Zhushan at 2,451 m, reached by the sunrise-oriented branch; Duigaoyue lies on that route at 2,405 m with a platform and passing track, but no independent passenger timetable is published for it.[^afr-home][^afr-branch-timetable] Mianyue is deliberately absent from the operating table because the operator records it as a closed historical tourist railway after earthquake damage and later collapses.[^afr-branch-timetable] A future historical-stations page can add its former stops when the operator or a conservation record supplies a complete, citable inventory.
