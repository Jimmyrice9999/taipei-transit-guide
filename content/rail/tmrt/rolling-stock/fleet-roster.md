## Eighteen trains, dimensioned to the centimetre

Taichung Metro's own FAQ, fetched and read in full rather than taken from a
search snippet, states the Green Line runs 18 electric multiple units, each
a two-car set with a walk-through gangway connecting the
cars.[^tmrt-faq] Car dimensions are given precisely: approximately 22.17 m
long, 2.98 m wide and 3.78 m high, for a full two-car train length of
approximately 44.34 m.[^tmrt-faq] Rated capacity is approximately 536
passengers per train, about 268 per car.[^tmrt-faq]

The vehicles are described as steel-rail, steel-wheel stock with a
stainless-steel car body — a medium-capacity, power-distributed design in
which each car carries two bogies, and each bogie carries two wheelsets
with its own traction motor.[^tmrt-faq]

## A consortium, not a named manufacturer

The rolling stock was procured as the Electric Multiple Unit System (EMU)
subsystem of the CJ900/CJ907 contract — the same contract that covers the
Green Line's signalling-equivalent Operation Control System, communications,
power supply, depot equipment and automatic fare collection. That contract
was won by a three-firm joint venture: Kawasaki Heavy Industries of Japan,
Alstom Transport of France, and the domestic China Engineering Corporation
(中鼎工程).[^cj900-bureau-record] The Taichung City government's own project
page names this consortium against the contract's whole scope, not against
the EMU subsystem individually — it does not state which of the three firms
actually manufactured the cars. Kawasaki Heavy Industries has a well-known
international rolling-stock manufacturing business, but this page does not
assert that Kawasaki built these cars specifically beyond what the primary
source itself states: a three-firm consortium won the whole contract.

## Remaining gaps

No source found gives the EMU fleet a model or series name, of the kind
this project's other operator rolling-stock pages carry (Innovia, CAF Urbos
3, and similar). Which CJ900/CJ907 consortium member manufactured the cars
specifically is TBC. Delivery dates, depot allocation, and whether all 18
trainsets are simultaneously in revenue service are not addressed by either
source read for this page.
