## A building split three ways, or built by the city if nobody bids

Taichung's own land-development bureau describes the mechanism as similar
to private joint construction: the city amends its urban plan to
designate a "metro development zone," landowners inside it provide their
land, and the bureau solicits an investor through a formal process to
fund and build a land-development tower on the site.[^tcrt-tod-overview]
Once complete, the landowner, the city and the investor each receive a
share of the building's real estate — and the same mechanism can supply
land the metro itself needs, for depots, stations or entrances, not only
commercial development on top of them.[^tcrt-tod-overview] A landowner
inside the zone can choose to skip a cash payment entirely and take space
in the finished building instead, or take the cash while keeping a
priority right to buy or lease space in the building later.[^tcrt-tod-overview]
If a qualified investor cannot be found in time to avoid delaying the
metro line's own opening, the city can build the shared structural space
itself first, then solicit an investor afterward to finish the
undeveloped floor area.[^tcrt-tod-overview]

The programme rests on four general laws — the Mass Rapid Transit Act, the
Mass Rapid Transit System Land Development Regulations, the Urban
Planning Act and the Building Act — plus nine Taichung-specific ordinances
covering everything from negotiated-purchase pricing to a
rights-allocation review committee built specifically for the Green
Line.[^tcrt-tod-law]

## Seven stations, seven different stages

Unlike this project's parallel research into Kaohsiung's and Taoyuan's
equivalent programmes — both of which found the mechanism but hit a wall
trying to reach any concrete case list — Taichung's bureau publishes a
detailed, working case page naming seven Green Line development
sites.[^tcrt-tod-greenline] Every site carries a site area, a building
coverage ratio, a floor-area ratio, named investors, a planned building
use and a dated procedural timeline.

**G8, Wenhua Senior High School**, is the sole site with a complete record
from signed contract to finished building: a single original landowner
applied as priority investor, signed in November 2014, broke ground in
July 2017, and handed over the completed residential-and-retail building
in November 2021 — now being marketed for sale and lease.[^tcrt-tod-greenline]
**G5 and G6** have both obtained building permits and are under
construction: G5 (four Elementary School) went to Kuo Der Construction
for an office-and-retail building, contracted in September 2021 with a
construction start reported in July 2023; G6 (Wenxin Chongde) went
jointly to 集順生活科技 and 日勝生活科技 for residential space plus a post
office, contracted in December 2020 but with a construction start not
reported until June 2025 — a gap of roughly four and a half years the
bureau's own page does not explain.[^tcrt-tod-greenline] **G8a and G11**
are both still applying for their building permits, both awarded to the
same joint venture (集順生活科技 and 日勝生活科技) for residential
space.[^tcrt-tod-greenline]

The two **City Hall sites, G9-1 and G9-2**, sit at very different stages
despite facing each other across the same intersection. G9-1, the larger
of the two at 6,787.9 m² with a 650% floor-area ratio and an entrance
already reserved for the future Blue Line, went to Kuo Der Construction
and 中鹿開發 for a retail-office-hotel building, contracted in April
2023.[^tcrt-tod-greenline] G9-2, next door, has secured no investor at
all — the bureau states it is still gathering potential investors' input
and periodically reviewing its solicitation terms, planning to relaunch
the tender when conditions allow.[^tcrt-tod-greenline] All seven sites
trace back to a single Taichung City Government public notice from 20
September 2011, which formally designated their development content and
control regulations under Article 7 of the Land Development
Regulations.[^tcrt-tod-greenline]

## Remaining gaps

No projected completion date is given for either City Hall site, G9-1 or
G9-2. The roughly four-and-a-half-year gap between G6's signed contract
and its reported construction start is not explained by the source. This
report reflects the bureau's page as fetched on 28 August 2026; whether
any additional site has since reached completion is not established.
