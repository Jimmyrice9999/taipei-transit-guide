## Not where a first pass at this research assumed

Taichung Metro's Green Line has one maintenance depot, 北屯機廠 (Beitun
Depot) — and it sits at the line's **northern** terminus, beside G0 Beitun
Main Station, not near the Wuri/HSR end that an initial hypothesis for this
research wrongly assumed.[^tcrt-cj910] Two independent primary sources
confirm the location from different directions: the depot's own civil
contract describes the site as north of Songzhu Road, running along Hanxi
Creek to the east and Jiushe Lane to the west, with G0 sited on the depot's
western edge as the line's original starting station.[^tcrt-cj910] The
system-equipment contract covering the rest of the line separately
describes its own scope as running "from Beitun Depot to G17" — placing the
depot unambiguously at the opposite end from G17, the line's southern
terminus.[^tcrt-cj900-907]

## The system's only Level 5 facility

Beitun Depot covers approximately 20 hectares and holds the only Level 5
maintenance rating (五級維修) anywhere on the Taichung Metro
system.[^tcrt-cj910] The site houses a main maintenance workshop, a
separate inspection and repair workshop, stabling and maintenance track
groups, a car-wash facility, and an administration building containing the
system's operations control centre, a training centre and
offices.[^tcrt-cj910] No second depot exists on the Green Line — every
contract record and project overview checked names only Beitun.

## From groundbreaking to handover

The whole Green Line held its groundbreaking ceremony on 8 October
2009.[^tmrt-major-events] The depot itself was built under a separate civil
contract, CJ910, awarded to Continental Engineering Corporation for
approximately NT$5.3745 billion, running from 31 December 2012 to
18 February 2019.[^tcrt-cj910] The first Kawasaki-built trainset reached
Taichung Port on 5 February 2017, and the first assembled trainset arrived
at the depot that June.[^tmrt-major-events] Taichung Metro Corporation took
over management of the depot and its 18 trainsets on 16 December 2019, the
same day it registered its "Taichung Metro" trademark — the operations
control centre followed in February 2020, ahead of the line's formal
opening in April 2021.[^tmrt-major-events] In 2021 the depot added a
rooftop solar power installation, projected to add roughly NT$7.5 million a
year in electricity-sale revenue.[^tmrt-major-events]

## Remaining gaps

No source read — including two PDFs fetched directly to check specifically
for this — states how many trainsets Beitun Depot can store or service at
once. No specific definition of what "Level 5 maintenance" comprises was
found on any TMRT or Taichung city-government page checked. No
depot-specific land-development (TOD) plan was found; the city's own
Green Line development case list names seven station sites and does not
include G0 or the depot site.
