## Scope and built form

Kaohsiung’s Circular Light Rail is a 22.1 km surface-light-rail loop with 38 stations in the city government’s completed-route description.[^mtbu-lrt] Its alignment uses the former harbour-rail corridor, port roads, Pier-2, the west-side railway corridor, the Museum of Fine Arts area and Dashun roads before returning through the south-western corridor.[^mtbu-lrt] The operator’s English guide map lists the same C1–C37 plus C21A station identity, while the current TDX Metro pull contains only the Red and Orange metro lines.[^krtc-guide-map-en][^tdx-krtc-line]

The approved planning page gives a combined construction cost of NT$211.16 billion, including NT$70.13 billion in central support and NT$141.03 billion borne by the city, including self-financing revenue.[^mtbu-lrt-approved] It describes a surface B-type right of way with the track separated from the road, 1,435 mm standard gauge, 100% low-floor vehicles, and platforms 2.5–4 m wide and 35 cm high.[^mtbu-lrt-approved] The government route page lists transfers to the Red Line at C3 and C24, to the Orange Line at C14 and C32, and to TRA at C18 and C30.[^mtbu-lrt]

## Two-phase construction and delay

The approved plan divided construction because the C17–C20 railway-garden section depended on the Kaohsiung urban railway-undergrounding project, while the C32–C36 corridor depended on the release of land from the Taiwan Railways East Harbour Line.[^mtbu-lrt-approved] Phase 1 was defined as 8.7 km from the Qianzhen shunting yard to Sizihwan, while Phase 2 covered the remaining 13.4 km.[^mtbu-lrt-approved] The planning page’s original schedule expected Phase 1 to finish by the end of ROC year 106 and Phase 2 to follow the 2017 railway-undergrounding schedule; those are planning milestones, not the eventual opening dates.[^mtbu-lrt-approved]

The second phase became a public dispute rather than a routine continuation. DORTS says the route had been planned since the 1990s, but when construction entered the site in ROC year 106, residents repeatedly protested the route and construction method.[^mtbu-lrt-delay] The city published five “reassurance” measures in July 2018, but opposition continued, and the mayor announced on 18 January 2019 that the disputed second-phase works should pause for broader public hearings.[^mtbu-lrt-delay]

DORTS then held five public hearings in February and March 2019 and recorded 1,874 opinion forms.[^mtbu-lrt-delay] The support submissions cited accessibility, relief of road and commercial-area congestion and connections to the Red and Orange lines; the opposition submissions cited lower capacity, slower speed, traffic effects, loss of curb parking and a preference for underground or elevated metro.[^mtbu-lrt-delay] The bureau appointed nine rail and transport experts to examine nine alternatives, including the original alignment and method, elevated, underground, a Tongmeng Road diversion, C-type right of way and cancellation.[^mtbu-lrt-delay]

The expert committee’s recommendations retained the southern noncontroversial section, proposed resident participation and human-centred streetscape treatment for the Art Museum Road and Dashun Road section, and suggested a future northward extension toward Taiwan High Speed Rail Zuoying.[^mtbu-lrt-delay] DORTS said the committee judged elevated or underground alternatives less economically suitable because of cost, construction risk and later operation, but that is the bureau’s account of the committee’s assessment rather than an independent finding by this page.[^mtbu-lrt-delay]

## Environmental revision and completion

The 2021 environmental-difference review recorded more than 44 public briefings and 20 expert meetings before the city retained the basic route with design changes.[^mtbu-lrt-eia] Those changes included a larger curve radius, moving the Art Museum Road alignment north, preserving four road lanes, adding C21A, adding a pedestrian bridge for Chung-Hua Arts School and placing a station in the United Hospital setback.[^mtbu-lrt-eia] DORTS said the changes reduced noise, preserved emergency access and road service, and did not increase environmental impacts compared with the approved environmental assessment.[^mtbu-lrt-eia]

KRTC’s current operator Q&A records the result as a 1 January 2024 full-circle trial with service expanded to all 38 stations, followed by formal full-line operation from 26 February.[^krtc-lrt-faq] It also records the change from a flat fare to distance-based charging and identifies C1 as both termini for the circular service.[^krtc-lrt-faq] The planning schedule and the operating record therefore show a clear conflict between the original 2017-era completion expectation and the actual 2024 full-line service date; neither is silently substituted for the other.[^mtbu-lrt-approved][^krtc-lrt-faq]

## Station sequence and data boundary

The government station table contains C1 through C37 plus C21A, so C21A Neiwei Arts Center and C21 Kaohsiung Museum of Fine Arts remain separate records.[^mtbu-lrt] The operator’s current timetable page uses some updated English and Chinese naming, including C26 Dashun Minzu and parenthetical names at C27 and C31, so the station layer retains the operator and government provenance rather than manufacturing a single “corrected” translation.[^krtc-lrt-guide][^krtc-guide-map-en][^mtbu-lrt]

The government and operator pages establish station identities, locations, loop structure, construction scope, route length, built form, formal operation and listed interchanges.[^krtc-guide-map-en][^krtc-lrt-guide][^mtbu-lrt][^mtbu-lrt-approved][^krtc-lrt-faq] TDX’s committed KRTC Metro dataset does not contain the LRT as a Metro line, so this page does not invent TDX LRT geometry or station ridership from the metro pull.[^tdx-krtc-line][^tdx-krtc-shape] Detailed station accessibility, platform coordinates, observed ridership, rolling-stock allocation, power substations and package-level construction costs remain TBC until their primary records are fetched and transcribed.[^krtc-station-guide-en][^krtc-lrt-faq]
