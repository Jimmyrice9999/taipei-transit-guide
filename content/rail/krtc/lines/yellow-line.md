## Route and structure

The Yellow Line runs from Niaosong's Shennong Road through Dabei, Chengqing,
Benguan, Jiangong, Minzu and Minquan Roads to Sanduo 5th Road, with a second
branch from Chengqing Road via Guotai, Nanjing and Wujia Roads to Zhenzhong
Road in front of Qianzhen Senior High School — 22.91 km in
total, crossing six districts: Niaosong, Xinxing, Lingya, Qianzhen, Fengshan
and Sanmin.[^mtbu-yellow-route] The line is almost entirely underground: 22
of its 23 stations are underground, with a single elevated station and one
depot.[^mtbu-yellow-route]

## Twenty-three stations, all provisionally named

The government's own route table lists every station from Y1 to Y23 with a
platform type and an approximate location, and states explicitly that every
name is provisional, subject to change through later design and
operational stages.[^mtbu-yellow-route] This page therefore uses only
station codes and location descriptions, not the working names the table
carries.

Six interchanges with other systems are named in the same table: Y10 with
TRA's Minzu Station, Y11 with the Orange Line's Xinyi Elementary School
(O6), Y14 with the Red Line's Sanduo Shopping District (R8), Y15 with the
Circular Light Rail's Travel Service Center (C9), Y17 with TRA's
Zhengyi-Chengqing Station, Y18 with the Orange Line's Weiwuying (O10), and
Y23 with the Red Line's Qianzhen Senior High School (R5).[^mtbu-yellow-route]
The sole elevated station, Y1, sits near the Shennong Road/Datong Road
intersection at the line's Niaosong end.[^mtbu-yellow-route]

## Approvals, and a construction budget that grew by two thirds

The Executive Yuan approved the Yellow Line's feasibility study on 24 May
2019, its comprehensive planning report on 21 March 2022 — with a total
construction budget of approximately NT$144.237 billion, split between a
central-government subsidy of about NT$83.384 billion and a
Kaohsiung city-government share of about NT$60.853 billion — and a revised
plan on 21 October 2025 that raised the total construction budget to
approximately NT$236.858 billion.[^mtbu-yellow-planning] That is roughly a
64% increase over the original comprehensive-planning figure; the bureau's
own planning-description page states both numbers without explaining the
cause of the increase, and this page does not guess at one.[^mtbu-yellow-planning]

## Six contracts, six different stages

Construction is split across at least six named packages, each tracked to
its own status as of the bureau's own progress page, last updated 27
August 2026.[^mtbu-yellow-progress] YM01 covers the electromechanical
systems for all 23 stations plus the depot and three energy dispatch
centres; as of the update, work covered depot foundations, the
administrative building's raft foundation, a reinforced retaining wall and
a temporary flood-detention pond.[^mtbu-yellow-progress] YT01, the track
works package, was awarded to Senye Construction (森業營造公司) after a
January 2023 bid evaluation and an April 2023 contract signing, and is
currently in detailed design.[^mtbu-yellow-progress]

The civil and facilities-electromechanical work is split three ways: YC01
(Y1 through Y5 exclusive) to a joint venture including a Malaysian
contractor; YC02 (Y5 exclusive through Y8) and YC03 (Y5 inclusive through
Y16–Y18) both to 工信工程股份有限公司.[^mtbu-yellow-progress] Each package
was, as of the update, at early civil-works stages — utility relocation,
diaphragm walls, ground-improvement piling and demolition of existing
structures at their respective stations.[^mtbu-yellow-progress] A separate
detailed-design contract, YD01, covers the remaining unassigned station
segment (Y8 station body exclusive to Y10); its first tender for that
segment failed on 21 July 2026 with no bidders, and the bureau states plans
to re-announce it by the end of August 2026.[^mtbu-yellow-progress]

## Remaining gaps

No opening date has been published for any part of the line in any source
read. The depot has no stated name in any source read here, unlike this
project's separate research into the Red/Orange system's three named
depots. The cause of the construction budget's large increase between the
comprehensive plan and the revised plan is not stated. This project's earlier technology
and rolling-stock research separately found the Yellow Line will run a
fully driverless Siemens CBTC signalling system and Stadler Rail car
bodies; those findings are not repeated as established facts of this
specific route description, which does not itself address signalling or
rolling stock.
