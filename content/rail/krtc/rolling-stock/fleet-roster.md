## The original fleet: sourced only secondhand

No primary KRTC or government page stating the Red/Orange Line's fleet size
or manufacturer in prose was found for this page — a genuine gap, not an
oversight. What is published rests on two independently fetched secondary
sources that agree with each other: a widely reported 42 Siemens-built
trainsets (126 cars, 3-car sets), ordered October 2001 and assembled in
Austria, with deliveries from October 2005.[^zhwiki-krtc-emu] A Liberty
Times article on the newer Hyundai Rotem order separately refers to "the
existing 42 Siemens trainsets" in passing, corroborating the
figure.[^ltn-krtc-new-order-2021] Vehicle dimensions and capacity figures
(3,750 mm body height, 750 passengers standard load per 3-car set, four
door-pairs per side) rest on a Wikipedia infobox only and are not
independently verified.[^zhwiki-krtc-emu]

KRTC's own live FAQ confirms directly that trains still run only as 3-car
sets: the system's signalling and platform-door hardware and software are
configured on a 3-car-unit basis, and coupling two sets into a 6-car train
is not something that can be done immediately — it would need dedicated new
6-car vehicles and a signalling upgrade.[^krtc-3car-faq] The same operator
confirms the Red Line's current extent runs from R3 Siaogang to RK1
Gangshan, reflecting the opened first phase of the Gangshan
extension.[^krtc-redline-extent-faq]

## A new fleet, compatible with the old

A joint venture of Hyundai Rotem (South Korea) and Singapore Technologies
Electronics won the design-build electromechanical contract for the
Gangshan–Luzhu extension. Reporting from 2021 describes an initial order of
10 trainsets (8 in a first phase plus a 2-set expansion contract); a 2023
article puts the total at 16 new sets once 6 more were added for the
Xiaogang–Linyuan extension.[^ltn-krtc-new-order-2021][^ltn-krtc-16-sets] The
new trainsets are explicitly stated to be electromechanically compatible
with the existing Siemens system, rather than a separate incompatible
sub-fleet.[^ltn-krtc-16-sets] No source found gives a model or series name
for the Hyundai Rotem trains, unlike the light rail's named rolling
stock below; that is TBC.

## Two more systems, two more manufacturers each

The Circular Light Rail — presented on this site as a KRTC-operated system alongside the metro, though no source read for this page states that operating relationship in one explicit sentence — has run two rolling-stock generations from
different manufacturers, per secondary sources not independently verified
in full: CAF Urbos 3 (Spain, 9 sets from 2014)[^zhwiki-lrt-caf] and Alstom Citadis X-05
(France, 15 sets, commercial service from 2 November 2020).[^zhwiki-lrt-citadis] A primary
Kaohsiung MRT Engineering Bureau FAQ independently confirms the physical
train — 5 cars, 34.16 m long, carrying up to 250 passengers at full load —
without naming either manufacturer itself.[^mtbu-lrt-car-faq]

The under-construction Yellow Line will run its own third fleet again: a
genuine primary source names Stadler Rail (Switzerland) as the car-body
manufacturer, building 3-car sets — two powered end cars with manual-drive
capability plus one unpowered middle car — up to 60 m long and carrying
more than 440 passengers, capable of fully automatic driverless
operation.[^mtbu-yellow-line-vehicle-systems] A planned fleet size of 25
sets appears only in a secondary source and is not confirmed here.

## Remaining gaps

No primary source for the Red/Orange fleet's size or manufacturer was
found. Neither the Hyundai Rotem nor the original Siemens fleet has a
confirmed model/series designation. The Hyundai Rotem order's contract
value is unresolved — the one figure found was internally inconsistent and
is not published. No source states a combined total fleet size once the
new trainsets arrive, and this page does not compute one. The Yellow
Line's planned fleet size is unconfirmed against a primary source.
