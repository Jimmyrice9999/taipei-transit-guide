## Three parties, one contribution-weighted building

Kaohsiung's joint land development programme rests on two named legal
instruments: Article 7 of the Mass Rapid Transit Act and the Mass Rapid
Transit System Land Development Regulations.[^mtbu-jdo-overview] The
bureau states its guiding philosophy in a compact phrase — "合建分坪、
利潤共享" (joint construction with divided floor space, shared profit) —
under which an investor, a landowner and the competent authority develop a
site together as three parties, splitting the completed building according
to each party's share of the development cost.[^mtbu-jdo-overview] The
bureau frames the public-interest case for the programme as protecting
landowners' rights by combining private and public-sector capacity, while
giving the community a share of the resulting development
benefit.[^mtbu-jdo-overview]

## A three-stage process, seven named steps

The bureau's own process description breaks a joint-development project
into three chronological stages, each with its own named
steps.[^mtbu-jdo-overview] Land acquisition comes first, in four steps:
urban-planning review, an appraiser's land valuation, a formal
urban-planning announcement, and negotiated purchase directly with
landowners.[^mtbu-jdo-overview] Investor solicitation and construction
management follows, in two steps: selecting an investor, then the
development construction itself.[^mtbu-jdo-overview] Rights allocation
comes last, again in two steps: allocating rights among the three parties,
then registering property and handing the site to ongoing
operations.[^mtbu-jdo-overview]

## Fifteen current cases

The bureau's own case-list page — unreadable to a previous research pass,
readable to this one — lists 15 current joint-development cases as of a
14 August 2026 update string on the page itself.[^mtbu-case-list] All 15
sit in either the investor/implementer-selection stage or the
development/construction stage; none is marked complete or shown at the
final rights-allocation stage the mechanism overview
describes.[^mtbu-case-list][^mtbu-jdo-overview]

The largest is O10, at Weiwuying on the Orange Line — self-described by
the bureau as Kaohsiung's first dual-metro-line development case, since
the same site also serves as a Yellow Line (Y18) entrance, with a stated
expected investment around NT$21.2 billion.[^mtbu-o10] The most unusual
winning applicant is Foxconn (鴻海精密工業), selected for the Y15 Cruise
Terminal site with a stated intended use as a 5G AIoT industry
headquarters and R&D base, not a conventional mixed-use
building.[^mtbu-y15] Cases also sit at O4 (Cianjin), O9 (three separate
adjoining sites, Lingya Sports Park), O13 (Dadong), R20 (Houjing), Y10
(Minzu), the not-yet-open RK1 (Gangshan, on the Gangshan–Luzhu extension,
co-owned in part with Taiwan Railways), and C34 (Wucyuan Elementary
School) on the Circular Light Rail.[^mtbu-case-list]

## Two different legal mechanisms under one case list

Twelve of the 15 cases use investor-solicitation language consistent with
the Article 7 mechanism described above.[^mtbu-case-list] Three, however, are titled as
public urban-renewal cases (公開評選實施者, "publicly selecting an
implementer") rather than investor solicitation — at O4/Cianjin, at R17
(World Games), and at a site near the Circular Line's C34.[^mtbu-case-list]
The bureau's own regulations page names only the Mass Rapid Transit Act
and its Land Development Regulations as this office's legal basis, with
no mention of the Urban Renewal Act, even though it is the same office
listing these three cases alongside the Article 7
ones.[^mtbu-jdo-regulations] Whether the bureau considers all 15 cases one
programme regardless of which statute a given site actually rests on, or
whether the "joint development" framing on this page should exclude the
three urban-renewal cases, is not settled by anything read for this
page.[^mtbu-case-list][^mtbu-jdo-regulations]

## What the list does not show

Formosa Boulevard (O5/R10) and Kaohsiung Main Station (R11) — the busiest
interchange and the system's main terminus — do not appear anywhere in
the current 15-case list.[^mtbu-case-list] That establishes only that
neither has an *active* case in the bureau's tracked pipeline today; it
does not establish that no joint-development building was ever proposed
or completed there before this online case-tracking system existed, since
the Red and Orange Lines opened in 2008 and the list's own stages describe
an active pipeline, not a historical archive.[^mtbu-case-list] This
project's separate depots research already describes commercial-
development zoning at three named Red/Orange depot sites, but no source
read connects those specifically to this Article 7 mechanism rather than
a different disposal method, so that connection is not assumed
here.[^mtbu-case-list]

## Remaining gaps

No case in the current list is confirmed complete, so this page cannot
show what a finished joint-development building actually delivers in
practice. Several cases (both RK1 sites, and the C34-area urban-renewal
case) do not state a developer name or a construction timeline on their
own bureau page. Whether a historical, already-closed-out case exists from
before 2008 remains unresearched — it would need Taiwanese news-archive
search, which this pass's exhausted search budget did not
reach.[^mtbu-case-list]
