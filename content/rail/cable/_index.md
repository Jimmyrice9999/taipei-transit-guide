One system, one line: the Maokong Gondola. It is an aerial ropeway — a POMA
installation, not a railway — and it is filed here rather than under its own
top-level heading because a heading for a single line is a heading that says
nothing. What it shares with the rest of this section is the thing this section
is organised by: it runs on its own fixed guideway, to published stations, in a
published order, inside the region's stored-value ticketing, and it meets the
metro at Taipei Zoo.

The gondola is part of the network in the ways that matter here: metro
affiliation, shared ticketing, and a genuine engineering and incident history
that English coverage — which is entirely tourist-oriented — has not touched.
The story worth telling is the long typhoon closure: a tower foundation
undermined, a multi-institute engineering assessment, and a reopening under
monitoring. That is the kind of material this site exists for, and it will be
written against the city's own engineering publications rather than travel
blogs.

![A green-liveried Maokong Gondola cabin waiting at a covered station platform](/images/gondola/cabin-800.webp "One of the gondola's cabins, at a station platform. — Solomon203, CC BY-SA 4.0, Wikimedia Commons")

Coming: the system (French-built, and what that lineage means for parts and
maintenance), the stations, the cabins — including the glass-floored ones —
and the closure history. Bounded scope, no churn, high value: this is early on
the build list for the same reasons YouBike is.
