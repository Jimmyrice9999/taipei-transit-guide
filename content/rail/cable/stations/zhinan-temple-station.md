Zhinan Temple Station sits at the rear-mountain car park of the temple it
is named for, reached by 指南路三段157巷200號[^gondola-stations-zhinan]. It
opened with the rest of the line on 4 July 2007, at an elevation of **264.3
metres**[^wiki-zhinan-station] — more than ten times higher than Zoo
Station below. Structurally it is an elevated, T-shaped building with four
above-ground floors and side platforms[^wiki-zhinan-station].

This is one of the two upper stations directly on the closed side of the
line during the 2008–2010 T16 closure: Chinese Wikipedia's own article for
this station independently dates the same suspension and reopening this
project's line page already established from the Control Yuan's corrective
resolution — service stopped **1 October 2008** and formally resumed **30
March 2010**[^wiki-zhinan-station] (see
[Maokong Gondola](/rail/cable/lines/maokong-gondola/) for the T16 tower
failure itself, which this station sits on the far side of).

A more recent addition: on **15 June 2014**, a stainless-steel dragon
sculpture titled 「金龍獻瑞」 ("Golden Dragon Presents Good Fortune"), plated
in titanium, was unveiled at the station's 迎仙亭 ("Immortal-Welcoming
Pavilion") pond[^wiki-zhinan-station] — a landmark this project has not
independently corroborated against a second source, but which is dated and
named specifically enough to record here rather than omit.

It is the third of the line's four passenger stations, between Zoo South
Station below and Maokong Station, the terminus, above[^gondola-system].
