## Overview and operator-published network

San Chung Bus’s company profile names 16 dispatch sites: Luzhou 1 and 2, Xinzhuang, Zhonggang, Tucheng, Huilong, Bali, Wugu, Taishan, Shulin, Tamhai, Neihu, Nangang, Gongxi, Linkou and the Jiou intercity-bus terminal [^sanchung-about]. It separately names two maintenance yards in Luzhou and Xinzhuang and says they occupy more than 1,000 ping in total [^sanchung-about].

The same profile says five sites have large automatic bus washers and other sites have semi-automatic washers [^sanchung-about]. This is evidence of the operator’s published facility network, not a current register of every vehicle stored at each site [^sanchung-about].

The profile's distinction between dispatch sites and maintenance yards matters:
the 16-site list describes places from which service is dispatched, while the
two-yard statement describes the locations the operator identifies for repair
and maintenance [^sanchung-about]. It does not say that every dispatch site has
overnight parking, a workshop or a charging installation [^sanchung-about].
The more-than-1,000-ping figure is a combined area for the two maintenance
yards, not a per-yard measurement and not the area of all 16 dispatch sites
[^sanchung-about].

The company page also does not date the opening of any named site or explain
which of the five automatic washers is at which address [^sanchung-about].
Those omissions prevent a historical depot timeline or a site-by-site facility
comparison from being sourced from this record alone.

The list is therefore best read as the operator's published network vocabulary:
it tells a rider or researcher which places San Chung names as operating or
maintenance sites, while leaving staffing, parking capacity and route ownership
unmeasured [^sanchung-about].

## A separate directory, with addresses, phones and routes — and a real discrepancy

A second operator page, distinct from the company profile, gives full address, phone number and route allocation for 15 named sites.[^sanchung-office] Luzhou 1 station, for instance, is listed at 「新北市蘆洲區中正路333號(設有電動輪椅充電區)」— noting an electric-wheelchair charging area — serving routes 225, 225區, 261, 306, 508區, 內科6, 市民小巴12, 紅9, 通勤9, 899 and the Tiaowa (跳蛙) bus.[^sanchung-office] Two sites in this directory sit in Taoyuan City rather than New Taipei: Huilong station at 「桃園市龜山區萬壽路一段553號」and Gongxi station at 「桃園市龜山區復興三路56號」.[^sanchung-office] The Jiou intercity terminal is listed at Taipei's main bus station, serving only route 9003.[^sanchung-office]

This directory's site list does not match the company profile's 16-site list exactly, and the discrepancy is published here rather than resolved.[^sanchung-about][^sanchung-office] The company profile names Taishan (泰山站) and Neihu (內湖站) and calls the Danshui-area site 淡海站 (Tamhai); the directory instead has no Taishan or Neihu entry, includes an extra site not named in the profile at all (五股二站, Wugu 2), and calls the same Danshui-area site 淡水站 (Tamsui), not 淡海站.[^sanchung-about][^sanchung-office] Both pages are primary, from the same operator, and both are currently live; nothing in either states whether this reflects a facility closure, a rename, or two lists serving different purposes (a dispatch-site roster versus a rider-facing contact directory).[^sanchung-about][^sanchung-office]

## Route and capacity limits

Neither operator page assigns individual TDX route records site-by-site beyond what the directory's own route lists state, publishes opening dates, or gives a capacity for each yard [^sanchung-about][^sanchung-office]. Those fields remain TBC rather than being inferred from route endpoints or from the operator's approximate fleet scale [^sanchung-about]. A current site register, operating notice or allocation table would settle them, along with the site-list discrepancy above.
