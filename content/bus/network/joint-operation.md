To a passenger, Taipei has one city bus network: one brand, one fare structure,
one route numbering scheme, one app. **It is fourteen private companies**, and it
has been a negotiated arrangement between them since 1977[^pto-intro].

The regulator's own summary is unglamorous and worth stating exactly: around
**280 routes**, **14 operators**, about **3,500 vehicles**[^pto-intro]. Nothing
about that is visible from a bus stop, which is the point of the arrangement.

## Where it came from

The city's buses began as a municipal monopoly. 臺北市公共汽車管理處 ran
**51 routes with 651 vehicles**[^pto-intro]. Private operation was opened up in
part from 1969, when 欣欣, 大有, 大南 and 光華 joined in succession and the
network grew to **90 routes and 847 vehicles**[^pto-intro].

Joint operation proper began on **30 April 1977**, when five more private
operators — 中興, 指南, 台北, 三重 and 三重市公車, today's 首都客運 — came
in[^pto-intro]. The regulator's account: **149 routes were allocated**, 2,325 vehicles were
planned for joint operation, and **1,984 actually ran**[^pto-intro].

### How many routes it started with — two official answers

The operators' own body describes the same year differently. It says the first
phase opened with **33 routes**, and that three further phases during 1977
opened **50, 31 and 16** more[^busgroup] — **130** in all.

| Routes at the start | Source | Kind |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **149**, 「當時分配路線149條」 | Public Transport Office[^pto-intro] | Primary |
| **33**, then 50 + 31 + 16 across four 1977 phases | 臺北市公車聯營管理委員會[^busgroup] | Primary |

One figure may count routes *allocated* on day one and the other routes
*opened* across the year. **Neither source says so**, both are the bodies'
own numbers, and this page publishes both rather than picking.

### Afterwards

Through the late 1980s and 1990s many intercity routes run by the former
臺灣省公路局 were absorbed into joint operation, either renumbered outright or
taken in as 區段聯營, joint operation over a section[^pto-intro]. The municipal
operator itself stopped being municipal: on **1 January 2004** 臺北市公共汽車管理處
was corporatised and renamed **大都會汽車客運**[^pto-intro].

A recent and easily missed change: some journeys on low-demand routes have been
moved to a **booking system** rather than a timetable —
「針對部分路線班次搭乘需求低且不固定之班次納入預約制」[^pto-intro].

## Who decides

The **臺北市公車聯營管理委員會** is formed by the participating operators
themselves, with the Public Transport Office as the supervising authority, and
it describes itself as the 「決策及業務執行單位」 — the body that both decides and
executes[^busgroup]. Its stated duties are narrower than the name suggests:
maintaining and managing bus stop signs, public relations, liaison between
passengers, regulator and operators, and **review of joint electronic-ticketing
business**[^busgroup].

Ten participants founded it: the municipal 臺北市公共汽車管理處, the four
operators admitted from 1969 (欣欣客運, 大有巴士, 大南汽車, 光華巴士) and the
five admitted in 1977 (中興巴士, 指南客運, 臺北客運, 三重客運 and
三重市公車, now 首都客運)[^busgroup].

**One body or two?** zh.wikipedia describes a two-tier structure — the committee
as the decision body and a separate 臺北市公車聯營管理中心 as the executive
one[^zh-taipei-bus]. The committee's own page describes a single body doing
both[^busgroup]. Published as the disagreement it is.

A standing city committee sits above all this and reviews costs, fares and
subsidies: seventeen members convened by the head of the Department of
Transportation, with seats for the Ministry of Transportation and
Communications, its Institute of Transportation, the Consumers' Foundation and —
notably — the operators' own 臺北市公車聯營管理委員會, whose tasks include
「定期檢討聯營公車營運成本及票價事項」[^law-evaluation-committee]. **The version
of that rule read here is marked repealed**; a successor exists and has not been
read, so treat the structure as indicative rather than current.

## How the money moves

The arrangement is usually described in one sentence about a central revenue
pool. No opened primary source establishes that sentence. The city's own
regulations instead set out a different and more specific machine, in four
parts.

### Routes are allocated, not owned

An operator does not own a route. The city allocates them, and it does so on
**points**: when the Public Transport Office allocates a joint-operation route
or reallocates a revoked one, **the operator with the most accumulated route
points chooses first, and is docked ten points for doing so** —
「得由累計獲有路線積分分數最多之聯營公車業者優先選線 1條，並扣除路線積分10分」[^law-route-allocation].
The alternative path is open tender: the city canvasses operators' interest and
a review committee, the 臺北都會區市區及公路客運路線審議委員會, selects the
operator[^law-route-allocation].

**The city can also take a profitable route away.** The rule carries a heading
that says so outright — 「本處收回業者高營收路線接駛原則」, principles for
recovering high-revenue routes from operators. The successor operator gets
**five years**, must apply to continue one year before that expires, and can be
extended by at most three more; it must run the original alignment and may not
apply to adjust it for a year[^law-route-allocation].

That single provision does more to explain the shape of the network than any
account of pooling: **a route that earns well is a thing the regulator can move
between operators.**

### Losses are subsidised route by route, on a published formula

Operating-loss subsidy is made under a *central* regulation, not a city one:
「特依交通部令頒大眾運輸事業補貼辦法訂定本作業規定」[^law-loss-subsidy]. An
operator may apply where a route it still ran at the end of the previous
December earned **less per bus-kilometre than its reasonable operating
cost**[^law-loss-subsidy]. The formula is stated:

> （每車公里合理營運成本－每車公里實際營運收入）×（車次數）×（路線往返里程）

Reasonable operating cost per bus-kilometre, minus **actual** operating revenue
per bus-kilometre, times the number of departures, times the round-trip route
length[^law-loss-subsidy]. The units are defined next door: 里程 is
single-direction kilometres and one 車次 is a round trip, two
departures[^law-route-allocation].

Three further provisions worth having:

| Provision | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| Cost sharing | For routes up to 30 km and up to 30 車次 a day, **central government one third, Taipei City two thirds**[^law-loss-subsidy] |
| Priority classes | Three tiers, and **捷運接駁 metro feeders are a named priority class** in their own right[^law-loss-subsidy] |
| The ceiling | An operator drawing more than **NT$10 million** in approved subsidy across two years can have the route put out to open recruitment; new-route franchises run **five years**[^law-loss-subsidy] |

**This is the load-bearing point on the whole money question.** The subsidy is
computed against 每車公里實際營運收入 — actual operating revenue per
bus-kilometre, *on that route, for that operator*[^law-loss-subsidy]. Revenue is
therefore attributed to individual routes and individual operators in the city's
own regulation. Any account of a central pool has to be reconciled with that,
and none of the accounts that assert a pool attempts to.

### Concessionary fares are reimbursed monthly, per operator

Where a passenger pays a concessionary fare, the difference between that and the
single tariff is budgeted and paid by government — 「票價與單一運價間之差額，
由政府編列預算補貼之金額」, under 發展大眾運輸條例 第九條[^law-fare-difference].
The recipients are named as 「經營本市聯營公車路線之公車業者」, the operators,
individually[^law-fare-difference].

The cycle is **monthly** — 「價差補貼款之審核核撥週期：每月1次」 — with operators
filing by the twentieth of the following month, and the claims are audited
against the **card issuers'** records via a route-by-route subsidy application,
an operating detail return and a card-tap record. Payments are docked for
abnormal taps, over-age vehicles, lapsed insurance, missing announcement
equipment and wage non-compliance[^law-fare-difference].

### The early split — a thread, not an answer

zh.wikipedia's article on Taipei bus tokens states that the early years of joint
operation used a **四分法**, a fixed-formula division of revenue, and that this
is why fare theft by one company hurt the other three:
「北市公車聯營初期是採用依固定公式分配收入的『四分法』，所以某家公司吃票嚴重，
會影響其他三家公司利益」[^zh-token-coin].

**Read that with its footnote showing.** The footnote resolves to 唯真,
〈關心話題 正本清源談公車票證〉, 聯合報, 13 April 1981 — a newspaper article
whose full body could not be fetched from the closed 聯合報 archive[^zh-token-coin].
It is also confined to the *early* period, and
nothing found explains why a "four-way" split describes an arrangement that had
ten participants. It is recorded here as the closest thing to a sourced
revenue-distribution mechanism anywhere, and as a lead rather than a finding.

## What a route number tells you

Enough that it is worth learning, and nothing in English explains it.

### The coloured routes are metro feeders

**紅, 綠, 藍, 棕 and 橘 in front of a route number are not decoration. They are
metro line colours**, and a bus carrying one is a 捷運接駁公車 — an MRT feeder,
serving the areas a line does not reach and terminating at one of its
stations[^zh-taipei-bus].

| Prefix | English | Feeds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **紅** | R | Tamsui–Xinyi Line |
| **綠** | G | Songshan–Xindian Line |
| **藍** | BL | Bannan Line |
| **棕** | BR | Wenhu Line |
| **橘** | O | Zhonghe–Xinlu Line |

So 藍26 is a Bannan Line feeder and 棕10 is a Wenhu Line feeder[^zh-taipei-bus],
and a visitor who knows the metro already knows roughly where either one goes. Feeder buses
are one of the six service categories the Public Transport Office
lists[^pto-intro].

**One caveat travels with that table.** The metro-line reading is zh.wikipedia's
and the news reports' — the latter give only 「捷運接駁公車」 for a colour prefix
without saying the colour names a line[^setn-numbering]. Nothing found in this
project's searches is a Public Transport Office publication stating that
紅/綠/藍/棕/橘 are metro line colours. The rule is almost certainly right; it is
not, on this site's evidence, regulator-published.

**The Taoyuan Airport MRT has no prefix**, and that is a real gap rather than an
omission here: the line opened in 2017, is run by a different operator, and its
feeder services were never folded into the colour scheme[^zh-taipei-bus].

### The leading digit records fare and service, not geography

Two Taiwanese outlets ran the same explanation of the numbering and both
attribute it to the same named official: **臺北市公共運輸處主任秘書楊欽文**,
Chief Secretary of the Public Transport Office[^setn-numbering][^tvbs-numbering].
These are news reports *about* what the regulator says, not the regulator — but
they are the first material this site has found that puts the numbering rule
in the regulator's own mouth.

| **7xx** | 冷氣公車, three segments — *disputed, see below* |

zh.wikipedia gives an independently worded version of the same axis: 1xx are
leisure routes; 2xx began as two-segment fares and 3xx as three; the 5xx, 6xx
and 7xx blocks were air-conditioned services; 9xx are express[^zh-taipei-bus].

**Three separate accounts, and not one of them mentions geography.** A
geographic reading also circulates in Taiwanese transport writing, in which the
leading digit records the part of the city a route was set up to serve — the
historic core, then the outward districts, then the routes crossing into what is
now New Taipei. No opened authority-backed account supplies those geographic
bands. Every account that names an authority instead describes fare segments
and service type[^setn-numbering][^tvbs-numbering][^zh-taipei-bus].

### 6xx and 7xx — the same officer, two answers

| Band | Reading A[^setn-numbering] | Reading B[^tvbs-numbering] |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **6xx** | 冷氣公車, two-segment fare[^setn-numbering] | 後期兩段票路線 — the 2xx block ran out at 299 and 6xx continued it[^tvbs-numbering] |
| **7xx** | 冷氣公車, three-segment fare[^setn-numbering] | 由公路客運轉成的聯營公車 — absorbed intercity routes[^tvbs-numbering] |

Both outlets attribute to 楊欽文. zh.wikipedia's main text gives Reading A for
7xx and **its own footnote gives Reading B**[^zh-taipei-bus]. This page publishes
the conflict rather than resolving it: the digits are not settled.

Reading B for 7xx is at least consistent with something the regulator does say —
that intercity routes run by the former 臺灣省公路局 were absorbed into joint
operation by renumbering from the late 1980s[^pto-intro]. That is a
compatibility, not a corroboration.

### The numbers that do not exist

There is **no route 8**, and **no route number beginning with 4**. The same PTO
Chief Secretary is quoted directly on why[^tvbs-numbering]:

> 國人的這個風俗習慣，所以避免使用4字頭，8路沒有用的原因，大概考慮是因為在大陸
> 時期，中共的軍隊編號是8路軍，所以那時候就比較忌諱用8路這樣的一個數字。

The four is the ordinary homophone taboo. The eight is not superstition at all:
八路軍, the Eighth Route Army, was a Chinese Communist formation, and a bus
route numbered 8 was avoided for that reason[^tvbs-numbering]. Note that this
bars the *route* numbered 8, not the 8xx band, which the same officer explains
as former Taipei County routes[^setn-numbering].

### Suffixes

| Suffix | Means |
| --- | --- |
| 區 / 區間 | A short working over part of the route |
| 副 | A branch variant |
| 延 | An extension |
| 左 / 右 | Anticlockwise / clockwise around a loop |

These are in retreat — zh.wikipedia records the directional pair surviving
mainly on the Maokong tourist service and one New Taipei route[^zh-taipei-bus].

### 小 and 市民小巴

**小** prefixes a minibus, typically on hill routes; the same prefix also covers
the 懷恩專車 cemetery services[^setn-numbering]. **市民小巴**, rendered **M**
in English, is a last-mile community service between neighbourhoods and metro
stations.

## The six categories

The regulator's own list, which is the closest thing to an official taxonomy:
一般路線 general routes, 幹線公車 trunk buses, 捷運接駁公車 metro feeders,
山區公車 hill routes, 休閒公車 leisure routes, and 市民小巴 community
minibuses[^pto-intro].

**Trunk buses are the interesting category.** They were assembled in 2017–18
from existing joint-operation routes running on major arterials, rebranded as
類捷運 — "metro-like" — and named after the streets they run along rather than
numbered: 信義幹線, 敦化幹線[^zh-taipei-bus]. The network is called
**八橫八縱**, eight horizontal and eight vertical[^zh-taipei-bus].

## Trunk buses, and the colour collision

The trunk network was not conceived on its own. The Public Transport Office's
January 2017 release sets out a **four-tier hierarchy** for the whole bus
network: 快速 express, 幹線 trunk, 支線 branch, 微循環
micro-circulation[^pto-trunk-network].

Phase one was five corridors, named in the release —
「南北向敦化、松江新生2線，東西向之民生、仁愛2線及內湖等共計5線幹線路廊」: Dunhua
and Songjiang–Xinsheng running north–south, Minsheng and Renai running
east–west, plus Neihu[^pto-trunk-network]. The same release gives peak headways
of **4–6 minutes** and off-peak **5–10 minutes**, though those figures came back
only in an English summary of the page and are carried at lower
confidence[^pto-trunk-network].

### The colour rule, from the regulator

This is the finding. **On a trunk route, colour encodes the compass direction of
the corridor**[^pto-trunk-identity]:

> 幹線公車依幹線路廊方向賦予其專屬代表色彩，南北向為綠色，東西向為藍色，非屬前述者為黃色

| Trunk colour | Corridor direction |
| --- | --- |
| **綠** green | North–south |
| **藍** blue | East–west |
| **黃** yellow | Neither of the above |

And the release states what the colours are meant to *mean*, which is the tell
that this is a branding scheme rather than a wayfinding code inherited from the
metro: 「藍色代表「幹線系統」、「城市」，綠色代表「綠意」、「生活美好」，
黃色代表「陽光」、「希望」」 — blue for the trunk system and the city, green for
greenery and a good life, yellow for sunshine and hope[^pto-trunk-identity].

**So the same colour word means two unrelated things on the same network, and
both are current.** On a feeder, 綠 means the Songshan–Xindian Line. On a trunk
route, 綠 means north–south. 藍 is a metro line on one and a compass bearing on
the other. Neither the regulator nor zh.wikipedia notes the clash.

The collision is not hypothetical, because routes have crossed from one side of
it to the other[^zh-trunk-bus]:

| Was | Became |
| --- | --- |
| **棕9** — a Wenhu Line feeder | 南京幹線[^zh-trunk-bus] |
| **紅32** — a Tamsui–Xinyi Line feeder | 民權幹線[^zh-trunk-bus] |
| **綠1** | 南環幹線[^zh-trunk-bus] |
| **綠16** | 東環幹線[^zh-trunk-bus] |
| **220** | 中山幹線[^zh-trunk-bus] |
| **642** | 松江新生幹線[^zh-trunk-bus] |
| **650** | 基隆路幹線[^zh-trunk-bus] |
| **620** | 北環幹線[^zh-trunk-bus] |

棕9 lost a prefix that told you which metro line it served[^zh-taipei-bus] and
gained a name that tells you which street it runs along[^zh-trunk-bus] — and,
under the April 2017 rule, a colour that would now tell you which way it
points[^pto-trunk-identity].

### How big is the trunk network

Not settled, and the units do not match.

| Figure | Source | Kind |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **16 corridors** planned, phased, 5 in phase one | Public Transport Office, January 2017[^pto-trunk-network] | Primary — but from an English summary of the page |
| **16 routes** as at 2 April 2018 | 中國時報, 1 April 2018, 吳堂靖 — *not located by this site and not cited here* | Secondary, unverified |
| **19 routes**, 「屆時將有19條幹線公車」, after the 1 July 2024 renamings | zh.wikipedia[^zh-trunk-bus] | Secondary |

**Corridors and routes are not the same unit**, and 八橫八縱 is sixteen of
something. A Public Transport Office release announcing 八橫八縱三環 and
nineteen routes surfaced as a search result title, but the page returns HTTP 404;
nothing found in this site's searches puts the nineteen-route figure in the
regulator's own words. It remains zh.wikipedia's, and secondary.
