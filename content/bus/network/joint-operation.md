To a passenger, Taipei has one city bus network: one brand, one fare structure,
one route numbering scheme, one app. **It is fourteen private companies**, and it
has been a negotiated arrangement between them since 1977[^pto-intro].

The regulator's own summary is unglamorous and worth stating exactly: around
**280 routes**, **14 operators**, about **3,500 vehicles**[^pto-intro]. Nothing
about that is visible from a bus stop, which is the point of the arrangement.

## What a route number tells you

Enough that it is worth learning, and nothing in English explains it.

### The coloured routes are metro feeders

**紅, 綠, 藍, 棕 and 橘 in front of a route number are not decoration. They are
metro line colours**, and a bus carrying one is a 捷運接駁公車 — an MRT feeder,
serving the areas a line does not reach and terminating at one of its
stations[^zh-taipei-bus].

| Prefix | English | Feeds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **紅** | R | Tamsui–Xinyi Line |
| **綠** | G | Songshan–Xindian Line |
| **藍** | BL | Bannan Line |
| **棕** | BR | Wenhu Line |
| **橘** | O | Zhonghe–Xinlu Line |

So 藍26 is a Bannan Line feeder and 棕10 is a Wenhu Line feeder[^zh-taipei-bus],
and a visitor who knows the metro already knows roughly where either one goes. Feeder buses
are one of the six service categories the Public Transport Office
lists[^pto-intro].

**The Taoyuan Airport MRT has no prefix**, and that is a real gap rather than an
omission here: the line opened in 2017, is run by a different operator, and its
feeder services were never folded into the colour scheme[^zh-taipei-bus].

### Suffixes

| Suffix | Means |
| --- | --- |
| 區 / 區間 | A short working over part of the route |
| 副 | A branch variant |
| 延 | An extension |
| 左 / 右 | Anticlockwise / clockwise around a loop |

These are in retreat — zh.wikipedia records the directional pair surviving
mainly on the Maokong tourist service and one New Taipei route[^zh-taipei-bus].

### 小 and 市民小巴

**小** prefixes a minibus, typically on hill routes. **市民小巴**, rendered **M**
in English, is a last-mile community service between neighbourhoods and metro
stations.

### What the numbers themselves mean — unresolved

Two explanations circulate and they are not compatible.

**The fare-segment reading**, which is zh.wikipedia's: the leading digit records
what a route originally *cost*, not where it went. 1xx are leisure routes; 2xx
began as two-segment fares and 3xx as three; the 5xx, 6xx and 7xx blocks were
air-conditioned services; 9xx are express[^zh-taipei-bus].

**A geographic reading** also circulates in Taiwanese transport writing, in
which the leading digit records the part of the city a route was set up to
serve — the historic core, then the outward districts, then the routes crossing
into what is now New Taipei. **The specific bands are not reproduced here**,
because nothing found attaches a source to them.

**This site cannot settle it.** The Public Transport Office publishes no
numbering principles at all on its introduction page[^pto-intro], and the two
readings disagree about what the digits encode rather than merely about details.
The fare-segment reading has a source attached and the geographic one does not,
which is why it is described here and not tabulated.

## The six categories

The regulator's own list, which is the closest thing to an official taxonomy:
一般路線 general routes, 幹線公車 trunk buses, 捷運接駁公車 metro feeders,
山區公車 hill routes, 休閒公車 leisure routes, and 市民小巴 community
minibuses[^pto-intro].

**Trunk buses are the interesting category.** They were assembled in 2017–18
from existing joint-operation routes running on major arterials, rebranded as
類捷運 — "metro-like" — and named after the streets they run along rather than
numbered: 信義幹線, 敦化幹線[^zh-taipei-bus]. The network is called
**八橫八縱**, eight horizontal and eight vertical[^zh-taipei-bus].

That rebranding did something to the numbering worth noticing. **棕9, a Wenhu
Line feeder, became 南京幹線** — a route named for the street it runs on. The
colour prefix, which had told you which metro line the bus served, was simply
dropped.

## What this page does not yet say

Deliberately, and it is most of what the page should eventually carry.

- **How the money works.** The arrangement is widely described as revenue pooled
  centrally and redistributed between operators, which would make it neither a
  franchise nor a contracted network but something closer to a regulated cartel.
  **The Public Transport Office's own introduction does not describe
  it**[^pto-intro], and this site will not publish a structural claim of that
  size on a paraphrase.
- **Which trunk routes are which colour.** A claim circulates that trunk-route
  green means north–south and blue means east–west — which would collide
  head-on with the feeder prefixes, where green and blue mean *metro line*.
  If true it is the best thing on this page. It is not sourced here.
- **The operators themselves**, their histories and their cross-holdings.
- **Fares, transfer discounts and concessions.**
- **The garages.**
