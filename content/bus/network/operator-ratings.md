Since 民國81年 (1992), Taipei has graded its joint-operation bus operators
twice a year on a formal service-quality evaluation — Taiwan's real
equivalent to the kind of published operator scorecard English-language
transit writing associates with Singapore, and effectively invisible
outside Chinese-language sources[^pto-113-1]. The current regulation, in
force since 1 September 2022, scores five categories: station facilities
and service; vehicle equipment and safety; passenger service quality and
driver management; accessibility across all of the above; and company
operations and management[^ly-fl055087]. Grades run 優等 (≥90), 甲等
(80–<90), 乙等 (70–<80), 丙等 (60–<70) and 丁等 (<60)[^ly-fl055087]. The exact
26 named indicators this site's brief refers to could not be sourced this
pass — the regulation's own indicator attachment table returned HTTP 404
on every URL tried, so the indicator list itself remains TBC.

## Grades are not a formality — they cost or win routes

A separate city regulation ties evaluation results directly to which
operator gets to run which route: 路線積分 (route points), assigned per
period from the evaluation score, are the reference the city uses both to
reallocate joint-operation routes and to decide the order in which
operators may choose a route when one comes up for
(re)allocation[^ly-fl055169]. See
[the joint-operation network](/bus/network/joint-operation/) for the full
mechanism. The exact number of route-points a given grade earns under the
*current* regulation is TBC — a predecessor, repealed rule gave 10/6/3
points to the top three scorers per period, but that formula does not
necessarily carry over.

The grades genuinely move: Sinbus (欣欣客運) went from 乙等 in 111年第2期 to
優等 in both 113年 rounds[^dot-111-2] [^pto-113-1] [^pto-113-2]. Small-fleet
operators are sometimes excluded from ranking outright and sometimes given
an actual grade — Dayou received a stated 乙等 in both 113年 rounds rather
than being excluded, while three other small operators were excluded in
111年第2期[^dot-111-2] [^pto-113-1] [^pto-113-2]. No source found states the
exact fleet-size threshold that decides which treatment applies.

## New Taipei runs its own, separate scheme

New Taipei's Department of Transportation maintains an entirely separate
evaluation, archived back to 2012[^ntpc-zone]. Its 113年第1期 results name
five 優等 operators — Taipei, Metropolitan, Capital, Sanchong and Taoyuan
Bus — with Taipei Bus and Capital Bus noted as having achieved 優等 for
16 consecutive years[^ntpc-113-1]. **This is not a republication of
Taipei's results.** Taoyuan Bus appears on New Taipei's 優等 list but on
neither Taipei list for the same nominal period, while Southeast, Sinbus,
Chungshing and Danan appear on Taipei's lists but not New Taipei's,
despite operating in both cities[^ntpc-113-1] [^pto-113-1] [^pto-113-2].
No source found explains the jurisdictional scoping — plausibly each city
evaluates only the portion of a shared operator's fleet or routes within
its own boundary, but that is inference rather than a sourced claim.
