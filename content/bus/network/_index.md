The page this folder exists for is an explainer, not a listing: how 聯營公車
works — who sets fares, who owns the buses, what the city controls and what
the companies do — and how the route numbering system encodes direction
suffixes, short-workings, branches, trunk routes and minibus services in ways
no English source explains. **One of those pages is now written** — see
[the joint-operation network](/bus/network/joint-operation/). Writing it cost
this section most of what it thought it knew. Several claims that had been
sitting in research notes did not survive their first check against the
regulator: the numeric bands turned out to have a competing and better-sourced
explanation, the feeder-colour table was missing a line, and the revenue-pooling
model that was supposed to be the whole point of the page is not described by
the office that runs the system. What is published is what survived those
checks.

A second page covers the national programme reshaping what those buses run
on: [the 2030 electrification programme](/bus/network/electric-bus-programme/),
a NT$64.3 billion plan[^ly-doc-electrification] the Legislative Yuan's own
oversight documents record as running well behind its own interim
targets[^ly-doc-electrification]. A third covers how the joint-operation
committee decides who runs the routes it has just described:
[operator service ratings](/bus/network/operator-ratings/), a twice-yearly
graded evaluation running since 1992[^pto-113-1] that ties directly into
the route-points mechanism above[^pto-113-1].
