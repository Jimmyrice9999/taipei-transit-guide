行政院 approved MOTC's 2030年客運車輛電動化推動計畫 on 26 May 2023, running
民國113–119年 (2024–2030) with a total budget of NT$64.327 billion, split
between the Highway Bureau (公路局, NT$45.0835 billion) and the Ministry of
Environment (環境部, NT$19.2432 billion)[^ly-doc-a]. The target is 14,500
electric buses by 2030 — 11,700 city buses and 2,800 intercity, general
highway and national-coach vehicles[^ly-doc-a]. A government-linked testing
foundation frames the programme in three phases: a completed pilot phase
(民國109–111年/2020–2022), the current rollout phase (112–115年/2023–2026),
and a universalisation phase (116–119年/2027–2030) — a breakdown not found
on any MOTC or Executive Yuan primary page checked for this
page[^artc-phases].

## The Legislative Yuan's own record: running behind target

Three Legislative Yuan committee review documents, all read in full, give a
consistent picture of a programme behind its own interim targets.

As of August 2024, city electric buses stood at 1,817 (17.31% penetration)
against a stated 2024 target of 3,300 buses at 25%[^ly-doc-b]. First-half
2024 manufacturing output was just 50 vehicles against an annual target of
1,070, with full-year capacity estimated at only 1,700–1,900
vehicles nationally[^ly-doc-b]. Purchase-subsidy execution for the year
stood at NT$412.411 million — 17.2% of the annual budget[^ly-doc-b]. Several
counties, including Keelung, Hsinchu City, Miaoli, Nantou and Penghu, showed
close to 0% penetration[^ly-doc-b].

A separate committee review, covering the same period from a different
angle, gives the 2024 target as 3,400 buses at 25% — the same percentage as
the figure above but 100 vehicles apart, with no source explaining the
difference[^ly-doc-c]. The same document sets the FY2025 (114年度) budget at
NT$6.8575 billion, split into vehicle-purchase subsidy (NT$5.92 billion),
operating subsidy (NT$797.2 million), route-network optimisation
(NT$130.8 million) and maintenance-system support (NT$9.5 million), with a
2025 target of 5,000 city buses at 35% penetration[^ly-doc-c].

## Registered fleet — several figures, several scopes

Published as separate measurements rather than one series, because they mix
different scopes (national vs. city-only) and bases (plated-and-operating,
registered, subsidy-approved, or domestically-manufactured
only)[^cna-jan2025] [^cna-oct2024] [^cnyes-dec2025], and no source bridges
them:

| Date | Figure | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| Nov 2024 | 1,926 buses nationally plated | National, all electric buses[^cna-jan2025] |
| Oct 2024 | 1,876 actual, ~4,000 projected by end-2025 | National, domestically-manufactured subset only[^cna-oct2024] |
| Dec 2025 | 4,261 buses, 40.8% penetration | National[^cnyes-dec2025] |

The most recent figure, from December 2025, quotes MOTC on charging
infrastructure: 13,482 public chargers nationally, an overall
vehicle-to-charger ratio of 8.8:1 and a fast-charge ratio of 35:1, both
stated as exceeding EU-recommended ratios of 10:1 and 80:1
respectively[^cnyes-dec2025].

## Gaps

Whether the December 2025 figure of 4,261 buses/40.8% covers city buses
only — matching the "35% target" language used for that category elsewhere
— or the full range of bus categories the 14,500-vehicle 2030 target
spans[^ly-doc-a], is not stated by its source[^cnyes-dec2025] and remains an
open scope question. A
government statistics-portal query for a clean year-by-year registered-fleet
series was not run for this page; only the ad hoc figures above, from news
reports and Legislative Yuan documents, are available.
