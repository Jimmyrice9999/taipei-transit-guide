## Historical profile

Citiair Bus Co., Ltd. is the English rendering used here for 大有巴士股份有限公司, the legal name displayed on the operator's own site [^citiair-history]. Citiair's history page dates its founding to 24 March 1969 and places its early headquarters near Nanjing East Road and Guangfu North Road [^citiair-history]. Before Taipei's joint-operation bus system, the company says its city-bus service was concentrated in Nangang, Songshan and the part of Xinyi separated from Songshan [^citiair-history].

The same page says the operating headquarters is now in Wenshan District, with management stations in Xinzhuang, New Taipei, and Nangang, Taipei [^citiair-history]. The fetched primary material does not publish the opening date, capacity or route allocation of any of those sites, so they remain operating-site statements rather than a complete depot inventory [^citiair-history][^pto-citiair-114-1]. It also does not establish a current parent group or controlling shareholder; that relationship remains TBC rather than being inferred from the company's branding or from unrelated bus-group pages [^citiair-history][^citiair-fleet][^thb-citiair].

## Fleet character

The operator's fleet page presents a dated brand-and-assignment snapshot rather than a full register. It lists 2019 Daewoo/大金龍 and 2023 Volvo entries for Taoyuan Airport bus service, plus Taiwan Yutong entries from 2016 and 2017, a 2020 Cheng Yun entry and Daewoo/大金龍 entries from 2020 and 2024 for Taipei joint-operation buses [^citiair-fleet]. The page does not state chassis/body combinations, capacities, propulsion totals or an electric/hybrid procurement programme [^citiair-fleet].

One vehicle is documented in greater detail by the TTSB's factual report: FAB-978, a Taiwan Yutong 6128HG with 25 seats and 24 standing places, manufactured by Taiwan Yutong and maintained by Citiair [^ttsb-citiair]. That vehicle record is evidence for a specific bus, not a claim that every current Citiair bus has the same model or layout [^ttsb-citiair].

The source pages do not say whether 212夜, 88區 or other variants are folded into a displayed parent label [^citiair-routes][^tdx-bus-operator-pull].

Taipei's 2012 chronology records that the city recovered Citiair's 307 route operating right on 16 March and arranged a three-month temporary succession by Taipei Bus; it records further three-month continuations in June and September [^pto-citiair-101]. The city's 110 committee minutes describe the route as having been recovered because Citiair's operation was poor, under the Road Act and related rules, and say that the successor would be subject to twice-yearly single-route evaluation [^pto-citiair-307]. This is a documented route-contract and regulatory event, not a complete history of every route transfer [^pto-citiair-101][^pto-citiair-307].

## Evaluation and incidents

The Taipei Public Transportation Office's 114 first-period and second-period result sheets both grade Citiair 乙 and list its Wenshan address and 02-8663-9000 contact [^pto-citiair-114-1][^pto-citiair-114-2]. Those grades are for Taipei city-bus evaluation. The Highway Bureau's separate highway-passenger record lists grades 乙 for 112, 109 and 107 and 甲 for 104, alongside eight historical incident rows including collisions and fires [^thb-citiair]. The two evaluation series measure different operating scopes and are both retained [^pto-citiair-114-1][^pto-citiair-114-2][^thb-citiair].

The TTSB factual report records that a Citiair 212 bus struck a Metropolitan Bus 49 bus near Zhenjiang Street at 09:05 on 17 November 2024 [^ttsb-citiair]. The chain collision killed one pedestrian, seriously injured one passenger on the Metropolitan bus and lightly injured 15 occupants [^ttsb-citiair]. The report's FAB-978 vehicle details and injury totals are presented as a sourced factual record; they are not used to infer a fleet-wide failure rate or a final legal conclusion [^ttsb-citiair].
