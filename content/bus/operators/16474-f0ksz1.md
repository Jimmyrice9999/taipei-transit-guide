## What this record is — and is not

The committed TDX pull identifies `bus-operator-16474-f0ksz1` as 大有巴士, assigns it to NewTaipei, and gives it zero associated route records [^tdx-bus-operator-pull]. That is a real data-layer record, but it is not evidence that the legal company operates no buses anywhere [^tdx-bus-operator-pull]. A separate Taipei TDX operator record uses the same Chinese name, so the two records are kept distinct rather than silently combined [^tdx-bus-operator-pull].

The operator's own company page uses “CitiAir Bus Co.” in English and says that the company was founded on 24 March 1969, before Taipei's bus network entered joint operation [^dayou-history]. Its historical account places the early service area around Nangang, Songshan and the part of Xinyi that developed from Songshan [^dayou-history]. The same page says the current operating headquarters is in Wenshan and that management stations are maintained in Xinzhuang and Nangang [^dayou-history]. Those are published operating sites, not a complete depot register: the page gives no opening dates, capacities, route allocation or ownership of the sites [^dayou-history].

The fetched primary pages do not establish a parent group, shareholder control structure, merger chronology or route-contract history for Dàyou [^dayou-history][^tdx-bus-operator-pull]. The current company page describes a “new team” and modern management, but it does not name a holding company or identify a legal merger [^dayou-history].

## Fleet and services

The operator's fleet page divides the displayed vehicles into highway passenger and city passenger groups [^dayou-fleet]. It lists King Long highway vehicles from 2019 and Volvo vehicles from 2023 for airport-bus work [^dayou-fleet]. For city service it lists Taiwan Yutong vehicles from 2016 and 2017, AEBUS vehicles from 2020, and King Long vehicles from 2020 and 2024 [^dayou-fleet]. The page does not give chassis codes, body builders beyond the displayed brands, seating/standing capacity, fleet totals, or an electric-versus-hybrid split, so those fields remain TBC [^dayou-fleet].

Taipei's Public Transportation Office recorded a dated accessibility deployment on 23 December 2016: Dàyou and San Chung Bus put 37 low-floor buses into service across the named joint-operation route group [^dayou-lowfloor]. The notice says the vehicles carried wheelchair spaces, restraint belts, emergency intercoms, stop announcements and passenger-information displays [^dayou-lowfloor]. It is evidence of a 2016 low-floor deployment, not a current fleet count or proof that every vehicle on the separate TDX record has the same equipment [^dayou-lowfloor].

The company's published airport-service page describes Route 1961 as running between central Taipei, Nankan and Taoyuan Airport, with direct and commuter variants, a 90–120 minute stated travel range, fare bands by segment and an accessible-vehicle note [^dayou-1961]. That page is an operator service page and is not used to hand-list routes for the zero-association New Taipei record; the route section above deliberately remains generated from TDX and therefore contains no route [^tdx-bus-operator-pull][^dayou-1961].

## Regulatory record

The Highway Bureau's full operator record places Dàyou under the Taipei Motor Vehicles Office for highway passenger transport and displays a 乙 evaluation for ROC year 112 [^dayou-thb-record]. Its accident table records dated collisions and fires, including a 2025-05-27 rear-end collision with one injury, a 2024-01-10 vehicle fire with no casualties and a 2023-05-02 rear-end collision with one injury [^dayou-thb-record]. Earlier rows include a 2013 Taipei bus-lane collision with eight injuries and two fire incidents on national highways [^dayou-thb-record]. These entries are retained as the regulator's displayed highway record; they are not generalized into a city-bus safety rate [^dayou-thb-record]. The same agency's directory lists the company in Banqiao with telephone 02-8663-9000, while the operator page publishes the Wenshan office and the 0800 hotline [^dayou-thb-directory][^dayou-history].

The mismatch is therefore part of the record's subject, not a hole to fill with invented service: TDX's New Taipei operator row has no bus route, while the company and responsible authorities document a wider Dàyou/CitiAir operating history [^tdx-bus-operator-pull][^dayou-history].
