## History and legal record

The Zhongxing Group’s company history says Zhinan Bus began as 指南遊覽汽車股份有限公司 on 12 February 1953, with two tour buses operating between Zhinan Temple and Muzha to serve temple visitors [^zhinan-history]. It records a May 1966 reorganisation as Zhinan Bus and approval for a Taiwan Province Tamsui branch in December 1974 [^zhinan-history]. The same page describes another reorganisation on 1 September 1996 and says the company then pursued station and equipment improvements, but it does not publish a merger agreement or a percentage ownership change [^zhinan-history].

The current MOEA record identifies 指南汽車客運股份有限公司 by company number 27043501 and marks it 核准設立 [^zhinan-company]. It gives a registered date of 1 June 1954, registered address in Tamsui, paid-in capital of NT$611,007,490, total authorised capital of NT$700,000,000 and representative 呂奇峯 [^zhinan-company]. These statutory dates are kept separate from the group history’s 1953 predecessor date and 1966 reorganisation date [^zhinan-history] [^zhinan-company].

The MOEA record lists highway passenger transport, urban passenger transport and tour-bus passenger transport among the company’s registered business categories, alongside vehicle repair and other activities [^zhinan-company]. Its current board table shows the chair represented by Tamsui Bus, with other listed corporate representatives being Tamsui Asset Management, Red Forest Advertising and King Long Automobile Manufacturing [^zhinan-company]. That is direct evidence of the current registered board representation; the fetched record does not establish a complete parent-company shareholding chart [^zhinan-company].

The same record lists the Taiwan Province Tamsui branch as 廢止, with an approval date of 1 November 1974 and last change date of 27 December 2010 [^zhinan-company]. This is a branch-registration result, not evidence that the legal company or its current municipal services ceased operating [^zhinan-company].

## Network, sites and fleet evidence

The two committed TDX records are municipality-scoped snapshots for the same operator code: the New Taipei record carries 34 route associations and the Taipei record carries 29 [^tdx-bus-operator-pull]. The page keeps those lists generated from the committed route data, so the association counts are not presented as a unique-route total or as a hand-maintained roster [^tdx-bus-operator-pull].

The group’s official station directory publishes 14 Zhinan Bus site entries, including sites in Wenshan, Xindian, Tamsui, Shulin, Wugu, Shilin, Taishan, Shenkeng, Xinyi, Taoyuan and Bali [^zhinan-sites]. It publishes addresses and phone numbers but no opening dates, capacities or route-allocation table, so those depot fields remain TBC [^zhinan-sites].

A full June 2021 operator schedule-adjustment PDF shows Zhinan’s New Taipei services as a mix of local, cross-district and joint-operation work during the COVID-19 reduction period [^zhinan-service-2021]. The document explicitly marks shared headways with Kuang-Hua Bus on one route and with Zhongxing Bus on two others, while another route is marked as jointly operated with Tamsui Bus [^zhinan-service-2021]. This is dated operational evidence, not a claim that those 2021 headways or partnerships still describe every current route [^zhinan-service-2021].

New Taipei’s 2021 vehicle-art programme included a Zhinan Bus 880 vehicle among the 15 city-bus vehicles supplied by operators for safety-themed livery [^ntpc-zhinan-art]. The announcement identifies the route and operator but does not give a chassis, body, capacity or propulsion model [^ntpc-zhinan-art]. A model-level fleet roster and current diesel/electric/hybrid split therefore remain TBC [^ntpc-zhinan-art].

## Evaluation, safety and regulatory record

New Taipei’s announcement for the 110 second-period service-quality evaluation lists Zhinan Bus among the seven operators receiving 優等 [^ntpc-zhinan-eval-110]. The announcement says the evaluation covered station facilities and service, vehicle equipment and safety, passenger service and driver management, accessibility, and company management [^ntpc-zhinan-eval-110].

The National Audit Office’s review records eight labour-law penalties against Zhinan Bus between 111 and 113, with cumulative fines of NT$5,020,000 across New Taipei and Taipei authorities [^zhinan-oversight]. The same review reproduces New Taipei’s response that eight Zhinan electric buses were among 18 buses of the same model stopped after a battery-fire incident involving Tamsui Bus, pending a qualified battery inspection before re-entry [^zhinan-oversight]. The audit record establishes the safety action and regulatory finding; it does not identify the vehicle model in the quoted table or establish the current disposition of those vehicles [^zhinan-oversight].
