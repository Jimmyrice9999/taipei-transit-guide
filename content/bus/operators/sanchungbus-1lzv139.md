This is a data record, not a claim that every TDX record with the same company name is a separate legal entity [^tdx-bus-operator-pull].

## Company history and identity

The operator's company profile dates its creation to 7 May 1968, when it began highway passenger transport with 20 vehicles [^sanchung-about]. It says the business added tour-bus rental in 1970, joined Taipei's joint-operation bus system on 30 April 1977, and received approval in 1996 to operate the Taipei–Hsinchu intercity route [^sanchung-about]. A government employment record gives the company a different establishment date, 2 April 1968, while agreeing on the legal name 三重汽車客運股份有限公司, representative 李博文 and business number 35025506 [^taiwanjobs-company]. Both dates are published here because they are different records, not because one has been silently treated as definitive [^sanchung-about][^taiwanjobs-company].

The same government record lists NT$190 million in capital, 1,300 employees and highway passenger transport plus large-bus rental as the business categories [^taiwanjobs-company]. The Highway Bureau's operator directory lists 三重客運 at the Sanhe Road address and gives 02-29882133 [^thb-operator-directory]. The TDX pull remains the source for this page's municipality label and generated route associations; it does not establish ownership, a parent company, fleet models or contract history [^tdx-bus-operator-pull].

## Operating footprint and facilities

The operator describes a near-1,000-bus commercial fleet, about 21,335 ping of parking area, about 28.15 ping per vehicle and approximately 109,236 vehicle-kilometres per day [^sanchung-about]. Its stated service area covers Taipei, much of New Taipei, and parts of Guishan, Taoyuan and Hsinchu [^sanchung-about]. That is an operator-published operating snapshot, not a current fleet count inferred from route pages.

The company profile names 16 dispatch sites: Luzhou 1 and 2, Xinzhuang, Zhonggang, Tucheng, Huilong, Bali, Wugu, Taishan, Shulin, Tamhai, Neihu, Nangang, Gongxi, Linkou and the Jiou intercity-bus terminal [^sanchung-about]. It also says that two maintenance yards in Luzhou and Xinzhuang occupy more than 1,000 ping in total, and that five sites have large automatic bus washers while other sites have semi-automatic washers [^sanchung-about]. The source gives neither a capacity nor an opening date for any site, so those fields remain TBC rather than being inferred from the generated route associations.

## Fleet transition and service record

New Taipei's transport department records 13 Model T electric buses entering service on San Chung Bus route 857 on 19 September 2023 [^ntpc-electric-857]. The release describes surround-view cameras, inner-wheel-difference warnings, electronic mirrors and passenger-facing route-request and stop-display equipment; it also records a municipal subsidy of NT$1.5 million per bus [^ntpc-electric-857]. A complete model roster remains TBC because the operator profile gives only a near-1,000-bus scale and the structured TDX record has no model field [^sanchung-about][^tdx-bus-operator-pull].

The Highway Bureau's 2015 evaluation reports two San Chung Bus results: 81.87, grade 甲, in its general-road table and 79.87, grade 乙, in its national-road table [^thb-evaluation-104]. They measure different service categories and are both retained. A New Taipei police release names San Chung Bus as one of four companies in the public-facing 首都客運集團 label, alongside Capital Bus, Taipei Bus and Metropolitan Bus; that statement does not by itself establish a statutory parent relationship [^ntpc-police-group].
