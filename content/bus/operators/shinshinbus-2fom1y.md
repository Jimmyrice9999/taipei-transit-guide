## Company history and ownership transition

Shin-Shin’s official company page says the business was jointly established in ROC year 58, January 1969, by Yulon Motor, Rongqiao and the Veterans Affairs Council [^shin-official-about]. Taipei PTO’s history separately records the late-1960s expansion of private operators and the 1977 joint-operation system, providing the public-transport context without replacing the company’s own founding account [^pto-bus-history]. The operator page also says Yulon’s shareholding was transferred in batches in July 2015 and acquired by the Chung Hsing and Capital groups [^shin-official-about]. That is an operator-published ownership-history statement, not a substitute for a current share register; the page does not establish a statutory parent after that transaction.

The MOEA record identifies 欣欣客運股份有限公司 as unified business number 18739212, with paid-in capital of NT$519,002,790, representative Zhang Yuan-xun and a registered address on Tingzhou Road in Taipei’s Zhongzheng District [^moea-shinshin]. MOEA gives 18 May 1969 as the approved establishment date [^moea-shinshin]. The operator’s January founding statement and the statutory May date are retained together because they describe different dated records.

## Network, fleet and facilities

The committed Taipei TDX record associates 57 routes with ShinshinBus, and the route section below is generated from those associations [^tdx-bus-operator-pull]. The operator’s own profile uses a wider description, saying it serves Taipei and New Taipei across more than 70 routes [^shin-official-about]. Those figures are not averaged: one is a municipality-scoped TDX snapshot, while the other is the company’s broader service statement.

The same official profile says the organization has five first-level units, a repair plant and nine operating stations, more than 700 employees, and 415 large, medium and low-floor buses [^shin-official-about]. It also describes charter rental, small-vehicle maintenance, body and paint work, and vehicle inspection contracted by Taipei’s motor-vehicle office [^shin-official-about]. No manufacturer/model, chassis/body or electric/hybrid breakdown is published there, so the page does not turn the broad 415-bus figure into an invented model roster [^shin-official-about].

The station cards name Shenkeng, Zhongxing, Muzha, Central, Jingde, Huajiang, Donghu, Jingxin and Muzha 1, plus a repair shop at Muzha Road Section 4, Lane 157-1 [^shin-official-about]. The cards publish addresses but no opening dates, vehicle capacities or route-allocation table [^shin-official-about]. MOEA separately lists two historical repair-factory entries, one later closed and another later revoked; those records are not silently treated as the current repair shop [^moea-shinshin].

## Regulatory record

New Taipei’s 111(2) evaluation report gives Shin-Shin 79.10 points and grade 乙等, after 甲等 in both 110(2) and 111(1) [^ntpc-shinshin-eval]. This is a dated service-quality result and does not establish a fleet-wide defect, current legal status or a cause for the change in grade [^ntpc-shinshin-eval].

The profile therefore has a sourced founding and ownership transition, a broad fleet statement and named operating facilities, while its model-level fleet, site capacities and route-contract history remain TBC. Those gaps would require a fleet register, procurement/subsidy records and depot or contract files.
