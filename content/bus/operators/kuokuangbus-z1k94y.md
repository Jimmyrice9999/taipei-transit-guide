## Legal identity and operating character

Kuo-Kuang Motor Transportation is the English rendering used here for 國光汽車客運股份有限公司. The Ministry of Economic Affairs registry identifies the company by unified business number 70810572, records it as approved, and gives 15 June 2001 as its approved-establishment date [^moea-kuokuang]. The same statutory record names 王雅萱 as representative, gives the registered address as 1F, No. 53, Section 4, Chongyang Road, Sanchong District, New Taipei City, and records NT$465 million in paid-in capital [^moea-kuokuang]. Its registered activities cover highway passenger transport, city-bus passenger transport and sightseeing-bus passenger transport, alongside parking, vehicle repair and other ancillary activities [^moea-kuokuang].

The registry lists corporate representatives on the current board from construction, vehicle-manufacturing and investment companies, but it does not identify a single parent group [^moea-kuokuang]. That distinction matters because a board representation list is evidence of the registered governance arrangement, not proof of a consolidated operating group [^moea-kuokuang].

The company’s self-authored profile gives a different date layer: it says operations began on 1 July 2001, and describes the business around safety and quality, professional efficiency and comfortable service [^kingbus-profile]. The profile also describes national intercity service, online and voice ticketing, maintenance capability, parking management, city-bus service and a sightseeing-bus division said to have been established in 2008 [^kingbus-profile]. Its more-than-860-bus and more-than-96-route figures, together with an average daily passenger figure tied explicitly to 2005, are retained as a dated historical snapshot rather than a current fleet census [^kingbus-profile].

## New Taipei network and electric buses

The committed TDX record is municipality-scoped. It associates Kuo-Kuang with ten New Taipei route records, including the 泰山 and 五股 corridors into Taipei, the 五股–榮總 service, and the rural 雙溪 services [^tdx-bus-operator-pull]. The route list below is generated from the committed operator associations, not hand-maintained prose [^tdx-bus-operator-pull].

New Taipei’s transport bureau records the operating transition behind three of the city routes: Kuo-Kuang took over routes converted from the New Bus system from July 2020 [^ntpc-kuokuang-electric]. By October 2021, routes 582, 578 and 579 had been fully converted to electric operation, with 30 electric buses reported across the three routes and seven assigned to route 579 [^ntpc-kuokuang-electric]. The notice describes route 579 as the 明志國小–臺北車站 service and identifies the 578 and 582 departure points at 泰山公有市場 and 五股立體停車場 [^ntpc-kuokuang-electric]. This establishes a dated electric-bus programme, but not a current manufacturer, chassis or complete propulsion roster [^ntpc-kuokuang-electric].

## Sites, fleet evidence and evaluation

The Highway Bureau’s operator directory places the legal company address in Sanchong and gives the public telephone number 02-23810731 [^thb-operator-list]. The MOEA page exposes one ancillary warehouse entry, 國光汽車客運埔里站(倉庫), in Puli, but explicitly warns that the warehouse section is cross-domain reference data rather than statutory company-registration data [^moea-kuokuang]. No fetched source publishes a New Taipei depot opening date, capacity or route-allocation table, so those fields remain TBC [^moea-kuokuang].

New Taipei’s city-bus evaluations show a dated sequence rather than a single timeless grade. Kuo-Kuang received 甲等 in the 112 first-period result [^ntpc-kuokuang-eval-112]. It received 乙等 in both the 113 first-period and 113 second-period results [^ntpc-kuokuang-eval-113-1][^ntpc-kuokuang-eval-113-2]. The Highway Bureau separately lists Kuo-Kuang as a New Taipei sightseeing-bus operator with 甲 for 112 and 優 for 111, and the displayed accident table says 查無資料 [^thb-kuokuang-tour]. The sightseeing-bus result is not substituted for the city-bus evaluation because the business categories and assessment systems differ [^thb-kuokuang-tour][^ntpc-kuokuang-eval-113-2].

There is also a documented national route-succession event outside this New Taipei snapshot. The Highway Bureau says that, from 8 September 2025, Ubus took over eight southern intercity routes after Kuo-Kuang applied to suspend them for operating reasons [^motc-kuokuang-succession]. That notice records a specific transfer and passenger-service continuity; it does not rewrite the ten New Taipei associations in the TDX pull [^motc-kuokuang-succession][^tdx-bus-operator-pull].
