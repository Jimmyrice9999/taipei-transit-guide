Six imported brands supply the chassis for Taiwan's highway coaches,
charter/tour coaches and part of its city-bus fleet — Taiwanese
coachbuilders construct the bodies. Only one of the six, MAN, has a
confirmed public government type-approval document trail; the rest were
searched for and not found on the one relevant VSCC page located,
"大客車底盤重心位置圖"[^vscc-man-cog]. Whether that absence means the
records exist elsewhere, or simply are not published in a searchable list
the way the electric-bus programme's are, is unresolved.

## Hino

和泰汽車 (Hotai Motor) has been Hino's Taiwan distributor since
1952[^wiki-hotai], and its joint venture 國瑞汽車 (Kuozui Motors,
Toyota 65%/Hotai 30%/Hino 5%, founded 1984) assembles Hino buses
domestically[^wiki-hotai]. 長源汽車 (Chang Yuan Motor) is Hotai Group's
dealer specialising in Hino/Toyota commercial vehicles, with 17 branches
nationwide[^cymotor-about]. Hotai's current live product site lists two
large-bus chassis: **RK2ARXA-PJF** (A05C-TL engine, 260ps, 14,500kg GVW)
and **RM1ASXU** (a 360-horsepower class, A09C-VN engine, 18,000kg
GVW)[^hino-tw-site]. A low-floor city-bus chassis, the HS8J (Taiwan's
first Japanese-brand low-floor bus, launched late 2015) and its
Euro-6 successor HS2A (2020), does not currently appear in the live
site's navigation — whether discontinued or a rendering limit on this
research pass's fetch is unresolved.

## Isuzu

台北合眾汽車有限公司 is the current distributor, though sources conflict on
when its agency began: 2014 per the distributor's own "About"
page[^isuzu-about], or 2012 per an independent account that also gives a
fuller history — first Taiwan exports in 1952, domestic manufacture
1996–2007 halted after the then-manufacturer's parent bank collapsed in a
2007 fraud scandal[^isuzu-tw-history]. Current chassis, fetched directly
from the live site: **NQR(Bus) 8.5t** (4HK1 engine, 190PS,
Euro-5-equivalent without urea injection) and **LT Bus 14.5t**[^isuzu-tw-site].
No full-size route-bus or highway-coach Isuzu model (Erga, Gala, Journey)
was found sold or deployed in Taiwan.

## Mercedes-Benz

裕益汽車股份有限公司, part of 順益集團 (Shun Yi Group, founded 1967), is the
general distributor for large Mercedes-Benz buses[^yuyi-profile]. The
**O500** coach (OM457 engine, 428ps/315kW, MB GO240-8 transmission)
launched in 2018[^shunyi-history] [^yuyi-o500]. The **OC500** — Daimler's
first bus designed specifically for Taiwan, chassis and body together,
locally assembled — launched 10 March 2021 in 33/35/36/45-seat
configurations[^yuyi-oc500]; its power/torque figures are numerically
identical to the O500's despite a different engine (OM470) and a
different peak-power rpm, an unresolved peculiarity in the source, not
smoothed over here. Deployment is charter/tour/contract work: 文境通運旅遊
集團 received the 500th OC500 delivered under this programme on
21 February 2025, operating 312 vehicles for hospital, government,
corporate and school shuttle and rental contracts[^yuyi-500th]. No source
found names a Mercedes-Benz bus in fixed public-route service.

## Volvo

太古商用汽車 (Taikoo Motors Group) has distributed Volvo trucks and buses
in Taiwan since 1978[^taikoo-distributor]. 大吉汽車 (Daji Motors, founded
1975) has been Volvo's sole certified Taiwan coachbuilder since 2014,
currently building the New B8R, B8RLE and All New B13R[^daji-about]; its
**B11R** (Euro 6, 460hp) uses a Volvo D11 engine and 12-speed I-Shift
transmission[^daji-b11r]. Separately, 唐榮車輛科技 (Tang Eng Vehicle
Technology — see its unrelated electric-bus "Alighter" on
[its own page](/bus/models/tang-eng-alighter/)) was licensed by Volvo in
2004 as its 16th global assembly plant, building roughly 2,000 Volvo
trucks and buses at Luzhu, Kaohsiung under a 1,350-unit contract that
ended November 2011[^ctwant-tangeng-volvo]; Tang Eng's own site has been
unreachable across two independent research passes.

**A conflict, published not resolved**: Daji's own client list names
國光客運 (Kuo-Kuang Motor Transport) as a Volvo-body recipient, and
zh.wikipedia's article on Daji repeats this[^wiki-daji]. But
zh.wikipedia's own article on 國光客運, as of its stated December 2024
fleet snapshot, lists only Daewoo models and makes no mention of
Volvo[^wiki-kuokuang]. Neither source explains the discrepancy.

## Scania

永德福汽車 is Scania's current Taiwan distributor, confirmed on Scania
Taiwan's own site[^scania-tw-home]. An unfootnoted wiki account states
Scania took back general-agency rights from a previous distributor at the
end of 2005 and invested NT$600 million building 10 service centres from
2006[^wiki-scania]. **K400IB4X2NB** and **K380IB4X2NB** coaches are used
by 統聯客運 (Tonglian/Ubus)'s dedicated tour-coach subsidiary, which
reportedly bought 30 Scania units in 2012 — but this rests on an
unfootnoted wiki claim not corroborated by the operator's own site, which
names no vehicle brand at all[^wiki-ubus].

## MAN

德曼汽車集團 (Deman Auto Group) has held exclusive Taiwan distribution for
MAN Truck & Bus since 1 July 2018, achieving second place in Type-A coach
sales within its first half-year and 400 units sold within eighteen
months[^chatcv-man-interview]. MAN is the only one of these six brands
with confirmed public Taiwan government type-approval documents found
this pass: the **19.400** and **19.510** coach chassis both carry VSCC
centre-of-gravity certification documents[^vscc-man-cog]. Deman's coach
subsidiary, 德揚實業, markets four MAN-based body/finish variants under
Taiwanese coachbuilder names, though no engine or seating specification is
given on its own marketing pages[^sfman-bus].
