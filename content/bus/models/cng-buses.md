Taiwan's bus fleets never adopted compressed natural gas (CNG,
壓縮天然氣) at any scale this site could find evidence of. The one
documented episode is small, undated beyond its year, and rests on a
single source.

## The one documented pilot

Taipei introduced six CNG buses in 1999 and built a slow-fill refuelling
station in Neihu[^artc-cng-article]. The source is a 2009 technical
article by the Automotive Research & Testing Center (ARTC), a
government-linked vehicle-testing body, reporting on the city's
programme rather than being the city's own record of it:

> 台北市於民國88年引進6輛壓縮天然氣公車，並且在台北內湖蓋了一座慢速加氣站，但因為車輛保養不易、巡航距離較短、加氣站太少且加氣速度過慢、天然氣品質不穩定等因素導致計畫最後宣告失敗[^artc-cng-article]。

The programme is described as a failure, for five stated reasons:
vehicles were hard to maintain, cruising range was short, refuelling
stations were too few and too slow, and gas quality was inconsistent[^artc-cng-article].
No operator, manufacturer, chassis brand or route number is named
anywhere this source or any other found — the six-vehicle figure and the
Neihu station are the whole of what is documented. A search across
zh.wikipedia's general Taipei bus articles, the Taipei Environmental
Protection Bureau's own published history timeline, and Taiwanese news
archives found nothing that names this programme independently of the
ARTC article, so the claim is carried at Medium confidence rather than
confirmed by a second source.

The same ARTC article, on its own institutional work rather than
Taipei's programme, states its diesel-engine laboratory had tested
converting diesel engines to run on CNG conversion kits and compared
emissions against unconverted diesel, with a liquefied-natural-gas
test rig planned to help domestic manufacturers localise conversion
kits[^artc-cng-article]. This frames CNG in Taiwan's bus context as a
retrofit technology applied to existing diesel chassis, not a
purpose-built model — which is consistent with no CNG entry existing
in any vehicle type-approval register found.

## No type-approval trail

The Vehicle Safety Certification Center (VSCC), the body responsible for
large-bus type-approval in Taiwan, publishes no CNG-specific qualified-
model list. Its coach-chassis registry index names exactly three document
sets, none referencing natural gas, and its only other large-bus register
is the electric-bus promotion programme[^vscc-coach-index]. This absence
was checked against VSCC's own published index directly, not inferred
from a search returning nothing.

## What Taiwan actually ran instead

This site has not independently verified it, so it is stated as an
unconfirmed lead rather than a fact: search results describe an
environmental-authority "油氣雙燃料車" (oil-gas dual-fuel vehicle) programme
running roughly 2008 to 2012, using LPG rather than CNG, for taxis and
light vehicles — not a bus programme. It is noted here only so the two
fuels are not conflated, which one AI-generated search summary
encountered during this research did do, wrongly attributing a taxi LPG
conversion to the CNG bus claim. This site's own
[electric bus programme](/bus/network/electric-bus-programme/) page
covers what actually reached Taiwan's city bus fleets at scale after 2020.

## What remains open

Manufacturer, chassis brand, model designation, operating end date, and
whether any of the six vehicles survive are all TBC. No Kaohsiung,
Taichung or Taoyuan CNG bus programme was found in this search, though
the search for those cities was not exhaustive.
