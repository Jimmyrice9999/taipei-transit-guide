This site's existing bus-model pages cover the current era: electric
buses since roughly 2019, and the imported diesel/coach chassis
(Hino, Isuzu, Mercedes-Benz, Volvo, Scania, MAN) on the market now. What
Taipei's fleets actually were in the 1970s through the 1990s is barely
documented anywhere in Mandarin or English, and this page reports exactly
how little survives rather than filling the gap with a plausible guess.

## The two dated purchases anyone has found

zh.wikipedia's article on public minibuses names two specific fleet
purchases for Taipei[^zh-minibus], and this site checked directly that no
other source repeats either one, including a sibling Wikipedia article on
the same general topic. Both figures are carried at Medium confidence for
that reason.

**1 July 1976**, Taipei's first air-conditioned bus route, predecessor of
today's 601, began service using 30 Mercedes-Benz **L508D** small
air-conditioned buses[^zh-minibus].

**6 September 1980**, the city added 60 Hino **BM400** small
air-conditioned buses, described as marking a "self-strengthening year"
(自強年) after the 1979 US–Taiwan derecognition — the same year saw Hino
**BY420** large air-conditioned buses (11.55 m) introduced on new
routes[^zh-minibus]. Quoted directly:

> 1980年9月6日起，添購60輛[日野]BM400型小型冷氣巴士[^zh-minibus]

Route renumbering (中1 → 601 and others) is dated to the same September
1980 changeover[^zh-minibus]. This site found nothing naming a
manufacturer, model or operator for any Taipei bus before 1976, or for
the years between these two purchases and the mid-1990s.

## The oldest specific model still findable at operator level

Enthusiast fleet-tracking pages (zh.wikiversity, uncited, treated as leads
rather than sources) name a 1995-model-year Hino **LRG2KRB** at 光華巴士 as
the oldest specific vehicle record this site found surviving for any of
the 14 聯營 operators, alongside two retired Hino models, **LRK2JMA**
(fully retired) and **LRK2JMB** (transferred to 基隆客運). None of this is
independently corroborated against a primary source and is reported here
only as the practical limit of what this site could trace.

## Domestic coachbuilders, after privatisation

Once Metropolitan Bus (大都會客運) was formed by privatising the municipal
operator in 2004, its early vehicle purchases paired **imported Isuzu
large-bus chassis with bodies built by a domestic coachbuilder, 日盈機械**,
later renamed **國際山霖汽車**[^zh-metropolitanbus]. That coachbuilder went
on to acquire shares in Metropolitan Bus from former employees — becoming
a shareholder in the operator whose buses it was bodying[^zh-metropolitanbus].

**When 國際山霖汽車 closed is not settled.** zh.wikipedia's Metropolitan Bus
article gives "late 2009" for a bankruptcy that ended the chassis-body
pairing and shifted subsequent purchases to Daewoo vehicles distributed
by 成運汽車[^zh-metropolitanbus]. An independent company-registry mirror
gives a formal dissolution date of **23 October 2013**[^twincn-shanlin].
Both are published; neither source explains the four-year gap, and this
page does not pick one — it may be the ordinary difference between
ceasing to trade and a company's legal dissolution, but no source says so.

Other domestic body-builders surface in enthusiast operator-fleet pages
(zh.wikiversity, uncited, Medium confidence at best): **健益**, bodying
大有巴士's Mitsubishi Fuso RK127JL; **豐榮**, bodying 大南汽車's Hino
ERK2JRL/ERK2JML and, separately, a Daewoo BC211MA for 指南客運; **柏昇車體**,
a body variant for 指南客運's Mitsubishi Fuso RM11FN2XC. **成運** (成運汽車)
appears repeatedly across several operators as both a Daewoo-brand
distributor and a body-builder in its own right. None of these was
independently verified beyond the single uncited fleet page naming it.

**中興巴士's own general characterisation** of its historic fleet, quoted
directly and undated: 「中興巴士採用的車輛廠牌以日本製造的日野及三菱扶桑為主」 — Hino
and Mitsubishi Fuso, mainly[^zh-zhongxingbus].

## What this site searched and found nothing

This site read China Motor Corporation (中華汽車工業)'s own Wikipedia
history, covering 1969 onward: trucks, light commercial vehicles, cars,
vans and electric motorcycles — no bus or bus chassis anywhere in the
article. Ford
Liuho (福特六和/六和汽車)'s history, from 1969, covers Toyota-then-Ford
passenger and light-commercial vehicles, and this site found no bus
connection. San Fu (三富汽車), often mentioned as a candidate domestic
manufacturer, assembled Isuzu Trooper, Isuzu Panther and Hyundai Accent
under contract before closing in 1999 — this site found no source
connecting it to bus bodies. Two 7car.tw articles that appeared, by
title, to cover early Taiwan-made coaches and the domestic body-building
process could not be reached at all (see below), so their content is not
reported here.

## What remains open

This site found nothing naming a manufacturer for any Taipei bus before
1976. The
fifteen years between the 1980 Hino purchase and the 1995 光華巴士 record
have no source at all — not a search failure to retry, a genuine absence
across zh.wikipedia, zh.wikiversity, Taiwanese news archives (聯合報 and
中國時報's 1980s archives require paid access, not checked), and the
Taipei Public Transport Office's own history page, which returned HTTP
404 at the URL found. Yulon (裕隆汽車)'s own corporate history names
"flat-nose large coaches" (平頭大客車) as an early product line with no
date, model or customer — whether this reached Taipei city-bus service is
unconfirmed. This site's search surfaced two 7car.tw articles describing
early domestic coachbuilding by title, but could not fetch either — the
whole domain returned a connection failure on every attempt.
