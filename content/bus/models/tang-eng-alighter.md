唐榮車輛科技股份有限公司 was spun off from parent Tang Eng Iron Works' road-vehicle
division in 2002, and became a Volvo-licensed commercial-vehicle assembly
plant when its Luzhu, Kaohsiung facility opened in 2004[^ctwant-teq-history].
Its electric bus, "Alighter" — called "漢唐1號" (Han-Tang No. 1) in some
2021-era coverage — was developed under a 10-company "CTP" alliance Tang Eng
led with 漢翔 (AIDC, Aerospace Industrial Development Corp.) and 大同 (Tatung,
supplying the powertrain), launched at Nangang Exhibition Center on 5 January
2022[^ltn-ctp-alliance] [^technews-alighter]. Contemporary press explicitly
framed it as a rival to the Foxconn/HIM alliance's [Model T](/bus/models/model-t-electric/)[^technews-alighter].

**Manufacturer note:** Tang Eng's own website (tangeng.com) was unreachable
for this research pass across six attempts, so every specification below
rests on news reporting of manufacturer claims, not a fetched manufacturer
primary.

## Specification, as reported

CTWANT's fetched coverage gives a 362 kWh LFP battery, liquid-cooled with an
8-year warranty; range up to 450 km; an aluminium-alloy body (6069-grade
cited) roughly 600 kg lighter than a steel equivalent; full low-floor
construction at 24 cm floor height; 34 seats; 67 dB operating noise; 0–20
km/h acceleration in 2.3 seconds; dual 12.3-inch LCD screens, a
head-up display, driver-assistance systems and over-the-air
updates[^ctwant-alighter-specs]. No overall dimensions, door count, or
motor power figure were found in any fetched source[^ctwant-alighter-specs].

**Conflict, published not resolved:** coverage of the first Guatemala export
batch gives a lower battery/range figure — "350kWh以上" and "250公里以上" — framed
as meeting an international minimum requirement rather than describing the
flagship configuration; no source states explicitly whether this is a
different trim or the same vehicle described more conservatively for an
export announcement[^ctee-guatemala] [^ctwant-alighter-specs].

## Export, not domestic VSCC qualification

The first export shipment — 20 buses to Guatemala — cleared customs 8
February 2023 and shipped two days later, arriving after a 42-day voyage on
30 March, deployed by operating partners Innovate Group and Luka Electric
Group in Antigua and Mixco[^ctee-guatemala] [^gvm-latam]. A magazine account
reports unverified order commitments of 100 buses/year from Guatemala and
200 buses/year from Paraguay, at a cited unit price of USD 450,000 — figures
not checked against any contract document[^gvm-latam].

**唐榮車輛科技 does not appear on any VSCC electric-bus qualified-model
register checked for this site** — current, lapsed-certificate, or the
earlier demonstration-programme list — see the [VSCC qualified-model
registry](/bus/models/vscc-qualified-registry/)[^vscc-list-0715]. This
directly contradicts a 2021-era secondary claim that Tang Eng was "among the
first companies to pass the electric bus demonstration programme vehicle
qualification review"[^bnext-teq-qualification]. Whether Tang Eng never
qualified domestically, or qualified and was later dropped from the register
entirely — rather than moved to the lapsed-certificate section where other
manufacturers' expired models do appear — is not explained by any source
found[^vscc-list-0715] [^bnext-teq-qualification].

## Domestic operation, and its collapse

Tang Eng operated Alighter buses in Taiwan through subsidiary 四方股份有限公司
(Four Square), founded 13 May 2015[^wiki-foursquare]. Four Square's Taichung
operation collapsed in a wage-arrears crisis in October 2023: after drivers
went unpaid, the lending bank seized 29 electric buses from the maintenance
yard[^ltn-foursquare-collapse]. A secondary wiki source separately gives a
higher figure — 47 buses seized 8 November 2023 — which is not resolved
against the 29-bus figure above; the wiki article's own supporting citations
were not independently re-checked this pass, so its figure carries lower
confidence[^wiki-foursquare]. Fleet-size figures for Four Square differ by
source and by year — roughly 68 buses across four cities on 13 routes as of
2021, against roughly 60 buses (around 20% of Taiwan's then roughly
350-vehicle national electric-bus fleet) as of 2023 — published as separate,
unreconciled figures from different points in time rather than resolved into
one trend[^ltn-foursquare-collapse].
