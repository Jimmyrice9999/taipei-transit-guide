**A note on the name.** The manufacturer's own website — in both its Chinese
and English versions — calls itself "RAC Electric Vehicles Inc." (華德動能科技
股份有限公司)[^racev-site]. No primary source found for this page uses "Tron
Energy" as the company's English name. This page uses the manufacturer's own
self-designation. It is a separate company from 鴻華先進科技 (Foxtron), the
maker of the unrelated ["Model T" electric bus](/bus/models/model-t-electric/)[^vscc-list-0715].

## Model family and VSCC type-approval history

RAC Electric Vehicles' bus family is registered with VSCC under a sequence
of type codes: RAC-700-ELCB-2800 (approved against 2021/民國110年 localisation
requirements), then RAC-700-ELCB-2802 (a separate demonstration-programme
qualification, 2022/111年 requirements), then several RAC-700-ELCB2900 type
codes (2023–2025/112–114年), and RAC-700-ELCB-3000, the newest, approved
against 2025/114年 requirements[^vscc-list-0715]. As of the most recent VSCC
snapshot (15 Jul 2026), one RAC-700-ELCB2900 entry (type code
A4916B11A02-22) is flagged as certificate-lapsed without renewal, while the
newer entries remain within the register's current section — though the
source document does not make unambiguously clear which entries the lapsed
flag covers[^vscc-list-0715]. Every current-generation battery pack is
described as compliant with UN R100.03[^vscc-list-0715].

## Current specification

The manufacturer's own product page, fetched directly, gives the RAC-700 as
12,000×2,500×3,206 mm with a 5,800 mm wheelbase, an 11 m turning radius,
13,400 kg curb weight and 16,100 kg total weight, seating 23+1 or 25
depending on configuration[^racev-spec]. It runs a permanent-magnet
synchronous motor (PMSM) rated 350 kW maximum, an LFP battery pack of
350 kWh, a 100 km/h top speed and a climbing ability above 20%[^racev-spec].
Charging is CCS1 with dual guns, reaching 60% state of charge in 15 minutes
on fast charge, or 1.5–2 hours on a normal charge[^racev-spec]. The page does
not state a range figure, door count or floor type, which remain TBC.

## Conflicting figures for earlier generations

Wikipedia's article on the RACE150 platform describes two earlier
generations with figures that do not match the current spec sheet above —
published here as a conflict, not resolved, since neither source states an
exact model-year boundary and the article's underlying footnotes were not
independently re-checked this pass[^wiki-race150] [^racev-spec]:

- **Dimensions and weight:** 11,990×2,480×3,220 mm, curb weight 12,140 kg,
  max weight 15,350 kg (Wikipedia, "2011–2017" generation)[^wiki-race150]
  against the current 12,000×2,500×3,206 mm, 13,400 kg, 16,100 kg
  above[^racev-spec]. The 5,800 mm wheelbase is the one figure that agrees
  across both[^wiki-race150] [^racev-spec].
- **Battery:** 250 kWh (Wikipedia, "2011–2017"); 282 kWh, ternary lithium
  (Wikipedia, "2018+")[^wiki-race150]; 350 kWh, LFP (current spec sheet,
  corroborated by a 2026 news report of a "newly launched" 350 kWh dual-gun
  fast-charge model)[^racev-spec] [^udn-h1-deliveries].
- **Motor:** "Fulton Electric" AC air-cooled, 150 kW (Wikipedia,
  "2011–2017"); Tatung (大同) water-cooled PMSM/SRPM, 189 kW (Wikipedia,
  "2018+")[^wiki-race150]; PMSM, 350 kW max (current spec sheet)[^racev-spec].

## Production scale

A 2026 news report states first-half deliveries of 189 buses, approaching
the prior full year's 205, with cumulative production nearing 1,000 buses
across 12 counties and cities, and a roughly 15% share of Japan's
electric-bus export market[^udn-h1-deliveries]. Deployment history by
operator, and confirmation of which VSCC type code corresponds to which
delivered configuration, remain TBC.
