The Taipei Small Rehabilitation Bus is a distinct accessible-mobility
programme, not an ordinary city-bus route. Taipei's Public Transportation
Office describes it as point-to-point, door-to-door transport that accepts
advance reservations and classifies service priority by disability category.[^taipei-rehab-booking]
The guide therefore keeps it in the eligibility-limited lane of the community
transport atlas rather than adding it to the national fixed-route bus count.[^taipei-rehab-booking]

The standard service area is the Taipei joint-operation bus service area, with
one endpoint required to be inside Taipei.[^taipei-rehab-booking] The same page
describes the standard fare as one-third of Taipei taxi fare. A shared trip is
charged using the published 66% discounted-mileage rule; highway toll treatment
is stated separately and is not folded into the fare formula.[^taipei-rehab-booking]

The ordinary service window is described as 06:00 until 23:00 at the passenger's
planned destination.[^taipei-rehab-booking] Taipei also publishes a limited late-night extension from
23:00 to 06:00, with ten vehicles and the ordinary fare rule.[^taipei-rehab-booking]
The booking page gives an advance-booking schedule, a short-notice window and
the unified customer-centre and voice-booking numbers. Those are booking
requirements, not a promise that a vehicle is available for every requested
time.[^taipei-rehab-booking]

Taipei also documents a separate expansion for the highest-priority service
category: selected districts of New Taipei, Keelung and Taoyuan are included,
with a project-based booking limit and a different long-distance fare boundary.[^taipei-rehab-booking]
That expansion must not be generalised to every rehabilitation-bus user or to
the ordinary service area.

The Public Transportation Office's service-current page says that the vehicles
are fitted with wheelchair lifts and that the programme is outsourced to Eden
Social Welfare Foundation, Yuan San Car Rental and Shi Hao Passenger Car Rental.[^taipei-rehab-current]
The operator statement identifies the programme's commissioning/operating
arrangement; it does not provide a current fleet census, so the vehicle total
remains TBC.[^taipei-rehab-current]

This page does not merge the programme with universal accessible taxis. Taipei's
own comparison material treats the rehabilitation bus as eligibility-limited and
advance-booked, while universal taxis are a different, more immediate service
model.[^taipei-rehab-booking] That distinction is operationally important for a
reader deciding whether a regular accessible public-transport option is usable
without registration or advance booking.
