## The Matra dispute — Two disputes, not one

Discussed on [The Matra dispute](/rail/history/matra-dispute/) in the source page's “Two disputes, not one” section.[^rail-history-matra-dispute-ltn-2005]

### Two disputes, not one

**The delay claim, 1988–2005.** Matra sued for the cost of a contract that ran
years longer than it was written for, because the civil works its equipment
depended on were late — the claim arises, in 自由時報's words,
「因土木工程部分包商施工延誤，連帶造成馬特拉的工期延長，增加成本費用」[^rail-history-matra-dispute-ltn-2005].
This is the dispute that went to arbitration in 1993 and was still before the
Supreme Court in 2005[^rail-history-matra-dispute-ltn-2005]. It is about money and contract law, and
**Matra won it**[^rail-history-matra-dispute-ltn-2005].

**The withdrawal, 1996–97.** After the line opened, Matra pulled its technical
staff out of Taiwan and stopped maintaining the system[^rail-history-matra-dispute-zh-wenhu]. This is the
dispute everybody remembers — the one that produced the most famous sentence
said about the Taipei Metro, and the one that steered the procurement that
followed.

They are connected: the withdrawal happened because the delay claim had
poisoned the relationship. But they are not the same event, and the
NT$1.64 billion that circulates belongs to the first, not the
second[^rail-history-matra-dispute-ltn-2005].

## Red Line — Construction and opening chronology

Discussed on [Red Line](/rail/krtc/lines/red-line/) in the source page's “Construction and opening chronology” section.[^rail-krtc-lines-red-line-mtbu-network-origin]

### Construction and opening chronology

The government’s network history places the Red and Orange lines in the first stage of Kaohsiung’s first-period MRT development plan, approved in ROC80, and says that the two routes were completed and opened in ROC97.[^rail-krtc-lines-red-line-mtbu-network-origin] KRTC’s company history gives a more granular institutional chronology: the city invited private participation on 1 February 1999, the China Steel-led application was selected on 10 May 2000, the company received its licence on 28 December 2000, and the construction and development agreements were signed on 12 January 2001.[^rail-krtc-lines-red-line-krtc-history] KRTC records construction beginning in October 2001 and describes the concession as 36 years from the start of construction, comprising more than six years of construction before the Red and Orange lines entered service.[^rail-krtc-lines-red-line-krtc-history]

MTBU treats the Red and Orange network as one private-participation construction and operating project: its page gives six years of construction, 30 years of operation and a 36-year concession, with a planned 42.7 km and 38 stations, three depots and a total construction cost of NT$183.963 billion.[^rail-krtc-lines-red-line-mtbu-red-orange] KRTC records the Red Line’s regular service opening on 9 March 2008 and the Orange Line’s opening on 14 September 2008; the latter date marks the full operation of the original cross-shaped network, not the first Red Line service.[^rail-krtc-lines-red-line-krtc-15th-anniversary]

The historical service-indicator document is a separate design snapshot. It describes a Red Line of 28.3 km, with 19.8 km underground and 8.5 km elevated, and 23 stations at the time of that document.[^rail-krtc-lines-red-line-mtbu-service-indicators] The current government page reports 28.3 km and 24 Red Line stations, while the TDX route pull reports 29.72 km and its current sequence includes R24 and RK1.[^rail-krtc-lines-red-line-mtbu-red-orange][^rail-krtc-lines-red-line-tdx-krtc-route][^rail-krtc-lines-red-line-tdx-krtc-station-of-route] Those values are retained as different dated or structured boundaries rather than made to agree by arithmetic.

assigned to DORTS[^rail-metro-lines-airport-mrt-dorts-airport]. The detailed design ran from March 1998
through 2002, according to the same project chronology[^rail-metro-lines-airport-mrt-dorts-airport].

The opening date itself moved repeatedly. The earliest announced target was
January 2009; by March 2016 it had changed six times[^rail-metro-lines-airport-mrt-cna-2016-delays]. The
fetched report gives that count but not a complete dated list of each change.
The line finally opened **2 March 2017**[^rail-metro-lines-airport-mrt-tymc-about][^rail-metro-lines-airport-mrt-dorts-airport].
TYMC's chronology records the 99% system-stability test on 20 November 2016,
Ricardo's safety certification on 2 December, initial inspection on 3–4
December, final inspection on 29–30 December, the operating permit on 25 January
2017 and trial service from 2 February[^rail-metro-lines-airport-mrt-tymc-history].

## Taoyuan Airport MRT — Engineering and contract disputes — two figures confirmed, a widely repeated total not found anywhere

Discussed on [Taoyuan Airport MRT](/rail/metro/lines/airport-mrt/) in the source page's “Engineering and contract disputes — two figures confirmed, a widely repeated total not found anywhere” section.[^rail-metro-lines-airport-mrt-cna-2017-marubeni]

### Engineering and contract disputes — two figures confirmed, a widely repeated total not found anywhere

The main electromechanical contract (ME01) went to a consortium led by the
Japanese trading house **Marubeni** (丸紅), with Kawasaki Heavy Industries and
Hitachi as named subcontractors. In January 2017, weeks before opening, the
High Speed Rail Bureau announced it would impose a **NT$2.5 billion delay
penalty** — the contract's maximum, calculated at 0.05% of contract value per
day of delay — and had already **withheld roughly NT$1.9 billion in
performance bonds**, over Marubeni's unauthorised subcontracting of the
signalling system, which its contract required it to design itself, to a
British firm[^rail-metro-lines-airport-mrt-cna-2017-marubeni]. Marubeni's own counter-claim against the
Ministry, seeking NT$12.9 billion over repeated design changes, was reported
in the same account[^rail-metro-lines-airport-mrt-cna-2017-marubeni]. Both of the Bureau's figures were
independently corroborated by two further contemporaneous reports naming the
same amounts and the same reasons.

**A combined "NT$7.2 billion" total for this dispute circulates widely.**
NT$2.5 billion plus NT$1.9 billion is NT$4.4
billion, not NT$7.2 billion, and no primary or secondary source read for this
page — not the three contemporaneous 2017 news accounts, not
zh.wikipedia's own article on the line, which contains no Marubeni monetary
figures at all — states a combined total of any kind. The NT$7.2 billion
figure appeared repeatedly in this research run's own search-engine result
summaries, never inside a quoted source sentence, and in one case alongside
an unrelated, later, and much smaller 2021 dispute — the Railway Bureau
withheld NT$726.93 million from Marubeni that year for missing express-train
travel-time, speed and headway targets[^rail-metro-lines-airport-mrt-cna-2021-marubeni] — that shares a
leading digit and may be the source of the confusion. The two confirmed
figures, and Marubeni's name, are published here on the strength of three
independent contemporaneous accounts; the total is not, because it could not
be found stated anywhere.

## Circular Line — Incident investigation and engineering findings

Discussed on [Circular Line](/rail/metro/lines/circular-line/) in the source page's “Incident investigation and engineering findings” section.[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114]

### Incident investigation and engineering findings

Read the report carefully and the first thing to notice is what is *not* a
finding[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114]. Its terms of reference recite the allegation it was asked to look at —
eleven suspected displacements, a repair initially costed at over NT$400
million, at least a year to fix[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114]. Those are the reported claims the
Control Yuan set out to test, not its conclusions, and they are quoted here as
such.

### The mechanism

The report's first finding is a construction fault, stated without hedging: the
contractor, BES Engineering (中華工程), **did not build to the
drawings**[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114]. It
milled through the grade-adjustment plate (調坡板) that sits between the girder
and the bearing, leaving void space above the locating pin; under the earthquake
the pin jumped upward into that void, its base left the interface between the
upper and lower plates of the pot bearing, and it lost its ability to resist
shear[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114].

The numbers behind that sentence are the heart of the case[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114]:

| Dimension | Depth |
| --- | --- |
| Milled recess, as shown on the contractor's own shop drawing | 5 mm[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114] |
| Milled recess, as measured on site by the investigation | 41 mm[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114] |
| Void left above the pin | 36 mm[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114] |
| Height of the pin itself | 40 mm[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114] |

36 mm of void plus the 5 mm of engagement the drawing required is 41 mm — the
report's own arithmetic — and 41 mm is more than enough to swallow a pin only 40
mm tall[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114]. The pins were found by the New Taipei City Civil Engineers
Association, which jacked the damaged bridges up and discovered them lodged in
the upper plate of the bearing or lying on the pier cap where they had
fallen[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114].

The section was built under contract CF651B, part of the CF650 section works,
and the structures carried a warranty running to 12 March 2026[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114] — which
is why the timing of the claim against the contractor matters.

### The shaking was smaller than the design case

This is the part most likely to be misread. The viaducts were designed to the
Ministry of Transportation and Communications' 2006 railway bridge seismic
design code, at a Taipei basin design ground motion of 0.24 g, with an
importance factor of 1.2 because metro bridges are classed as important
structures — so the minimum design seismic force is 0.288 g, which on the
Central Weather Administration's 2020 intensity scale falls in the
6-weak band[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114]. The "designed for 6-weak" formulation is the report's own
arithmetic on 0.24 g × 1.2 followed by a lookup on the 2020 scale, not a design
document that says 6-weak[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114].

What the instruments recorded near the damaged bridges was
smaller[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114]:

| Station | North–south peak ground acceleration |
| --- | --- |
| TAP026, 頂溪國小 | 224.60 gal[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114] |
| TAP032, 積穗國小 | 206.77 gal[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114] |
| TAP115, 安和國小 | 223.17 gal[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114] |

At 積穗國小 the peak ground velocity reached 32.16 cm/s, an intensity of
5-strong, and the peak horizontal acceleration measured there was 0.229 g —
below the 0.288 g of the 475-year return-period design
earthquake[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114]. New Taipei DORTS said the same thing three weeks after the
earthquake: intensity 5-strong at the nearest recording station[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-ntpc-dorts-damage].

The comparison the report itself draws is between seismic microzones rather than
between metro lines. The adjacent zones — the DF111 contract range covering
Wenshan and Xindian, and the DF113 range covering Sanchong, Xinzhuang and
Banqiao — recorded far lower ground motion in the same earthquake and suffered
**no bridge damage at all**[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114]. In the DF112 range covering Zhonghe and
Yonghe, horizontal peak ground acceleration was around twice its neighbours' and
horizontal peak ground velocity around fifteen times[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114].

Which produces the single most quotable line in the document: 「也就是說，輸入比
規範要求小20%的地震，卻得到比預期大50%的力量，顯示出反應譜分析不能反映當地地震反應
的實況」 — an input earthquake 20% smaller than the code requires produced forces
50% larger than expected, showing that response-spectrum analysis does not
reflect the local seismic response[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114].

### The design, and what nobody checked

The report's second finding is against the design. The structure is described in
it as **Taiwan's first stacked viaduct** — 國內史無前例的疊式高架橋 — carried on
branching piers through climbing and descending sections, and the finding is
that no higher design standard was adopted and no displacement-limiting devices
were added to provide a further line of defence, and that this unprecedented
structural form went through **no external structural review**[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114].

Note the word: viaduct, not station. Nothing found in the research behind this
page describes stacked *stations*.

The structural engineers' report the investigation relied on sets out the
hierarchy plainly: displacement-limiting devices assist the bearings and resist
seismic force, and fall-prevention equipment or sufficient seat length prevents
the span dropping — the second and third lines of defence respectively for a
bridge bearing system in an earthquake[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114]. The line was built with neither
of the two backups.

Three further design failures are recorded[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114]:

- The detailed design consultant, CECI (世曦), **double-deducted dead load** when
  calculating the bearings' uplift design force, and so underestimated the
  required uplift resistance[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114].
- The pot-bearing detail drawing **contradicts itself about the pins**[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114].
  Note 1 in its lower-left corner gives every pin a height of 40 mm with
  diameters of 120, 140, 150, 160, 200, 210, 220 and 280 mm; the table on the
  same drawing gives every pin a diameter of 100 mm with heights of 15, 20 and 25
  mm; and neither set matches the pins actually found in the damaged
  bearings[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114]. The report's own word for this is 令人費解 — baffling.
- **Nobody senior signed that drawing**[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114]. Only the handling officer at
  Taipei DORTS's third civil works office stamped it; neither the design unit
  responsible for reviewing it nor DORTS as the approving authority signed at
  all, which the report finds is a failure to carry out the review and approval
  the contract's specification chapter on pot bearings required[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114].

888 pot bearings were supplied to the works in total, sampled in six batches
against the production schedule and tested at National Taiwan University of
Science and Technology's structural mechanics laboratory[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114].

### The plate that nobody in Taiwan inspects

The third finding is the one most easily missed, and it is the reason the fault
survived construction. The grade-adjustment plate is a secondary component
between the girder and the bearing; it is designed by the contractor, assembled
in the fabrication shop, and concealed once the girder is lifted into place — so
it was **not an inspection hold point**[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114].

The investigation then checked whether it is one anywhere else, and found that it
is not: not on the Directorate General of Highways' viaducts, not on the
Hualien–Taitung railway works, not on the Tamkang Bridge, not on the Ankeng LRT,
not on the Sanying Line, not on the Taichung Metro, not on the Wenhu Line, not on
the second phase of the Wanda Line, and not in the Public Construction
Commission's inspection checkpoints for steel bridge erection[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114]. Taipei
DORTS's remedy, dated 13 December 2024 — the day after the line reopened — was to
add a pot-bearing inspection flow chart and a sampling standard[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114].

### The seismometer that was told not to report

The fourth finding concerns an instrument that was installed, worked, and was
disconnected from anyone who could act on it. Zhongyuan station has triaxial
accelerometer-type seismometers at both ends of its platform level[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114]. On 28 August
2019 Taipei DORTS's electrical and mechanical systems engineering office wrote to
Taipei Rapid Transit Corporation that the seismometer detection signals need not
be relayed to the control centre — and from the line's opening in 2020 to the
date of the report, the Circular Line control centre held **no seismic record
from that instrument at all**[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114].

The 2019 letter identifies the instruments by the codes `Y9` and `Y13`, which are
not the codes the stations at those positions carry today; the two numbering
schemes remain unreconciled, and the station the report names is Zhongyuan[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114].

The report also states as fact that when Taipei City Government handed operations
to New Taipei City Government on 23 May 2023, New Taipei Metro took the equipment
as it stood, and received training on the anemometer only — nothing on the
seismic instrumentation[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114].

### The code itself

The sixth finding is against the ministry. Through years of revision, including
the substantially updated 2021 edition of the railway bridge seismic design code,
MOTC has never addressed **transverse** displacement: the rules still deal only
with the longitudinal, running direction[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114]. The line failed sideways.

### Who was actually corrected

This is worth stating precisely, because the operator of the line and the builder
of it are different cities and the two are easily transposed. The formal 糾正 — a
correction, the Control Yuan's censure of an agency — was proposed against
**Taipei City Government**, on findings one and three: the construction fault and
the supervision gap[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114]. Taipei DORTS built the line and supervised its own
construction[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114].

New Taipei City Government and MOTC received 函請檢討改進 — a request to review
and improve, and report back — not a correction[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114]. Findings two and four
went to Taipei City for review, finding four also to New Taipei City, and
findings five and six to MOTC[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114].

  the contract's specification chapter on pot bearings required[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114].

888 pot bearings were supplied to the works in total, sampled in six batches
against the production schedule and tested at National Taiwan University of
Science and Technology's structural mechanics laboratory[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114].

On 3 January 2025 New Taipei City Government announced that the two city

and the bearing failure are **not** the main cause of the damage, which it says
needs further examination from the direction of structural design
strength[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-pts-bes].

### Whose fault it was

Four positions, and they are not reconciled[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114][^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-ntpc-claim][^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-pts-bes]:

- **The contractor did not build to the drawings**, and milling through the
  grade-adjustment plate is the cause — Control Yuan's first finding, and New
  Taipei City Government's basis for the claim[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114][^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-ntpc-claim].
- **The contractor denies it**, says it built strictly to the approved drawings,
  and says the plate and the bearings are not the main cause[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-pts-bes].
- **The design was at fault** — no displacement-limiting devices, dead load
  double-deducted in the uplift calculation, drawings neither reviewed nor
  approved, no external structural review of an unprecedented
  structure[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114].
- **The supervision was at fault** — a concealed component with no inspection
  hold point[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114].

The Control Yuan apportions responsibility to contractor, designer, supervisor
**and** the ministry's own code[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-cy-114]. The contractor rejects the
first[^rail-metro-lines-circular-line-pts-bes]. The litigation filed in March 2025 has not been resolved on this
page.

## Sanying Line — Where the sources disagree

Discussed on [Sanying Line](/rail/metro/lines/sanying-line/) in the source page's “Where the sources disagree” section.[^rail-metro-lines-sanying-line-ntpc-early-works]

### Where the sources disagree

**Project cost.** Three official values survive. The October 2014 early-works
release gave **NT$55.022 billion**.[^rail-metro-lines-sanying-line-ntpc-early-works] The April 2015 NDC-stage
release and August 2018 engineering report gave **NT$50.5 billion**; the report
says value engineering used shorter trains, tighter headways, shorter stations,
well foundations and U-girders to reduce the earlier NT$55 billion proposal.[^rail-metro-lines-sanying-line-ntpc-ndc-2015][^rail-metro-lines-sanying-line-ntpc-technical-report]
The live builder page now gives **NT$50.200 billion**.[^rail-metro-lines-sanying-line-ntpc-dorts-route] These
are dated project estimates or current programme figures, not interchangeable
measurements of final expenditure.

**Journey and peak interval.** In 2014 the city forecast an end-to-end journey
of no more than **23 minutes** and a **three-minute** peak interval.[^rail-metro-lines-sanying-line-ntpc-early-works]
Opening service was about **30 minutes** and **six minutes**.[^rail-metro-lines-sanying-line-ntmetro-opening]
The engineering report's mature design pattern put LB01–LB08 at 2.2 minutes but
LB08–LB12 at 4.4 minutes.[^rail-metro-lines-sanying-line-ntpc-technical-report] The sources describe an early
forecast, initial operations and a design-year branching pattern respectively;
none is substituted for another.

**Bade approval.** The extension page's dated record says the Executive Yuan
approved the comprehensive plan on 1 August 2025, while its introduction says
the project has not been approved.[^rail-metro-lines-sanying-line-ntpc-dorts-bade] The page gives no update
date for the stale sentence, so its provenance cannot be reconstructed.

## Tamsui–Xinyi Line — Construction history

Discussed on [Tamsui–Xinyi Line](/rail/metro/lines/tamsui-xinyi-line/) in the source page's “Construction history” section.[^rail-metro-lines-tamsui-xinyi-line-dorts-tamsui]

### Construction history

The Tamsui section came in three pieces, and DORTS records an inspection date
against each[^rail-metro-lines-tamsui-xinyi-line-dorts-tamsui]:

| Section | Trial inspection | Final inspection | Opened |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tamsui–Zhongshan | 12 Feb 1997 | 21 Mar 1997 | **28 March 1997**[^rail-metro-lines-tamsui-xinyi-line-dorts-tamsui] |
| Zhongshan–Taipei Main | — | 17 Dec 1997 | **25 December 1997**[^rail-metro-lines-tamsui-xinyi-line-dorts-tamsui] |
| Taipei Main–CKS Memorial Hall | — | — | **24 December 1998**[^rail-metro-lines-tamsui-xinyi-line-dorts-tamsui] |

DORTS calls the result 「國內第一條通車的高運量捷運系統」 — the first
high-capacity metro line in Taiwan to enter service[^rail-metro-lines-tamsui-xinyi-line-dorts-tamsui]. The last of
those three stretches was not built with the rest of the Tamsui line at all: the
project record notes against both R08 and R09 that
「本站工程併入捷運新店線辦理」, the station works were folded into the Xindian
line contract[^rail-metro-lines-tamsui-xinyi-line-dorts-tamsui].

The Xinyi section followed fifteen years later, inspected on 13 October and
8 November 2013 and opened on **24 November 2013**, against a budget of
NT$33.862 billion[^rail-metro-lines-tamsui-xinyi-line-dorts-xinyi]. Its seven-station count includes R08 and
R05[^rail-metro-lines-tamsui-xinyi-line-dorts-xinyi], both of which already existed on other lines — so a source
giving a smaller number of stations "opened in 2013" is counting new stations
rather than the project's, not contradicting it.

The whole thing descends from one decision: the Executive Yuan approved the
initial network on 4 April 1986 with a total budget of NT$444.4 billion, and
the Tamsui line was let as 13 design contracts and around 120 construction
contracts[^rail-metro-lines-tamsui-xinyi-line-dorts-tamsui]. The earliest of them is worth naming — contract CT107,
earthworks for Beitou Depot, signed on **19 January 1988**, which DORTS itself
calls the earliest of them[^rail-metro-lines-tamsui-xinyi-line-dorts-tamsui].

## Tamsui–Xinyi Line — Where the sources disagree

Discussed on [Tamsui–Xinyi Line](/rail/metro/lines/tamsui-xinyi-line/) in the source page's “Where the sources disagree” section.[^rail-metro-lines-tamsui-xinyi-line-dorts-ck570h-2005]

### Where the sources disagree

**Dongmen dimensions and shield drives.** The 2005 DORTS paper reports a box
about **217 m × 24.7 m**, excavated about **31.2 m**, with Xinyi shield drives of
**1,240.5 m** and **1,247.0 m** (about 2,487.5 m combined).[^rail-metro-lines-tamsui-xinyi-line-dorts-ck570h-2005]
The 2014 DORTS construction paper instead reports about **216 m × 25 m**, an
excavation of about **32 m**, and drives of **1,219.3 m** and **1,225.8 m**
(about 2,445.1 m combined).[^rail-metro-lines-tamsui-xinyi-line-dorts-ck570h-2014] The difference is published as
a conflict because neither source states why its measuring basis differs.

**Route length.** DORTS's two published figures are 23.8 km for the Tamsui
section including the branch[^rail-metro-lines-tamsui-xinyi-line-dorts-tamsui] and 6.4 km for the Xinyi
section[^rail-metro-lines-tamsui-xinyi-line-dorts-xinyi]. They meet end to end at R08, so they sum to 30.2 km with
the branch, or 29.0 km for the main line alone — but that sum is this page's
arithmetic on two primary figures, not anybody's published length. Chinese
Wikipedia's infobox gives **32.3 km**[^rail-metro-lines-tamsui-xinyi-line-zh-tamsui-xinyi]. Nothing read for this
page explains the two-to-three kilometre gap, and the whole-line spec row
therefore reads TBC rather than picking one. The Wenhu Line's three lengths
turned out to be three correct measurements of three different things; this may
be the same, and saying so is not the same as knowing it.

**Station count.** DORTS's 22[^rail-metro-lines-tamsui-xinyi-line-dorts-tamsui] and 7[^rail-metro-lines-tamsui-xinyi-line-dorts-xinyi] share R08,
which nets to 28 — the number in service today, R02 to R28 plus R22A. Chinese
Wikipedia says 29, counting the
unopened `R01`[^rail-metro-lines-tamsui-xinyi-line-zh-tamsui-xinyi]. Those are compatible once you know what each
counts.

**Rolling stock.** Chinese Wikipedia's line infobox names C301, C381 and
C381A[^rail-metro-lines-tamsui-xinyi-line-zh-tamsui-xinyi]; its Xinbeitou article names C371 for the
branch[^rail-metro-lines-tamsui-xinyi-line-zh-xinbeitou]. Two articles on one encyclopedia, two fleets. Neither was
checked against a TRTC or manufacturer record, so the facts panel names no fleet
at all. The obvious reconciliation is obvious enough to be suspicious.

See also: [Public art in the Taipei-region rail network](/rail/history/public-art/), [Station naming and renames](/rail/history/station-naming/), and [Metro incidents and service disruptions](/rail/history/incidents/).

## Wenhu Line — Length records and measurement conflicts

Discussed on [Wenhu Line](/rail/metro/lines/wenhu-line/) in the source page's “Length records and measurement conflicts” section.[^rail-metro-lines-wenhu-line-tdx]

### Length records and measurement conflicts

Four figures circulate, and they are not in conflict — they measure four
different things. **This site publishes 25.17 km, the operating length**, and
says what each of the others counts.

| Figure | What it measures | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **25.17 km** | **Operating length** — the railway that carries passengers, BR01 to BR24 | TDX `CumulativeDistance` against the last station of route BR-1[^rail-metro-lines-wenhu-line-tdx] |
| 25.7 km | The two construction projects added together | 10.9 km + 14.8 km, both DORTS's own figures[^rail-metro-lines-wenhu-line-dorts-wenhu] |
| 25.04 km | The same railway, measured by this site along MOTC's surveyed geometry from the first station to the last | the map below |
| 26.42 km | The whole published alignment, including 635 m of depot lead beyond BR01 and 744 m of tail track beyond BR24 | the same geometry, untrimmed[^rail-metro-lines-wenhu-line-tdx] |

The last row is the one this site got wrong: it printed the untrimmed figure as
the route length for four builds, because measuring everything MOTC draws counts
depot lead and tail track that no passenger travels on[^rail-metro-lines-wenhu-line-tdx]. Trimmed to the
termini, the same geometry lands within 130 m of the operator's own
figure[^rail-metro-lines-wenhu-line-tdx] — and that agreement is the check working, which is why both are
still printed side by side on the [network page](/rail/network/).

The 25.7 km that appears as 路線長度 on zh.wikipedia[^rail-metro-lines-wenhu-line-zh-wenhu] is the **sum of
the two published section lengths**: DORTS records 木柵線 約10.9公里 and 內湖線
約14.8公里, and 10.9 + 14.8 = 25.7 exactly[^rail-metro-lines-wenhu-line-dorts-wenhu]. Those are project
lengths — the extent of what was built, including non-revenue track at each
end — which is why the total exceeds the 25.17 km of railway that carries
passengers[^rail-metro-lines-wenhu-line-tdx].

## C370 (Bombardier Innovia) — Passenger systems and capacity

Discussed on [C370 (Bombardier Innovia)](/rail/metro/rolling-stock/innovia-apm-256-c370/) in the source page's “Passenger systems and capacity” section.[^rail-metro-rolling-stock-innovia-apm-256-c370-dorts-safety]

### Passenger systems and capacity

Each car has four externally sliding doors, two per side, driven by DC motors.[^rail-metro-rolling-stock-innovia-apm-256-c370-dorts-safety]
Microprocessor controllers monitor direction, speed, obstacle detection and ATC
commands.[^rail-metro-rolling-stock-innovia-apm-256-c370-dorts-doors] Door-side lights flash for five seconds before opening,
and multi-tone audible warnings provide an equivalent cue before opening and
closing.[^rail-metro-rolling-stock-innovia-apm-256-c370-dorts-doors] Four dynamic route displays, four passenger-information
screens, two emergency intercoms, CCTV and two extinguishers were specified per
car.[^rail-metro-rolling-stock-innovia-apm-256-c370-dorts-safety]

Capacity has an unresolved primary-source disagreement. The design paper gives
20 seats and 93 people per car at five standing passengers per square metre,
which implies 372 per four-car train.[^rail-metro-rolling-stock-innovia-apm-256-c370-dorts-design-spec] TRTC's current network
introduction uses the same five-person standing density but states approximately
386 people per Bombardier train.[^rail-metro-rolling-stock-innovia-apm-256-c370-trtc-network] The 14-person difference is not
explained in either opened page, so both values are published rather than one
being chosen.

## TOD and joint development — Meihe City and the legal dispute

Discussed on [TOD and joint development](/rail/projects/tod-joint-development/) in the source page's “Meihe City and the legal dispute” section.[^rail-projects-tod-joint-development-ntpc-meihe-news]

### Meihe City and the legal dispute

Meihe City is the region's essential caution against treating joint development
as an uncomplicated financing success. New Taipei's official 2014 account says
the review lasted more than 15 years, reduced an original 26-building design to
13 buildings, and required greater setbacks and public space after urban-design
and environmental review.[^rail-projects-tod-joint-development-ntpc-meihe-news] A later New Taipei government
research report instead describes 16 towers, 2,220 units, about 400,600 m² of
floor area, and Rich Development as the selected private developer.[^rail-projects-tod-joint-development-ntpc-meihe-research]
Those are conflicting project descriptions from different official records; the
site publishes both rather than choosing one.[^rail-projects-tod-joint-development-ntpc-meihe-news][^rail-projects-tod-joint-development-ntpc-meihe-research]

The legal issue is not just a design disagreement. Judicial Interpretation No.
743 states that land expropriated under MRT Law Article 6 cannot be used in the
same plan for Article 7 joint development, and that transfer to a third party
requires clear legal authority.[^rail-projects-tod-joint-development-judicial-743] The interpretation records the
underlying Xindian depot case, the different statutory purposes of expropriation
and joint development, and the disagreement among the Control Yuan, executive
agencies and courts before the interpretation.[^rail-projects-tod-joint-development-judicial-743] Meihe City is
therefore published here as a legal and governance conflict as well as a
large-scale station-area project.[^rail-projects-tod-joint-development-judicial-743]

## Taiwan High Speed Rail — BOT contract and financial disputes

Discussed on [Taiwan High Speed Rail](/rail/thsr/lines/thsr/) in the source page's “BOT contract and financial disputes” section.[^rail-thsr-lines-thsr-rb-thsr]

### BOT contract and financial disputes

The Railway Bureau describes the project as Taiwan’s first major national infrastructure project promoted through private investment participation.[^rail-thsr-lines-thsr-rb-thsr] It records the 23 July 1998 signing of a 35-year construction-and-operation concession and a separate 50-year station-area development concession.[^rail-thsr-lines-thsr-rb-thsr] The stated project cost was approximately NT$513.3 billion, with the government responsible for land, the Taipei underground co-construction section, supervision and management, while the concession company funded civil works, stations, core electromechanical systems, operation and maintenance.[^rail-thsr-lines-thsr-rb-thsr]

The original financial model did not remain unchanged. The Railway Bureau says a 27 July 2015 amendment extended the concession to 70 years, cancelled the station-area development contract, returned development land, reduced capital to clear accumulated losses and added NT$30 billion of government investment.[^rail-thsr-lines-thsr-rb-thsr] The Control Yuan’s official record names an investigation into whether direct and indirect public investment violated BOT principles.[^rail-thsr-lines-thsr-cy-bot] In a related statement, the Control Yuan says public and state-enterprise entities supplied 37.42% and describes that as a serious departure from the government-zero-investment commitment.[^rail-thsr-lines-thsr-cy-funding] Those are the source’s figures and characterization; this page does not average them into a new ownership percentage.

## How bus fare stages (段) actually work — How crossing multiple segments is counted

Discussed on [How bus fare stages (段) actually work](/ticketing/guides/bus-fare-stages/) in the source page's “How crossing multiple segments is counted” section.[^ticketing-guides-bus-fare-stages-wiki-ntpc-bus]

### How crossing multiple segments is counted

Chinese Wikipedia's article on New Taipei's own city buses gives a worked
example of the counting logic. For a corridor running through zones A, B and
C, with B as the segment point: riding within one zone, or from A to B, or
from B to C, is one segment; riding all the way from A to C is two segments
(the single-segment fare, doubled)[^ticketing-guides-bus-fare-stages-wiki-ntpc-bus]. On a longer corridor with
two segment points, A–B–C–D–E, both B and D count as crossing
points[^ticketing-guides-bus-fare-stages-wiki-ntpc-bus]. This is an illustrative example rather than a
government fare rule in itself, but it does not contradict, and directly
illustrates, the primary rule already confirmed above.

## Fare history and concessions — Bus fares

Discussed on [Fare history and concessions](/ticketing/guides/fare-history/) in the source page's “Bus fares” section.[^ticketing-guides-fare-history-ebus-fare-info]

### Bus fares

Taipei and New Taipei buses use segment fares, not one flat price for every
journey.[^ticketing-guides-fare-history-ebus-fare-info] The current passenger table is NT$15 full fare per
segment, NT$12 for a student EasyCard segment and NT$8 for elderly,
disabled/companion and eligible-child concessions.[^ticketing-guides-fare-history-ebus-fare-info] Taipei PTO
records the latest joint-operation bus operating-rate approval as 1 July 2020
while passenger fares stayed at 15/12/8 and the city subsidised the
difference.[^ticketing-guides-fare-history-pto-fare-maintenance]

This is a measurement conflict, not a price contradiction: operating rate is
the regulated cost/revenue figure, while passenger fare is what the rider
pays. The historical report records a 1997 bus-rate-related transfer-discount
change, but a complete gazette-backed chronology of every passenger-fare
adjustment remains TBC.[^ticketing-guides-fare-history-taipei-transfer-history]
