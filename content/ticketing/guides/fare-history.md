Taipei Metro’s first published fares were NT$20, NT$25, NT$30 and NT$35 when
the Muzha Line opened on 28 March 1996.[^taipei-metro-history] The current TRTC
data set is a station-to-station record in the NT$20–65 range and identifies
the Circular Line Phase 1 charge from 1 March 2020 as the basis of the current
published data.[^trtc-fare-dataset]

## Taipei Metro fares

The opening fare is the anchor: four bands, not the ten bands in the current
fare table.[^taipei-metro-history][^trtc-fare-setting] The official fare-setting
paper says the distance-banded price had remained unchanged for more than ten
years when written, but it does not provide a gazette-by-gazette list of every
later adjustment.[^trtc-fare-setting]

| Date | Fare event | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| 28 Mar 1996 | Muzha Line opened with NT$20, NT$25, NT$30 and NT$35 fares.[^taipei-metro-history] | Opening gazette itself: TBC. |
| 1 Mar 2020 | Circular Phase 1: NT$20 to 5 km, then published distance increments, up to NT$65 above 31 km.[^trtc-circular-fare-notice] | Official fare notice, distinct from the open-data mirror.[^trtc-fare-dataset] |
| Current dataset | TRTC publishes the current station-to-station grid through 31 Mar 2026.[^trtc-fare-dataset] | Current publication, not a dated change gazette. |

The 1996 and 2020 rows measure different scopes: opening Muzha service versus
the later integrated network.[^taipei-metro-history][^trtc-fare-dataset] Both
values are published; neither is averaged away.

## Transfer discounts and concessions

The official report supplies the strongest dated sequence found:

1. **25 November 1996:** transfer vouchers began, with an initial NT$4
   discount.[^taipei-transfer-history]
2. **1 July 1999:** stored-value tickets replaced vouchers, allowing a one-way
   free transfer within two hours.[^taipei-transfer-history]
3. **1 November 2003:** the first two-way EasyCard stage gave NT$8 to a full
   fare and NT$6 to a student card.[^taipei-transfer-history]
4. **27 December 2003:** the second stage added a NT$4 welfare-card discount.[^taipei-transfer-history]
5. **1 April 2005:** the window became one hour while the discount stayed
   unchanged.[^taipei-transfer-history]

The current bus portal publishes NT$15 per segment, NT$12 for a student
EasyCard segment and NT$8 for concessions.[^ebus-fare-info] TRTC’s current FAQ
gives MRT–bus discounts of NT$8, NT$6 and NT$4 within 60 minutes.[^trtc-faq-transfer]
The old report’s one hour and the current FAQ’s 60 minutes are the same
duration, retained without inventing a separate change.

## Bus fares

Taipei and New Taipei buses use segment fares, not one flat price for every
journey.[^ebus-fare-info] The current passenger table is NT$15 full fare per
segment, NT$12 for a student EasyCard segment and NT$8 for elderly,
disabled/companion and eligible-child concessions.[^ebus-fare-info] Taipei PTO
records the latest joint-operation bus operating-rate approval as 1 July 2020
while passenger fares stayed at 15/12/8 and the city subsidised the
difference.[^pto-fare-maintenance]

This is a measurement conflict, not a price contradiction: operating rate is
the regulated cost/revenue figure, while passenger fare is what the rider
pays. The historical report records a 1997 bus-rate-related transfer-discount
change, but a complete gazette-backed chronology of every passenger-fare
adjustment remains TBC.[^taipei-transfer-history]

## New Taipei Metro and the trial line

The current linked NTMC tables publish Danhai station-pair fares of NT$20–30,
Ankeng fares of NT$20–25 and Circular Line fares of NT$20–40.[^ntmc-danhai-fare][^ntmc-ankeng-fare][^ntmc-circular-fare]
These are current station-pair tables, not a claim that each line has held one
fare since opening.

The Sanying page says electronic-ticket users ride free from 16–31 August
during trial operation while other passengers buy a single ticket.[^ntmc-sanying-fare]
Its fetched HTML does not publish a normal post-trial station-pair table, so
that value is TBC rather than an inference from another line.[^ntmc-sanying-fare]

## Passes and recent rebates

TRTC’s chronology records the NT$1,280 30-day unlimited pass from 16 April
2018.[^trtc-chronology] The Highway Bureau records the NT$1,200 TPASS launch
on 1 July 2023 and its named MRT, light-rail, bus, TRA, coach and YouBike
coverage.[^tpass-launch] The predecessor ended sales on 30 June 2023.[^trtc-chronology]

TPASS 2.0 is a 2025 frequent-user rebate programme, while TRTC separately
publishes its own ride-count cashback scheme beginning 3 January 2026.[^tpass-2-0][^trtc-frequent]
Neither is rewritten as a change to the underlying station-to-station fare.

## Accessibility

TRTC’s fetched station API covers all 121 records returned by its station-label
endpoint and preserves station-specific lift/ramp, accessible-toilet,
tactile-paving and stair anti-slip fields.[^trtc-station-disabled] The fetched
response has no nursing-room location or numerical platform-gap field, so the
station template marks both TBC.[^trtc-station-disabled]

NTMC publishes accessible lifts and gates at every Circular Line station,
tactile routes from entrances to platforms, accessible toilets at every
Circular station and nursing-room station codes.[^ntmc-accessibility] For
light rail it distinguishes elevators at elevated stations, ramps at
at-grade stations, tactile warnings and accessible toilets at V01, V26 and
K09.[^ntmc-accessibility] TYMC publishes accessible elevators, gates, toilets,
tactile warnings and ramps at level differences for every Airport MRT station,
but the fetched page does not publish nursing-room locations or platform-gap
measurements.[^tymc-accessibility]

## What remains unresolved

This is a sourced timeline of events that survived full primary-page checking,
not a claim that one complete gazette series has been found. Missing original
approval notices for some historic passenger fares, the normal Sanying
post-trial table, TYMC nursing-room locations and all platform-gap measurements
remain TBC until the responsible authority publishes them.
