Two different companies issue the two cards that work almost everywhere in
Taiwan, and neither is a government agency. EasyCard came out of Taipei's own
metro company; iPass began life as Kaohsiung Metro's own card. The regional
commuter pass that sits on top of them is younger still, run by Taipei Metro
on behalf of four separate city governments, and it is one of the few transit
policy changes anywhere that made the product both cheaper and broader at the
same time[^megacity-notice].

## EasyCard — a metro operator that founded a payments company

EasyCard Corporation (悠遊卡股份有限公司) has NT$700 million in registered
capital and was established in March 2000[^easycard-about]. Its predecessor,
台北智慧卡票證股份有限公司 (Taipei Smart Card Corporation), was set up on 25
February 2000 by Taipei Metro itself, together with the Taipei City
Government's parking authority, Greater Taipei's bus operators, and a vendor
consortium — 神通 (Mitac), 台新 (Taishin), 國泰世華 (Cathay United), 中信銀
(CTBC), 富邦 (Fubon), 三商行 and 所羅門科技 (Solomon Technology)[^wiki-easycard-corp].
A metro operator founding the region's dominant payments company is an
unusual origin story, and it explains why TRTC is still a named shareholder
today, alongside the Taipei City Government, twelve Taipei-area bus
operators and a roster of banks[^easycard-about].

The card reached Taipei Metro in June 2002 — at ten stations initially — and
went live system-wide across Greater Taipei's buses and the metro on 30
September 2002[^wiki-easycard]. One million cards had been issued by that
December[^wiki-easycard][^wiki-easycard-corp]. **The two Chinese Wikipedia
articles about this launch do not agree with each other**: one describes June
as ten metro stations only, with September as the full system going live;
the other describes June as the *entire* metro line already running, plus one
bus route and fifteen car parks, with September described instead as the
card going on general retail sale (「悠遊卡全面上市」)[^wiki-easycard][^wiki-easycard-corp].
Both are read here in full and neither cites the other; this page carries
the more specific account without treating that as resolving the
disagreement.

By the company's own account, as of end-October 2022 it had **98.32 million
cards in circulation**, more than 9 million of them active, with NT$42.13
billion spent January–October that year and a 73.7% share of the stored-value
market[^easycard-ipo]. That is the most recent figure this page can source to
the company directly; it listed on Taiwan's emerging stock market on 3
January 2023, under code 6035[^easycard-ipo]. The card now reaches well
beyond transport — convenience stores, supermarkets, dining, scenic-site
admission, hospitals and clinics, government fee payment and library book
loans, on top of High Speed Rail, TRA, intercity coaches, YouBike and the
Blue Highway ferries[^easycard-about].

## iPass — Kaohsiung's card, not a Taipei one

iPass (一卡通) is not a Taipei product at all. It began as an electronic
ticket issued directly by Kaohsiung Rapid Transit Corporation, first issued
in December 2007 and activated with the opening of the Kaohsiung MRT Red
Line on 9 March 2008[^wiki-ipass]. A dedicated card company —
一卡通票證股份有限公司 — was approved by the Financial Supervisory
Commission on 6 December 2013 and took over the ticketing business on 13
February 2014[^ipass-about][^wiki-ipass], with shareholders including the
Executive Yuan's National Development Fund, the Kaohsiung City Government,
Union Bank of Taiwan and Kaohsiung Rapid Transit Corporation
itself[^ipass-about]. It gained its own electronic-payment business licence
in July 2017[^ipass-about]. Today Taipei's own Public Transport Office names
iPass, alongside EasyCard and icash (愛金卡), as one of the electronic
tickets approved on the joint-operation bus network[^pto-bus-cards] — two
cards from two different cities' metro operators, both now interchangeable
across the same buses and trains.

## The regional pass: 基北北桃1200都會通

The current commuter pass is issued by Taipei Metro on behalf of four city
governments — Keelung, Taipei, New Taipei and Taoyuan — under the Executive
Yuan's public-transport promotion programme[^megacity-notice]. It costs
**NT$1,200 for 30 days**, unlimited rides, unlimited distance, within that
zone[^megacity-notice]. It replaced a Taipei/New Taipei-only pass that had
cost **NT$1,280** and covered less[^ntpc-1280-notice] — a rare case of a
public transport product getting both cheaper and broader in the same
change.

### What it actually covers

The operating notice is specific, and worth quoting rather than
summarising[^megacity-notice]:

- **Metro and light rail**: every New Taipei Metro and Taipei Metro line, plus
  the Taoyuan Airport MRT.
- **City buses**: Keelung, New Taipei and Taoyuan city bus routes, and
  Taipei's joint-operation network.
- **TRA**, but only between named stations — the main line from Keelung to
  Taoyuan's Xinfu station, the Yilan Line between Badu and Fulong, and the
  Shen'ao and Pingxi branch lines — and **not** on reserved-seat-only
  services that sell no standing tickets, which the notice names explicitly:
  觀光 (sightseeing), 團體 (group), 太魯閣, 普悠瑪 and EMU3000 trains.
- **Highway and intercity coaches**, on a named list of more than ninety
  route codes across roughly a dozen operators, from 國光客運 to 基隆客運 to
  葛瑪蘭客運.
- **YouBike**, with the first 30 minutes free at Taipei and New Taipei docks,
  and the first 60 minutes free at Taoyuan docks[^megacity-notice].

### One card, one pass at a time

A single EasyCard can carry only one active monthly pass — the notice's own
words are that a card can be set to one product 「僅能設定一種定期票」, and a
rider has to wait for the current pass to expire before setting up a
different one[^megacity-notice]. That rule is why the transition from the old
1,280 pass required a genuine choice rather than simply layering the new
pass on top: a card already carrying an active 1,280 pass had to have it
cancelled and refunded before the 1,200 pass could be set on the same
card[^megacity-notice].

### You do not need a special "TPASS card"

The pass is not tied to any particular physical card. The operating notice
lists the whole family of EasyCard products it can be loaded onto: ordinary
cards, co-branded bank cards, EasyCard Debit cards, Samsung Wallet EasyCards,
SuperCard, telecom-issued EasyCards, student cards, and digital student
IDs[^megacity-notice]. Co-branded and Debit cards need at least 60 days of
remaining validity to qualify, but nothing requires buying a distinct "TPASS"
card to use the pass[^megacity-notice]. **SuperCard specifically** is
EasyCard's name for a newer card generation the company describes as
「新一代悠遊卡，手機就是你的加值機」 — a card that can be topped up from a
phone rather than only at a machine[^easycard-supercard] — and it is one
option among the family above, not a requirement.

What *is* sold separately is a commemorative TPASS-branded EasyCard, issued
by the central government's Directorate General of Highways: it costs
NT$100 to buy, and that NT$100 is refunded as an account top-up once the
buyer completes real-name registration and taps the card at a convenience
store[^thb-tpass-faq]. That refund is the source of the "the card is
effectively free" claim that circulates about the pass — it is correct, but
it describes a specific commemorative card, not a requirement to use the
pass at all.

### The launch, dated

Presale for the TPASS EasyCard opened at four convenience-store chains on 18
May 2023; card collection and 1,200-pass presale at MRT stations began 15
June 2023; the predecessor 1,280 pass was withdrawn from sale on 30 June
2023; and the new pass, with its expanded coverage, went live on 1 July
2023[^trtc-1200-presale][^taipei-gov-launch]. On launch day TRTC put its own
scale in numbers: 1,360 fare gates and 645 ticket machines across the system
configured to sell and refund the new pass[^taipei-gov-launch].

### The 1,280 pass's refund rule was not as simple as it sounds

New Taipei City's own operating document for the old 1,280 pass gives its
general, voluntary refund formula as the pass price **minus a front-loaded
daily deduction — NT$180 for the first day, NT$130 for every day after —
minus a NT$20 handling fee**[^ntpc-1280-notice]. That is not a flat pro-rata
split, and it is not fee-free: cancelling early cost proportionally more than
cancelling late. A separate clause in the same document, covering only a
card that has *physically failed* through no fault of the holder, does use a
simple pro-rata formula with no stated fee — pass price minus (pass price ×
elapsed days ÷ 30)[^ntpc-1280-notice]. The two are easy to conflate, and this
page keeps them separate rather than publishing the more generous one as the
general rule.

## Taipei Metro's own fare structure

Underneath any card or pass sits Taipei Metro's base fare system, which is
banded by distance rather than by zone or station count. The system's own
fare table gives ten tiers, each five dollars apart, running from the
minimum to the maximum single fare[^trtc-fare-table]:

| Distance | Fare |
|---|---|
| ≤5 km | NT$20[^trtc-fare-table] |
| >5–8 km | NT$25[^trtc-fare-table] |
| >8–11 km | NT$30[^trtc-fare-table] |
| >11–14 km | NT$35[^trtc-fare-table] |
| >14–17 km | NT$40[^trtc-fare-table] |
| >17–20 km | NT$45[^trtc-fare-table] |
| >20–23 km | NT$50[^trtc-fare-table] |
| >23–27 km | NT$55[^trtc-fare-table] |
| >27–31 km | NT$60[^trtc-fare-table] |
| >31 km | NT$65[^trtc-fare-table] |

That is the full/adult fare. Two discounts apply at a flat **40% of the full
fare per ride**: the children's fare, for ages 6–11 with a digital student
ID or a recognised children's card[^trtc-faq-child], and the concessionary
fare on 社福卡 — 敬老卡 (senior), 愛心卡 (disability) and 愛心陪伴卡
(companion) cards. Below age 6, or below 115cm regardless of age, a child
rides free alongside a fare-paying passenger, and one adult can bring up to
four such children[^trtc-faq-free].

Taipei-registered 敬老卡/愛心卡 holders get a separate, richer benefit on
top of the 40% floor: **600 points a month**, one point worth NT$1, usable
across TRTC, New Taipei's light rail and Circular Line, Maokong Gondola,
Taipei's joint-operation buses, and YouBike rental within Taipei
City[^trtc-faq-welfare]. Points do not carry over to the next month, and once
they run out the fare reverts to 40% off on rail and 50% off on
bus[^trtc-faq-welfare]. A 愛心陪伴卡 used on its own, without the primary
card tapping immediately before it, earns no points of its
own[^trtc-faq-welfare].

### Transferring between MRT and bus

A bidirectional transfer between Taipei Metro and a Taipei or New Taipei
joint-operation bus, completed within 60 minutes, discounts the second leg:
**NT$8 for an ordinary card, NT$6 for a student card, NT$4 for a
social-welfare card**[^trtc-faq-transfer]. Taipei's Public Transport Office
states the same NT$8/NT$6 figures independently on its own FAQ
page[^pto-bus-cards] — agreement between the regulator and the operator,
not one source repeating itself. The discount does not extend to
distance-charged buses, or to any other mode — YouBike, the Danhai and
Ankeng light rail lines, TRA, Maokong Gondola, intercity coaches, High Speed
Rail or taxis are all outside it[^trtc-faq-transfer].

### A new scheme: cashback for frequent riders

Separately from any pass, TRTC introduced a frequent-rider cashback scheme
on 3 January 2026, for full-fare stored-value or QR-code riders only — 社福卡
and other already-discounted card types do not qualify[^trtc-frequent-rider].
It is based on the *previous* calendar month's ride count: **11–20 rides
earns 5% cashback, 21–40 earns 10%, 41 or more earns 15%**, calculated
against that month's total fare spend and credited automatically to the same
card's e-wallet on the first tap of the following month, valid for one year
from the 1st of the crediting month[^trtc-frequent-rider].

## EasyCard, iPASS and icash: the overlap has edges

The current Taipei Metro payment table, fetched on 23 August 2026, lists
EasyCard, iPASS and icash as stored-value cards, but marks the NT$1,200 TPASS
as an EasyCard product rather than a pass available on all three cards[^trtc-payment-table].

iPASS's own current acceptance directory lists Taipei, New Taipei, Taoyuan,
Taichung and Kaohsiung rail and bus systems, TRA, and YouBike, while warning
that acceptance can still vary by named operator or route[^ipass-range-2026].

TRA's current electronic-ticket rules accept EasyCard, iPASS and icash on the
listed island and branch-line services, but exclude sightseeing, group,
Taroko, Puyuma, EMU3000 and other trains for which the rule says electronic
tickets are not valid[^tra-multi-card-2025].

The three cards therefore overlap on Taipei Metro and much of the ordinary
regional network without being interchangeable products: the TPASS container
is EasyCard-only, and iPASS publishes a wider national acceptance directory
than any single Taipei operator's fare table[^trtc-payment-table][^ipass-range-2026].

EasyCard's own company page lists non-transit uses including convenience
stores, supermarkets, dining, attractions, hospitals, government fees and
libraries[^easycard-about].

iPASS's current directory separately lists non-transit categories including
convenience stores, supermarkets, shopping, dining, attractions, cinemas,
hotels, libraries, government fees, school services, medical services,
donations and sports centres[^ipass-range-2026].

The current icash transport PDF establishes selected bus acceptance, but it is
not a complete current Taipei-region acceptance or refund catalogue, so this
guide does not infer broader icash non-transit coverage from it[^icash-transport-2026].

## Concessions: the identity document matters

The Taipei Metro ticket page retrieved on 23 August 2026 defines concessionary
eligibility for nationals aged 65 or over, a disability certificate holder and
one companion, and Taipei or New Taipei Indigenous residents aged 55 or over
with the required household documentation[^trtc-ticket-types-2026].

The same page defines the Taipei/New Taipei child ticket for a locally
registered child aged 6 to under 12 with household-registration proof, while
the existing metro fare guidance records the separate free-travel threshold
for a child under 6 or under 115 cm accompanied by a fare-paying passenger[^trtc-ticket-types-2026][^trtc-faq-free].

TRA's current rules use a different national definition: senior nationals are
65 or over, children are 6 to under 12, and a disability certificate holder
may travel with the necessary companion at the concessionary rate[^tra-multi-card-2025].

## Single-journey tokens and refunds

The Taipei Metro single-journey fare retrieved on 23 August 2026 is NT$20–65,
valid on the day of issue, and sold by station vending machines and station
information counters, with a QR single ticket also available through Taipei
Metro Go[^trtc-ticket-types-2026].

The token is a contactless ticket: the gate reads it at entry and recovers it
at the exit gate, so it is not a souvenir to carry through the journey[^trtc-ticket-types-2026].

An unused single ticket can be refunded only on its purchase day, while a used
ticket or one left after a midway exit is not refundable under the passenger
regulations retrieved on 23 August 2026[^trtc-passenger-rules-2025].

## Deposits and stored-value refunds

EasyCard's current refund FAQ requires the card to be locked before a
termination refund, charges NT$20 unless the card has been used at least five
times and held for at least three months, and gives no balance refund for an
anonymous lost card[^easycard-refund-2026].

For an EasyCard deposit product, the current formula is refundable deposit plus
usable balance minus the refund fee, with the card cost also deducted when the
card is damaged[^easycard-deposit-2026].

iPASS has no rental or deposit system: its current product page says only the
stored balance is refundable, not the product purchase price, and its return
process can charge NT$20 when the card has not met the five-use or three-month
threshold[^ipass-adult-2026][^ipass-refund-2026].

iPASS's current return process requires the physical card to be sent by
registered mail, and an anonymous lost card is not a recoverable balance under
the product rules[^ipass-refund-2026][^ipass-adult-2026].

The fetched icash transport document does not establish a current complete
icash refund rule, so that figure and procedure remain TBC rather than being
borrowed from EasyCard or iPASS[^icash-transport-2026].

## The NT$1,200 TPASS: coverage, purchase and take-up

The TPASS operating notice prices the 基北北桃 pass at NT$1,200 for 30 days of
unlimited use on the named MRT and light-rail systems, municipal buses, the
specified TRA segment, listed intercity-coach routes and YouBike coverage; the
TRA and coach clauses contain their own route and train exclusions[^megacity-notice].

The notice does not list Blue Highway ferries among those named coverage
categories, so this guide does not assume that a ferry fare is included in the
pass without a separate operator or government confirmation[^megacity-notice].

The current Taipei Metro purchase instructions say to select TPASS/1200 at
the station process, and explain how an active 1280 pass on the same EasyCard
is handled when the 1200 pass is purchased[^tpass-purchase-2026].

The latest take-up figure found in a full official page is dated 19 February
2025: Taipei City reported cumulative issuance above nine million passes and
more than 700 million uses, with the page updated on 13 March 2025[^tpass-takeup-2025].

Those are dated cumulative totals, not a 2026 monthly sales rate, so no newer
growth figure is inferred here[^tpass-takeup-2025].
