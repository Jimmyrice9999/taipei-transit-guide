Domestic aviation in Taiwan is two different businesses under one regulator.
On the main island, Taoyuan, Songshan, Kaohsiung and Taichung sit inside a
rail and highway network dense enough that flying between them is a
convenience, not a necessity. For Penghu, Kinmen, Matsu, Orchid Island and
Green Island, the same short-haul flights connect a sea crossing that can
run several hours to a scheduled flight of well under one, and a standing
national subsidy law prices those flights for island residents
accordingly — see [Airports](/air/airports/) for the rates by route.

Start with [Domestic route families](/air/routes/) for the dated airport-pair
view, then [Airports](/air/airports/) for the network station by station —
including the fare-subsidy law and CAA's own operating-loss subsidy scheme,
covered on the outlying-islands page where they apply — then
[Operators](/air/operators/) for UNI Air and Mandarin Airlines, the two
domestic carriers, both descended from older regional airlines folded into
the China Airlines and EVA Air groups.

This coverage sits alongside the site's rail, bus, ferry and bike-share
sections rather than duplicating them: an outlying island's ferry service is
covered under [Ferry](/ferry/), and this section instead covers the transport
some of those same islands depend on when the sea crossing is too slow,
too weather-dependent, or both.
