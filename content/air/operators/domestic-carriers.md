This page focuses on UNI Air and Mandarin Airlines, two carrier profiles tied
to Taiwan's two international full-service airline groups.[^uniair-history][^mandarin-history]
Daily Air's distinct fixed-wing island network is documented separately rather
than folded into either carrier's airport list.[^dailyair-flight-info]

## UNI Air

UNI Air is the EVA Air Group's domestic and near-regional carrier. Its
direct predecessor was 馬公航空 (Makung Airlines), a Penghu-based regional
carrier that the EVA Air Group bought into in 1995, renaming it 立榮航空
with a new corporate image the following year.[^uniair-history] The larger
change came in 1998: the carrier's own history page records a merger that
formally absorbed two other regional carriers, 大華航空 (Great China
Airlines) and 台灣航空 (Taiwan Airways), while simultaneously taking on EVA
Air's own domestic route network — its own account of the result is direct:
「立榮航空正式與大華航空及台灣航空合併，同時承接長榮航空之國內航線，成為台灣飛航國內航線最大的航空公司」,
becoming Taiwan's largest domestic-route carrier at that point.[^uniair-history]
UNI Air introduced its current corporate identity on 30 March
2012.[^uniair-history]

The carrier's own airport-information page lists ground service points at
11 airports: Taipei Songshan, Taichung, Chiayi, Tainan, Kaohsiung, Taitung,
Hualien, Penghu, Kinmen, and both Matsu fields at Nangan and
Beigan.[^uniair-airports] That list does not include Lanyu or Green Island,
which this section's [outlying-islands page](/air/airports/outlying-islands/)
records as served instead by Daily Air's smaller Twin Otter aircraft.[^dailyair-flight-info]

## Mandarin Airlines

Mandarin Airlines is China Airlines' domestic subsidiary, founded on 1 June
1991 as a joint venture between China Airlines and the 和信集團 (Hsin Hwa
Group): 「華信航空公司（MANDARIN AIRLINES）成立於1991年6月1日。最初是由中華航空與和信集團共同出資成立」.[^mandarin-history]
Its fleet, per the same page, is a mix of ATR72-600 turboprops, Boeing
737-800s and Airbus A321neos.[^mandarin-history] The carrier's own
destinations interface lists eight domestic airport codes it serves from
its Taipei Songshan base: Taichung, Kaohsiung, Hualien, Taitung, Penghu,
Kinmen and Matsu Nangan — seven destinations beyond the base
itself.[^mandarin-destinations]

## The networks do not fully overlap

Mandarin Airlines' seven-destination domestic network is narrower than UNI
Air's 11-airport one, and the two lists are not identical rather than one
simply being a subset of the other in every respect worth noting: Mandarin
Airlines does not appear on its own site to serve Chiayi, Tainan or Matsu
Beigan, all of which UNI Air lists as ground service points.[^mandarin-destinations][^uniair-airports]
A traveller choosing between the two carriers for, say, Chiayi or Matsu
Beigan does not actually have a choice — only one operator's own published
list includes that airport at all.

## What remains TBC
