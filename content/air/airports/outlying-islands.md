Taiwan's outlying-island airports share one reason for existing: the ferry
crossing was too slow, too weather-dependent, or both. Four are
covered here — Kinmen, Matsu's two fields, and Penghu — plus the
Taitung-administered strips at Lanyu (Orchid Island) and Green Island, which
CAA's own airport map lists by name alongside the others.[^caa-airport-list]

## Kinmen

An airfield existed at 西洪五里埔 from 1949, and 復興航空 was approved for a
weekly Kinmen flight in June 1951.[^kma-history] Service stopped entirely on
23 August 1958, when the station's own history page records it was halted
by the 823 artillery bombardment — 「因砲戰緣故而停航」.[^kma-history] Flying
later resumed from a relocated site at 尚義, and 遠東航空 (Far Eastern Air
Transport) opened the first civil Kinmen-Taipei flight in September
1987.[^kma-history] The station was formally established as a Class-C
(丙種) aviation station on 1 March 1994.[^kma-history] A new East Terminal,
costing NT$300 million, opened in February 2010, and the station was
upgraded to Class-B (乙等) status on 1 January 2011 after three consecutive
years above 1.5 million annual passengers.[^kma-history]

## Matsu: Nangan and Beigan

Matsu has two fields on two different islands. Nangan's was approved
1997-12-31, began construction in July 1998, was completed in December 2002
at a stated cost of NT$2.276 billion, and opened on 23 January
2003.[^msa-history] Beigan's opened earlier, on 17 January 1994 (民國八十三年元月十七日).[^msa-history]
Its runway was later relocated 160 metres east and rebuilt at 1,150 by 30
metres, work the station's own page dates to July 2000 (民國89年7月) — a
date worth stating precisely, since an ROC-to-Western date conversion error
elsewhere had this as 1989 before being checked against the primary
text.[^msa-history] Beigan's current terminal opened 28 January
2005.[^msa-history]

## Penghu

Civil Air Transport began C-46 service to Penghu in 1957 (民國46起).[^penghu-history]
Terminal construction began in 1966, when a waiting room was established
under the administration of Kaohsiung Aviation Station.[^penghu-history]
Penghu was not established as its own formal Class-C station until 1 August
1977 — the same primary page states both the 1966 terminal-building start
and the 1977 formal-establishment date, which are two different events, not
a conflict between sources.[^penghu-history] Penghu took over the smaller
Qimei and Wangan island stations on 11 May 1991, and was itself upgraded to
Class-B status on 18 January 1993.[^penghu-history] Most recently, Magong,
Qimei and Wangan stations were merged into one Penghu Aviation Station,
effective 15 September 2023.[^penghu-history]

## Lanyu and Green Island

CAA's own airport-distribution page names Lanyu (蘭嶼) and Green Island
(綠島) airports alongside every other station on this page.[^caa-airport-list]
Both are served by Daily Air (德安航空) using 19-seat DHC6-400 Twin Otter
aircraft on short island-hopping routes from Taitung.[^dailyair-flight-info]
The operator's full service page names five fixed-wing island route families,
including Taitung–Lanyu and Taitung–Green Island, and its live route page was
dated 6 September 2026 when fetched.[^dailyair-flight-info][^dailyair-live]
Detailed per-flight times and current fares remain TBC on the operator profile
because the linked timetable and fare pages did not complete a full fetch.
Whether Lanyu and Green Island are run as independent CAA stations or as
branch units of Taitung Aviation Station is still TBC: the airport-distribution
page lists them by name without stating administrative subordination either
way.[^caa-airport-list]

## The subsidy framework common to all of them

A single national regulation, the Regulations on Air Fare Subsidies for
Outlying-Island Residents (離島地區居民航空票價補貼辦法), sets resident fare
discounts for every route on this page.[^island-fare-subsidy-law] Its legal
basis is Civil Aviation Act Article 55 paragraph 6 and Offshore Islands
Development Act Article 15-1 paragraph 2.[^island-fare-subsidy-law] The
subsidy rate varies by route: 20% for Penghu-Magong and Kinmen-Shangyi
routes, 30% for the Lienchiang (Matsu) Nangan and Beigan routes, and 40% for
Penghu's Qimei and Wangan routes and Taitung's Lanyu and Ludao (Green
Island) routes, with a further 10% supplemental subsidy for Penghu and
Kinmen residents travelling to Taiwan proper.[^island-fare-subsidy-law]
Separately, CAA runs a recurring operating-loss subsidy scheme for carriers
on outlying and remote routes, published annually by fiscal year, though the
specific per-carrier amounts remain in linked documents this page has not
opened.[^caa-loss-subsidy]
