# DRAFT — The Matra dispute

**Not published.** This is a working draft for review, held outside `content/`
deliberately. Nothing here has been promoted to a page.

Researched 6 August 2026. Sources are cited in the original language with their
titles. Every claim carries a confidence level, and where sources disagree both
readings are recorded rather than one being chosen — there is a lot of that
here, because this was a politically contested affair that ran for seventeen
years and most English-language accounts of it are a paragraph long.

## How to read the confidence marks

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **High** | A primary source, or two independent secondary sources agreeing on specifics. |
| **Medium** | One good secondary source, internally consistent, nothing contradicting. |
| **Low** | Single source, or sources disagree, or the claim is plausible and unattributed. |
| **None** | Nothing found. Recorded so the gap is documented rather than re-searched. |

## 1. What this is actually about — and the thing almost everyone conflates

There are **two separate disputes**, thirteen years apart, and they are
routinely run together into one story about a French company storming off.

**Dispute A — the delay claim (1988–2005).** Matra sued for the cost of a
contract that ran years longer than it was written for, because the civil works
it depended on were late. This is the one that went to arbitration in 1993 and
was still in the Supreme Court in 2005. It is about money and contract law, and
Matra won it.

**Dispute B — the withdrawal (1996–97).** After the line opened, Matra pulled
its technical staff out of Taiwan and stopped maintaining the system. This is
the one everybody remembers, the one that produced 「馬特拉不拉，我們自己拉」,
and the one that drove every procurement decision afterwards.

They are connected — B happens because A poisoned the relationship — but they
are not the same event, and the NT$1.64 billion figure that circulates belongs
to A. **Confidence: High** on the distinction; the 自由時報 report of the 2005
judgment is explicit that the claim arises 「因土木工程部分包商施工延誤，連帶造成
馬特拉的工期延長，增加成本費用」.

## 2. The original contract

| Claim | Value | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Matra selected as preferred bidder | 20 April 1988 | Medium |
| Negotiations concluded | 25 June 1988 | Medium |
| Contract signed | 13 July 1988 | **High** — two sources, one contemporaneous news report |
| Value | approx. NT$7.87 billion | Medium |
| Scope | Muzha Line E&M, detailed design, 51 two-car sets (102 cars), testing, training | Medium |
| Expected completion | 1991 | Medium |

The 自由時報 report gives the signing as 「七十七年七月」 — July 1988 in ROC
years — which agrees with the 13 July date from 〈木柵線〉.

**Why Matra won.** 黃令名's account is that the tender was deliberately written
to a 「歐美標準」 rather than a US-only standard, opening it to European
bidders, and that VAL was at the time 「最成熟、先進的系統」. He also records,
without endorsing, that the award 「還是讓流言四起」 — rumours connecting it to
arms purchases from France. **Confidence: Medium** on the tender framing,
**Low** on the rumours, which he reports as rumours and so should we.

**Not established:** the tender documents themselves, the competing bids, and
the evaluation. These would be in DORTS's construction record. Not found online.

## 3. The technical failures

This is the part worth writing properly, because there is an actual engineering
explanation and it is better than "the French trains kept catching fire".

**The capacity change and what it did to the signalling.** 黃令名's account:
the specification was changed from two-car to four-car trains to increase
capacity, but the automatic train control was not redesigned to match. Instead
two two-car trains were coupled and run on a shared signal path. When the link
between them failed 「前車已經開始前進而後車因為沒有接收到訊息仍然將列車煞車鎖死」
— the leading unit accelerated while the trailing unit still had its brakes
locked.

On a steel-wheel railway that produces flats and a lot of noise. On rubber
tyres dragged along a concrete running surface it produces heat, and then it
produces fire. That is the mechanism behind the two 1993 incidents.

**Confidence: Medium.** 黃令名 is a sociologist of technology writing from his
own doctoral research, which is a good deal better than a forum post, but this
is a single scholarly account of a technical causal chain and I found no
engineering report confirming it. An accident investigation would settle it.

**The recorded incidents**, from 〈木柵線〉:

| Date | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 5 May 1993 | First fire, during testing, near 辛亥站. Attributed to 「煞車系統故障及人為疏忽」 — brake system failure and human error. Matra apologised publicly on 14 May. |
| From April 1993 | Cracked cap beams found at 六張犁 and 萬芳社區 among others; structural investigation and reinforcement demanded. |
| 19 June 1993 | Testing suspended again; Matra later attributed smoke and fire events to 「晶片問題」 — chip faults. |
| 24 September 1993 | Second fire, between 六張犁 and 麟光. |
| 1 July 1994 | Matra stops work; resumes 23 July. |
| 25 August 1994 | Final inspections conclude. |
| 28 March 1996 | Line opens, four and a quarter years late. |

**Confidence: Medium** on the individual dates — they come from one
encyclopedia article, and the two fires are corroborated by 民報's retrospective
and by the 自由時報 special report's reference to 「接連被兩把大火燒得焦黑的
電聯車，以及將近兩百根暴露著裂縫的捷運支柱」.

**The cap beams are worth noting separately.** Nearly two hundred columns with
visible cracks is a civil-works failure, not an E&M one — it belongs to the
other contractors, and it is part of why Matra had a delay claim at all.

## 4. The arbitration and the litigation

The full sequence, from the 自由時報 report of the final judgment. This is the
best-sourced part of the whole story and the part least often told accurately.

| Date | Stage | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| — | Matra's claim | 「廿多億元」 — over NT$2 billion |
| — | Negotiations | Collapse |
| **6 Oct 1993** | 中華民國商務仲裁協會 arbitration award | For Matra: NT$300m+, US$5.2m+, FRF 100m+ — 「總計折合台幣約十億兩千五百萬元」 ≈ **NT$1.025 bn**, plus 5% annual interest |
| — | DORTS files 撤銷仲裁判斷訴訟 to annul the award | Loses |
| **Nov 2000** | Supreme Court | Award stands |
| Late 2000 | Matra applies to enforce; DORTS files 債務人異議訴訟 | Argues the claim is time-barred: a 「承攬報酬合約」 carries a two-year limitation, five with arbitration, so it expired 6 Oct 1998 |
| — | District Court, then High Court | **DORTS wins** — claim held extinguished |
| — | Supreme Court remands to the High Court | — |
| — | 更一審 (High Court, rehearing) | Reclassifies the contract as a 「買賣、承攬的混合契約」 — a mixed sale-and-works contract — which carries a **fifteen-year** limitation. Matra is in time. |
| **22 July 2005** | Supreme Court, final | DORTS's appeal dismissed. **Principal plus interest ≈ NT$1.64 bn** |

**Confidence: High.** This is a contemporaneous news report of a named judgment
with figures, stages and reasoning, and the reasoning is internally coherent —
the reclassification turns on the contract covering basic design, route
definition, detailed design of fixed installations, civil structural design,
*and* the purchase, installation and testing of vehicles and E&M equipment,
which is a description of something more than a works contract.

**The interesting legal point**, if the article gets written: DORTS very nearly
won on limitation. Two courts agreed with it. What defeated it was the
characterisation of the contract, and that characterisation follows directly
from Taipei having bought a *turnkey system* rather than hiring a contractor —
which is the same decision that produced the technical lock-in later.

**Conflict to record.** A search result attributes a High Court rehearing in
Matra's favour to **28 July 1999**, and the 自由時報 account has the annulment
suit running 「近七年」 to a Supreme Court decision in **November 2000**, with
the debtor's-objection suit following. These may be different stages of the same
long sequence, or the 1999 date may be wrong. **Not resolved.** Do not publish a
1999 date without checking it against the judgment itself.

## 5. The withdrawal, and the year it happened

**This is where the sources actually disagree, and the disagreement matters.**

〈木柵線〉 says, of the period after the March 1996 opening:
「在通車兩個月之後，還發生機電商法商馬特拉無預警撤離」 — two months after
opening, so around **May 1996**.

The same article elsewhere, and 〈文湖線〉, put it at **1997**:
「1997年，馬特拉與台北市政府捷運工程局發生合約糾紛後，將技術人員全數撤離，不再
提供維修服務」.

**Confidence: Low on the date, High on the fact.** Both readings are in
circulation and I could not separate them. One plausible reconciliation is that
staff began leaving in 1996 and the formal end of maintenance came in 1997, but
that is a guess and is labelled as one. **Do not publish a year for this
without a contemporaneous news report.**

**What is not in dispute:** Matra stopped providing maintenance, and Taipei had
a driverless metro it could not service.

## 6. What Taipei did about it

黃令名's central claim, and the reason his piece is the most valuable source
found across three runs: Taiwanese engineers kept the line running by methods
that were improvised, effective and legally uncomfortable. He lists
「偷偷複製法方半成品的列車控制程式、請台廠製造甚至赴光華商場取得電子零件」 —
covertly copying the French side's half-finished train control software, having
parts made domestically, and buying electronic components at Guanghua Market.

He frames this as 「技術能動性」 — technological agency — and contrasts it
explicitly with Jacksonville, Florida, which faced a comparable situation with a
Matra system and demolished the whole thing, rebuilding with a Bombardier
monorail.

**Confidence: Medium** on the substance, and this is the load-bearing judgement
in the whole draft. It is one scholar's account, drawn from his own doctoral
research, and it is not neutral — the framing is a thesis about technological
dependence. It is also referenced, specific and published under his own name,
which is more than any other account of these events manages.

**The Guanghua Market detail is the one to be careful with.** It is close kin
to the hardware-dongle story that this project has twice declined to publish for
lack of a source, and it may be the same anecdote with the serial numbers filed
off. If the article is written, this needs either a second independent source or
explicit attribution to 黃令名 in the text rather than a footnote.

**The hardware-lock story remains unsourced.** The claim that Matra locked the
control software with hardware dongles on withdrawal, and that the city sourced
matching dongles from a computer retailer, was recorded in run 1 as circulating
without attribution. Three runs, no primary source, and no secondary source
better than a partisan blog. **Confidence: Low. Do not publish.**

## 7. The political fallout

- **The fires cost a mayor his job.** 自由時報's commentary states that the
  木柵線 fires 「間接導致了當時台北市長黃大洲的敗選下台」 — Huang Ta-chou lost
  the 1994 election. **Confidence: Medium.** It is a newspaper's causal claim
  about an election, which is a genre with a poor record, but the timing works
  and it is widely repeated.
- **陳水扁 succeeded him**, and the line opened on his watch on 28 March 1996.
  His 「馬特拉不拉，我們自己拉」 — roughly, *if Matra won't pull, we'll pull it
  ourselves* — is the single most quoted line about the Taipei Metro.
  **Confidence: High** on the quotation; it appears in every account.
  **Confidence: Low** on the date and setting, which vary between tellings.
- **The bill fell to a third mayor.** By July 2005 the mayor was 馬英九, who
  said the city would pay what the judgment required. The DORTS director 常岐德
  「躲起來，手機關機」 — went to ground with his phone switched off.
  **Confidence: High**, reported directly.
- **The public paid.** 自由時報's commentary is blunt about it: 「全民買單繳
  『學費』，足足要花十六億四千萬元」.

## 8. Why Bombardier got the Neihu extension

This is the question the site actually needs answered, and 黃令名 answers it
directly. Two changes to the procurement:

1. **CBTC was specified**, rather than continuing the Matra fixed-block system.
2. **Civil works and E&M were let as one integrated contract**, rather than
   split between contractors.

His argument is that this combination is what stopped **Siemens** — which by
then owned VAL, having taken Matra Transport International to 100% in 2001 —
from 「開出高價」, naming its own price as the only party who could extend the
existing system. Instead Canada's Bombardier won.

**Confidence: Medium.** It is one account of a procurement rationale, and
procurement rationales are exactly the thing organisations rewrite afterwards.
But it is consistent with what was actually bought.

**The engineering consequence, and it is the good bit.** Bombardier could not
supply a VAL, because VAL belonged to a competitor. So it built the **Innovia
APM 256** — a vehicle dimensioned and interfaced to run on somebody else's
guideway. Same running-surface spacing, same side guide beams, same 750 V taken
off those beams, and a car body twenty millimetres *narrower* than the VAL256 it
would run beside.

And then, rather than running two signalling systems, the whole line was
switched to CITYFLO 650 on the day the extension opened — 4 July 2009 — and the
1996 fleet was withdrawn for eighteen months to be converted, returning to
service on 26 December 2010. **Confidence: High** on the dates; corroborated
across the Chinese and English Wikipedia articles and consistent with TRTC's own
2010 announcements.

黃令名 adds a detail worth checking: that Bombardier's CITYFLO was tested at
night on the Muzha Line's own tracks while the Matra VAL carried passengers by
day, 「without public knowledge」. **Confidence: Low** — striking, single-source,
and exactly the kind of claim that gets repeated because it is good.

## 9. What I could not find

- **The contract itself**, or any DORTS publication of its terms.
- **The arbitration award**, or any of the judgments. Taiwan's judicial
  decisions are searchable, and the 2005 Supreme Court civil judgment ought to
  be retrievable by date. That would settle the whole of §4 at primary level and
  is the single highest-value follow-up in this draft.
- **A contemporaneous report of the withdrawal**, which would settle the
  1996-vs-1997 question in §5.
- **Any accident investigation** into the 1993 fires, which would confirm or
  kill the coupling explanation in §3.
- **Anything from Matra's side.** Every source here is Taiwanese. Lagardère's
  archive, French trade press and Siemens's own history of the VAL business
  would give the other account, and its absence is the largest structural bias
  in this draft.

## 11. If this gets written

**Recommended shape.** Lead with the engineering, not the lawsuit. The fires
have a mechanical explanation involving a capacity change that outran the
signalling design, and that is a better and more transferable story than a
procurement row. Then the money. Then the consequence, which is that Taipei's
answer to being locked in was to require a competitor to build to the incumbent's
guideway — and got it.

**Leave out:** the hardware dongles, the 1999 date, and any specific year for
the withdrawal.

**Get first:** the 2005 Supreme Court judgment. It would move most of §4 from
Medium to primary-sourced, and it is a document that exists and is indexed.
