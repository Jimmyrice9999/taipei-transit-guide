## The technical story — why 2.0 exists

**1.0 and 2.0 are incompatible systems.** Docks are not interchangeable, and
during the overlap the city had to warn people to check which type they were
returning to.

### The key difference: 2.0 docks need no electricity

1.0 docks required a power supply, which meant **road excavation and a Taipower
connection** for every station. That made siting slow, expensive and politically
fraught.

2.0 uses **輕樁 "light posts"** — no electricity, no excavation, no utility
connection. **Intelligence moved from the dock to an onboard unit on the bicycle.**

**That single design decision is why 2.0 could expand so much faster**, and it's
the heart of any article on this.

### Other 2.0 changes

- **QR-code hire** rather than card-only
- **Onboard lock**, so a rider can stop temporarily without needing to keep a
  separate key
- More intuitive saddle adjustment

### The changeover mechanics

Managed with cross-city grace periods. A **NT$350 misplacement fee** applied to
1.0 bikes returned across the city boundary after cutover — New Taipei retained
1.0 briefly after Taipei retired it.

Fleet numbers through the transition:

| Point | Fleet |
|---|---|
| Peak, during 1.0/2.0 overlap | **≈23,000** |
| At 1.0 retirement | **≈13,000** |

### What happened to the 1.0 bikes

Transferred to city agencies, sold to outside bodies, or sold through Taipei's
second-hand goods platform. **Each bike costs roughly NT$10,000** because they're
built for heavy shared use. Retired 1.0 docks were removed and pavements restored.

## The Putian oddity — worth a paragraph

Giant's Kunshan operation developed a separate **"YouBike electric assist bike"**
for **Putian, China**, based on the **Giant MIYA E+**.

**It uses YouBike 1.0 docks**, so it has no compatibility problem — the opposite
of the situation in Taipei.

- **Fifty units deployed on Meizhou Island, 16 October 2020**
- Three power modes
- ~80 km range
- 3-speed internal hub gear
- **Handlebar dashboard instead of an onboard unit**

A Taiwanese bike-share system's retired dock standard finding a second life in a
Chinese city, with a different bike and a different control architecture. Nobody
has written this in English.
