## The structural fact English sources miss

Taipei's city buses run as **聯營公車 — a joint-operation system**. Around twenty
private companies operate under one fare structure, one route numbering scheme and
one brand. To a passenger it looks like a single network. **It is not.**

**Revenue is pooled and centrally redistributed** (營業收入統一，統籌分配) while
operators otherwise remain fully independent companies.

This is neither a franchised model (like London) nor a contracted model. It's a
revenue-pooling cartel with government oversight, and it's the single most useful
thing a page could explain.

## History

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **1969** | Market opens to private operators under district-operation rules (分區營運管制規則) |
| **1976** | 臺北市公民營公車聯營籌備委員會 formed under Mayor **林洋港** |
| **30 April 1977** | **Joint operation begins** — **149 routes**, 2,325 vehicles planned, **1,984 actually running**, 5 private operators joining the municipal fleet |
| June–July 1977 | **319 cases of ticket fraud (吃票)** recorded in two months under the initial four-part revenue split, prompting the introduction of tokens (代幣) |
| **1 January 2004** | The municipal 臺北市公共汽車管理處 is privatised, becoming **大都會客運 Metropolitan Transport** |

**The 吃票 problem is a good detail.** The original four-part (四分法) revenue
split gave drivers an incentive to pocket fares, so the city introduced physical
tokens to remove cash from the transaction. A specific, human failure of
mechanism design.

### Today

Roughly **280+ routes, 14 operators, ~3,500 vehicles**.

Governance: the **臺北市公車聯營管理委員會 / 管理中心**, with **twice-yearly
service evaluation** by **Taipei PTO (公共運輸處)**.

*Sources: Taipei PTO (pto.gov.taipei) — **primary**; zh.wikipedia 臺北市市區公車;
academic papers via Airiti and TASPAA.*

## Route numbering — SOLVED, and it's more interesting than expected

The numbering is not arbitrary. It encodes **era, geography and network role**, and
the coloured routes turn out not to be colours at all.

### The number bands — geography and era

Routes were first set up jointly by the **Taiwan Provincial Government Bus
Department** and Taipei City from the **1950s**, banded by operating area and
function:

| Band | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **1–99** | **Core urban routes** — the old city: Zhongzheng, Datong, Zhongshan, Wanhua |
| **100–199** | **Outward expansion** — Neihu, Nangang, Wenshan |
| **200–299** | **Cross-district trunk routes** — Banqiao, Sanchong, Luzhou into Taipei |

**So a route number is a rough date stamp and a geography clue at once.** A 2xx
route tells you it crosses a municipal boundary; a 2-digit route tells you it serves
the historic core.

*Source: vocus.cc feature — **secondary**, and the band definitions should be
confirmed against Taipei PTO before publishing as fact.*

### The coloured routes are MRT feeder buses — this is the key finding

**紅 red, 綠 green, 藍 blue, 棕 brown are not decorative. They are the MRT line
colours.**

From **24 December 1999**, Taipei introduced **捷運接駁公車 MRT feeder buses**,
serving passengers between MRT stations and areas the metro doesn't reach. The rule:
**the route must pass an MRT station, and usually terminates at one.** The number is
prefixed with **the Chinese and English colour name of the MRT line it feeds**.

| Prefix | English | MRT line |
|---|---|---|
| **紅** | **R** | Tamsui–Xinyi (Red) |
| **綠** | **G** | Songshan–Xindian (Green) |
| **藍** | **BL** | Bannan (Blue) |
| **棕** | **BR** | Wenhu (Brown) |

Worked examples from TRTC's own documentation:
- **藍7 (BL7)** — 捷運藍線（板南線）市政府站 to 大直
- **棕10 (BR10)** — 東湖 to 捷運棕線（文湖線）南京復興站

**Nothing in English explains this**, and it means a visitor seeing 藍26 currently
has no way to know it's a Bannan Line feeder. **This single table is probably the
highest-value thing the bus section could publish.**

Note the Airport MRT had not been given its own prefix as of the source consulted —
**confirm whether it has since.**

### Suffixes and modifiers

| Suffix | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **區** | 區間車 — **short working** | 紅35區, 藍20區, 小5區, 紅55區 |
| **副** | **Branch route** | 藍7副, 棕11副, 藍21副 |
| **延** | **Extension** | 藍41延和, 藍43延南天母, 藍44延 |
| **左 / 右** | **Anticlockwise / clockwise** on a loop | 綠2左, 綠2右 |
| Named variant | Route variant via a named place | 棕7建業路, 棕7綠野香坡, 綠9耕莘, 綠9北新國小, 紅37行經海洋都心社區 |

**The named variants are unusual** — rather than a number suffix, the destination
blind carries the road or estate name. Worth showing a photograph of.

### 小 minibus routes

Prefix **小** with a number: 小1, 小2, 小5, 小9 and so on, plus 區 short workings.
Typically hill and feeder routes using smaller vehicles.

**小9 doubles as a Taiwan Tourist Shuttle route** — 小9(台灣好行-北投竹子湖) — so
one route carries both a city bus number and a national tourism-branded identity.

### 市民小巴 Citizen Minibus

From **2007**, Taipei's Transport Bureau planned **"last mile" community feeders**
between neighbourhoods and MRT stations. Rules: **routes mostly do not cross
district boundaries**, and must pass an MRT station or bus interchange.

All are prefixed **市民小巴**; in English, **M**.

**Eleven routes were planned.** Four in Datong and Wanhua were redirected into the
西區風華再現 West District revival policy and became 主題專車 instead. **The other
six opened progressively from December 2007.**

### 主題專車 Theme buses — a failed experiment worth documenting

From **2008**, tourism-focused routes sitting between the citizen minibus and a
leisure bus, concentrated in the older western districts of Wanhua and Datong.

**Only two routes ever ran, and both failed:**

- The **Ximending theme bus** stopped when the 2008 Ximending pedestrian-zone
  expansion trial ended
- The **Dadaocheng waterfront bus** stopped in **2011**, for insufficient ridership

**A short, complete story about a category of service that was created, tried twice
and abandoned.** Nobody has written it up.

### 通勤 commuter routes

A separate prefix exists — 通勤7, 通勤9, 通勤11, 通勤12, 通勤13, 通勤16, 通勤21,
通勤22, 通勤24, 通勤25, 通勤26, plus 907通勤. **Purpose and rules TBC.**

### 9xxx routes

National highway coaches — a different regulatory category entirely, not part of the
joint-operation system.

## 幹線公車 Trunk buses — Taipei's "metro-like" bus network

Officially **臺北市幹線公車**, English **Taipei Metro Bus**. Also called the
**八橫八縱** network — "eight horizontal, eight vertical" — after its grid shape.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Routes | **19**, formed from existing joint-operation routes on major arterials |
| Character | **類捷運** — "metro-like" |
| Peak headway | **4–6 minutes** |
| Off-peak headway | ~5 minutes |
| Infrastructure | Largely runs in **bus lanes** |
| Transfers | Discounts across TRTC metro, Taipei joint buses, and New Taipei express and trunk buses |

### The colour coding is directional

Among the 19 trunk routes:

| Colour | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Green** | **North–south** |
| **Blue** | **East–west** |
| **Yellow** | Neither |

**Note the collision:** trunk-route green and blue mean *compass direction*, while
feeder-route 綠 and 藍 mean *MRT line*. Same colours, two entirely different
meanings, on the same network. **That's confusing even to residents and it's exactly
what a reference site should disentangle.**

### Origin — a 2014 election promise

The trunk network came out of **Ko Wen-je's 2014 Taipei mayoral campaign**, whose
central transport policy was to restructure the bus network on four principles:

1. **Replan routes**
2. **Merge duplicated routes**
3. **Cancel inefficient long routes**
4. **Adopt efficient short-distance feeders**

And to re-tier the whole network into four levels:

**快速 express · 幹線 trunk · 支線 branch · 微循環 micro-circulation**

**That four-tier hierarchy is the modern organising logic of Taipei's bus network**
and it should probably lead the explainer, with the historical number bands
underneath it.

### The renaming, April 2018

Existing routes were rebranded as trunk routes:

| Old | New | Operator |
|---|---|---|
| **棕9 (BR9)** | **南京幹線** Nanjing Trunk | Capital Bus |
| **紅32 (R32)** | **民權幹線** Minquan Trunk | Capital Bus |
| **220** | **中山幹線** Zhongshan Trunk | Kuanghua Bus |

**Note what this did to the numbering system:** a Wenhu feeder (棕9) and a Tamsui–
Xinyi feeder (紅32) both became arterial trunk routes named after the streets they
run on. The colour-prefix logic was abandoned for those routes entirely.

## The bus network responds to new rail — a live example

When the **Sanying Line opened on 30 June 2026**, the bus network was restructured
around it:

- Routes **706, 707, 889 and 731** were adjusted
- Route **812** was **shortened to 捷運橫溪站** and **renumbered 三鶯2線**

**A numbered joint-operation route becoming a metro feeder with a line-named
identity, on the day the line opened.** That's a perfect, dated, concrete example of
how the bus network reorganises around rail — and it happened five weeks ago.

*Source: Taipei PTO / 大臺北公車 service notices — primary.*

### Cross-holdings — a genuine research topic

Taipei's operators are not as independent as the list suggests, and **the
ownership graph is completely undocumented in English.**

**The documented case:** in **December 2010**, Capital Bus chairman **李博文** took
**7 of 11 board seats at Metropolitan Transport** with a combined **52.5%
holding**, beating the four government-held seats. MTC had been privatised in
2004.

Independently, EasyCard corporate history identifies 李博文 as the **bus
association chairman and a former TRTC chairman** who "推動艱難的交通整合…第二次
合作" — pushing through difficult transport integration. That confirms he was
central to both bus consolidation *and* the EasyCard project.

**Find the other cross-holdings.** A single figure sitting at the centre of bus
consolidation, EasyCard and TRTC is a story in itself.

## Vehicles

### Historic and current chassis

Mitsubishi Fuso, Hino, **Volvo** (including the **B8RLE** low-floor), Daewoo, and
**Foton hybrid low-floor buses from September 2011** — Metropolitan Transport
being the third Taipei operator to adopt them.

### Electrification — the current story, and domestic industry is winning

**MOTC's three-phase plan:**

| Phase | Years |
|---|---|
| 先導期 Pilot | 2020–2022 |
| 推廣期 Promotion | 2023–2026 |
| 普及期 Universal | 2027–2030 |

Funding: **≈NT$20 billion (200億)** committed, rising to as much as **NT$60
billion (600億) by 2030**. National subsidy targets: **1,600 vehicles in 2025,
2,000 in 2026**.

**Five domestic electric bus manufacturers:**

| Chinese | Notes |
|---|---|
| **華德動能 Master Transportation** | Brand **RAC**. First to pass BSMI charging equipment safety standards. **Over 65% domestic content.** ~25–26% market share — the largest. ~**771 vehicles** cumulative across 12 counties by late 2025; claims "累計量產近千輛". 2026 H1 revenue **NT$1.838 billion, +142.7% YoY** |
| 成運汽車 | |
| 凱勝綠能 | |
| 唐榮汽車 | |
| 創奕能源 | |

**The constraint nobody reports:** the MOTC demonstration programme approved
**270 vehicles** from Master Transportation in 2022, of which **230 were
delivered**. The remaining ~40 were delayed because **operators' charging
infrastructure wasn't ready.** The bottleneck is depots and grid connections, not
vehicle supply.

**This is the frame for a bus models section** — not a chassis catalogue, but an
industrial policy story where a domestic manufacturing base was built on the back
of a public procurement programme.

(Master Transportation).*

## Bus routes — pull, don't research

Roughly **400 joint-operation routes**. Route names, stop sequences, operators and
geometry are all in **TDX's static bus datasets**, in the same form as the metro
data already pulled.

**Hand-researching 400 routes would be slower and less accurate than a script.**

What genuinely needs writing rather than pulling:
- The joint-operation system itself
- Route numbering conventions
- Operator histories and corporate structure
- Vehicle types and the electrification programme
- Garages and depots
- Bus lanes and priority corridors
- Fare structure and transfer discounts

## Sources for fleet data

- **Operator websites carry 車型介紹 vehicle-type pages** — Metropolitan Transport
  has one. **Primary, and the best starting point.**
- **台灣巴士文化百科** (twbuswikib.fandom.com) — a Taiwanese enthusiast wiki with
  detailed **配車表** fleet lists, including registration ranges by year and which
  operators bought the same model. Comprehensive but user-generated: **treat as a
  lead, not a citation.**
- **MOTC Highway Bureau** publishes national electric bus counts by manufacturer
  as open data. **Primary.**
