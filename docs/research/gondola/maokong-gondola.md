## Why this deserves a page

- **TRTC-operated** — genuinely part of the metro network's organisation, not a
  separate tourist attraction
- Taipei's first tourist cable car, Taiwan's longest, and the first with a public
  transport character
- **An 18-month closure caused by a tower foundation failure**, assessed by four
  separate engineering institutes
- **Crystal cabins with a 213 kg glass floor** that carry three fewer passengers
  than standard cars — an engineering trade-off you can explain in one sentence
- English coverage is entirely tourist-oriented. The engineering and incident
  history are untouched.

## The T16 failure — the real story

### What happened

**Typhoon Jangmi (薔蜜) struck on 24 September 2008.** Tower **T16** suffered
**severe foundation ground loss**.

September 2008 rainfall reached **1,271.5 mm**, driving progressive slope failure.
The technical assessment found the **井式基礎 (well foundation)** was left
**cantilevered by roughly 4 metres** and **no longer met seismic safety
requirements**.

The city suspended service at **15:00 on 1 October 2008**.

### The assessment

**Four professional engineering institutes** were engaged, covering:
- Civil engineering
- Structural engineering
- Geotechnical engineering
- Soil and water conservation

**Four separate institutes for one tower** gives a sense of how serious it was.

### The closure

**Roughly 18 months.** Reopened **23 March 2010** (railfan sources; zh.wikipedia
gives "March 2010").

### Reopening and early wobbles

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **21 March 2010** | Reopening rules published |
| **28 March 2010** | Six-day free-ride period ends — **102,000 people carried** |
| **7 April 2010** | **Tower T15 tilt monitoring alarm** — subsequently confirmed a **false alarm** |
| **9 April 2010** | 100,000 cumulative riders in the first week |
| **9 April 2010, 20:09** | Cable detection alarm, **4 minutes 5 seconds** stoppage, no passengers trapped, normal service by 20:50 |
| **29 April 2010** | 270,000 cumulative |

**The T15 false alarm three weeks after reopening** is a telling detail — the
monitoring was clearly set conservatively after T16, which is exactly what you'd
expect and exactly what nobody documents.

## The crystal cabins — 貓纜之眼 "Eyes of Maokong"

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Entered service | **2 November 2010** |
| Quantity | **20** |
| Cabin numbers | **1–10 and 138–147** |
| Frequency | Roughly one every **five minutes** |
| Floor construction | **Three layers of tempered glass** |
| Floor thickness | **≈48 mm** |
| Floor weight | **213 kg** |
| Capacity | **5 passengers** (vs 8 standard) |

Installed under supervision from **SIGMA**, POMA's cabin manufacturer. **CE
certified.**

**The trade-off is the story:** a 213 kg glass floor costs you three passengers
per cabin. That's a clean, concrete engineering explanation that any reader
understands immediately, and it's the kind of thing this site should specialise
in.

## Operating rules and suspension conditions

**Service suspends when:**
- Gust **≥16 m/s sustained for 3 seconds**, or **≥18 m/s**
- **Lightning** on the alignment
- **Earthquake intensity ≥4**

**Maintenance closure:** the **first Monday of each month**, except when that
falls on a public holiday.

*Source: gondola.taipei — **primary**.*

The explicit numerical suspension thresholds are unusually well published and
worth reproducing, because "closed due to weather" is all English sources say.
