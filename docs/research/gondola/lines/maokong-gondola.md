## Why this matters

- TRTC-adjacent tourist gondola with a genuine engineering incident: a tower
  foundation undermined by a typhoon, a four-association forensic assessment,
  a Control Yuan corrective resolution naming five separate parties at fault,
  and a relocated tower.
- English coverage is tourist-only. Nothing in English covers the T16 failure,
  the Control Yuan's findings, or the station renaming history.
- The Control Yuan's 糾正案文 (corrective resolution) against Taipei City
  Government is a primary document that settles several points the existing
  lead file left as open questions, including whether T16 was rebuilt in place
  or relocated.

## What is established

### The system, as built

- **Length 4.03 km**, opened **4 July 2007**, TRTC-affiliated.
  Source: 台北市政府產業發展局/新建工程處 as summarised by 游裕偉 (臺北市大地工程處
  坡地整治科股長), ["臺北市貓空纜車T16塔柱下邊坡整治工程（上）"](http://etimes.twce.org.tw/%E5%9C%B0%E5%B7%A5%E6%8A%80%E8%A1%93/1791-%E8%87%BA%E5%8C%97%E5%B8%82%E8%B2%93%E7%A9%BA%E7%BA%9C%E8%BB%8At16%E5%A1%94%E6%9F%B1%E4%B8%8B%E9%82%8A%E5%9D%A1%E6%95%B4%E6%B2%BB%E5%B7%A5%E7%A8%8B%EF%BC%88%E4%B8%8A%EF%BC%89.html)
  (台灣省土木技師公會 技師報, published in the association's own journal, author
  named and affiliated with the city government's own geotechnical office).
  PRIMARY. High confidence — the author is a city government engineer writing
  in his own professional capacity, and the article's project chronology is
  independently corroborated by the Control Yuan's corrective resolution
  (below).
  「94年5月2日以最有利標方式決標，由春原營造股份有限公司以統包方式得標...94年11月10日
  工程開工，96年6月28日完成履勘複驗，並於96年7月4日交由捷運公司正式啟用營運，於96年
  12月19日完成驗收。」
- The **current official length is given as 4.033 km**, current station and
  tower count as **"6 stations (4 passenger, 2 angle), 25 pier bases, 47
  tower columns."**
  Source: gondola.taipei, ["路線與系統" (Route and System)](https://www.gondola.taipei/cp.aspx?n=8FE9610BDDB5332A),
  fetched directly (raw HTML) 12 August 2026. PRIMARY, current operator page.
  「纜車全程共計6個場站（4個車站、2個轉角站）及25處墩座、47支塔柱，全長4.033公里，略呈
  7字型。」
  This resolves a granular point the Control Yuan document states differently
  (see Conflicts): the operator's own current page distinguishes 25 **pier
  bases** (墩座) from 47 **tower columns** (塔柱) — i.e. most piers carry a
  two-legged portal frame, so "25 towers" and "47 towers" are both defensible
  depending on which structure is being counted.
- **Contract and construction chronology**, corroborated independently by two
  primary sources (the technician-association article above, and the Control
  Yuan resolution below): planning by Taipei City Department of Transportation
  (交通局); construction by the New Construction Office (工務局新建工程處);
  project management/supervision by 財團法人中華顧問工程司 (renamed 台灣世曦工程
  顧問股份有限公司 on 27 March 2004) and 武林建築師事務所; design-build contractor
  春原營造股份有限公司, awarded 2 May 2005 under 最有利標 (most-advantageous
  tender); detailed design began 3 May 2005; construction began 10 November
  2005; 履勘 (final inspection) completed 28 June 2007; opened to the public
  4 July 2007; formal acceptance 19 December 2007.
  Source: 監察院 (Control Yuan), 糾正案文 [Corrective Resolution against Taipei
  City Government](https://cybsbox.cy.gov.tw/CYBSBoxSSL/edoc/download/63975)
  (PDF, downloaded and read directly). PRIMARY — the Control Yuan is the
  constitutional oversight body and this is its own investigative finding.
  「本工程由台灣世曦工程顧問股份有限公司（前財團法人中華顧問工程司）及武林建築師事務所
  負責監造業務，並於94年5月2日以最有利標方式決標，由春原營造股份有限公司以統包方式
  得標...94年11月10日工程開工，96年6月28日完成履勘作業，96年7月4日通車啟用。」
- **Route/ropeway manufacturer: POMA (France).** Confirmed independently by
  both the Control Yuan resolution ("纜車系統採法國POMA公司產品") and
  gondola.taipei's own crystal-cabin page (below, referring to POMA's cabin
  manufacturer SIGMA). PRIMARY (both).

### The T16 failure

- **Landslide chronology**: the slope below T16 began failing on **27
  September 2008**; the first and second sections of the slide slumped on
  **28 September 2008**; a third, residual section collapsed after further
  heavy rain on **9 November 2008**. The typhoon responsible, **薔蜜 (Typhoon
  Jangmi)**, affected Taiwan **26–30 September 2008** and made landfall near
  Nan'ao, Yilan, at **15:40 on 28 September 2008** — it did not strike on 24
  September, the date it *formed* over the Philippine Sea.
  Sources: 監察院, 糾正案文 (PDF, as above) — 「97 年 9 月 27 日貓纜系統 T16 塔柱下方
  邊坡因持續豪雨坍滑」and「97 年 9 月 28 日，第 1 段及第 2 段土體陸續坍滑，殘留第 3 段
  土體則在 11 月 9 日歷經一陣豪雨後亦告崩落」; landfall timing corroborated by news
  search results summarising 中央氣象局/公開報導 on Typhoon Jangmi's track. PRIMARY
  (Control Yuan) for the slope chronology; the landfall time is from
  aggregated secondary reporting, not independently fetched from a single
  primary page — Medium confidence on the exact landfall minute, High
  confidence that landfall (not formation) was 28 September.
- **Rainfall**: cumulative September 2008 rainfall in the Muzha area reached
  **1,271.5 mm**, per the Central Weather Bureau's own statistics as quoted in
  the Control Yuan resolution — the second-highest September figure the CWB
  had on record for the area in 22 years (behind 1,843 mm in September 2001;
  1,318 mm in October 1998 was higher still but a different month). This
  1,271.5 mm figure is the **cumulative total across three consecutive
  typhoons** (辛樂克 11–16 Sept, 哈格比 21–23 Sept, 薔蜜 26–30 Sept), not rainfall
  from Jangmi alone. Separately, the geotechnical-office article states
  **Jangmi alone dropped roughly 500 mm** on Wenshan District on 28 September,
  which is what triggered the reported landslide behind the 政大御花園社區
  community that night.
  Sources: 監察院, 糾正案文 (PDF) — 「9 月累積降雨量依中央氣象局統計資料高達 1271.5
  公釐，在木柵地區近 22 年的月降雨量中，僅次於 87 年 10 月的 1,318 公釐及 90 年 9 月的
  1,843 公釐」; 游裕偉, 技師報 article (as above) — 「97年9月28日薔蜜颱風於文山地區連續
  降下約500公釐之超大豪雨」. Both PRIMARY. High confidence — these are two
  different, compatible measurements (monthly cumulative vs. single-storm),
  not a contradiction; see Conflicts for how they were initially
  cross-checked.
- **Evacuation**: about 72 households / 200 people evacuated the night of 28
  September 2008 after the initial slide, with the collapse point roughly 20 m
  from the nearest building. Source: 游裕偉, 技師報 article. PRIMARY.
- **Service suspension**: announced for **15:00, 1 October 2008**.
  Sources: both the technician-association article and the Control Yuan
  resolution give this date/time independently. PRIMARY (both). High
  confidence.
- **The assessment body was four Taipei professional-association bodies**
  (臺北市土木、大地、水土保持及結構等4大技師公會 — the Taipei associations of
  civil engineering, geotechnical engineering, soil and water conservation,
  and structural engineering technicians), not four separate "institutes."
  Commissioned by 台北市政府產業發展局. Initial report **30 November 2008**; full
  report, titled 「臺北市文山區萬壽路75巷政大御花園薔蜜風災土石崩塌鑑定報告」, **12
  December 2008**.
  Sources: 游裕偉, 技師報 article and the Control Yuan resolution, which quote
  the same report and give the same two dates independently. PRIMARY (both).
  High confidence. This corrects the lead file's looser wording of "four
  professional engineering institutes."
- **T16's well-type foundation (井式基礎)** was exposed/cantilevered by
  approximately 4 metres after the slide (one Council member's site visit on
  9 November 2008 measured exposure at roughly three-quarters of the base
  area and over 4 m deep). The four-association report found that, under
  earthquake loading, the increased overturning moment on the exposed
  foundation exceeded what the existing reinforcement could safely carry, and
  that if exposure continued to grow the lower slope might no longer supply
  enough lateral resistance. Continuous tilt monitoring installed from 29
  September–8 October 2008 onward found **no measurable tilt, displacement or
  settlement** in T16 or any of the other towers throughout the following
  three months of monitoring.
  Source: 監察院, 糾正案文 (PDF). PRIMARY. High confidence — directly quoted from
  the resolution's own technical summary.
- **T16 was relocated, not rebuilt in place.** The four-association report
  recommended relocation as the preferred option (though in-place
  reinforcement was assessed as technically feasible too, if executed early
  in the slide — which the city did not do). The Control Yuan resolution
  explicitly endorses the relocation decision as correct. The new tower site
  was selected by comparing two candidate drill points (NH-1, NH-2) toward
  T17; POMA's own engineers judged NH-1 preferable as closer to the original
  centreline and elevation, and geology at both candidates was found better
  than at the old T16 site. **The rebuilt T16 sits approximately 30 m from
  the original tower**, announced by Mayor Hau Lung-bin at a press conference
  on **23 January 2009**.
  Source: 監察院, 糾正案文 (PDF). PRIMARY. High confidence. This directly
  answers the open question in the existing lead file.
  「臺北市政府遷移 T16 塔柱，應是正確決策」;「地質條件及地理環境均較佳的新 T16 塔柱與舊
  T16 塔柱僅相距 30 公尺」.
- **The Control Yuan investigation** examined and *rejected* several public
  allegations as unsubstantiated, including that T16's foundation piling was
  built with substandard materials/methods (偷工減料) and that the tower site
  had no bedrock at all — while separately criticising the planning
  contractor (鼎漢公司), the project-management contractor (臺灣世曦), and the
  design-build contractor (春原營造) for inadequate geotechnical survey work,
  and criticising Taipei City Government for its slow initial emergency
  response and for not requiring proper site-wide geological survey in the
  original planning contract. The resolution recommends the city pursue legal
  and disciplinary responsibility against the responsible companies and
  officials.
  Source: 監察院, 糾正案文 (PDF). PRIMARY.
- **Control Yuan investigators and release**: per Control Yuan's own case
  summary page, the investigation was carried out by committee members 杜善良,
  葛永光 and 陳永祥 over 11+ months and the corrective resolution was released
  4 November 2009.
  Source: 監察院, ["「臺北市貓空纜車T16塔柱下方邊坡崩塌」案"](https://www.cy.gov.tw/News_Content.aspx?n=124&s=4853),
  fetched via WebFetch tool summary (not independently re-verified against
  raw HTML). PRIMARY. Medium confidence — this fetch was a tool-generated
  summary rather than a directly read raw page, unlike the PDF resolution
  itself, which was read in full.

### Operating rules and suspension thresholds

- **Suspends when**: sustained gust ≥16 m/s (roughly Beaufort 7) for 3+
  seconds, or any gust ≥18 m/s (roughly Beaufort 8); lightning on the route;
  earthquake intensity ≥4; or when a land typhoon warning covering the Taipei
  area is in effect, "depending on wind/rain conditions or after Taipei City
  Government announces work/school suspension."
  Source: gondola.taipei, ["暫停營運條件" (Suspension Conditions)](https://www.gondola.taipei/cp.aspx?n=94839749F15BBAB1),
  fetched directly (raw HTML) 12 August 2026. PRIMARY. High confidence — this
  confirms the lead file's three numeric thresholds and adds a fourth
  (typhoon-warning) criterion the lead file did not have.
  「當瞬間風速達16公尺/秒（約7級風）且持續3秒以上，或瞬間風速18公尺/秒（約8級風）以上時，
  將暫停營運。」
- **Monthly maintenance closures do not follow a simple "first Monday of the
  month" rule.** The operator's published 2026 closure calendar lists routine
  Monday maintenance days scattered through each month (e.g. 1/12, 2/9, 3/9,
  4/13, 6/1, 9/7...) plus a separate, longer block of consecutive Monday
  closures in some months (e.g. four Mondays running in August 2026) that the
  page labels **年度檢修 (annual overhaul)**, distinct from the routine
  **週一例行保養維護日 (routine Monday maintenance day)**. No stated rule ties
  the routine day to "the first Monday"; the schedule is simply published
  month by month.
  Source: gondola.taipei, ["營運時間" (Operating Hours)](https://www.gondola.taipei/cp.aspx?n=BFE4B2ABA0D9FB61),
  fetched directly (raw HTML) 12 August 2026. PRIMARY. This corrects the lead
  file's "first Monday of each month, except when that falls on a public
  holiday," which does not match the published 2026 calendar.

### Reopening in 2010 — resolving the lead file's date question

The existing lead file flagged a conflict: "railfan sources say reopening was
23 March 2010, zh.wikipedia says 'March 2010'." Reading zh.wikipedia's own
article directly (not a summary of it) resolves this without a real
contradiction: **zh.wikipedia gives both dates, precisely, for two different
events**, footnoted individually:

- **18 March 2010** — Mayor Hau Lung-bin announces the gondola will formally
  reopen 30 March.
- **23–28 March 2010** — a six-day **free trial period** before formal
  reopening, 10,000 free rides/day, 102,000+ riders over the six days.
- **21 March 2010** — TRTC publishes the detailed reopening rules.
- **30 March 2010** — **formal (paid) reopening**, 「正式復駛」.
- **7 April 2010** — a T15 tilt-monitoring alarm, subsequently confirmed a
  false alarm with no actual movement in T15.
- **9 April 2010** — one week after reopening, cumulative ridership passes
  100,000; the same evening (20:09) a cable-detection alarm halts the system
  for 4 minutes 5 seconds, no passengers trapped, resumed 20:50.
- **29 April 2010** — cumulative ridership since reopening reaches 270,000.

So "23 March 2010" (what the lead file's railfan sources apparently gave) is
the correct date for the **start of the pre-opening free trial**, not the
formal reopening, which was **30 March 2010**. Both figures are real dates
for real, different events; neither source is wrong once the event each is
describing is made explicit.

Source: zh.wikipedia, "貓空纜車", raw HTML fetched directly 12 August 2026 (not
a tool summary). SECONDARY, but the individual line items are footnoted
in-article (footnotes 29–35), and their internal chronology is self-
consistent — 18 March announcement, 21 March rules published, 23–28 March
free trial, 30 March formal reopening is an ordinary, plausible sequence with
no internal contradiction. The underlying footnoted news sources were not

## Contradicts the existing lead file

`docs/research/gondola/maokong-gondola.md` (v1.0, 6 August 2026) is the
project's own lead file for this subject, explicitly marked "Not published.
Verify before promoting to content." Checked against primary sources here,
it was wrong or unconfirmed on:

- **T16 relocated vs. rebuilt in place.** The lead file listed this as an
  open question ("Whether T16 was rebuilt in place or relocated"). The
  Control Yuan's own corrective resolution settles it: T16 was **relocated**,
  approximately 30 m from the original site, a decision the resolution
  explicitly endorses as correct. See "The T16 failure" above.
- **Crystal cabin fleet size.** The lead file states "**20**" crystal cabins,
  "cabin numbers **1–10 and 138–147**." The operator's own current page
  states **31** crystal cabins; zh.wikipedia independently gives the same
  count (31) with numbering **1–15, 66, and 133–147**. No source found in
  this pass supports the lead file's "20" or its numbering range. See
  Conflict 4 above.
- **"Four professional engineering institutes"** wording. The lead file's
  characterisation as engineering "institutes" is imprecise: both primary
  sources checked here (the technician-journal article and the Control Yuan
  resolution) identify the assessing body specifically as **four Taipei
  professional associations** (技師公會) — for civil, geotechnical, structural,
  and soil-and-water-conservation engineering — a term with a more specific
  meaning (statutory professional bodies, not research institutes).
- **Monthly maintenance closure rule.** The lead file states maintenance
  closure is "the first Monday of each month, except when that falls on a
  public holiday." The operator's own published 2026 closure calendar does
  not match a simple first-Monday rule and additionally shows a separate,
  longer annual-overhaul closure block in some months. See "Operating rules"
  above.
- **Typhoon Jangmi strike date.** The lead file states "Typhoon Jangmi (薔蜜)
  struck on 24 September 2008." 24 September is when the storm *formed* over
  the Philippine Sea; it made landfall in Taiwan (Nan'ao, Yilan) on **28
  September 2008**, and both primary sources used here date the T16 slope's
  initial failure to 27–28 September, not 24 September.
- **Rainfall figure attribution.** The lead file attributes "1,271.5 mm" to
  "September 2008 rainfall" without qualification. That figure is the
  **three-typhoon monthly cumulative** per the Central Weather Bureau, not
  rainfall from Typhoon Jangmi alone — Jangmi's own contribution on 28
  September is separately given elsewhere as roughly 500 mm for that single
  day. Both figures are real and now published with what each measures; the
  lead file's single unqualified figure risked being read as "what Jangmi
  dropped."

The lead file's T16 foundation dimensions (~4 m cantilever), the 1 October
2008 15:00 suspension time, the POMA attribution, the contract/construction
date chronology, and the four numeric suspension thresholds all survived
verification against primary sources and are carried forward into the
content page.
