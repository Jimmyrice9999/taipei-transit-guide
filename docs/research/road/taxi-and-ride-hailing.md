# Taiwan taxi and ride-hailing regulation

**Status: researched and built 29 August 2026 (Run 306, Part 6d).** No prior
docs/research file existed for this subject. Built as
`content/road/regulation/taxi-and-ride-hailing.md`.

## What is established

**Legal definition and the diversified-taxi (多元化計程車) category**
(汽車運輸業管理規則, law.moj.gov.tw pcode K0040003, PRIMARY statute text,
High confidence): Article 2 defines a taxi business as renting a small
passenger car to carry passengers within an approved area, and separately
defines diversified-taxi service as app-based booking-only dispatch. This
category was added by order 交通部交路字第10550120738號 dated 2016-10-25
(independently confirmed on Taipei City's own legal database history page,
laws.gov.taipei/Law/LawSearch/LawInformation/FL012445, PRIMARY, High
confidence — a genuine cross-primary corroboration).

**The dispatch-method and colour rules that actually distinguish the two
categories** (same regulation, Article 91, PRIMARY, High confidence): a
diversified taxi may only accept pre-booked rides and may not street-cruise
or wait at a taxi rank. Standard taxis must use pure-yellow livery (Taiwan
Paint Manufacturers' Association colour card no. 1-18); diversified taxis
are explicitly barred from that colour.

**The 2019 rental-car-platform provision** (Article 103-1, added by order
交通部交路字第10850075501號 dated 2019-06-06, confirmed on both law.moj.gov.tw
and the Taipei legal database, PRIMARY, High confidence): requires drivers
used by rental-car businesses partnering with information platforms to hold
taxi-driver qualifications, with minimum booking durations and no
street-hailing.

**Unlicensed-operation penalty** (公路法 Art.77, law.moj.gov.tw pcode
K0040001, PRIMARY, High confidence): NT$100,000-25,000,000 fine range, set
by a 2017-01-04 amendment.

**The 2020 Grand Chamber ruling on Uber** (司法院/Judicial Yuan press
release, judicial.gov.tw/tw/cp-1888-287772-4d6f8-1.html, PRIMARY, High
confidence): case 109年度大字第2號, ruled 2020-09-18. Holding is
jurisdictional, not a finding that Uber's model was lawful: the central
Highway Bureau lacks authority to fine or close an unlicensed taxi operator
whose head office is in a special municipality; that power belongs to the
municipality. Parties: 交通部公路總局 v. 台灣宇博數位服務股份有限公司.

**Uber's operating timeline** (數位時代/BusinessNext, bnext.com.tw/article/59299,
SECONDARY, Medium confidence): trial launch reported as either 2013-05-07 or
2013-06-27 depending on source (see Conflicts), official UberBLACK launch
2013-07-31 (both sources agree on this date); 2017-02-02 shutdown-order fine
of NT$231m against a cited 48 cumulative citations; 2019-10-01 Uber
statement declining to form its own diversified-taxi fleet.

## Conflicts found

- **Uber's trial-launch date**: BusinessNext says 2013-05-07; zh.wikipedia
  (lead only) says 2013-06-27. Both agree on the 2013-07-31 official launch.
  Not resolved — both trial dates published nowhere on this site since
  neither is used in the built content page, which cites only the agreed
  2013-07-31 date.
- **Total accumulated Uber fines/case count**: three different scales
  appear across secondary sources with no source explaining how they
  relate — CTWANT reports 196 cases/NT$1.77bn as the total ultimately
  withdrawn after the Grand Chamber ruling; other secondary accounts of the
  Grand Chamber case itself cite NT$20m/215 notices; BusinessNext's earlier
  timeline cites 48 citations/~NT$1.156bn as of February 2017 alone. The
  built content page states this range without resolving it.
- **August 2016 Investment Commission action against Uber**: one secondary
  account frames it as an attempted revocation that was paused the next
  day; another frames it as an actual revocation. Not resolved; not
  published on the built page.
