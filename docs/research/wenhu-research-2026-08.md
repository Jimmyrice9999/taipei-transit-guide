# Wenhu Line — research findings, 6 August 2026

Mandarin-source research against outstanding TBC values. Every claim carries a
source. Where sources conflict or a figure is provisional, it says so.

**Not yet published.** Verify before promoting to content.

## 1. Fleet renewal programme

The largest hardware programme in the line's history, announced on the Muzha
line's 30th anniversary.

| Item | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Announced | 28 March 2026 | 30th anniversary of Muzha line opening |
| Scope | All 76 trains + signalling system | Complete replacement, not refurbishment |
| Full completion target | ROC 130 = **2041** | |
| First phase target | **2036** — 25 new trains + signalling switchover | |
| Estimated cost | ~NT$41 billion (410億元) | **Preliminary.** TRTC states it depends on consultant evaluation, equipment selection and project scope |
| Method | 邊營運、邊更新 — renew while operating | Mayor Chiang Wan-an's requirement; commuter service must not be disrupted |
| Sequence | Signalling first, then trains adapt progressively | Allows old and new stock to coexist during transition |

**Why now:** the Matra fleet reaches 40 years in ROC 125 (2036) and TRTC has
confirmed it will **not** be life-extended.

**New train design:** reported as 車廂全貫穿 — fully gangwayed walk-through
cars, versus the current married-pair arrangement. Reported to add roughly 5,000
passengers per hour at peak. Treat as a design intention, not a specification.

**Reliability context** (Taipei City Councillor Chin Hui-chu, figures 2022 to
April 2026):

| Line | Failures over 5 min, 5 years |
|---|---|
| Songshan–Xindian | 0 |
| Tamsui–Xinyi | 2 |
| Zhonghe–Xinlu | 4 |
| Bannan | 5 |
| **Wenhu** | **13** |

Wenhu's total exceeds the other four combined. Cause breakdown: train faults
61.5%, signalling and platform doors 7.7%, human and natural causes 30.8%.
Wenhu's own annual figures trend downward — 5, 4, 2, 2, 0 — which is worth
stating alongside the headline number.

**Sources:** UDN 17 & 18 May 2026; Yahoo News Taiwan / CNA 28 March 2026; Storm
Media 30 March 2026; Mirror News 8 May 2026. All secondary, but reporting direct
TRTC statements. **A primary TRTC source would be better — worth chasing.**

## 2. Seat removal programme

Two distinct phases that most coverage conflates.

### Phase 1 — Bombardier BT370, completed January 2023
Seats modified and some luggage racks removed. TRTC estimated roughly **+1,000
passengers per hour** at peak. From 18 February 2023 the 80-second minimum peak
headway period was also extended.

### Phase 2 — Matra VAL256, trial from June 2023
Trial on **3 trains**, explicitly modelled on Singapore and New York practice.

Per train: 4 cars, 20 seats each. In cars **2 and 3** only —
- 12 central seats removed
- 8 priority seats retained at car ends
- Luggage racks removed
- 16 grab handles added to ceiling
- Lateral handrails added to both sides

Cars 1 and 4 unchanged, so passengers wanting seats are directed there.

**Capacity: ~400 → ~420 per train.**

### Phase 3 — full rollout, announced 14 June 2024
Survey reported **70% satisfaction** with the trial, so all **25 Matra trains**
were modified, targeted for completion by mid-2025.

Note the 2024 rollout spec differs from the 2023 trial:
- Some pillars removed at car entrances
- Door-side handrails narrowed from 48 cm to 26 cm
- In cars 2 and 3: the **two-person seats beside the doors** removed plus luggage
  racks, **retaining 4 sets of back-to-back seats** in the car centre

That is not the same as the trial's "remove the 12 central seats". Either the
spec changed after the trial, or reporting is imprecise. **Flag as unresolved —
this is exactly the kind of detail an English reference site should get right.**

**Political reaction:** criticised across party lines. DPP councillor Ho
Meng-hua called it unfriendly to passengers; KMT councillor Li Ming-hsien called
it treating symptoms rather than causes.

**Peak flow context** (May 2023): heaviest inter-station flow was Zhongxiao
Fuxing southbound at ~13,000 passengers/hour; evening peak was Nanjing Fuxing
towards Taipei Zoo at ~12,000/hour.

**Sources:** CNA 20 June 2023 and 14 June 2024; Mirror Media 20 June 2023; USTV
20 June 2023; Taiwan News 14 June 2024; GVM City 21 June 2023.

## 3. Depots — both resolved

### Muzha Depot 木柵機廠

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Area | **6.2 hectares** |
| Stabling roads | **12** |
| Stabling capacity | **84 pairs** |
| Maintenance level | Level 3 (was Level 5 before Neihu opened) |
| Address | No. 21, Sec. 2, Xinguang Rd, Wenshan District |
| Location | Between Xinguang Road and the Jingmei River, NE of Taipei Zoo |
| Opened | 28 March 1996, with the Muzha line |

Facilities: stabling yard, workshop, M&E building, control centre, traction
substation, main substation, emergency power and pumping station, test track.

**Notable:** houses the original Muzha line medium-capacity control centre, which
became the Wenhu line's **backup control centre** after Neihu opened.

**Incident worth covering:** on 22 August 2023 a lightning strike caused power
loss at Muzha Depot, triggering the fleet's fail-safe design and stopping the
entire line for about 40 minutes.

*Source: zh.wikipedia — secondary. Needs a primary source.*

### Neihu Depot 內湖機廠

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Area | **7.11 hectares** |
| Function | **Full-function maintenance depot** — the primary facility |
| Location | NE of the Nangang Economic and Trade Park special district |
| Site shape | Narrow NW–SE strip adjacent to the Keelung River |

Functions: stabling, washing, inspection, servicing, repair, testing, emergency
recovery, traffic control.

**This confirms the run 3 correction.** Neihu Depot is in **Nangang**, not Neihu.
The two depots share Wenhu's storage and maintenance workload rather than
duplicating it.

*Source: DORTS 文山內湖線 project page — **primary**. Archive this URL; it's a
CMS node id.*

## 4. Fleet numbers — official, and they reconcile

DORTS publishes procurement by contract:

| Contract | Stock | Quantity |
|---|---|---|
| CC350 | Original Muzha line (Matra VAL256) | **51 pairs (102 cars)** |
| CB370 | Wenhu line (Bombardier) | **101 pairs (202 cars)** |

51 + 101 = 152 pairs = **304 cars = 76 trains**, which matches the "all 76
trains" figure in the renewal reporting. Four-way cross-check holds.

*Source: DORTS FAQ, 機電設計 — **primary**, published 8 July 2008. Note the date:
predates later additions to other lines, but Wenhu figures are complete.*

## 5. Infrastructure — a significant find

### Steel running surface on the Neihu section

The Muzha section runs on **reinforced concrete**. Northern Taiwan's wet climate
made maintenance difficult, so the Neihu section was built with **steel running
surfaces** (鋼製行駛路面) instead, following international practice.

Manufacture: special section steel produced at the mill, pre-bent mechanically to
the alignment's curvature, rust-treated, then coated on the tyre contact face
with a paint-and-filler traction mixture to provide grip.

**This is genuinely good material.** It's a concrete engineering difference
between the two halves of the same line, it explains itself, and it's absent from
English coverage.

### Signalling

The Neihu section uses **moving block CBTC**, maintaining train separation
automatically and continuously monitoring all train positions — different from
both the original Muzha system and Taipei's high-capacity lines.

*Source: 李政安 & 蘇瑞文, 臺北捷運內湖線中運量系統概述 — a technical paper hosted
by New Taipei DORTS. **Primary-adjacent and high quality.** Worth reading in full;
likely answers more TBCs than are captured here.*

### Neihu section dimensions

14.8 km, 12 stations, of which **3.9 km underground with 2 underground stations**,
plus 1 depot.

**This independently confirms BR13/BR14 as the only underground stations.**

*Source: DORTS 文山內湖線 — primary.*

## 6. The 1993 fire and the impeachments

Not currently on the site, and it's the most dramatic event in the line's
construction.

**24 September 1993:** electric multiple unit no. 47 caught fire between Liuzhangli
and Linguang. The Ministry of Transportation and Communications halted all work
on the Muzha line.

**3 February 1994:** the Control Yuan impeached DORTS director-general Chi Pao-cheng
(齊寶錚) and **seven other officials**.

This is a substantial part of why the line's reputation was damaged before it
opened, and it connects directly to the Matra dispute. **Should be in the Matra
article, or its own section.**

### Construction figures
- Cost: **NT$25 billion**
- Duration: 8 years
- Groundbreaking: 15 December, at Heping East Road Sec. 3 / Fuyang Street

### Opening day service, 28 March 1996
- Peak: 10 trains in service
- Off-peak: 8 trains
- Headway: **4 min 50 sec to 5 min 50 sec**

Useful contrast with today's 80-second minimum peak headway — a concrete measure
of how the line's capacity has changed.

*Sources: zh.wikipedia 木柵線; UDN 報時光 archive, 29 Feb 2024, quoting
contemporaneous 聯合報 reporting. Secondary — the Control Yuan impeachment should
be verifiable from primary records.*
