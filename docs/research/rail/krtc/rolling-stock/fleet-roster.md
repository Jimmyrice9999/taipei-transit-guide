# Kaohsiung Metro rolling stock — research findings, 27 August 2026

> Corpus warning: files under `docs/research/` are leads and working notes, not sources. Every claim below records whether the underlying page was fetched and read in full, or only found via a search summary; the latter is not published as an established fact on the content page. `docs/research/rail/krtc.md` explicitly lists fleet data as an unaddressed TBC — this fills that gap and contradicts nothing already established there.

This is a one-subject report on Kaohsiung Metro's rolling stock. Confidence
is generally lower here than on this project's TRA/Taipei Metro fleet pages:
no primary KRTC or government page stating the Red/Orange fleet size or
manufacturer in prose was successfully read, so several figures rest on
secondary sources only, clearly flagged.

## What is established

- **KRTC's own live FAQ confirms current Red/Orange Line trains run only as 3-car sets; 6-car coupling has never been implemented and cannot be done immediately, because the system's hardware/software — including the signalling and platform-door systems — is configured on a 3-car-unit basis.** Source: Kaohsiung Rapid Transit Corporation, `高雄捷運目前是三節車廂，可以立刻加掛為六節車廂嗎？`, `https://www.krtc.com.tw/Service/faq_more?id=cfcf9e266ae4469eac293cb5d1289986`, PRIMARY, High confidence — operator's own live FAQ, fetched in full. Original-language basis: `目前高雄捷運系統內相關軟硬體設定及人員設備操作，包含號誌系統、月台門系統皆以三節車廂為單位之營運模式`. The page further states simply coupling two 3-car sets is not possible without dedicated new 6-car vehicles and a signalling upgrade.
- **KRTC's own live FAQ confirms the Red Line, as currently described by the operator, runs from R3 Siaogang to RK1 Gangshan — i.e. the Gangshan extension segment is open and in revenue service.** Source: `高雄捷運紅橘線全線通車，紅橘線指的是哪裡?`, `https://www.krtc.com.tw/Service/faq_more?id=3431e868fa1c4bd08433b1a2749dfb5c`, PRIMARY, High confidence, fetched in full. Original-language basis: `捷運紅線係指由R3小港站至RK1岡山車站`.
- **A joint venture of Hyundai Rotem (South Korea) and Singapore Technologies Electronics (STEE) won a design-build electromechanical-systems contract (RKM01) for the Gangshan–Luzhu extension, signed 1 December 2021, with the new trainsets explicitly stated to be electromechanically compatible with the existing Siemens system.** Source (compatibility statement): Liberty Times (自由時報), `高雄捷運路線延伸 將增購16組新列車`, `https://news.ltn.com.tw/news/life/breakingnews/4426433`, SECONDARY, Medium confidence, fetched in full, published 2023-09-13. Original-language basis: `新採購列車委由現代樂鐵設計製造，機電將和西門子系統完全相容`. The 1 December 2021 signing date and RKM01 designation come from a search-tool summary of a Kaohsiung MRT Engineering Bureau listing page, not an independently fetched primary page — flagged as lower confidence, see Checked and failed.
- **A second, independently fetched Liberty Times article corroborates the trainset-order history: originally 10 sets for the Gangshan–Luzhu extension (8 in an initial phase, 2 under an expansion contract), with 6 further sets ordered around March 2023 for the Xiaogang–Linyuan extension, for a total of 16 new Hyundai Rotem sets.** Source: 自由時報, `睽違20年將再採購新車 高市捷運局證實`, `https://news.ltn.com.tw/news/life/breakingnews/3739163`, SECONDARY, Medium-High confidence, fetched in full, published 2021-11-17. Original-language basis: `高捷紅線岡山路竹延伸線有計畫採購10列新車（第一階段8列，擴充合約2列）`, attributed to a statement by the Kaohsiung City Government MRT Engineering Bureau — a secondary report of a primary body's statement; the underlying Bureau press release itself was not located and read directly.
- **A public mock-up of the new Hyundai Rotem train for the Red Line extension was unveiled, with a stated design basis in Kaohsiung's cultural and artistic elements; a headline reference to a "hydrogen-energy integration plan" was not elaborated in the article body and is not published as established.** Source: United Daily News (聯合報/udn.com), `影／韓國現代樂鐵「高捷新列車模型」首度亮相 氫能整合方案曝光`, `https://udn.com/news/story/7241/9393353`, SECONDARY, Medium confidence, fetched in full, published 2026-03-20.
- **The existing Red/Orange fleet is widely reported (secondary sources only) as 42 Siemens-built trainsets (126 cars, 3-car DM1+T+DM2 sets), ordered October 2001 and assembled in Austria, with car deliveries from October 2005.** Source: zh.wikipedia, `高雄捷運高運量電聯車`, SECONDARY, lead index only, Medium confidence — corroborated independently by the fully-fetched Liberty Times article above, which separately refers to "現有西門子42列車" (the existing 42 Siemens trainsets) when reporting the new order. **No primary KRTC or government page stating this fleet size or manufacturer in prose was successfully fetched in this research** — see Checked and failed.
- **Vehicle technical specifications (dimensions, capacity, power) are sourced to a Wikipedia infobox only, not independently verified against a primary spec sheet.** As extracted: leading-car length 21,945 mm, middle-car length 21,560 mm, body width 3,150 mm, body height 3,750 mm, gauge 1,435 mm, power DC 750 V third rail, operating speed 80 km/h (design maximum 90 km/h), standard capacity 750 persons per 3-car set (maximum 1,010), 42 seats per car, four pairs of external sliding doors per side. SECONDARY, Low-Medium confidence, not published as primary-confirmed.
- **The Circular Light Rail (operated by KRTC under an entrustment arrangement from Kaohsiung City Government, per secondary sourcing not independently confirmed by an explicit primary statement) has run two rolling-stock generations from different manufacturers: CAF Urbos 3 (Spain, Phase 1, 9 sets, first unit arrived 2014) and Alstom Citadis X-05 (France, Phase 2, contracted via China Steel as prime contractor, 15 sets, first arrival September 2018, commercial service began 2 November 2020).** Both manufacturer/set-count claims are SECONDARY (zh.wikipedia model-specific articles), not independently fetched in full in this research — Low-Medium confidence, flagged as leads.
- **A primary DORTS source confirms the Circular Light Rail's train composition directly, without naming a manufacturer.** Source: Kaohsiung City Government Mass Rapid Transit Bureau, `高雄環狀輕軌一列車有幾個車廂？可以載運多少人？`, `https://mtbu.kcg.gov.tw/FAQs/C004600?id=e285a523-d9b2-427b-9cc5-e1e2b86413ee&PageType=1`, PRIMARY, High confidence, fetched in full. Original-language basis: `高雄輕軌一列車有 5 節車廂`; `滿載的時候共可以容納 250 人`; `列車全長 34.16 公尺、寬 2.65公尺`; on manufacturer, only: `車輛是由國外廠商製造` (the vehicles are manufactured by an overseas company) — the Bureau's own FAQ does not name CAF or Alstom.
- **The under-construction Yellow Line's rolling stock is confirmed by a genuine DORTS primary source: Stadler Rail (Switzerland) designs and produces the car body; trains are 3-car sets (2 powered end cars with manual-drive-capable cabs plus 1 unpowered middle car), no more than 60 m long, carrying more than 440 passengers, capable of driverless automatic operation.** Source: `高雄黃線電聯車系統之機電設備簡介`, `https://mtbu.kcg.gov.tw/Activities/C002100?CategoryID=fb12bab3-733e-4218-9c6d-0375d3101dc9&id=e7693a8c-cf09-4548-97bb-b2c4418e1be9&PageType=1`, PRIMARY, High confidence, fetched in full. Original-language basis: `高雄黃線電聯車由瑞士軌道車輛製造商施泰德（Stadler Rail）公司設計生產`; `由兩輛有手動駕駛控制區的動力端車(M Car)和一輛拖車中間車(T Car)組成`; `列車編組長度不超過60公尺，可容納440人以上乘客`. A planned fleet size of 25 sets appears only in zh.wikipedia (SECONDARY), not confirmed by either DORTS primary page fetched — TBC.

## Conflicts

- **Car body height: 3,750 mm (zh.wikipedia infobox) versus approximately 3,600 mm (a search-summary claim attributed to a krtc.com.tw product page).** The krtc.com.tw URL the second figure traces to returned a dead/removed-resource error on direct fetch (see Checked and failed) — this is not treated as a genuine two-source conflict, since only the Wikipedia-infobox figure is backed by a page this research could actually read. The 3,600 mm figure is not published.
- **Gangshan–Luzhu electromechanical-contract award date: 1 December 2021 signing (per a search-tool summary of a Bureau listing page) is the only date found; no competing date was located.** Recorded because the underlying page itself was not independently fetched — see Checked and failed — so this date carries lower confidence than a directly-read primary source would.

## Stated gaps

No primary KRTC or government page stating the Red/Orange fleet size (42
sets / 126 cars) directly was found — this rests on two independent
secondary sources that agree, which is reasonable corroboration but not
primary confirmation. No model/series designation was found for the
Siemens Red/Orange fleet, nor for the incoming Hyundai Rotem fleet — only
manufacturer names. The Hyundai Rotem order's total contract value is
unresolved: the one figure found (a reported "USD 176 million" alongside an
arithmetically inconsistent "2.078 billion Korean Won" in the same
extraction) is not published pending a careful re-fetch. No source gives a
combined post-delivery total fleet size once the new trainsets arrive; this
page does not compute one. The Yellow Line's planned fleet size (25 sets,
Wikipedia only) is unconfirmed against a primary source.
