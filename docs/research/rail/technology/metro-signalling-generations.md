# Taipei Metro signalling generations

Research date: 27 August 2026 (Asia/Taipei).

This is a one-subject research record for the signalling boundary between the
Taipei high-capacity network’s conventional fixed-block family and the later
moving-block CBTC systems. It does not treat a supplier, a block method and a
formal automation grade as interchangeable facts.

## What is established

### High-capacity corridors use one published fixed-block family

**Claim — Confirmed:** DORTS’s high-capacity signalling chapter groups the
Tamsui-Xinyi, Songshan-Xindian, Zhonghe-Xinlu, Bannan, Tucheng,
Xinzhuang-Luzhou and Nangang eastern-extension corridors under an Alstom
(formerly GRS) conventional fixed-block and audio-frequency-track-circuit
signalling design.

  high-capacity main-line signalling*

## Conflicts

### Broad high-capacity category versus current equipment register

DORTS’s official pages agree on the high-capacity family’s fixed-block,
audio-frequency architecture and on Wenhu’s later CBTC transition. They do not
provide a comparable current software, relay-room, supplier-renewal or
fallback-mode register for every route. This is a scope gap, not a numeric
conflict.

### Historical shorthand versus named current examples

The summary page describes a general progression from early high-capacity GoA2
to CBTC GoA4, while the other pages name particular systems and corridors. The
page preserves these as different scopes and does not project the summary onto
Airport MRT, light rail or every future line.

## Checked and failed

- **Complete current signalling-equipment register by line** — checked
  27 August 2026. The full DORTS signalling volume pages identify broad families,
  suppliers, the relay-to-VPI boundary and a four-line computer-reset plan, but
  do not list current post-reset software, relay-room equipment, acceptance
  records and fallback arrangements for every line. An operator asset or
  signalling-maintenance register would settle it.
- **High-capacity line-by-line renewal chronology** — checked 27 August 2026.
  The sources now provide the relay/VPI construction boundary, the historic
  control-centre reset window and the 113-117 reset schedule, but not a complete
  field-level renewal and acceptance chronology for every route. Contract,
  commissioning and acceptance records for each package would settle it.
- **Formal signalling generation for Airport MRT and the light-rail lines** —
  checked 27 August 2026. The sources used here do not provide a comparable
  current generation label for A, V or K. The page leaves those assignments TBC
  rather than inferring them from CBTC, ATP/ATO/ATS or a train supplier.
- **One current fallback mode per line** — checked 27 August 2026. The opened
  DORTS technical pages explain architecture and redundancy examples but do not
  publish a current route-by-route degraded-mode matrix. An operator operating
  rule or signalling failure-response manual would settle it.
