# Wenhu Line station architecture

Research date: 27 August 2026 (Asia/Taipei).

This is a one-subject record of the official design rationale for the Wenhu
Line's Muzha and Neihu station architecture. The DORTS pages were read in full;
the article keeps the builder's design statements separate from synthesis about
what those statements explain.

## What is established

### The primary architecture record

**Claim - Confirmed: DORTS describes the Muzha section as an elevated station system designed with steel, glass and simple forms because urban-road sites and single-column supports constrained the architecture.**

- **Source title:** *Station architectural design — Wenhu Line*
- **Original title:** `車站建築設計—文湖線`
- **Publisher:** 臺北市政府捷運工程局 / Taipei City Government, Department of Rapid Transit Systems
- **URL:** <https://www.dorts.gov.taipei/cp.aspx?n=980C85299DA2890A&s=6B0F524CA1EB5C9F>
- **Archive snapshot:** not captured
- **Kind:** primary
- **Confidence:** High - current official DORTS page fetched and read in full on 27 August 2026; the same wording was also present in the official search result used to locate the page
- **Original-language evidence:** `文湖線初期路線(木柵段)為高架車站，因穿越市中心為降低都市景觀之衝擊，利用鋼構及玻璃建材搭配簡易造型，定位為展現都市現代建築風貌。`; `由於其基地多在市區道路中且為高架型式，設計形式受基地的限制極大`; `由於單柱支撐系統的荷重限制，在設計上必需減少任何不必要的裝飾`; `車站的量體必需盡可能的遠離道路旁的建築`

DORTS's wording makes the causal chain explicit: the section is elevated,
urban-road sites constrained form, the single-column support system limited the
load for unnecessary decoration, and the station volumes were to be kept away
from roadside buildings. The page presents steel and glass with simple forms as
the selected modern-urban expression, not as an undocumented stylistic
inference.

The technical-book page for the Muzha chapter gives the structural alternatives
more specifically: stations in road medians used a cantilevered form supported
by one column, while sites with available open space could use an ordinary
beam-and-column system.

**Claim - Confirmed: DORTS describes the Neihu section as a three-vocabulary design with a “river in the sky” concept and a “Lake City Story” theme.**

- **Source:** the same DORTS architecture page above
- **Confidence:** High - explicit full-page text
- **Original-language evidence:** `藉由捷運設施所穿越地區之環境特質及人文特色，區分為「住宅城鄉風貌」、「商業休閒環境」、「工業科技景觀」等三個造型設計之語彙。再利用高架橋之機能，將之串聯塑造一條「空中之河」，並設定文湖線內湖段之設計主題為「湖城故事」。`

The three terms are residential/urban-rural character, commercial/leisure
environment and industrial/technological landscape. The “river in the sky” is
the builder's description of using the elevated bridge to connect them; “Lake
City Story” is the named overall design theme for the Neihu section.

The full official technical-book page independently describes “Lake City Story”
as the overall Neihu theme and says the design uses architecture, landscape and
public art to express the area's human, natural and ecological character.

**Claim - Confirmed: Neihu station design uses a shared vocabulary adjusted to local conditions, with public-art subthemes, colour plans, simple geometry and lightweight steel, enamelled steel and glass elements.**

- **Source:** DORTS architecture page above
- **Confidence:** High - explicit full-page text
- **Original-language evidence:** `以公共藝術主題思維為出發點賦予各車站設計子題及色彩計畫`; `予以簡潔與幾何形式`; `利用相同之建築語彙及元素配合環境之條件而調整變化`; `採以簡潔明亮之鋼構設計，外牆以烤漆彩色金屬板、琺瑯鋼板與大片玻璃等材料來表現，以期使建築量體輕巧及具有明亮穿透感`; `如劍南路站、大湖公園站、南港展覽館站等`

DORTS says the approach was intended both to simplify maintenance and to express
the rapid-transit image. It names Jiannan Road, Dahu Park and Nangang Exhibition
Center as examples of stations given special forms rather than the standard
station type.

**Claim - Confirmed: The Neihu section's viaduct, sound control and underside landscape were designed as part of the same environmental response.**

- **Source:** DORTS architecture page above
- **Confidence:** High - explicit full-page text
- **Original-language evidence:** `本段約有長十公里之高架橋，大部分架設在道路中央安全島上方，採用預力混凝土U型樑橋設計`; `因區域環境之現況有不同高度之隔音牆設計`; `車站下方之空間，因陽光照射不足及植物生長條件不佳等因素，則以仿枯山水之方式利用景石、不同大小及顏色之卵石塑造景觀特色，並於站體下方增加照明設備`

The source reports approximately ten kilometres of viaduct, mostly above road
medians, using prestressed-concrete U-girders. It describes sound walls of
different heights and a dry-landscape treatment with rocks and coloured pebbles
under the stations, supplemented by lighting for a night-time urban effect.

### Route record and the underground pair

**Claim - Confirmed: DORTS's route record gives the Neihu section as 14.8 km with twelve stations, 3.9 km underground and two underground stations.**

- **Source title:** *Wenshan–Neihu Line route description*
- **Original title:** `文山內湖線路線說明`
- **Publisher:** 臺北市政府捷運工程局 / Taipei City Government, Department of Rapid Transit Systems
- **URL:** <https://www.dorts.gov.taipei/cp.aspx?n=DBAC040496EFAB94>
- **Archive snapshot:** not captured
- **Kind:** primary
- **Confidence:** High - current official DORTS route page fetched and read in full on 27 August 2026
- **Original-language evidence:** `內湖線...全長約14.8公里，設12座車站(地下段3.9公里，2座地下站)`

The same full page lists the Wenhu stations in operating order. It does not put
an above-ground/underground label beside every row, so the identification of the
pair below is a synthesis from the two official records rather than a claim that
one sentence names both.

**Claim - Confirmed as a two-source synthesis: BR13 Songshan Airport and BR14 Dazhi are the two underground stations.**

- **Source A:** DORTS route record above, primary
- **Source B:** DORTS architecture record above, primary
- **Confidence:** High - the route record supplies the two-station total and list; the architecture record names Dazhi as underground and counts ten other Neihu stations as elevated
- **Original-language evidence:** `除大直站為地下車站外，其餘共有十座高架車站`

The route record supplies twelve Neihu stations and exactly two underground
stations. The architecture record names Dazhi as underground and says ten other
Neihu stations are elevated. With the route list's remaining unaccounted station
being Songshan Airport, the pair is BR13 Songshan Airport and BR14 Dazhi. This is
the same arithmetic already used in the line research, now carried into the
focused architecture page with its evidentiary boundary stated.

## Conflicts

The two official records use slightly different counting prose for the Neihu
section: the route record says twelve stations and two underground stations,
while the architecture record says that Dazhi is underground and that ten other
stations are elevated. They do not explicitly name both underground stations in
one sentence. The station-pair identification is therefore published as a
two-source synthesis, not as a verbatim single-source statement.

The route record says the underground section is 3.9 km; the architecture record
does not repeat that length. No conflict is asserted between them.

## Stated gaps

This primary record supports the design logic, structural constraints, three
Neihu vocabularies, line-wide theme, material choices, sound-control approach,
underside landscaping and the two-source identification of the underground pair.
It does not support a complete architect roster, every station's public-art
subtheme, a current condition survey or a precisely bounded ten-kilometre
measurement. Those would require station design contracts, public-art registers,
as-built/maintenance records or a route survey with a stated measurement basis.
