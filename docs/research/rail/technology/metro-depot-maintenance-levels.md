# Taipei Metro depot maintenance levels

Research date: 27 August 2026 (Asia/Taipei).

This is a one-subject research record for the meaning of the Level 1 through
Level 5 depot labels used in Taipei Metro records. It distinguishes the legal
vehicle-inspection levels from the DORTS facility-planning categories that use
the same numbering.

## What is established

### The legal five-level inspection scale

**Claim - Confirmed: Taipei's MRT vehicle rules divide periodic inspection into
five levels, with Level 3 covering partial disassembly and Level 5 covering
thorough inspection and overhaul of important components.**

- **Source title:** *Taipei Municipal MRT System Vehicle and Machinery Inspection Rules*
- **Original title:** 《臺北市大眾捷運系統車輛機具檢修規則》
- **Publisher:** 臺北市政府法規查詢系統 / Taipei City Government
- **URL:** <https://laws.gov.taipei/law/LawSearch/LawExport/FL013134>
- **Archive snapshot:** not captured
- **Kind:** primary
- **Confidence:** High - official consolidated legal text, full four-page PDF read on 27 August 2026
- **Original-language sentences:**
  > 「定期檢修分為五級」
  >
  > 「三級檢修：以局部拆卸分解施行檢驗、調整、校正、測試等方式保持動力、傳動、行駛、煞車儀錶等機件裝置性能正常之檢修。」
  >
  > 「五級檢修：對一般機件施行徹底檢查，各重要機件施行重整之檢修。」
  >
  > 「前項所列較高等級檢修應含次級檢修項目。」

The same rule says the operator proposes the inspection items and cycles for
each vehicle type, subject to approval by the competent authority. It also
requires a test run after Level 4 or higher work, after certain disassembly
repairs, and after a new or rebuilt vehicle is completed.

### DORTS's facility-planning definitions

**Claim - Confirmed: DORTS's high-capacity depot planning table treats Level 3
as the level that adds annual vehicle maintenance and wheel reprofiling to the
lower levels, while Level 5 adds network-wide maintenance, emergency repair,
spares and major overhaul.**

- **Source title:** *Taipei Metro Network Planning Practice*, pp. 196-197
- **Original title:** 《捷運工程叢書 精進版－1 捷運路網規劃實務》
- **Publisher:** 臺北市政府捷運工程局
- **URL:** <https://ebook.dorts.gov.taipei/ebook/no1/files/basic-html/page197.html>
- **Archive snapshot:** not captured
- **Kind:** primary
- **Confidence:** High - DORTS's own planning manual, pages 196-197 read in full on 27 August 2026
- **Original-language table entries:**
  > 「三級機廠」的功能包括「電聯車車輪車削整修」及「執行路線電聯車年檢定期維修任務」。
  >
  > 「五級機廠」的功能包括「捷運系統整體路網主線上設施設備定期檢查、維修」、「進行整體路網車輛緊急搶修任務」、「提供捷運系統整體路網上車輛、設施及相關零件之備品儲放」、「執行捷運系統整體路網車輛及設備之主要翻修（overhaul）或大修任務」及「行車、維修……等人員之訓練」。

The table makes the nesting explicit: a Level 3 depot has all Level 2
functions, and Level 5 has all Level 4 functions. The preceding definitions
describe Level 3 as work using lifting equipment for substantial train
disassembly and Level 5 as detailed component disassembly with machine-tool or
test-equipment inspection.

### Main and secondary depots

**Claim - Confirmed: DORTS distinguishes a main maintenance depot from a
secondary depot by the ability to perform major overhaul, while both provide
maintenance facilities; the planning text groups medium-capacity main depots
as Level 5 and secondary depots as Levels 2-4.**

- **Source title:** *Taipei Metro Network Planning Practice*, pp. 198-199
- **Original title:** 《捷運工程叢書 精進版－1 捷運路網規劃實務》
- **Publisher:** 臺北市政府捷運工程局
- **URL:** <https://ebook.dorts.gov.taipei/ebook/no1/files/basic-html/page199.html>
- **Archive snapshot:** not captured
- **Kind:** primary
- **Confidence:** High - full DORTS pages read on 27 August 2026
- **Original-language sentences:**
  > 「主維修機廠為功能完整之主要維修基地，除可執行電聯車大修作業外並包含測試軌及各項維修設備。」
  >
  > 「次維修機廠為進行除電聯車大修外之維（檢）修，並具備軌道特種車輛整備之處所。」

DORTS's official presentation classifies high-capacity depots as Zhonghe at
Level 1; Xindian, Nangang and Xinzhuang at Level 3; Tucheng and Luzhou at Level
4; and Beitou at Level 5. In the medium-capacity group it lists Muzha and North
Depot as secondary depots at Levels 2-4, and Neihu, South and Jincheng as main
depots at Level 5. This is a DORTS planning classification, not a current
vehicle-by-vehicle allocation register.

## Conflicts

### Level 5 versus quasi-Level-5

- DORTS explicitly calls Beitou, South, Neihu and Jincheng Level 5 in its
  planning/presentation material.
- DORTS's FAQ calls Luzhou **準五級** (quasi-Level-5), not simply Level 5, and
  says it has a test track.

The opened sources do not define the qualification or identify which Level-5
tasks Luzhou cannot perform. The page keeps the published distinction.

### Facility grade versus inspection schedule

The law defines vehicle inspection work and says operators set the detailed
items and cycles for each vehicle type. DORTS's planning text defines the
capabilities and equipment expected of a depot. Those are related but not the
same register: a depot's grade does not by itself publish the current calendar
for every fleet.

## Checked and failed

- **a current TRTC maintenance manual with the inspection items and mileage or time cycle for every fleet** — checked 27 August 2026 against the full municipal inspection rule and DORTS planning pages. The rule delegates detailed items and cycles to each operator and the opened public sources do not provide a single current fleet-wide schedule.
- **a current official roster assigning every active trainset and every maintenance task to a named depot** — checked 27 August 2026 against the DORTS depot planning tables, current FAQ collection and depot pages. The sources describe facility capability and selected responsibilities, not a current unit-by-unit allocation register.
- **the definition of DORTS's “quasi-Level-5” Luzhou designation** — checked 27 August 2026 against the full Xinzhuang/Luzhou FAQ and the full Level 1-5 planning definitions. The FAQ publishes the term and test-track fact but does not explain which Level-5 capability is qualified or omitted.
- **a current regrading date and decision record for every depot** — checked 27 August 2026 against the full depot planning, FAQ and current depot records. The opened sources give classifications and selected historical changes, but not one dated network-wide regrading register.

## Stated gaps

The page can explain what the five levels mean as a facility-capability scale,
why Level 3 is more than cleaning and routine inspection, and why Level 5 is
associated with network-level overhaul and major repair. It cannot yet publish
the detailed current maintenance calendar by fleet, a complete current depot
responsibility matrix, or the technical boundary of Luzhou's quasi-Level-5
designation. Operator-approved maintenance schedules, a current depot work
allocation register, and the design/acceptance record defining quasi-Level-5
would settle those gaps.
