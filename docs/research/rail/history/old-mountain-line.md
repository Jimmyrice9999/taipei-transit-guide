# Taiwan Railways Old Mountain Line (舊山線) — research findings, 27 August 2026

> Corpus warning: files under `docs/research/` are leads and working notes, not sources. Every claim below records whether the underlying page was actually fetched and read, or only summarized by a search tool; snippet-only claims are marked as such and are not published as established facts on the content page.

This is a one-subject report on the abandoned original Sanyi–Houlong–Fengyuan
section of the Western Trunk Line, commonly called 舊山線 ("the Old Mountain
Line"), bypassed by a new alignment in 1998. **The Ministry of Culture's
National Cultural Heritage Network (國家文化資產網, nchdb.boch.gov.tw) — the
primary source that would carry authoritative heritage-designation dates for
Shengxing Station, Longteng Bridge and the two 1938-era iron bridges — could
not be read.** It is a JavaScript-rendered site; every direct case-record
fetch returned only the site's static shell. Wayback Machine fetches are
also blocked in the tooling used, so an archived snapshot could not be tried
either (see Checked and failed). Every heritage-designation date below is
therefore secondary-sourced, and several figures below rest only on a search
tool's AI-generated summary of multiple results rather than a direct fetch of
any one page; those are marked explicitly and are treated as leads, not
sources, on the content page.

## What is established (from a direct fetch)

- **The line was built in stages: Miaoli–Sanyi (then 三叉河) opened 7 October 1903; Sanyi–Houlong completed 20 February 1908; Houlong–Fengyuan finished 20 April 1908, completing the route.** Source: zh.wikipedia, [舊山線](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-tw/舊山線), fetched directly, citing 《臺灣鐵道史》(1911) as its underlying primary (not independently read here). SECONDARY, Medium confidence. Two primary pages fetched in full — the Tourism Administration's page and the Ministry of Culture's BOCH World Heritage potential-site page — both simply state the line was "completed in 1908" without the staged dates, which is consistent with, but does not confirm, the finer breakdown.
- **Shengxing Station (elevation 402.326 m, TRA's highest) opened 1 April 1907 as a signal station and was upgraded to a full station on 1 April 1930.** Source: zh.wikipedia, [勝興車站](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-tw/勝興車站), fetched directly, citing 臺灣總督府告示第33號 (not independently read here). SECONDARY, Medium confidence for the dates; the 402.326 m highest-station elevation is corroborated independently across every source checked and is High confidence.
- **Longteng/Yutengping Bridge (龍騰斷橋/魚藤坪斷橋) was destroyed in the 21 April 1935 Hsinchu–Taichung (Kuandao Mountain, 關刀山) earthquake**, and the Railway Bureau built a replacement bridge roughly 80 m to the west rather than repairing it. Corroborated across every source fetched (zh.wikipedia, udn.com, twh.boch.gov.tw, Tourism Administration). High confidence. The replacement's completion is dated 1938 by the Ministry of Culture's BOCH page, fetched in full, which gives 15 July 1938 specifically; Medium-High confidence.
- **The ruined original bridge, "Longteng Broken Bridge," was damaged again in the 1999 (921) earthquake.** Corroborated across all sources fetched. High confidence.
- **The Sanyi–Fengyuan section was, per the Ministry of Culture's Culture Memory Bank, "the most treacherous and difficult to traverse on the entire [Western Trunk] line" — steep grades, sharp curves, many hills and rivers — which made double-tracking the existing alignment impractical, leading to a new, straighter double-track alignment roughly 4 km shorter.** Source: [舊山線與新山線](https://tcmb.culture.tw/zh-tw/detail?indexCode=Culture_Place&id=332813), hosted by the Ministry of Culture's Culture Memory Bank but credited to a private content creator (唐菓創意有限公司／洪伊君) and archived by Miaoli County Government's Culture and Tourism Bureau — treated as SECONDARY (a curated county/cultural-agency account, not the operator's own statement), fetched in full. Medium-High confidence.
- **The last train on the old line, 29-次莒光號 (Chu-Kuang Express No. 29), passed Shengxing Station on the night of 23 September 1998, after which the line switched to the new alignment effective the next morning, 24 September 1998.** Source: [舊山線鐵道停駛與復駛](https://tcmb.culture.tw/zh-tw/detail?indexCode=Culture_Event&id=233563), Ministry of Culture Culture Memory Bank 2.0, citing *苗栗文獻》第49期：苗栗大事記* (2012, Miaoli County Cultural and Tourism Bureau) — fetched in full, quoted directly: `1998年9月23日：三義＝后里間的舊山線在最後列車（29次莒光號）於晚上通過勝興站後切換線路`. SECONDARY (county-compiled chronology, cited source not independently read), High confidence for the mechanics of the changeover.
- **Partial heritage-tourism service resumed 5 June 2010, Sanyi to the old Taian, initially with a steam-hauled train (CK124).** Same Culture Memory Bank event page, fetched in full. SECONDARY, Medium-High confidence.
- **Miaoli County Government subsequently redeveloped the corridor as "舊山線鐵道自行車" (Old Mountain Line Rail Bike), described by the Tourism Administration as Taiwan's first rail-bike attraction under a "dynamic preservation" (動態保存) model.** Source: 交通部觀光署, [舊山線鐵道](https://www.taiwan.net.tw/m1.aspx?sNo=0001110&id=A12-00494), PRIMARY (Tourism Administration, Ministry of Transportation and Communications), fetched in full. High confidence for the fact of the conversion; the exact launch year is a conflict (below), not settled by this page.
- **Two later replacement bridges on the line, 內社川鐵橋 (Neishechuan Iron Bridge) and 魚藤坪鐵橋 (the 1938 Yutengping replacement span), were designated Miaoli County monuments by the county's Tangible Cultural Assets Review Committee, approved 10 March 2019 and announced 11 March 2019 — the line's fourth and fifth protected heritage sites, after Shengxing Station, Longteng Broken Bridge and Taichung City's Taian Station.** Source: 環境資訊中心 (Taiwan Environmental Information Center), [具珍貴價值 舊山線2座鐵橋獲指定為古蹟](https://e-info.org.tw/node/216883), fetched in full. SECONDARY, Medium confidence — a single secondary source, not cross-checked against the county gazette or the national heritage database.

## Conflicts

- **Opening date granularity.** zh.wikipedia gives staged dates (7 Oct 1903 Miaoli–Sanyi; 20 Feb 1908 Sanyi–Houlong; 20 Apr 1908 Houlong–Fengyuan). The Tourism Administration's and BOCH's primary pages, both fetched in full, say only "completed in 1908." A separate search-summarized claim of "the whole line opened September 1903" does not match either account and looks like a conflation error; it is not published as a value on either page.
- **Longteng/Yutengping Bridge's original construction year.** Search-summarized claims variously give 1905, 1906 and 1907 (with 1908 as a separate "opened to traffic" date from zh.wikipedia, sourced to an archived heritage-database case record that could not be independently verified — see Checked and failed). None of these year claims comes from a page fetched and read in full for this report.
- **Which pier failed in the 1999 earthquake.** A fetched udn.com article says the 4th pier's upper section broke; a search-summarized tourism page says the 6th pier. Not resolved.
- **Tunnel and bridge counts.** Some sources give "9 tunnels, 4 bridges" for the original alignment; the Tourism Administration and BOCH primary pages, both fetched in full, give "8 tunnels, 3 bridges, 4 stations." A proposed explanation (that 9/4 is the original Japanese-era count and 8/3 reflects a post-1935 reduction) was found only via search summary and is not confirmed.
- **Closure date: 23 versus 24 September 1998.** The Culture Memory Bank account frames the last train as passing Shengxing the night of 23 September, with the new line effective the next morning; zh.wikipedia's Shengxing Station article gives the station's own closure date as 24 September 1998. Not a hard contradiction — consistent with a midnight changeover — but the two accounts state different single dates depending on framing, so both are retained.
- **Heritage-tourism/Rail Bike relaunch year: 2010, 2019 or 2021.** The 5 June 2010 steam-train revival (Sanyi–old Taian) is corroborated by a fetched Culture Memory Bank page. A search-summarized 2025 county tourism-statistics report implies the Rail Bike itself dates to 2019. A separate search-tool summary of zh.wikipedia's 舊山線 article states the Rail Bike began operating in 2021. None of these three figures has been reconciled by any source read for this report; they may describe different phases (steam-train era versus Rail Bike era versus a possible route extension), but no source states that explicitly.
- **Shengxing Station's founding year: 1907 versus "民國5年" (1916).** zh.wikipedia, fetched directly, gives 1 April 1907. A search-summarized tourism-blog source states a marker at the station reads "建於民國5年" (built in Taishō/Republic year 5, i.e. 1916); it is unclear from the summary whether this refers to the station's founding or to a specific commemorative structure, and it was not independently fetched and read.
