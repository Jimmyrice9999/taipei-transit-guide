# Taiwan Railways Donggang Line — research findings, 27 August 2026

> Corpus warning: files under `docs/research/` are leads and working notes, not sources. Every claim below was checked against the linked page or PDF that was actually fetched; no search snippet is used as evidence on its own.

This is a one-subject report on the closed 東港線 (Donggang Line), a branch of
the Pingtung Line running to Donggang, Pingtung County. TRA's own
construction-history PDF — the primary record used across this project's other
closed-line pages — could not be read for this subject (see Checked and
failed). The strongest source found is a magazine article published by
Academia Historica (國史館), a government archival-research institute; it is
treated here as **secondary**, not primary, because it is a researched
historical account citing period documents rather than the operator's or a
contemporary government body's own record of its own work. That distinction
matters for confidence ratings throughout this file.

## What is established

- **Groundbreaking on 17 March 1938; the Donggang station building completed 17 December 1939; passenger service began 19 July 1940 with eight round trips a day.** Source: 温文佑 (國史館 staff), *The Vanished Military Railway — the Donggang Line*, titleOriginal `消失的軍用鐵道─東港線`, publisher Academia Historica (國史館), `https://www.ntl.edu.tw/public/Attachment/891716315715.pdf`, SECONDARY (government-institute historical research, not the operator's own record), Medium-High confidence — full PDF fetched and read; the article itself cites period primary documents (臺灣總督府交通局《鐵道職員錄》, 《臺灣鐵道旅行案內》) that were not independently re-checked here. Original-language basis: `1938年3月17日東港線舉行「起工式」，1939年12月17日東港站完工，1940年7月19日開始載客營運，一天共有八班列車往返。`
- **The 1936 Imperial Diet approved a NT/JPY 2.48 million budget covering the 26 km Xizhou–Fangliao Pingtung Line extension together with the Xizhou–Donggang branch.** Same source. Original-language basis: `直到1936年，日本帝國議會才通過溪州至枋寮、溪州至東港的26公里屏東線鐵路預算248萬元`. Confidence: Medium-High.
- **The branch left the Pingtung Line at 社邊 station (today's 鎮安/Chen'an station), called only at 大鵬 (Dapeng), and terminated at Donggang; total length 6.2 km, narrow (1,067 mm) gauge, single track, non-electrified.** Same 國史館 source, corroborated independently by zh.wikipedia's [東港線](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-tw/東港線) article and by zh.wikipedia's [鎮安車站](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-tw/鎮安車站) article, both fetched directly. SECONDARY, Medium-High confidence (three independent secondary accounts agree; no primary TRA record was readable to cross-check the figure). Original-language basis: `東港線起自屏東線鐵路的「社邊」（今鎮安）站，中途只經過「大鵬」站，就來到終點站東港，全長6.2公里。`
- **A military-only spur, nicknamed 「倒吞線」 because trains reversed tail-first into it, ran beyond Dapeng station into the 東港海軍航空隊 (Donggang Naval Air Corps) seaplane base at Dapeng Bay; its platform was nicknamed 「牛奶臺」.** 國史館 source, Medium-High confidence.
- **The line was built primarily to serve Japan's southward-advance (南進) military logistics; Donggang station was deliberately sited away from the town centre (in 牛寮) for military rather than commercial convenience, which the article says limited the station's effect on local commerce.** 國史館 source, Medium confidence — this is the article's own interpretive claim, not a single dated document.
- **The base and line were heavily bombed by Allied forces from October 1944.** 國史館 source, Medium confidence.
- **After the 雙園大橋 (Shuangyuan Bridge, linking Kaohsiung's Linyuan and Pingtung's Xinyuan) opened in the 1980s, road travel to Kaohsiung became viable and ridership collapsed; in the closure year, average daily ridership was about 60 passengers, and the line was closed "on grounds of deficit" (在赤字考量下).** 國史館 source, Medium confidence — the article gives "1980年代" for the bridge opening, not an exact date, and does not cite a dated ridership record. This is the only reason for closure found in any source; **no source found connects the 1991 closure to South Link Line electrification or routing changes.**
- **After passenger service ended, the line carried sporadic military freight (armoured vehicles and personnel carriers) supporting the Air Force base that remained at Dapeng Bay, until the base withdrew in July 2002, ending the line's history.** 國史館 source: `直到2002年7月空軍營區撤離，東港線一甲子的鐵道歲月正式畫上休止符。` Confidence: Medium-High.
- **Within the Dapeng Bay National Scenic Area, the former military-transport platform and an interpretive sign are preserved as the only formal remnant of the line; a short stretch of track survives nearby.** 國史館 source (including a photo captioned `今日殘存的一小段東港線鐵路`), Medium confidence.
- **The former Donggang station site was redeveloped for civic use.** Per two 自由時報 articles fetched directly: a 2003 urban-plan land-use change enabled Donggang Township Office to build a combined office/representative-assembly building on the site (groundbreaking 2018, construction started June 2020, opened 25 July 2022); a separate **Donggang Transit/Transfer Center** then broke ground on the same site 19 May 2022 (NT$86 million, NT$56.5m from the Directorate General of Highways and NT$29.5m from the county government) and opened 7 January 2024. Sources: 自由時報, `東港火車站廢站沉寂30年 轉型再成城鎮轉運中心`, `https://news.ltn.com.tw/news/life/breakingnews/3932320`; 自由時報, `東港舊火車站成「新都心」！ 出站可望就是捷運站`, `https://news.ltn.com.tw/news/life/breakingnews/4544882`. SECONDARY, Medium-High confidence (two independent news reports from the same outlet, both fetched directly, not cross-checked against a second outlet).
- **A 2016–2017 revival study, then a 2017–2019 "Donggang Tourism Railway" (東港觀光鐵道) proposal that would have rerouted the line directly into Dapeng Bay rather than following the original alignment, was cancelled.** MOTC approved NT$8.5 million for an eight-month feasibility study by the Railway Reform Bureau (鐵改局) with consultant 中興工程顧問, targeting completion April 2017. Source: 自由時報, `台鐵東港線是否復駛 將投入850萬評估`, `http://news.ltn.com.tw/news/life/breakingnews/1867395`, SECONDARY, High confidence (direct fetch, specific figures). The cancellation is reported in 自由時報, `燈會塞爆讓「東港支線」再現懷舊風潮 居民盼高捷南延`, `https://news.ltn.com.tw/news/life/breakingnews/2728001`, SECONDARY, High confidence: `除地方持續反彈，最後可行性評估中的經濟效益也不足而喊卡` (continued local opposition, plus insufficient economic benefit in the final feasibility assessment). Cited local objections: the original alignment's low efficiency, level-crossing accident history, flood-prone crossings, and an unattractive route (looping through Chaozhou/Pingtung City) for tourists from northern Taiwan. Local preference reported as shifting toward a Kaohsiung MRT southern extension instead.

## Conflicts

- **Last day of passenger service.** 國史館 states the last passenger train ran **28 February 1991** (`1991年2月28日，東港線開出最後一班（客運）列車`). zh.wikipedia's 東港線 article, fetched twice independently, states passenger service **ended 1 March 1991**. No source found states both figures together or explains the one-day gap; both values are retained rather than picked.
- **Formal abolition date.** 國史館 gives only "July 2002," tied to the Air Force base's withdrawal. zh.wikipedia gives a specific date, **1 July 2002**. The month agrees; the day is sourced only secondarily and was not found in any primary or primary-adjacent record.
- **A search-engine-generated summary claimed a 2004 date for switch/turnout removal at Chen'an station** (`2004年鎮安車站的東港線轉轍器被拆除`). A direct fetch of zh.wikipedia's 鎮安車站 article, read specifically for this detail, does not contain any such date — only 2019 South Link electrification dates appear in the article text. This is treated as a snippet artifact, not a sourced claim, and is not published on the content page.

## Stated gaps

TRA's own account of the line — its construction, operating history and the
reasoning behind its 1991 closure — was not found in a readable form; this
page rests on a government-archival-institute historical article, two
independent news reports and a wiki index rather than the operator's own
record. A readable TRA construction-history entry, a Railway Bureau abolition
order, or a 1991 TRA timetable/gazette notice would settle the exact
last-service and abolition dates. A field survey or Miaoli/Pingtung-county
cultural-heritage record would settle what, if anything, survives of the
alignment beyond the scenic-area platform.
