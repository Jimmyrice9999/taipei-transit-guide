# Wenhu Line 文湖線 (BR)

Line 1 · Brown · TRTC · **Taiwan's first metro line**

**Version 1.0 — 6 August 2026**
**Status: research. Not published. Verify before promoting to content.**

Supersedes nothing. Companion files: `wenhu-research-2026-08.md` (fleet renewal,
seat removal, depots) and `wenhu-research-part2.md` (Matra dispute, 2009 opening,

## Why this line matters

- **Taiwan's first metro line to open** — 28 March 1996
- **Fully automated, driverless, rubber-tyred** medium capacity
- **Two incompatible signalling and rolling stock generations on one route**
- **Three separate numbering systems** exist for its stations, and two of them run
  in opposite directions
- Its construction produced the **Matra dispute**, the **1993 fire**, and
  **eight Control Yuan impeachments**
- Its 2009 extension was so troubled it earned a mocking nickname and a
  fourteen-month fare discount
- Its **entire fleet and signalling will be replaced** by 2041

A **麟光 – 劍南路 short working** was planned but never introduced. Worth a
sentence — it's the only alternative pattern ever proposed for this line.

**Publish 25.17 km as the operating length and explain the others.** This is the
site's model case for "state what a figure measures."

**What this reveals:**

1. **The BR engineering series runs north to south**, from 松山機場 as BR1 down to
   動物園 as BR13 — the exact reverse of the operating codes.

   was folded into the BR series rather than the B series.
3. **The Neihu stations use a separate B1–B11 series**, reflecting a separate
   design and contract package.

**Three numbering systems, one line, two of them inverted relative to each other.**
That is a complete article in itself, and it's a perfect illustration of the site's
central thesis — Taipei's network was built project by project and the seams show.

### Reading the exit counts

Eighteen of 24 stations have **one to three exits**. Four have five or more:
**南京復興 (8), 南港展覽館 (8), 大安 (6), 忠孝復興 (5)** — all interchanges.
**Eleven stations have a single exit**, which is a real accessibility and
crowd-flow characteristic of an elevated medium-capacity line and worth stating.

### Three future interchanges are already known

- **BR01 動物園** → Circular Line **South Ring**
- **BR15 劍南路** → Circular Line **North Ring**
- **BR22 東湖** → **Xidong Line**

**Wenhu will gain three new interchanges** as those lines open. Nothing in English
says so.

## The two underground stations — now confirmed by primary source

**BR13 松山機場 and BR14 大直 are the only underground stations.**

DORTS states the Neihu section has **12 stations, of which 3.9 km is underground
with 2 underground stations**. Its station architecture page independently confirms:
"除大直站為地下車站外，其餘共有十座高架車站" — apart from Dazhi being underground,
the remaining ten are elevated. Ten elevated plus Dazhi plus Songshan Airport = 12.

**The site previously marked BR13/BR14 as `structure: unknown` with a dotted tick.
That can now be upgraded to confirmed, with a primary citation.**

## Alignment, in detail

### Muzha section — 10.9 km, 12 stations, entirely elevated

From **動物園**, via 新光路, 萬芳路, 興隆路, 辛亥路, passing the **north side of the
Xinhai Tunnel 辛亥隧道**, joining 和平東路, turning into 復興南路 and 復興北路, to
**中山國中** at the 民權東路 junction.

### Neihu section — 14.8 km, 12 stations, 3.9 km underground

From the **tail tracks at 中山國中**, elevated along 復興北路 to the 民族東路
junction, then into **Songshan Airport** where a transition section takes it
**underground**. 松山機場 station sits **beneath the surface car park in front of
the terminal building**.

It then runs north **under Songshan Airport and the Keelung River**, surfacing via
**北安路458巷41弄** on the east side of 北安路, beside the roundabout at the south
end of the **Ziqiang Tunnel 自強隧道** at Dazhi.

From there elevated east along 北安路, 內湖路, 文德路, 成功路, 康寧路, into the
**Nangang Economic and Trade Park**.

**A line that runs elevated, dives under an active international airport and a
river, resurfaces beside a road tunnel, and climbs back onto viaduct** — that
paragraph alone is better than anything currently in English.

## Construction history

### The Muzha line

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Part of | The initial **70.3 km network** — Tamsui, Xindian, Zhonghe, Nangang, Banqiao and Muzha lines |
| Network approval | **4 April 1986**, Executive Yuan, single comprehensive approval |
| Network budget | **NT$444.4 billion (4444億)** |
| Groundbreaking | **15 December**, morning, at the 和平東路三段 / 富陽街 junction, presided over by DORTS director-general **齊寶錚** |
| Muzha line cost | **NT$25 billion (250億)** |
| **初勘** | **19 August 1994** |
| **履勘** | **1 September 1994** |
| **Opened** | **28 March 1996** |

**Contract structure** — fixed facilities were designed under the detailed-design
clauses of the medium-capacity system contract **CC350**:

| Package | Contracts |
|---|---|
| **Substructure** (5) | C401, C404, C405, C409, C412 |
| **Superstructure** (5) | CC402, CC406, CC410A, CC410B, CC413 |
| **Station buildings** (4) | CC403, CC407, CC411, CC414 |

**Fourteen contracts for a 12-station line**, all hanging off one system contract.
That structure is what later made the Matra dispute so tangled.

### The Neihu line — and the eight-year argument

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **27 March** | Executive Yuan approves the network plan; the medium-capacity brown line is listed as a **secondary corridor** |
| **7 August 1993** | Executive Yuan approves the Neihu line |
| **1993, during system testing** | **A tyre ignition accident (輪胎起火意外事故)** on the Muzha line |
| — | **Taipei City Council demands the Neihu line be re-evaluated for a high-capacity system** |
| **Eight years of study follow** | |
| **9 January 2001, 19:19** | **The City Council votes** to proceed with the elevated medium-capacity system as originally approved in 1993 |

**The council recorded the vote to the minute.** 19:19 on 9 January 2001. After
eight years of argument. That detail alone is worth the page.

### The fire — DORTS settles the contradiction

The research files have carried an unresolved conflict: was the September 1993
incident a **vehicle fire** (火燒車) or a **tyre overheating incident** misreported?

**DORTS's own project page calls it 輪胎起火意外事故 — a tyre ignition accident.**

That is the builder's own description, in its official published account, and it
supports the enthusiast reading over the "car fire" framing.

**It does not fully settle it** — zh.wikipedia describes a 火燒車 on EMU no. 47
between 六張犁 and 麟光 on **24 September 1993**, and both could describe the same
event at different levels of precision. But the site should now lead with the
primary-source wording and note the secondary framing, not the other way round.

**Consequences of the fire:**
- MOTC halted all Muzha line work
- **3 February 1994** — the Control Yuan impeached DORTS director-general
  **齊寶錚** and **seven other officials (8 total)**
- The Neihu line was frozen for eight years

### The Songshan Airport station — added late

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **February 2000** | MOTC directs a study on serving Songshan Airport directly with a station |
| — | Adding a station on the Neihu line assessed as **more engineering-feasible and schedule-compatible** than alternatives; route micro-adjusted |
| **1 August 2001** | Executive Yuan agrees in principle |
| **4 January 2002** | Public Construction Commission approves the funding |
| **Total Neihu budget** | **NT$60.997 billion (609.97億)** |

**This is why 松山機場 carries engineering number BR1** — it was inserted into the
project after the B-series had been allocated.

### The procurement strategy — genuinely clever, and worth explaining

DORTS faced two problems: **system compatibility** across an extension to an
existing proprietary line, and **too few qualified bidders**.

Its solution: **區段標土木帶電機** — civil section contracts *carrying* the E&M.

The constraint driving it: **the E&M system had to run through from Muzha to Neihu
as a single journey with no passenger transfer.** So rather than tender E&M
separately, DORTS folded it into the civil section contracts, **with the civil
contractor as the signing party**.

| Contract | Scope |
|---|---|
| **CB410** | Civil section — **with E&M contract CB370 folded in** |
| **CB420** | Civil section |
| **CB430** | Civil section |

| Contract | Signed |
|---|---|
| **CB420** | **11 April 2002** — **the earliest work on the whole line** |
| **CB410** | **12 June 2003** |

Detailed design: **5 contracts** — DB144A, DB144B, DB144, DB145, DB146.

**A civil contractor holding the signalling contract for a driverless metro** is an
unusual arrangement, and it is directly relevant to how the 2009 opening went. Worth
connecting explicitly.

### Neihu opening

| Item | Date |
|---|---|
| **初勘** | **7 May 2009** |
| **履勘** | **6 June 2009** |
| Free trial rides, elevated section 劍南路–南港展覽館 | **28 June – 1 July 2009** (underground 松山機場 and 大直 not opened) |
| **Opened** | **4 July 2009** |
| Fare promotion | 4–6 July 2009, **50% off** with EasyCard, whole network |

**On 4 July 2009 the Muzha line switched to the Bombardier system, cutting off the
Siemens VAL system after 13 years of use**, and the Neihu line was formally
connected.

**8 October 2009** — because the abbreviation 柵湖線 was considered
inauspicious (see the 詐胡 homophone in part 2), DORTS resolved to rename the line
**文湖線** after the Wenshan and Neihu districts it serves.

## Station architecture — a section nobody has written

DORTS publishes its design rationale, and it's unusually articulate.

### Muzha section

Elevated stations in **steel and glass with simple forms**, positioned as
"展現都市現代建築風貌" — expressing modern urban architecture.

The design constraints were severe and are stated plainly:

- Sites are **mostly within city roads** and elevated, so form was heavily
  constrained
- **Single-column support systems** imposed load limits, requiring the removal of
  **any unnecessary decoration**
- Station volumes had to be kept **as far as possible from roadside buildings**

Under those constraints, modern form in steel and glass was chosen as the best
expression of "a transport technology system in a modernising city."

DORTS's own illustrative example: **中山國中站**.

**That is a designer explaining why a station looks the way it does, in terms of
structural load and setback.** It's exactly the material a reference site should
carry and it is completely absent in English.

### Neihu section

All elevated except **大直**, which is underground — **ten elevated stations** in
the architecture page's count.

Three design vocabularies were assigned by the character of the areas passed:

| Vocabulary | Chinese |
|---|---|
| Residential and urban-rural character | 住宅城鄉風貌 |
| Commercial and leisure environment | 商業休閒環境 |
| Industrial and technological landscape | 工業科技景觀 |

The viaduct itself was then used to link them into a single conceit: **「空中之河」
— a river in the sky.**

**A metro line designed as a river in the sky, with three architectural dialects
along its length.** That's the framing for the whole page.

## Rolling stock — with a conflict to resolve

Full detail in `rolling-stock.md`. Line-specific points:

### VAL256 (Matra, later Siemens)

**Operating periods: 28 March 1996 – 3 July 2009, then 26 December 2010 – present.**

Note the precision: withdrawn the day *before* the Neihu line opened, absent for
almost eighteen months.

Car width **2.56 m**.

### Innovia APM 256 / C370 (Bombardier)

Car width **2.54 m** — **2 cm narrower than VAL256**.

**Interior height only 190 cm** — lower than the Muzha line cars. That's a
tangible passenger-facing difference and nobody mentions it.

### A manufacturing conflict — flag, don't resolve

zh.wikipedia 臺北捷運列車 states the Innovia fleet comprises **101 pairs (202 cars)
built at Bombardier's Pittsburgh plant** plus **51 pairs (102 cars) final-assembled
at 唐榮車輛科技 (Tang Eng)**.

That totals **152 pairs of Innovia alone** — which contradicts DORTS's contract
figures of **CC350 = 51 pairs (Matra)** and **CB370 = 101 pairs (Bombardier)**,
totalling 152 pairs *across both fleets*.

**Both cannot be right.** Possible readings: the wiki sentence is in error; or the
51 Tang Eng pairs are a later addition; or "final assembly at Tang Eng" describes
the **conversion** of the Matra fleet rather than new build.

**Do not publish either version until resolved.** The 76-train / 304-car total is
well corroborated, so start from there and work backwards.

### The conversion deadline Bombardier missed

DORTS required Bombardier to **complete the VAL256 conversion by 14 February 2010**
— moved from an original 14 January because network optimisation work had consumed
the schedule.

**Bombardier could not meet it.** DORTS treated that as breach of contract and
stated it would levy penalties.

Converted trains were tested for **three and a half hours nightly (00:30–04:00),
three times a week**, after service ended.

**A contractor formally in breach over a fleet conversion, tested in the small
hours three nights a week** — that belongs in the Matra article's aftermath section.

## Ridership — one station as a worked example

**BR16 西湖 Xihu**: approximately **27,427 daily passengers** (December 2024),
ranking **62nd** among Taipei Metro stations.

Xihu is also **structurally combined with 西湖市場 (Xihu Market)**, whose site hosts
a YouBike station, and it has an exit built on the market's land. Its public art is
**「跨越‧蛻變」** — "Crossing, Transformation" — on the east and north walls and the
floor of the Xihu Composite Building.

**Per-station ridership rankings exist for every station** and would make the
station pages substantially more useful. Worth a systematic pull.
