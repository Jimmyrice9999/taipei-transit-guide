# Danhai LRT 淡海輕軌 (V)

Run: 2026-08-12. New Taipei Metro Corporation (NTMC), New Taipei City
Department of Rapid Transit Systems (DORTS, 新北市政府捷運工程局).

## What is established

### 1. The 1992 feasibility study rejected extending the heavy Tamsui line

zh.wikipedia's history section states: 「台北市政府捷運工程局於1992年11月完成
《淡海輕軌可行性研究報告》，否決了自台北捷運淡水線淡水站以高運量大眾捷運系統延伸
的構想，而建議以中運量大眾捷運系統型式自紅樹林站將捷運延伸至淡海地區。」— the
Taipei DORTS completed a Danhai Light Rail Feasibility Study in November 1992
which rejected extending the existing heavy-rail (高運量) Tamsui Line from
Tamsui Station, recommending instead a medium-capacity (中運量) system
extending from Hongshulin.
- Source: 淡海輕軌, 維基百科 (zh.wikipedia), https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-tw/淡海輕軌,
  accessed 2026-08-12, SECONDARY, confidence Medium — read in full; the article
  cites this to a "comprehensive planning report" footnote that was not itself
  located. No DORTS or MOTC primary document stating the 1992 study's
  conclusion in these terms was found independently. The 1999–2002 follow-on
  (Taipei DORTS submitted the medium-capacity proposal to MOTC in 1999; MOTC
  in 2002 declined to approve construction, citing budget priority and
  recommending bus feeder service instead — 「以目前政府財政狀況…建議短期內以
  加強運用公車接駁營運方式…」) is on the same page, same sourcing status.
- **Checked and failed:** searched for the 1992 report or a MOTC/DORTS primary
  page describing it directly; not found. Recorded here as secondary only.

### 2. Green Mountain Line — length, stations, opening

DORTS's own project page for the Green Mountain Line states: 「路線長度：
7.34公里」「車站數量：11座」「通車日期：107年12月24日」(route length 7.34 km,
11 stations, opening date 24 December 2018 [ROC year 107]). It also gives
construction milestones: feasibility study approved by the Executive Yuan 8
June 2010 (民國99年), comprehensive plan approved 25 February 2013 (民國102年),
main-line stability testing completed 26 October 2018, MOTC on-site inspection
10 December 2018 (per the routeProgress-style figure quoted in the Blue Sea
Phase 1 page, see below — the Green Mountain page itself gives 19 December
2018 as the date MOTC approved commencement of operations, 「107年12月19日
交通部准予營運」), and total project funding of 128.1億元 (NT$12.81 billion).
- Source: 淡海綠山線, New Taipei City DORTS,
  https://www.dorts.ntpc.gov.tw/about/route/kq4VmGpM2BoY, accessed
  2026-08-12, PRIMARY, confidence High — builder's own project record, read
  in full (raw text dump).

### 6. Rolling stock — 行武者號, confirmed independently from Ankeng

NTMC's own light-rail rolling stock page states directly: 「全國第一列國車國造
之輕軌列車為淡海輕軌列車」— the first domestically-manufactured (國車國造) light
rail train in Taiwan is the Danhai LRT train. The same page's spec table
gives, for what it labels a general "light rail train" specification shared
by both Danhai and Ankeng: steel wheel/steel rail traction; 30 trains total
fleet, split 「淡海15列車+安坑15列車」(15 for Danhai, 15 for Ankeng); 5 cars per
train; minimum curve radius on the main line 25 m; maximum gradient 70‰;
dimensions 34,450 mm × 2,650 mm × 3,750 mm; floor height 350 mm; body
material stainless steel plus weathering steel; capacity 265 passengers (62
seated, 203 standing); mixed longitudinal/transverse seating; electric,
hydraulic and track brakes; maximum speed 70 km/h under catenary, 50 km/h
off-wire; power supply 750 V DC catenary or lithium-battery storage. A
companion NTMC overview page independently gives the same fleet split (「共30
列車(淡海15列車+安坑15列車)」) and the same capacity and speed figures.
- Source: 輕軌列車, New Taipei Metro Corporation,
  https://www.ntmetro.com.tw/basic/?mode=detail&node=19, accessed
  2026-08-12, PRIMARY, confidence High — read in full (raw text dump).
- Source: 列車介紹, New Taipei Metro Corporation,
  https://www.ntmetro.com.tw/basic/?node=10012, accessed 2026-08-12, PRIMARY,
  confidence High.
- **On the lead file's caution:** the lead file said Danhai's specs were
  "very likely the same or closely related" to Ankeng's, based only on
  exterior similarity, and told the researcher to confirm separately rather

### 8. The 2017–2020 Kaohsiung Rapid Transit Corporation arrangement

zh.wikipedia states: 「2017年新北市政府與中鋼公司簽約將淡海輕軌委由高雄捷運公司
代維修與經營3年」and a fuller account, also on the page and corroborated by a
search-engine synthesis of the cited footnotes (TVBS, 8 June 2017; newtalk.tw,
8 June 2017; New Taipei City Government press office, 8 June 2017), describes
the structure as: the EPC turnkey contractor for the line was China Steel
Corporation (中鋼), which subcontracted operation and maintenance to Kaohuiung
Rapid Transit Corporation (高雄捷運公司) for the first three years, while New
Taipei Metro Corporation (NTMC), itself only formed and registered on 26
January 2018, handled administration, finance, fares and the line's
profit/loss. Kaohsiung Rapid Transit Corporation had, at that point, the only
prior light-rail operating experience in Taiwan (the Kaohsiung Circular Light
Rail). After the three-year term, operation transferred to NTMC directly, which
is consistent with NTMC's own light rail train page (above) presenting itself
as the current operator with no reference to KRTC.
- Source: 淡海輕軌, 維基百科, https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-tw/淡海輕軌, accessed
  2026-08-12, SECONDARY, confidence Medium — read in full. The page's own
  footnotes (TVBS 2017-06-08, newtalk.tw 2017-06-08, and a New Taipei City
  Government press item of the same date) were located by title via search
  but the TVBS and newtalk.tw originals could not be fetched directly (see
  Checked and failed); the New Taipei City Government's contemporaneous
  framing — that Mayor Chu Li-luan announced formation of a New Taipei Metro
  Company in a council question session on 8 June 2017, with the interim
  Danhai arrangement being KRTC for O&M under the China Steel EPC contract —
  is corroborated by the company-registry fact of NTMC's 26 January 2018
  incorporation date, independently found via a company-registry search.
- **This remains the weakest-sourced claim on the page.** The lead file
  called this "completely undocumented in English" and flagged the contract
  and handover mechanics as TBC; that is still true here. What this research
  adds beyond the lead file is the China Steel intermediary detail and the
  NTMC formation date, neither of which was in the lead file, but both rest
  on a secondary account whose own primary citations were not independently
  read.
- **Checked and failed** (2026-08-12): attempted to fetch the TVBS
  (2017-06-08) and newtalk.tw (2017-06-08) articles zh.wikipedia cites for
  this arrangement, via their web.archive.org snapshots — the fetch tool
  cannot retrieve web.archive.org in this environment. The claim above is
  therefore sourced to zh.wikipedia's synthesis, not to the underlying news

### 9. Public art — Jimmy Liao (幾米), 《閉上眼睛一下下》

NTMC's own public-art page states the Green Mountain Line's public art was
made in collaboration with illustrator 幾米 (Jimmy Liao), based on his book
《閉上眼睛一下下》("Close Your Eyes for a Moment"), covering all 11 stations,
inspired by scenery near each station, and describing the project as taking
「耗時二年多的時間」(more than two years). It calls the project 幾米's first
formal large-scale public art work: 「淡海輕軌公共藝術案是幾米的第一個正式的
大型公共藝術作品」, and states 「整條綠山線就是幾米的作品」— the whole Green
Mountain Line functions as one work.
- Source: 淡海輕軌公共藝術, New Taipei Metro Corporation,
  https://www.ntmetro.com.tw/basic/?mode=detail&node=17, accessed
  2026-08-12, PRIMARY, confidence High — read in full.
- The artist's own site gives a different account of scope and duration: 11
  stations plus two bus shelters (中山濱海路口, 濱海義山) — 13 installations in
  total — and a timeline "from a 2015 invitation to 2018 completion," which it
  describes as "nearly four years," not "two years plus." It also states the
  project won the 2020 National Excellence Construction Award, Best
  Environmental Culture category (2020國家卓越建設 最佳環境文化類 卓越獎).
  Source: 淡海輕軌綠山線．幾米公共藝術, jimmyspa.com (the artist's official
  site), https://www.jimmyspa.com/tw/PublicArt/2016-2020/2018DanhaiLRT,
  accessed 2026-08-12, PRIMARY (the artist's own studio site, describing its

### 11. Depot — 淡海機廠

Location and functions are corroborated across two secondary sources: a New
Taipei City tourism-site feature on the depot's public open days states it
sits beside V11 Kanding, is where trains are maintained, tested and repainted
(維修、測試、塗裝), and describes it as the highest classification of light
rail maintenance facility in Taiwan (五級, "grade 5"), with functions
including sanding (加砂作業) for wheel-rail friction and a multi-stage wash
process. A dedicated zh.wikipedia article on the depot gives an address (新北
市淡水區新市六路一段161號), an area of approximately 5.38 hectares, a stated
capacity of 16 trains (flagged on the page itself with "[citation needed]"),
and describes the depot as intended eventually to also serve two further
planned lines sharing the same numbering scheme, 三芝線 (Sanzhih) and 八里線
(Bali) — but the zh.wikipedia depot article as a whole states at the bottom
that, as of the version read, it carries no listed references at all
(「沒有列出任何參考或來源」).
- Source: 女力帶路｜Open淡海機廠!, 新北市觀光旅遊網 (New Taipei City tourism
  site), https://newtaipei.travel/zh-tw/column/939, accessed 2026-08-12,
  SECONDARY (city tourism marketing content, not an engineering record),
  confidence Medium for functions and classification, not independently
  corroborated for the "grade 5" claim.
- Source: 淡海機廠, 維基百科, https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-tw/淡海機廠, accessed
  2026-08-12, SECONDARY, confidence LOW for area and capacity specifically —
  the page itself discloses it has no sources, and the 16-train capacity
  figure additionally carries the page's own "citation needed" tag. The area
  figure (5.38 ha) repeats across several travel-blog aggregations found via
  search, but no primary DORTS/NTMC document stating it was located.
- A 自由時報 news report from the line's opening period gives a construction
  detail not found elsewhere: the depot was not finished when the Green
  Mountain Line opened. 「淡海輕軌第一期路網（綠山線和部分藍海線）預計年底前
  通車試營運，但是集行控中心、儲車和檢修等多項重責大任的機廠，卻因用地取得
  問題，無法在淡海輕軌通車前完成，遲至明年8月才完工」— the depot, housing the
  operations control centre, stabling and maintenance, could not be finished
  before opening because of land-acquisition problems (the site was
  originally private farmland with landowners who disagreed on terms), and
  was not expected to be complete until August 2019, about eight months after
  the Green Mountain Line's opening.
  Source: 淡海輕軌年底就要營運 機廠竟8個月後才蓋好, 自由時報, 14 December
  2018, https://news.ltn.com.tw/news/life/breakingnews/2642984, accessed
  2026-08-12, SECONDARY, confidence High for the reported facts — a
  contemporaneous news report, read in full, quoting a named DORTS deputy
  director.
- **Stated gap:** the depot's area and functions are reasonably established;
  its exact opening/commissioning date, and how the line operated with an
  unfinished depot for its first eight months, are not.

### 12. V11 → V26 numbering: now substantially explained

DORTS's own Phase 2 page (see item 5) gives the missing piece directly: Phase
2 stations are V21, V22, V23, V24, V25 and V26A, running from a new station at
Tamsui MRT station's rear plaza to a rejoin point at the existing V26 Tamsui
Fisherman's Wharf. That answers what V21–V26A are reserved for. It does not
explain V12–V20, which remain unaccounted for by any primary source found in
this research. The depot's zh.wikipedia article (unsourced, see item 11)
mentions two further planned lines — 三芝線 (Sanzhih) and 八里線 (Bali) — that
might plausibly draw on that range if they are ever built as part of the same
V-numbered network, but no source found here states that they would use it,
and this is recorded as an inference, not a claim.

  「通車日期」("date of opening to traffic") may record the date public fare
  service or the trial-operation status began, which zh.wikipedia's account
  calls 24 December's "試營運" (trial operation), while zh.wikipedia's "23
  December opened (通車)" may record a ceremonial or soft-opening date one
  day earlier that DORTS's field does not distinguish. This is not stated
  outright by either source; it is offered here as the most likely reading of
  the terms used, not as a settled fact. Both dates are published rather than
  one being silently preferred.
