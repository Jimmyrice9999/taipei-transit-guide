## Why this line matters

- **Northern Taiwan's first modern light rail**
- Its public art is a **single continuous Jimmy Liao picture book spanning all 11
  stations** — one artwork, thirteen locations, two years in the making
- Home to **one of Taiwan's last two surviving rail route stamps**
- Was operated by **Kaohsiung's** metro company for its first three years, which is
  an odd institutional arrangement nobody explains
- Its rolling stock, **行武者號**, is Taiwan-built

## Timeline

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **1992** | First proposed |
| **November 1992** | Taipei DORTS feasibility study **rejects extending the heavy Tamsui line** and recommends light or medium rail instead |
| **23 November 2014** | Stage 1 main works begin |
| **23 December 2018** | **Green Mountain Line opens** — V01 紅樹林 to V11 崁頂, 11 stations |
| **15 November 2020** | **Blue Sea Line phase 1 opens** — 台北海洋大學, 沙崙, 淡水漁人碼頭 |
| **28 March 2024** | Blue Sea phase 2 plan approved by MOTC |
| 2017–2020 | **Operated by Kaohsiung Rapid Transit Corporation** |

**The 1992 decision is the origin point.** Rather than extend the existing heavy
metro north from Tamsui, DORTS concluded light or medium rail suited the corridor —
a rare case of Taiwan choosing *not* to build heavy metro, and worth explaining
against the Ankeng decision two decades later.

**Blue Sea phase 2** adds 6 stations, currently in design, roughly 5 years to
completion after Executive Yuan approval.

### The V11 to V26 numbering gap

Green Mountain occupies V01–V11; the Blue Sea numbers start at V26. **Higher V
numbers are reserved within the 20-station master plan**, but the exact allocation
logic is **TBC**.

**This gap needs explaining** — an unexplained jump from 11 to 26 looks like an
error to any reader.

## The Jimmy Liao artwork — the line's signature

The Green Mountain Line's public art is by **Jimmy Liao 幾米**, based on his
picture book **《閉上眼睛一下下》** — *"Close Your Eyes for a While."*

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Artist | **Jimmy Liao 幾米** |
| Source work | 《閉上眼睛一下下》 |
| Coverage | **All 11 Green Mountain stations** |
| Total works | **13** — 11 stations plus **2 bus shelters** |
| Development time | **Two years** |

**This is not station decoration — it's one continuous narrative distributed across
a railway line.** A reader travelling the line reads the book in sequence. That is
genuinely unusual and it's the single most compelling thing about this line for a
general audience.

**Important copyright note:** do not reproduce the artwork. Describe it, cite it,
and link to official sources. Photographs of the stations that incidentally include
the art are a different matter — but check licensing carefully, since the art itself
is copyrighted even when photographed in situ.

## The route stamps

Station stamps (紀念章) were withdrawn after Blue Sea phase 1 opened. **The route
stamp survives at V01 紅樹林** — and it is **one of only two rail route stamps left
in Taiwan**.

For a transit enthusiast audience, that's a genuine draw and exactly the sort of
detail LTG would carry.

| One-day pass | **NT$50** |

**Bicycles are carried on the trains** — hence the NT$50 bike fare. That's worth
stating prominently; it's a real feature for a coastal tourist line and English
sources don't mention it.

Integrated with **TPASS 基北北桃1200**, and transfer discounts apply to feeder
buses.

## The Kaohsiung operating arrangement

**From 2017 to 2020 the line was operated by Kaohsiung Rapid Transit Corporation** —
a company from the other end of the island, running a New Taipei light rail line.

Kaohsiung had Taiwan's only prior modern light rail experience, which is presumably
the reason. **But the contractual arrangement, why it ended, and how the handover to
NTMC worked are all TBC.**

**This is a genuinely odd institutional fact and it's completely undocumented in
English.** Worth chasing properly.
