## 1. The Matra dispute — there are two, and they get conflated

This confirms the run 3 draft's central finding. Two separate conflicts, often
merged into one story.

### Dispute A — delay and compensation (the long one)

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1985 (ROC 74) | Taipei decides to build a medium-capacity system |
| July 1988 (ROC 77) | Matra signs with DORTS for detailed design and E&M works |
| — | **Civil works subcontractors fall behind.** Matra's own schedule extends, costs rise |
| — | Matra claims **NT$2 billion** citing 遲延履約 — delayed performance by the client |
| — | Taipei fights through commercial arbitration, then an action to set aside the arbitral award, then a debtor's objection suit |
| 2005 | Supreme Court rules against Taipei, final |
| — | **NT$1.025 billion principal; NT$1.64 billion with interest** |

Litigation ran **over twelve years**. The decisive point was that Matra secured
the **fifteen-year general civil claim limitation period** rather than a shorter
one — Taipei's debtor's objection suit had argued the claim was time-barred.

### Dispute B — the walkout (the famous one)

**May 1996, two months after opening.** Matra withdrew **without warning**, over
disputes about final acceptance and the performance bond. It also **barred its
subcontractors from selling spare parts to TRTC**, and no technology transfer had
taken place.

**Four days later the control centre computer crashed.**

This produced the line most Taipei residents still remember — Mayor Chen
Shui-bian's 「馬特拉不拉，我們自己拉」, roughly *"If Matra won't pull, we'll pull it
ourselves."* It puns on 拉, the last character of Matra's Chinese name, meaning
"pull."

Chen also convened a Muzha line inspection committee, which found **cracks in over
one hundred cap beams**.

### Why the conflation matters

Dispute A is a construction-delay claim that Matra won. Dispute B is an
abandonment that damaged Matra's reputation permanently. English sources merge
them into "Matra sued and left," which loses that **Matra was found legally in
the right on the delay claim while behaving badly on the exit** — and that the
public memory is of B while the money was in A.

**Sources:** Liberty Times 13 Nov 2008 and 23 July 2005 (via Epoch Times);
Peoplenews 28 March 2017; Business Weekly issue 445, 30 May 1996 (paywalled —
likely the best single account, worth buying). All secondary. **Court records
would be primary and should be findable.**

## 2. The 1993 fire — and a complication

**24 September 1993:** EMU no. 47 caught fire between Liuzhangli and Linguang.
MOTC halted all Muzha line work. On **3 February 1994** the Control Yuan
impeached DORTS director-general Chi Pao-cheng (齊寶錚) and seven other officials.

**Complication worth resolving.** At least one enthusiast account states that
Taipei was the **first application of VAL256 coupled as two pairs (four cars)**,
and that testing produced unexpected problems including **tyre overheating and
ignition, which was misreported as a vehicle fire** (火燒車).

If that refers to the same event, then the incident that triggered impeachments
may have been a tyre fire rather than a car fire — a materially different thing.
**Do not publish either version until this is settled.** A court or Control Yuan
record would resolve it.

## 3. The 2009 opening — "the swindle line"

The Neihu section opened **4 July 2009** and failed repeatedly almost immediately.

**The nickname.** The combined line was called 木柵內湖線, shortened to 柵湖線 —
which sounds like **詐胡**, a mahjong term for a **falsely declared winning hand**.
The joke was that the line had claimed victory without actually having one.

It was renamed **文山內湖線 → 文湖線** partly to escape this. Councillors claimed
resigning stations, announcements and print material would cost over NT$100
million; the city said NT$1–2 million. Mayor Hau Lung-bin denied the higher
figure publicly.

**The "assembled" critique.** Taiwanese academic commentary characterised the
line as 拼裝捷運 — a bolted-together metro — for running Bombardier CITYFLO 650
over infrastructure built for Matra's VAL. The same criticism was levelled at
Taiwan High Speed Rail as a Euro-Japanese hybrid.

**How it was resolved:** the Matra fleet was withdrawn "for maintenance" and all
service handed to the Bombardier stock. The failures stopped. The Matra units
returned to service **26 December 2010**, after a safety review committee cleared
the refit of all 51 pairs on 19 December — permitting headways of 1.5 to 3
minutes.

**Fares as an admission:** from **25 July 2009** EasyCard fares were cut to **64%
of normal**. The normal 80% discount only returned on **17 September 2010**, after
TRTC declared system stability verification passed on 31 August. That fourteen-month
discount is the clearest measure of how bad it was.

**Documented incidents in the first period:**

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 22 Oct 2009, 09:17 | Network anomaly at Jiannan Road station stops the entire line for **47 minutes** |
| 26 Nov 2009, 08:33 | Trains **101 and 107 collide while shunting at Neihu Depot**. Both windscreens destroyed; car caps, couplers, guide wheels and obstacle-detection bars damaged |
| 29 Sept (year to confirm) | Train 157 suffers a **tyre burst** between Jiannan Road and Xihu. 80 passengers evacuated, no injuries. Train immobilised |

*Sources: zh.wikipedia 文湖線 and 內湖線; Liberty Times 10 Oct 2009; twstreetcorner
academic blog 6 April 2021; Wealth Magazine on Bombardier.*

## 4. Reliability — the numbers, and why they're contested

### 2018 to mid-2022 (councillor Keng Wei, 4.5 years)

Network total: **270 failures.**

| Line | Failures |
|---|---|
| **Wenhu** | **68** |
| Bannan | 56 |
| Tamsui–Xinyi | 56 |
| Zhonghe–Luzhou | 36 |
| Songshan–Xindian | 31 |
| Circular | 23 |

Most common fault network-wide: **door failures, 91**, of which **40 were door
controller failures**. Wenhu's leading causes were door faults and **brake caliper
temperature detection failure**.

Circular Line note: nearly **90% of its failures were wheel slip**.

### 2022 to April 2026 (councillor Chin Hui-chu, >5 minute delays)

Wenhu **13**, versus 0, 2, 4 and 5 on the other four lines. Wenhu's annual figures:
5, 4, 2, 2, 0 — **declining sharply**.

### TRTC's own delay counts
2023: **21** · 2024: **10** · 2025: **8**

### TRTC's explanation, and it's a fair one

General manager Huang Ching-hsin: Wenhu records more >5-minute failures **because
it is driverless**. With no crew aboard, station staff must be dispatched to the
train, so clearance takes longer. The same underlying fault produces a longer
delay than it would on a crewed line.

**That's an important caveat for any comparison table on the site.** Wenhu is not
straightforwardly "less reliable" — it is slower to recover.

### Equipment life dates — the reason for the renewal

- **Signalling life expires 2029**
- **Rolling stock life expires 2036**

A Wenhu replacement planning task force exists. **NT$5.25 billion** was committed
to the replacement fund in the year before November 2025.

**November 2025:** six experts inspected the line and issued **seven improvement
recommendations**, including review of semiconductor switching element failures in
train power control, driverless-system risk, wet-weather speed reduction
principles, and **water film slip**.

Mitigations now in place: wet-weather speed reduction, revised maintenance
standards for the **steel running surface**, improved evacuation procedures,
strengthened propulsion protection design, adjusted critical part replacement
intervals.

*Sources: UDN 1–2 Aug 2022, 20 Nov 2025, 17–18 May 2026.*

## 5. Muzha section specifics

- Length **10.9 km**, entirely elevated
- **End-to-end running time 22 minutes**
- Route: Zhongshan Junior High School → Fuxing N/S Rd → Heping E Rd → Fuzhou
  Mountain Tunnel → Xinhai Rd → Xinglong Rd → Wanfang Rd → Muzha → Xinguang Rd →
  Taipei Zoo
- **Fuzhou Mountain Tunnel** — worth noting, since the Muzha section is otherwise
  described as fully elevated

### VAL256 technical notes
- DC rubber-tyred medium-capacity EMU
- ATO fitted, driverless
- **Taipei was the first application of VAL256 coupled as two pairs into a
  four-car set** — a genuinely notable first, and the source of early teething
  problems
- Siemens later acquired Matra's transport division, making VAL a Siemens product

## 6. Political and institutional context

Worth a section of its own, because it explains the line's reputation better than
any technical account.

- The Neihu extension was **shelved for four years** after the Muzha line opened.
  Stated reason: unresolved elevated-versus-underground alignment. Contributing
  factors: Matra's problems, complaints that elevated structure damaged the
  streetscape, and low capacity. Other planned lines were delayed in the same
  period.
- Residents actively campaigned to change the system type.
- Bombardier's local agent, Lifu (來福), **hired former deputy DORTS director Wu
  Hsiu-kuang as a consultant** — reported at the time and worth verifying, since
  it bears on the procurement.
- Wealth Magazine's framing: whether the Neihu problems were the city rushing to
  open or Bombardier's technical shortcomings, Matra's reputational collapse had
  made choosing Matra politically impossible regardless of engineering merit.

**This is the thread that makes the line's story coherent** — technology choices
driven by political memory rather than engineering assessment. It's absent from
English coverage entirely.

## Contradictions to resolve before publishing

1. **The 1993 fire.** Car fire, or tyre fire misreported? Sources differ.
2. **Renaming cost.** NT$1–2 million (city) versus over NT$100 million
   (councillors). Publish both.
3. **Train 157 tyre burst** — year not established.
4. **Failure counts** use different definitions (all failures vs >5 minutes) and
   different periods. Don't merge them into one table.
5. **Matra claim amount** — NT$2 billion claimed, NT$1.025 billion awarded,
   NT$1.64 billion paid with interest. Three figures, all correct, easy to
   conflate.

## Checked and failed

Claims in this file verified against a source that did not survive. Kept here
rather than deleted, so the same lead is not chased twice.

- **The 福州山隧道 on the Muzha section's route** — DORTS's own alignment text
  says the line 「穿越辛亥隧道北側接和平東路」, passing the north side of the
  Xinhai road tunnel, and closes 「全線以高架方式興建」, built entirely on
  viaduct. Checked 7 August 2026, run 7, against the builder's project page.
  zh.wikipedia 文湖線 does read 「穿越福州山隧道後」, so the two accounts of that
  half-kilometre disagree — but a viaduct does not run through a hill, and the
  site's section drawing follows the builder. Not settled; do not publish a
  tunnel here.
- **"The 1993 fire" as a single event** — there were two, and the second is
  recorded as exactly that. Checked 7 August 2026, run 7, against zh.wikipedia
  麟光站: 「1993年5月5日：辛亥站前發生火燒車事件」 and 「1993年9月24日：發生第二次
  火燒車事件」. Treating the year as one incident understates the pattern that
  produced the Control Yuan impeachments.
