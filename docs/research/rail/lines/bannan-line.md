## Why this line matters

- **The network's first east–west line**
- **The busiest line on the network**
- Connects **three 三鐵共構 stations** — Banqiao, Taipei Main and Nangang — each
  combining metro, conventional rail and high speed rail
- Built through the densest commercial districts in Taipei, including
  **tunnelling beneath a first-grade historic monument**

## Operations — the most complex on the network

Three service patterns run simultaneously:

1. **Full-length:** Nangang Exhibition Center – Dingpu, ~48 minutes end to end
2. **Short working:** Nangang Exhibition Center – Far Eastern Hospital,
   ~38 minutes, densifying the central section
3. **Extra workings:** on weekday mornings, **empty trains are dispatched from
   Jiangzicui or Banqiao towards Kunyang** purely to absorb over-saturated
   loading between Jiangzicui and Longshan Temple

That third pattern is a striking detail — the line is busy enough that empty
trains are run into the peak direction to pick up. Worth stating plainly.

## Opening sequence

| Date | Section | 初勘 | 履勘 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **24 December 1999** | Municipal Government – Ximen, Ximen – Longshan Temple | 24 Nov 1999 | 13 Dec 1999 |
| 30 December 2000 | Municipal Government – Kunyang | 25 Nov 2000 | 18 Dec 2000 |
| 4 July 2009 | Nangang Exhibition Center platform opens with the Neihu line | | |
| 27 February 2011 | Nangang – Nangang Exhibition Center | | |
| **6 July 2015** | Dingpu – Yongning, completing the line | | |

**The 1999 opening was deliberately rushed.** DORTS states the Municipal
Government–Ximen and Ximen–Longshan Temple sections were **accelerated to
complete the 雙十 "double cross" network** with the north–south lines and start
generating network effects sooner.

**A curious interim arrangement:** from 4 July 2009, when Nangang Exhibition
Center opened on the Wenhu line but not yet on Bannan, TRTC ran a **free shuttle
bus** between Nangang and Nangang Exhibition Center — operated by **Zhinan
Bus** — specifically to relieve loading at Zhongxiao Fuxing. It stopped at 15:00
on 27 February 2011 when the rail link opened.

## Construction

Part of the initial 70.3 km network, approved 4 April 1986.

**The original approved Blue Line ran Banqiao to Songshan.** DORTS assessed that
extending from Songshan to Nangang would serve Songshan, Nangang and Xizhi
commuter trips, guide balanced metropolitan development, and create interchange
with the TRA trunk line at both Songshan and Nangang. The Executive Yuan
approved the change on **21 January 1988**.

The revised Nangang line runs from Ximen along Zhonghua Road, Zhongxiao West
Road and Zhongxiao East Road, becoming **elevated at the Dongxin Street tunnel
exit**, crossing the TRA main line east of Xiangyang Road, then running along
the north side of the TRA line to Nangang — originally with **one elevated and
one at-grade station**. Taipei City Council intervened on 14 December 1989
(outcome needs checking).

### Beneath Beimen

DORTS names this line as **among the network's most technically difficult**,
because it runs through the densest commercial areas with the heaviest traffic,
adjacent to Taipei Main Station, the Executive Yuan and the Control Yuan — and
because **construction had to pass beneath Beimen (北門), a first-grade historic
monument**.

### The 2014 attack

On **21 May 2014** a mass stabbing occurred on a Bannan Line train between
Longshan Temple and Jiangzicui stations. It is the most serious incident in the
Taipei Metro's history and led to lasting changes in security, staffing and
emergency procedure across the network.

**If the site covers it, it should be brief, factual, and focused on the
transport consequences rather than the perpetrator or the violence.** Details of
casualties, the trial and the individual belong elsewhere. The relevant material
for a transit reference is what changed operationally afterwards — and that
still needs researching.

## A footnote

The romanisation "Bannan" and its resemblance to "banana" became a running joke
after opening, and is noted in Chinese sources as a known quirk of the line's
English name.
