## Why this line matters

- **Taiwan's first high-capacity metro line to open**
- **The only high-capacity line with elevated AND at-grade running** — three
  construction types on one route
- The longest high-capacity line on the network
- Built along a **converted railway corridor**, not a new alignment
- Has an extension **opening this month** (see below)

### Alignment, north to south

From Tamsui station the line runs south along the **former TRA Tamsui railway
(北淡線) corridor**. It descends to **at grade** at the Guizikeng Creek north of
Beitou, rises to **elevated** from the Guizikeng embankment, crosses the Keelung
River, then descends **underground** after Minzu West Road, running underground
to CKS Memorial Hall.

The Xinyi section from CKS Memorial to Xiangshan is **entirely underground**.

**That three-way transition is the line's defining physical characteristic** and
would make an excellent diagram — a profile drawing showing elevation against
distance.

## Service patterns — a history of its own

This line's operating patterns changed repeatedly, and the changes tell the
story of the network being built around it.

| Date | Pattern |
|---|---|
| 24 Dec 1998 | **Tamsui – Nanshijiao** (through to Xindian line and Zhonghe line) |
| 11 Nov 1999 | **Tamsui – Xindian** and **Beitou – Nanshijiao** |
| 30 Sept 2012 | Nanshijiao pattern dropped; **Beitou – Taipower Building** added |
| 23 Nov 2013 | Last Beitou – Taipower Building train, ~23:00 |
| 24 Nov 2013 | **Beitou – Xiangshan**, interleaved with Tamsui – Xindian, 6 min each, 3 min on the shared section |
| **14 Nov 2014** | **Cross-colour operation ends permanently** |

### The end of cross-colour running

On 14 November 2014, with the Songshan line opening, the last Tamsui–Xindian
trains left Tamsui and Xindian at midnight. Trains had been running across what
are now two differently coloured lines — an arrangement that made sense when the
network was described by construction project names rather than colours.

**Line names and numbers were only formalised on 11 October 2014**, a month
earlier, when TRTC announced the Red Line would be the Tamsui–Xinyi line,
numbered 2.

**This is a strong article on its own** and nothing covers it in English.

## Construction context

Part of the initial 70.3 km network — Tamsui, Xindian, Zhonghe, Nangang,
Banqiao and Muzha lines — approved by the Executive Yuan on **4 April 1986**
with a total budget of **NT$444.4 billion**.

The Tamsui line comprised **13 design contracts** (DT151, 152, 153, 154, 154A,
155, 155A, 158, 163, 164, 184, 185, 186) and **120 construction contracts**,
plus E&M packages covering rolling stock (CT301), signalling (C302), power
(C303), communications (C305), escalators (C306A), lifts (C306B), automatic fare
collection (C307), depot equipment (CT309A), locomotives (CT309B), works
vehicles (CT309C), depot auxiliary equipment (CT309D) and a driving simulator
(CT309F).

**Earliest contract: CT107, Beitou Depot earthworks, signed 19 January 1988.**
That makes Beitou Depot the first physical work on the entire Taipei Metro.

*Source: DORTS (primary).*
