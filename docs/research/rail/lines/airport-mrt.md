## Why this line matters

- **Taiwan's longest single metro line**
- **Asia's fourth airport rail link**
- **The only line in Taiwan with skip-stop operation** (跳站停靠) — express and
  commuter services on the same track
- **28 years from planning to opening**, with six publicly announced delays and a
  NT$7.2 billion claim against the contractor
- Operated by **TYMC**, a third operator distinct from TRTC and NTMC
- The line most foreign visitors actually use, and the thinnest English coverage
  relative to that demand

## The delays — the story of this line

Planning began in **1989**. Construction did not start until **2006**. Opening
was **2 March 2017**.

Causes cited include the **1997 Asian financial crisis**, an early BOT model that
collapsed, and repeated contractor and systems-integration failures.

**The line missed its opening date six times** (六度延宕).

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 17 July (2015) | Another missed deadline announced; integration said to need 9 more months, pushing opening to February 2016 |
| **5 March (2016)** | After the sixth delay, MOTC's High Speed Rail Bureau resolved to claim **NT$2.5 billion in contract damages** and **forfeit NT$1.9 billion in performance bonds** — **NT$7.2 billion total** against Japanese contractor **Marubeni 丸紅** |
| 5 August | Taipei DORTS handed over Taipei Main Station civil works, plus the elevated and underground sections to Sanchong (exclusive), to the HSR Bureau; the HSR Bureau completed track and handed E&M to TYMC |
| 2 December | Fares published — Taipei Main to Taoyuan Airport **NT$160** single, also the maximum fare; **express and commuter priced identically** |

**A NT$7.2 billion claim against a major Japanese contractor over a metro line is
a substantial story and nothing covers it in English.**

Also worth noting: **28 June** — an afternoon thunderstorm burst a drainage pipe
at Taoyuan Airport car park 4; the pipe ran above the A13 concourse ceiling and
water poured into the hall. Staff intervened and no E&M equipment failed.

## Service patterns — unique in Taiwan

Four distinct patterns operate:

**Express 直達車 (purple)** — destination 機場第二航廈 T2
Stops: **A1 – A3 – A8 – A12 – A13**

**Commuter 普通車 (blue)** — destination 老街溪 Laojiexi
All stops A1 through A22. The **A21–A22 section is commuter-only**.

**Late-night northern commuter** — A1 to A13, single direction, airport-bound
from Taipei Main.

**Late-night southern commuter** — A22 to A12, single direction, airport-bound
from Laojiexi.

| Route | Time |

## Rolling stock

Two distinct fits, which is unusual and worth documenting properly.

### Express sets (purple)

- **2+2 transverse seating**, plus **four sets of facing seats** for groups
- The facing seats have **wireless charging pads**, with adapters provided for
  both iPhone and Android
- **4G and WiFi throughout**
- Luggage racks in every car, positioned **beside the doors**
- Dedicated wheelchair and pushchair space
- A dedicated **luggage car** in the formation

### Commuter sets (blue)

- **Longitudinal seating** with grab straps, for higher standing capacity
- Luggage racks in every car
- 4-car sets, **≈82 m**, **188 seats**, **≈1,000 crush capacity**

## In-town check-in

**A1 Taipei Main Station** and **A3 New Taipei Industrial Park** both offer free
advance check-in and baggage drop, up to **3 hours before departure**.

Participating airlines at A1: **China Airlines, EVA Air, Mandarin Airlines, UNI
Air, Starlux, AirAsia (excluding Japan AirAsia), Cathay Pacific**. Note tax
refund and customs declaration must be completed first, and passengers should
confirm baggage clears security before leaving.

This makes the Airport MRT **Asia's fourth airport rail link with in-town
check-in**, after Hong Kong, Kuala Lumpur and Incheon.

## The A22 extension and its signalling problem

**A22 老街溪 Laojiexi** was originally due to open by end December 2022. It
finally opened **31 July 2023**.

The cause was **signalling integration failure** — reportedly express trains
could only be routed into the extension by **manually changing signals**, and
integrating express and commuter signalling took additional time.

That is the same class of problem that plagued the main line's opening, more than
six years later. Worth drawing the connection.

### Zhongli–Xinwu extension

Full line estimated for **2029**. A23 中壢 planned. A14 T3 planned to serve the
airport's third terminal.

## Financial turnaround

An unusually good data story.

| Year | Result |
|---|---|
| 2023 | **First profit** since opening |
| 2025 | Ridership **over 46 million**, **+12% YoY** |
| 2025 | Surplus **NT$680 million** — a record |
| April (2026) | **All bank borrowings repaid in full** |

Ridership now exceeds pre-pandemic levels. Drivers cited: the TPASS monthly pass
launch and post-pandemic recovery.

A line that opened six years late amid a NT$7.2 billion contractor dispute is now
profitable and debt-free. **That arc is the article.**

## Ticketing specifics

- Coin-operated ticket machines plus staffed counters
- Single-journey tokens are returned to a collection slot on exit; day passes and
  EasyCard are tap-out
- Combination tickets: airport line return + Taipei Metro 48-hour pass **NT$520**;
  + 72-hour pass **NT$600**
- No smoking anywhere; no eating, drinking or chewing gum in the paid area
- Included in **TPASS 基北北桃1200** coverage
