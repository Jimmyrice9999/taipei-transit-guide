## Why this line matters

- **The only branching high-capacity line** — Y-shaped, and the hardest line on the
  network for a visitor to read
- Built as **three separate projects** later joined into one operation, which is why
  its station numbers come in **two disjoint series with a gap of 29**
- **Xinzhuang Depot sits on the site of the Losheng Sanatorium**, a Japanese-era
  leprosy hospital, and the preservation fight over it is one of Taiwan's
  best-known heritage disputes — **DORTS itself confirms the depot layout was
  altered to accommodate preservation**
- It contains **Zhonghe Depot, the smallest depot on the network at 1.47 hectares**,
  built on a former TRA station site and constrained enough that it can only do
  stabling and light maintenance
- It took **nearly 15 years to complete** — 1998 to 2013

### The three component lines and their boundaries

Per DORTS, the divisions are precise and worth stating:

- **古亭 Guting** is the southern boundary. **South of Guting is the Zhonghe line.**
- **North of Guting is the Xinzhuang line.**
- At **大橋頭 Daqiaotou** the route forks: **north-leaning is the Luzhou line**,
  **south-leaning continues as the Xinzhuang line.**

TRTC merged Luzhou and Xinzhuang into "**新蘆線 Xinlu line**" as the formal name;
once Xinlu was connected to Zhonghe, the whole became **中和新蘆線**.

**That naming history explains the awkward compound name** and nothing covers it in
English.

### Why the numbering looks broken

There is a **29-number gap between O21 and O50**. To a reader that looks like an
error. It isn't — it reflects the line having been built as separate projects with
independently allocated number ranges, and the O5x range being reserved for the
Luzhou branch.

**Nothing explains this in English, and it's exactly the kind of thing a reference
site should clear up in two sentences.**

## Opening sequence — now fully established

All inspection and opening dates below are from **DORTS, primary**, with ROC years
converted.

### Zhonghe line 中和線 — the first section

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Section | 古亭 Guting – 南勢角 Nanshijiao |
| **初勘** preliminary inspection | **6 November 1998** |
| **履勘** formal inspection | **8 December 1998** |
| **Opened** | **24 December 1998** |
| Length | **≈5.4 km** |
| Stations | **4** |
| Structure | Fully underground |
| Depot | Zhonghe 中和機廠 |

**On opening it ran through with the Xindian and Tamsui lines** as a **淡水 –
南勢角** service — providing the initial network's north–south backbone corridor.

**On 11 November 1999**, when the Xindian line opened fully, the pattern changed to
**北投 – 南勢角**.

### Alignment, Zhonghe line

From the **Roosevelt Road / Heping East Road junction** (connecting to 古亭 station
on the Xindian line), along Roosevelt Road, turning into Shida Road, to Shuiyuan
Road, then **crossing the Xindian River**, then along Yonghe Road, Zhonghe Road and
Jing'an Road to 南勢角 in Zhonghe.

### Xinzhuang and Luzhou — the second phase

**Construction began 2002**, as part of the metro's second-phase network.

| Section | 初勘 | 履勘 | **Opened** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Xinzhuang line, Taipei section:** 大橋頭 – 忠孝新生 | 4 Sept 2010 | 16 Oct 2010 | **3 November 2010** |
| **Xinzhuang line, New Taipei section:** 大橋頭 – 輔大 | 26 Nov 2011 | 17 Dec 2011 | **5 January 2012** |
| **東門 Dongmen** (忠孝新生 – 古亭) | 24 Aug 2012 | 14 Sept 2012 | **30 September 2012** |
| **輔大 – 迴龍** | 24 May 2013 | 14 June 2013 | **29 June 2013** |

**東門 Dongmen is the structurally important one.** Its opening on 30 September 2012
is what **connected the Zhonghe section to the Xinlu section**, turning three
separate projects into one line. Before that they were operationally distinct.

**Luzhou line opening date needs confirming.** The Luzhou branch (蘆洲 – 大橋頭)
almost certainly opened alongside the 大橋頭 – 忠孝新生 section on **3 November
2010**, but DORTS's page quoted here describes the Xinzhuang line sections
specifically. **Verify separately.**

### A testing detail worth including

**9 October 2011:** the Xinzhuang line's **輔大 – 忠孝新生** section ran **train
operation testing interleaved between Luzhou line services**, not open to the
public. Running test trains in the gaps of a live timetable is a nice operational
detail.

**26–27 November 2011:** Taipei's Transport Bureau conducted further testing.

### The completion timeline

**1998 to 2013 — nearly fifteen years** from the first section to the last. Worth
stating plainly as the headline framing.

## Project approvals and budget

The Xinzhuang line and Luzhou branch were **reported for approval together as one
project**:

| Item | Date | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Route plan approved by the Executive Yuan | **17 September 1994** | |
| EIA final report filed with the EPA | **28 January 1995** | |
| Finance plan approved | **15 July 1998** | |
| **Original total budget** | | **NT$167.69 billion (1676.9億)** |
| **5th revised plan approved by the Executive Yuan** | **10 June 2019** | **NT$161.19 billion (1611.9億)** |

**Note the budget went *down* between the original approval and the fifth
revision** — NT$167.69bn to NT$161.19bn. That's unusual for a Taiwanese
infrastructure project and worth investigating; most revise upward.

**A fifth revised plan approved in 2019, six years after the last section opened**,
also suggests financial reconciliation continued long after completion. Worth
chasing.

## Contract structure

Unusually well documented, and worth including because it shows how a
cross-municipal line is procured.

**Taipei City section** — civil works, water and environmental control, lifts and
escalators, other finishes: **4 section contracts (區段標)** —
**CK570A, CK570B, CK570G, CK570H**

**New Taipei section** — same scope: **5 section contracts** —
**CK570C, CK570D, CK570E, CK570F, CK570J**

**E&M systems and rolling stock were procured jointly** with the Luzhou line, the
Nangang line east extension, Xiaobitan station on the Xindian line, and TRTC's

## Zhonghe Depot 中和機廠 — the network's smallest

Easily missed, and genuinely interesting because of how constrained it is.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Location | **South of Heping Street, Zhonghe** |
| Site | **The former TRA Zhonghe station site** (原臺灣鐵路管理局中和車站基地) |
| Area | **≈1.47 hectares — including 南勢角 Nanshijiao station** |
| Facilities | Stabling yard, maintenance equipment building, main substation |
| Functions | **Stabling, general maintenance, cleaning only** |

**DORTS states the limited facilities are a direct consequence of the site's
size** — "因用地面積之限制". At 1.47 hectares including a passenger station, this
is a fraction of Beitou's or Neihu's footprint.

**A metro depot built on a closed railway station's land, so small it can only
stable and clean trains.** That's a complete, self-contained story — and it also
explains why the line needed Xinzhuang and Luzhou depots later.

**This depot was missing from the depots research file — add it.**

## Xinzhuang Depot and the Losheng Sanatorium

The most substantial untold story attached to this line.

### The depot

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Grade | **Level 3** |
| Location | Adjacent to **迴龍 Huilong** station, **straddling New Taipei and Taoyuan** |
| Original expropriation | **17.4 hectares** (of a ~30 ha site) |
| Final site area | **≈13.9 hectares** |
| Contract | **CK570F** (區段標) |
| Contractor | **工信工程** |
| Functions | Stabling, inspection, maintenance, washing; storage of station and main-line power and track maintenance equipment |

**DORTS confirms the depot's layout was altered for preservation.** Its own page
states that Xinzhuang Depot's facility configuration was **adjusted to accommodate
the Losheng Sanatorium preservation plan** (因配合樂生療養院保存案計畫，調整機廠
設施的配置). That's the operator acknowledging the campaign changed the
engineering — a **primary-source** confirmation, not a campaigner's claim.

### Losheng 樂生療養院

Established **1930** under Japanese rule as **Taiwan's first and only leprosy
(漢生病) isolation hospital**, at Huilong on the Xinzhuang/Guishan border.

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **1995** | The provincial health department sells the site to the metro authority; DORTS confirms the depot location. **17.4 ha expropriated**, covering the most historically valuable building cluster |
| **21 May 2002** | CK570F contract signed to 工信工程 |
| **20 June 2002** | Groundbreaking |
| **2004** | **青年樂生聯盟** Youth Losheng Alliance forms |
| **2005** | **樂生保留自救會** forms among residents (院民) |
| **16 March 2007** | One-month demolition notice issued; clashes with police |
| **18 March 2007** | **NT$200,000 raised in 24 hours** via the 黑米 platform to run a half-page *Apple Daily* advertisement comparing the 90% and 41.6% preservation plans |
| **11 April 2007** | Premier **蘇貞昌** apologises and pledges "90% preservation as the maximum goal" |
| **15 April 2007** | **5,000–6,000 people march** |
| **30 May 2007** | The Executive Yuan's Public Construction Commission announces the **"530 plan"** |
| **13 August 2008** | The **漢生病病患人權保障及補償條例** is promulgated |
| **3 December 2008** | Second forced clearance |
| **29 June 2013** | The 輔大 – 迴龍 section finally opens |

### The preservation figures conflict — publish both

The **Ministry of Culture's** potential-World-Heritage page records the 530 decision
as **「原地保留39棟建物，拆除6棟，9棟建物…重建」** — 39 buildings preserved in place,
6 demolished, 9 rebuilt.

**Campaigners counted only 28 actually preserved**, calling it the **41.6% plan**
against the promised **90%**.

Actors: 青年樂生聯盟, 樂生保留自救會, 新莊文史工作會, 台灣人權促進會, and professor
**李乾朗**.

### A date tension to resolve

One source gives the CK570F award as **21 May 2002**, another as **19 April 2004**.
**Resolve before publishing.**

### Why this belongs on the site

A metro depot built on a segregated medical institution, a multi-year preservation
campaign by former patients and students, a Premier's public apology, a
human-rights compensation act passed in the middle of it, a disputed preservation
count — **and the operator's own confirmation that the engineering was changed as a
result.**

**It is the second-best untold story this project has found**, after the Matra
dispute — and unlike Matra, the human stakes are immediate.

Handle it with care: these were people confined by the state for a disease, then
displaced again for infrastructure. The transport facts and the human facts both
belong, and neither should be used to decorate the other.

## A figure to be careful with

One enthusiast source gives the line as **19.7 km with 17 stations**. That
contradicts the 26-station figure and almost certainly refers to **the Xinlu portion
only** (Xinzhuang plus Luzhou, excluding Zhonghe). It also lists opening dates as
2010.11.03, 2012.01.05, 2012.09.30 and 2013.06.29 — which **match DORTS exactly**.

**Don't merge that length figure with the whole-line station count.** This is
precisely the "what does this figure measure" problem that produced the three
different Wenhu lengths.
