## Why this line matters

- **Entirely underground** — unlike Tamsui–Xinyi and Bannan, which mix structures
- Its alignment was **redesigned from part-elevated to fully underground because of
  public objection**, an early instance of the argument that later delayed Wenhu's
  Neihu extension by four years
- The **Xiaobitan branch exists because residents campaigned for it**, and climbs to
  an elevated station **inside a depot**
- Its opening in November 2014 **ended cross-colour through-running** across the
  whole network

## The all-underground decision

Part of the initial 70.3 km network, approved by the Executive Yuan **4 April 1986**
with a total network budget of **NT$444.4 billion (4444億)**.

**The original approval was part underground, part elevated.**

DORTS states the route was changed to **fully underground** for two reasons:
1. **Street width** along the alignment
2. **The environmental and visual impact of elevated structure**

These were weighed against urban development and engineering factors together. The
Executive Yuan approved the change on **20 May 1987**.

### Why this matters

Set this against Wenhu, which stayed elevated and drew exactly the criticism this
change was meant to avoid — and against the **Neihu extension, delayed four years**
partly over the same elevated-versus-underground argument, with residents
campaigning to change the system type entirely.

**The same debate, three times, with three different outcomes.** That's a
cross-cutting article: how Taipei decided, line by line, whether to build in the air
or underground, and what it cost each time.

*Source: DORTS — primary.*

## The Xiaobitan branch 小碧潭支線

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Length | **≈1.5 km** |
| Station | **G03A 小碧潭** |
| Junction | G03 七張 Qizhang |
| Rolling stock | **Three-car C371 sets** (397, 398, 399) |
| Approved | **17 September 1999** by the Executive Yuan |

### It exists because of public pressure

During **1998**, residents and elected representatives **campaigned strongly for a
station to be added at Xindian Depot**. The branch was approved the following year.

### The engineering is genuinely unusual

The branch **rises from underground to grade, then climbs to an elevated passenger
station inside Xindian Depot itself.**

There is no other arrangement like it on the network. **This wants a section drawing**
— elevation against distance, showing the line emerging from tunnel, running at
grade, and climbing into the depot.

### The three-car operation

Xiaobitan and **Xinbeitou (R22A)** are the only two three-car operations on the Taipei
Metro, both served by the same three C371 sets. **They should be covered together**,
since the shared fleet is the connection.

Note the **'A' suffix convention** — G03A and R22A both mark branch stations
inserted into an existing sequence. Worth explaining.

## The end of cross-colour running

**On 14 November 2014**, the night before the Songshan section opened, the last
**Tamsui ↔ Xindian** trains left each terminus at midnight.

Until then Taipei ran trains across what are now two differently coloured lines — an
arrangement that made sense when the network was described by construction-project
names rather than colours. **Line names and numbers were only formalised on 11
October 2014**, a month earlier.

**The Songshan Line's opening is therefore the event that made the modern colour-coded
network real.** That's a strong framing and nothing covers it in English.

Service patterns before and after:

| Date | Pattern |
|---|---|
| 24 Dec 1998 | Tamsui – **Nanshijiao** |
| 11 Nov 1999 | Tamsui – **Xindian**, and Beitou – Nanshijiao |
| 30 Sept 2012 | Nanshijiao pattern dropped; Beitou – Taipower Building added |
| 24 Nov 2013 | Beitou – Xiangshan interleaved with Tamsui – Xindian |
| **15 Nov 2014** | Songshan opens; **single-colour running only** |

## The NTU Hospital anomaly

**NTU Hospital station (R09) belongs operationally to the Tamsui–Xinyi line**, despite
sitting on the Xindian line's construction alignment.

That kind of mismatch between construction project and operating line recurs across
this network and **deserves explaining somewhere** — probably in a
`/rail/systems` article rather than on individual line pages.

The mechanism is likely the **小南門線 Xiaonanmen line** heritage, but this is **TBC**.
