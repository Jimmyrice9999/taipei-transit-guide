## What is established

- **TRA's own internal regulation formally manages five steam locomotives as heritage assets, classified into three rental/display tiers: 特甲級 ("special-A": CT273, CK124), 甲級 ("A": DT668, CK101), and 乙級 ("B", 762 mm narrow gauge: LDK59), each with a stated dynamic-running rental rate.** Source: Taiwan Railways Administration, `臺灣鐵路管理局蒸汽機車管理及租借要點`, `https://www.railway.gov.tw/tra-tip-web/tip/file/6dc22469-5878-45f8-b4f4-d3ee6e751e35`, PRIMARY (TRA's own internal administrative regulation, dated, revision-stamped: originally 2001, last amended 2021), fetched and read in full, not a snippet. High confidence. Original-language basis: `特甲級蒸汽機車(CT273、CK124)為507,856元整`; `甲級蒸汽機車(DT668、CK101)為303,106元整`; `乙級蒸汽機車(LDK59)為172,402元整`.
- **The same regulation bars CT273 and DT668 from running on the Pingxi, Shen'ao and Jiji lines, and restricts CK101 to static or motionless display on major festival/commemorative days only, because its original manufacturer has ceased production and parts/wheels can no longer be sourced.** Same source, PRIMARY, High confidence. Original-language basis: `CT273及DT668禁止行駛於平溪線、深澳線及集集線全區間`; `蒸汽機車CK101因車齡老舊原製造廠已停產，致各部零件及車輪無法取得，限重大節慶日或特殊紀念日方能靜態或動態展示。`
- **CT273 ("Steam Queen"), a CT270-class locomotive built 1943 by Kawasaki (based on the Japan Railways C57 design), was withdrawn from regular service in 1984 — TRA's last steam locomotive withdrawn — and restored to operation in 2014.** Source: zh.wikipedia, SECONDARY, lead index only; corroborated for current operational role by CNA, `蒸機女王CT273限定復活3趟次 行駛玉里至台東`, `https://www.cna.com.tw/news/ahel/202506020030.aspx`, SECONDARY, read via search synthesis rather than a full page fetch — Medium confidence, flagged as needing a full re-fetch to raise confidence. That source reports CT273 hauling a 2025 summer tourist train (`仲夏寶島號`) between Yuli and Taitung, based at Hualien.
- **DT668 ("Steam King"), a DT650-class locomotive (based on the Japanese National Railways D51 type) built 1941 by Kawasaki, TRA's heaviest and highest-tractive-effort steam class, was withdrawn March 1984 and restored to dynamic operation on the Neiwan Branch Line in November 2011.** Source: zh.wikipedia, SECONDARY, lead index only; corroborated as a currently TRA-managed asset by the primary rental regulation above.
- **CK124 ("Steam Prince"), a CK120-class locomotive (a Japanese C12-type equivalent), was restored to dynamic operation in 2001 and remains in the same top rental tier as CT273 under TRA's regulation.** Source: zh.wikipedia, SECONDARY, lead index only; corroborated as a currently TRA-managed asset by the primary rental regulation.
- **LDK59, a narrow-gauge (762 mm) LDK50-class locomotive, was restored to dynamic operation in 2011 and is confirmed as a TRA-managed heritage asset by the primary rental regulation's 乙級 tier.** Source for the restoration year and base location (Hualien Depot): zh.wikipedia's TRA steam-locomotive list article, SECONDARY, lead only, not independently verified against a primary page for this specific claim.
- **The National Railway Museum's Phase 1 opened 31 July 2025, comprising six restored heritage buildings and eight exhibitions, plus a weekend heritage-train ride using restored DR2203/DR2303 diesel railcars ("blue-skin train") over roughly 650 m, and displays 24 preserved heritage vehicles.** Source: National Railway Museum Preparatory Office (國家鐵道博物館籌備處), `國家鐵道博物館7月31日第一階段正式開放「鐵博．前行」打造文化新地標`, `https://www.nrm.gov.tw/News_Content.aspx?n=3307&s=241584`, PRIMARY, High confidence — read via full fetch of two separate nrm.gov.tw pages stating the same date and detail, and corroborated independently by five separate secondary outlets. The six buildings: 柴電工場 (Diesel-Electric Workshop), 總辦公室 (General Administration Building), 技工養成所 (Technical Training Institute), 材料試驗室 (Materials Testing Lab), 大禮堂 (Grand Hall), 澡堂 (Bathhouse). The itemised list of the 24 displayed vehicles was not found in the fetched page.
- **The museum is confirmed open and operating in 2026, on the same Phase 1 footprint, Tuesday–Sunday 09:30–17:00 (last admission 16:30), with stated 2026 special-holiday opening dates and admission fees.** Source: National Railway Museum, `https://www.nrm.gov.tw/cp.aspx?n=3319`, PRIMARY, High confidence, fetched in full. No source read states a Phase 2 opening date or scope.
- **Taipei Railway Workshop (臺北機廠), the museum's site, was designated a national historic monument (國定古蹟) in 2015.** Corroborated by zh.wikipedia (SECONDARY) and by the URL structure of a Bureau of Cultural Heritage case page (which embeds the date `20150416`), though the case page itself could not be fetched for content — see Checked and failed. The exact date is a conflict (see below).
- **A December 2016 Executive Yuan approval of the "Taipei Railway Workshop revitalisation into the National Railway Museum precinct" plan, and an August 2019 establishment of the Preparatory Office, are reported by secondary sources.** Source: zh.wikipedia, SECONDARY, lead only for the Executive Yuan approval (its own cited footnote was not independently followed to a primary document); the August 2019 Preparatory Office date is corroborated across zh.wikipedia and an ETtoday news headline dated 2019-08-16, Medium confidence — not independently fetched as a full primary government announcement.
- **Three CT270-class locomotives — CT271, CT278, CT284 — are reported by secondary sources as statically preserved outside TRA custody**, at Keelung's Lovers Lake Park (CT271, restoration begun 2015), Ershui's water park (CT278, reportedly placed there through the advocacy of former Vice President 謝東閔), and Yilan's Zhongxing Cultural Creative Park (CT284) respectively. None of the three appears in TRA's own steam-locomotive rental regulation, consistent with — but not itself proof of — their having passed out of TRA's direct management; this inference is not confirmed by any primary source read. All three locations are SECONDARY, unverified against a primary or county-government source in this research; a direct fetch of the Yilan county park's own page failed (see Checked and failed).

## Conflicts

- **CK101's build year:** 1916 versus 1917, both appearing in zh.wikipedia-derived material without a clean resolution in this research.
- **CK101's withdrawal year:** 1978, per a full fetch of zh.wikipedia's CK101 article, versus 1974, per a separate search-summary account. Possibly a formal-deregistration-versus-operational-withdrawal distinction, but no source read states that explicitly.
- **CK101's current physical location:** Changhua Roundhouse, per an older framing, versus Fugang (Taoyuan) Depot, per zh.wikipedia's TRA steam-locomotive list article (fetched directly) and a more recent search-summary describing ongoing restoration there. Both are published; the difference between a long-term display base and a current maintenance location was not established with certainty from any single source.
- **DT668's current physical location:** Changhua Roundhouse, grouped with other locomotives in one search summary, versus Fugang Depot, per the fetched zh.wikipedia list article and a 2020-dated CNA article headline describing DT668 arriving at Fugang (`台鐵DT668蒸汽火車駛進桃園富岡`) — that CNA piece describes a 2020 movement, not necessarily DT668's current 2026 location. Not resolved.
- **Taipei Railway Workshop's monument-designation date:** 15 March 2015 (a committee-approval date per zh.wikipedia, citing a document not independently read) versus 16 April 2015 (embedded in the Bureau of Cultural Heritage's own case URL, which could not be fetched for content). These may represent a committee vote versus a formal public gazettal, but that distinction was not confirmed from any source actually read.

## Stated gaps

No individual locomotive (CK101, CT273, DT668, CK124, LDK59) was confirmed
to hold its own cultural-asset (古物) designation in the national heritage
database — only the Taipei Railway Workshop site itself is confirmed to hold
monument status. This is a "not found," not a "does not exist": the
database's search function is JavaScript-rendered and could not be executed
by the fetch tooling used. An itemised list of the museum's 24 displayed
heritage vehicles was not found. CT284's ownership/transfer status (TRA-owned
on loan versus fully transferred to Yilan County) is unconfirmed. Whether a
National Railway Museum Phase 2 has been announced is unresolved — nothing
found in this search answers it. No source dated later than mid-2025 was
found describing which heritage locomotives are currently operable as of
2026.
