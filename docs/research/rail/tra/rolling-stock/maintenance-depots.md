# Taiwan Railways depot and maintenance-facility network — research findings, 27 August 2026

> Corpus warning: files under `docs/research/` are leads and working notes, not sources. Every claim below records whether the underlying page was fetched and read in full, or only found via a search summary; the latter is not published as an established fact on the content page. No prior `docs/research/` file on this subject existed to check against.

This is a one-subject report on TRA's physical maintenance-facility network —
the sites themselves, their legal structure and capacity, not the
group-level EMU depot assignments already covered by
`content/rail/tra/rolling-stock/fleet-roster.md`, which this page does not
duplicate.

## The key finding: two legally distinct tiers, not one list of depots

The commonly assumed list of TRA "depots" (Qidu, Taipei, Hsinchu, Changhua,
Chiayi, Chaozhou, treated as parallel sites) does not match what TRA's own
primary sources establish. There are two distinct tiers with different
legal bases:

- **機廠 (Workshops)** — heavy-overhaul facilities performing Level 3/4
  maintenance, each established under its own separate bylaw per Article 16
  of the TRA Organization Act. TRA's own approved maintenance plan names
  exactly three currently: **富岡機廠 (Fugang)**, **潮州機廠 (Chaozhou)**,
  **花蓮機廠 (Hualien)**.
- **機務段 (operating/locomotive sections) and 檢車段 (inspection sections)**
  — a lighter tier handling stabling, dispatch and running maintenance. The
  general branch-organization law names seven 機務段 (七堵、臺北、新竹、彰化、
  嘉義、高雄、花蓮, plus three 機務分段: 樹林、宜蘭、臺東) and four 檢車段
  (七堵、臺北、彰化、高雄, plus one 高雄港檢車分段).

The existing fleet-roster.md page's "operating groups" (Qidu/Taipei/Hsinchu
for EMU900; Hsinchu/Changhua/Chiayi for EMU800) are 機務段-level stabling
assignments, not heavy-overhaul depot assignments — Level 3/4 overhaul for
those same fleets is reserved to the three workshops, per the maintenance
plan.

## What is established

- **TRA's rolling-stock maintenance is governed by 「鐵路機車車輛檢修規則」, a MOTC regulation defining a four-level inspection system (一至四級檢修) — a different framework from the five-level facility-grading scale this project has already documented for Taipei Metro depots, which is a depot-capability grade rather than a per-vehicle inspection-depth level.** Source: Laws & Regulations Database, `鐵路機車車輛檢修規則`, Article 8, `https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0030003&flno=8`, PRIMARY, High confidence — article text fetched and quoted verbatim. Original-language basis (locomotive definitions): `一、一級檢修：以視覺、聽覺、觸覺、嗅覺，就有關行車主要機件之狀態及作用施行檢修。...四、四級檢修：對一般機件施行全盤檢修，各重要機件施行重整之檢修。`
- **The Organization Act authorises TRA to establish workshops (機廠) separately from its general branch structure, each governed by its own bylaw.** Source: `交通部臺灣鐵路管理局組織條例`, Article 16, `https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=K0000106`, PRIMARY, High confidence. Original-language basis: `得設餐旅服務總所、貨運服務總所，並得分設各機廠，其組織另以法律定之。`
- **The general branch-organization law names seven 機務段 and four 檢車段, with duty wording limited to inspection and upkeep (檢車保養) rather than the workshops' comprehensive overhaul.** Source: `交通部臺灣鐵路管理局所屬分支機構組織通則`, Article 5, `https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=K0000107`, PRIMARY, High confidence — article fetched and quoted. Original-language basis: `七堵、臺北、新竹、彰化、嘉義、高雄、花蓮機務段，樹林、宜蘭、臺東機務分段...動力車及客貨車之檢車保養及動力車之駕駛、檢查、保養`.
- **TRA's approved 2024–2027 (113–116) rolling-stock maintenance plan names Fugang, Chaozhou and Hualien as the current three workshops, with stated Level 3/4 core-fleet maintenance reserved to them; it gives each workshop's planned annual maintenance volume by year.** Source: National Taiwan Railways Corporation, `臺鐵公司營業所需車輛維修第1期計畫（113年至116年）（核定版）`, `https://www.railway.gov.tw/tra-tip-web/tip/file/72d6c901-8166-4a32-ab59-7602a45072a9`, PRIMARY, High confidence — full 65-page PDF read directly. Original-language basis: `臺鐵公司持續辦理各式營業車輛及維修工程車之三、四級檢修，營業車輛除核心業務因專業考量需由臺鐵公司所屬三機廠進行檢修外...`. Planned annual volumes (113/114/115/116, vehicles): Fugang 438/413/445/446; Chaozhou 286/514/504/504; Hualien 86/89/109/109.
- **The same plan explains Chaozhou's expanded role: EMU900 (520 cars, 52 groups, fully delivered by end-2023) and EMU3000 (600 cars, 50 groups, delivered to the 42nd group by end-2023) were planned for periodic Level 3/4 maintenance at Chaozhou from 2024, because Fugang and Hualien were already at full maintenance capacity.** Same source, PRIMARY, High confidence. Original-language basis: `新購長編組之電聯車於109年起陸續交車，將於113年起進廠辦理3~4級車輛檢修，惟經評估維修基地量能，富岡及花蓮機廠檢修容量已滿載，亟需新廠辦理新購車輛之檢修作業`.
- **The plan dates Chaozhou base Phase II's construction status as of end-2023: pre-construction piling and equipment contracts awarded and partly started; main-works and equipment contracts had basic design approved 5 December 2023 with detailed design in progress; the project period runs to September 2025.** Same source, PRIMARY, High confidence. Original-language basis: `本計畫期程至114年9月...CL111標(基樁)先期工程：112年12月12日決標...CL131標(設備)先期工程：112年8月28日決標、9月28日開工。CL121標主體工程及CL132標檢修設備工程：112年12月5日基本設計核定，目前細部設計中。`
- **Phase II's first-stage building was planned to house 12 underground lifting jacks and equipment sufficient for Level 3 EMU900/EMU3000 work by 2024–2026, with full Level 4 capability requiring Phase II's full completion after 2027; a separate table footnote states Phase II adds 48 car-spaces of capacity in 2026.** Same source, PRIMARY, High confidence.
- **A separate TRA operator policy page gives Chaozhou base Phase II's total investment as NT$12.148 billion, approved 27 September 2021, project period September 2021–September 2030 (under revision), covering a comprehensive workshop, an EMU-dedicated maintenance facility, a preparation workshop, materials storage and a test-line inspection shed.** Source: National Taiwan Railways Corporation, `臺鐵公司高雄機廠潮州基地二期工程建設計畫`, `https://www.railway.gov.tw/tra-tip-web/adr/about-policy?selectIndex=2`, PRIMARY, High confidence — full page fetched, last updated 12 February 2026 per the page itself.
- **A government body's own news release (Executive Yuan South Taiwan Service Center) confirms a commissioning ceremony for the Chaozhou base, attended by the then-Premier, who is quoted remarking that the old name "Kaohsiung Workshop" no longer matched reality after the physical move.** Source: Executive Yuan South Taiwan Service Center (行政院南部聯合服務中心), `台鐵高雄機廠遷建潮州基地 蘇揆見證啟用`, `https://eysc.ey.gov.tw/Page/DAB11F5DBDC11168/95c3621b-cb4d-4d7a-96ca-80affd5e505d`, PRIMARY (a government body's own release), High confidence for the fact of the ceremony and quote; the exact date is a conflict — see Conflicts. Original-language basis: `遷廠後還稱高雄機廠，名實不符，應該正名為潮州機廠或屏東機廠`.
- **Hualien Workshop's own operating bylaw divides its work by rolling-stock subsystem across three internal workshops: engines/transmission/braking for diesel railcars and locomotives (第一工場); bogies, brakes, couplers and bodywork (第二工場); and electrical/HVAC/interior systems (第三工場).** Source: `交通部臺灣鐵路管理局花蓮機廠辦事細則`, `https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=K0010073`, PRIMARY, High confidence — fetched in full. Original-language basis (第一工場, Article 6): `主辦柴聯車、柴油客車、DHL機車、PBK電源行李車之引擎、液體變速機、冷卻循環系統、軔機裝置`.
- **Fugang and Chaozhou's own bylaws in the national law database still carry their pre-rename names (Taipei Workshop, Kaohsiung Workshop) and were each last amended in 2017 — before the 2022 renaming and, for Fugang, even before reflecting its 2013 physical relocation in the bylaw's title.** Sources: `交通部臺灣鐵路管理局臺北機廠辦事細則` (K0010071, last amended 28 July 2017) and `交通部臺灣鐵路管理局高雄機廠辦事細則` (K0010072, last amended 30 June 2017), both `law.moj.gov.tw`, PRIMARY, High confidence for the amendment dates and internal organisation. The Taipei/Fugang bylaw organises 13 vehicle-type-specific internal workshops (diesel-electric locomotives, EMUs, passenger cars, etc.); the Kaohsiung/Chaozhou bylaw organises four workshops by subsystem rather than vehicle type. **Neither bylaw should be read as a current description of EMU900/EMU3000 maintenance scope**, since both predate the fleets' arrival at these sites — that current scope is established instead by the 2024 maintenance plan above.
- **Secondary reporting, not independently confirmed by a full primary-page fetch, traces Fugang's origin to a 1935 Japanese-era Taipei railway works (later a protected monument site), a post-war rename to Taipei Workshop, relocation to Yangmei, Taoyuan in 2013, and a rename to Fugang Workshop on 28 June 2022; it currently maintains roughly 1,400 vehicles across heritage steam locomotives, diesel-electric and electric locomotives, and EMUs.** Source: Central News Agency (中央社), `台鐵北部車輛維修重鎮 富岡機廠正名揭牌`, `https://www.cna.com.tw/news/ahel/202206280371.aspx`, SECONDARY, Medium confidence — full article fetched, but a TRA primary press release on the same renaming ceremony could not be fetched (see Checked and failed).

## Conflicts

- **Chaozhou base commissioning/ceremony date.** A search-summary-derived account gives 18 June 2022, with a separate 30 June 2022 rename date. The Executive Yuan's own news page, fetched in full, appears to date the same ceremony to 18 June 2023, but the fetch tool's date rendering could carry a ROC-to-Gregorian conversion error and was not independently re-verified against the raw page text or against a CNA article on the same visit. Both dates are recorded here rather than one being picked; this needs a direct re-check before either is treated as settled.
- **Fugang and Chaozhou site areas.** Both are reported, via independent search summaries, as approximately 52 hectares — an identical figure for two different facilities from two separately-summarized sources, which may be a genuine coincidence or a search-tool conflation. Neither figure was confirmed by reading a full primary page stating a hectare figure, so neither is published as an established fact on the content page.

## Stated gaps

Chaozhou Phase I's exact budget, dates and site area rest only on
unconfirmed search summaries, not a fetched primary page. The Chaozhou
commissioning date is an unresolved conflict (see above). What distinguishes
the "A" and "B" sub-grades used within the maintenance plan's own Level 3
and Level 4 schedules (e.g., 三級檢修B, 四級檢修A, 四級檢修B) is not defined
in any source read for this page. A precise, explicitly stated boundary
between what 機務段/檢車段 handle versus what the three 機廠 handle was not
found as a single sentence in any primary source — the two-tier structure
above is an inference from three separate primary sources (the Organization
Act's structural separation, the maintenance plan's reservation of Level 3/4
core-fleet work to the three workshops, and the branch-organisation law's
narrower duty wording for 機務段/檢車段), not a directly quoted claim.
