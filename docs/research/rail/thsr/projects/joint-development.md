## What is established

- **The core mechanism (Medium-High confidence, with a significant caveat)**: a named mechanism covers only 5 of the 12 stations — Taoyuan, Hsinchu, Taichung, Chiayi, Tainan — not all 12. Source: MOTC 交通年鑑 (Transportation Yearbook), 第三篇 鐵路 第三章 第四節 車站特定區開發, `https://www.motc.gov.tw/ch/app/yearbook/doc?id=21&year=110&...`, PRIMARY in nature but **not independently rendered** — WebFetch could not parse the PDF (flate-encoded, no pdftotext access in this environment) on either the 100年 or 110年 edition. What's used instead is a WebSearch synthesis reproducing what reads as verbatim yearbook text, identically across both editions — that cross-edition consistency is the basis for Medium-High rather than discarding it, but it is **not independently confirmed by a direct read**. Quoted (as returned by search, purportedly verbatim): "高速鐵路桃園、新竹、台中、嘉義及台南等五個車站地區開發，係由原臺灣省政府、交通部高速鐵路工程局及各站所轄縣政府共同合作，以區段徵收方式辦理開發，88年精省後，相關作業由內政部接續辦理。" Area: 1,507 hectares planned, 1,386 hectares expropriated, NT$60.534 billion invested. Zone expropriation announced from 1 March 1999; original landowners' replacement land returned by October 2004; full completion 23 May 2012; financial closeout end of December 2019, with surplus distribution starting 2020.
- **Miaoli, Changhua and Yunlin have their own special districts, administered separately from the 5-station MOI mechanism above** — they opened later (December 2015) and their districts are referenced in county-government planning documents (苗栗縣政府, 雲林縣政府, 彰化縣政府) rather than the named 5-station mechanism. No primary source was found explicitly stating who legally designates these three — an inference from the pattern visible in Zuoying and Pingtung (below), not a confirmed fact for these three specifically.
- **The Ministry of Interior's Urban Planning Committee (內政部都市計畫委員會) approves district-scale plan changes** — directly confirmed for Zuoying (below), a working example of the general pattern.
- **Changhua — the most-documented "underdeveloped" case.** Changhua is last of all 12 stations by ridership, the only one never to pass 1 million annual boardings (2024: 89.3万 in, 89.9万 out, per a WebSearch synthesis of UDN reporting, Medium confidence, not independently full-fetched). Directly confirmed: since 2014, the county has offered ~66.34 hectares of buildable land for sale, with ~36.76 hectares still unsold as of the article's date — "土地開發成效不如預期" (development has fallen short of expectations). Source: 自由時報, `https://news.ltn.com.tw/news/life/paper/1766076`, SECONDARY, Medium-High confidence, fetched in full. A comprehensive review has since rezoned part of the district from a transfer-hub-dedicated zone to a Type-2 industrial-service zone with a raised floor-area ratio, and the county is courting elder-care industry with instalment land payments.
- **Miaoli**: 400-hectare special district implemented 2007 (before the station itself opened, December 2015); two county-owned industrial parcels (~2.2ha) went to bid 42 times with zero successful bids, asking price near NT$5 billion; 38% of the district is agricultural land (per a WebSearch synthesis of UDN reporting, Medium confidence, not independently full-fetched). In October 2025, county officials pitched the site to Nvidia, describing "dual-rail convergence, one hour to both Taipei and Kaohsiung" and proximity to Hsinchu Science Park, Central Taiwan Science Park and Taichung Industrial Park. Source: 中央社, `https://www.cna.com.tw/news/afe/202510200331.aspx`, SECONDARY, High confidence for what CNA reported the county said, fetched in full.
- **Yunlin**: 422-hectare special district (zh.wikipedia, SECONDARY, footnote not independently chased — a gap); the district sits on a former Japanese Imperial Navy airfield site and is envisioned as a new development core alongside the nearby Central Taiwan Science Park's Huwei site. A real-estate-marketing-site synthesis (not independently fetched, Low-Medium confidence) describes large vacant tracts near the station with no nearby schools or shops, and an unrealised "science-education living city" vision dating to 2002.
- **Taoyuan (Qingpu) and Hsinchu (Zhubei) as the successful comparators**: at THSR's 20th-anniversary event, the chairman characterised the youth influx around these two stations as an "HSR baby boom" ("高鐵嬰兒潮"), part of a shift from "移動" (movement) to "移居" (relocation) to "宜居" (liveability). Qingpu: ~70% of local population under 45, 23% under 20, new commercial floor space 2017–2025 approximately 80% of the area's current total office stock, 2025 ridership ~10.4x opening-period level. Zhubei: population under 45 grew 43.8% — "the only one of the 12 station areas showing positive growth in this age band" — population under 20 at 26%, total output value NT$462.5 billion (~4x pre-THSR level), 2025 ridership ~8.2x opening level. Source: 中央社, `https://www.cna.com.tw/news/ahel/202608190128.aspx`, SECONDARY, High confidence for the quotes, Medium for statistics not given verbatim, fetched directly.
- **A statistical trap worth flagging explicitly**: the same event reported Miaoli, Yunlin and Changhua ridership growing by the highest percentage multiples of all 12 stations (Miaoli 14x, Yunlin 12.8x, Changhua 10.6x) — while Changhua is separately, and correctly, reported as dead last in absolute ridership among all 12. Both facts are true simultaneously; high percentage growth from an extremely low opening-period base is not evidence against underdevelopment. Both figures published, not allowed to imply a resolved contradiction.
- **Chiayi (Taibao) — an in-between case**: Taibao City, where the Chiayi HSR station sits, is the only one of Chiayi County's 18 townships/cities with 13 consecutive years of positive population growth, reaching 39,369, against the county's overall population falling below 480,000 (自由時報, via search synthesis, Medium confidence, not independently full-fetched). Cited drivers: the National Palace Museum Southern Branch, a hotel, Chang Gung Hospital, county-government offices, and newer catalysts (Chiayi Science Park, a TSMC CoWoS packaging plant expected operational 2028).
- **Literal building-level joint development exists, but as adjacent-site superficies-right (地上權) tenders on government-held land, not the exact building-integrated MRT pattern.** Taichung: the largest THSR station-area tender in Taiwan, ~48,085 ping (≈159,000 m²) directly facing the station, 70-year superficies right, no royalty for the first 4 years, awarded August 2021 to a joint venture (廣三SOGO/桂宇). Source: 中央社, `https://www.cna.com.tw/news/ahel/202108040181.aspx`, SECONDARY, Medium-High confidence (the Railway Bureau's own tender-announcement page could not be fetched — empty content), corroborated independently by 自由時報, `https://estate.ltn.com.tw/article/10591`, on the site area, term and royalty holiday — two independently-fetched sources agree on these figures. Planned uses: department store, international conference centre, multi-function exhibition space, hotel, cinema, restaurants, leveraging the "three-rail convergence" of THSR/TRA/Taichung Metro Green Line.
- **Chiayi's equivalent tender — TBC, not independently verified.** Reported (via WebSearch synthesis only, not a fetched page) as jointly held by MOI, Chiayi County Government and the Railway Bureau, ~30,000 ping, 50-year superficies right, won by a Gloria Outlets subsidiary, NT$4.8 billion planned investment, phase 1 targeted 2028. This entire item rests on unfetched search synthesis and does not meet this project's sourcing bar as published fact — flagged explicitly as requiring a direct fetch (best candidate: `https://www.cyhg.gov.tw/News_Content.aspx?n=16&s=237507`) before use.
- **Zuoying — a directly confirmed, primary-sourced interchange-hub development.** Source: 高雄市政府財政局 (Kaohsiung City Government Finance Bureau), `https://finance.kcg.gov.tw/kpc/News_Content.aspx?n=7637A6E50461213F&sms=A79D3501CFE28448&s=1942C78C2F6DE4F6`, PRIMARY, High confidence, fetched directly. Zuoying is Taiwan's only THSR/TRA/Kaohsiung Metro three-rail interchange. Project "左營高鐵科技之心" approved by the Ministry of Interior's Urban Planning Committee at its 1,033rd meeting; site ~2.4 hectares, entirely publicly-owned, west of the station, rezoned from transfer-hub/parking to commercial/park/parking, planned floor area over 70,000 ping (~231,000 m²) for mall, office, business, exhibition, transfer and parking space. Explicitly invokes TOD and cites TSMC's move into nearby Nanzi as a demand driver. A separate 2026-dated real-estate report (not independently verified against this primary, an open gap) describes a subsequent public-led urban-renewal contract for the same site with a much larger ("hundreds of billions NT$") reported budget.
- **Nangang — the "East District Gateway" framing, not explicitly "sub-centre."** Source: 臺北市政府都市發展局 (Taipei City Urban Development Bureau), `https://udd.gov.taipei/events/djdawka-17607`, PRIMARY, High confidence, fetched directly, on the Nangang Skywalk project: "臺北市政府配合南港地區都市計畫通盤檢討及『東區門戶』重大建設，指定東西向立體連通系統之設置，以南港車站為核心". Nangang is a three-rail interchange (THSR/TRA/MRT); combined monthly ridership across the three systems reached 1.896 million/month as of 2017 data cited on the page. The skywalk itself grew from 1.3km to 3.56km, in four functional segments. No primary source using the specific term "副都心" (sub-centre) for Nangang in connection with THSR was found — the confirmed framing is "東區門戶" (East District Gateway), a related but not identical characterisation.
- **Banqiao — predates THSR, not originated by it.** Source: zh.wikipedia, 新板橋車站特定專用區, SECONDARY, footnotes not independently chased (a gap). The special zone originated from Taipei County's own late-1980s/1990s planning under County Magistrate 尤清, driven by the old Banqiao station's inability to handle growing ridership on limited land — before THSR's route was finalised. Area grew from an initial 19 hectares to approximately 54 hectares, described as intended to be a "都會政經中心" (metropolitan political-economic centre) — not the term "副都心" either. THSR is one of four rail systems converging at Banqiao (TRA, THSR, two MRT lines), a later addition to an existing plan rather than the plan's origin.

## Conflicts

- **How many of the 12 stations have a dedicated station-area district, and under what mechanism**: not a single clean number. Five stations (Taoyuan, Hsinchu, Taichung, Chiayi, Tainan) sit under one named MOI-led mechanism (Medium-High confidence, unverified by direct PDF read). Three more (Miaoli, Changhua, Yunlin) have districts under an apparently separate, unconfirmed governance path. Zuoying and a comparator case at Pingtung (not covered in depth here) use a different mechanism again (urban renewal / new-district). Taipei, Banqiao and Nangang have no THSR-originated greenfield district at all — Banqiao's predates THSR. A total of roughly 8 stations with some form of dedicated district is a reasonable characterisation, but the two/three distinct governance mechanisms should not be flattened into one number or one process. Not resolved to a single figure; the content page states this explicitly rather than picking a number.
- **Tainan/Zuoying ridership-growth multiples**: one WebFetch pass (via a cardu.com.tw mirror of a CNA wire story) rendered a confusing figure that appeared to conflate Tainan and Zuoying into one; a second pass (a Yahoo mirror of the same story) cleanly separated them as Tainan 4.3x, Zuoying 3.3x. The CNA original itself, as fetched, gave only the Zuoying 3.3x figure clearly. The Tainan 4.3x figure is not independently confirmed against the primary wire source and is not published as settled.
- **Zuoying's development cost**: a 2023 city-government figure (>70,000 ping floor area, no cost stated) versus a 2026 real-estate report citing "hundreds of billions NT$" for what may be the same or a redefined project — not reconciled.
