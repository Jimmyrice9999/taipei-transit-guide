## What is established

- **TRTC/DORTS states Taipei Metro was the first mass-transit system in Taiwan to adopt accessibility-facility concepts, since evolved into "universal design."** Source: DORTS, 無障礙設施設計, `https://www.dorts.gov.taipei/cp.aspx?n=1858C5F7A376F6A7`, PRIMARY, High confidence, fetched in full (last-updated 2025-10-15). Quoted: 「臺北捷運系統為國內首先導入「無障礙設施」觀念之大眾運輸」; 「從無障礙設施觀念進一步深化為無關使用者之年齡大小、有無身心障礙，並超越國籍、能力、性別之差異性而提供為任何人均可使用」. **No specific legal/regulatory citation appears anywhere on this page** — checked directly, the citation is absent, not overlooked.
- **A worked single-route design per station is described**: e.g. for medium-capacity (Wenhu) stations, 「地面層電梯→非付費區→無障礙驗票閘門→付費區電梯→月台（第3節車廂第1個車門）」. Source: DORTS, `https://www.dorts.gov.taipei/ct.asp?xItem=77972568&CtNode=15090`, PRIMARY, High confidence. No numeric completion year or percentage is stated on either DORTS page — checked directly, not found.
- **TRTC's four accessibility sub-pages, all PRIMARY, High confidence, fetched in full**: in-station facilities state every station has a lowered accessible ticket machine, an accessible fare gate, elevators with braille panels/voice systems/side handrails, anti-slip stair-edge strips, and accessible restroom fittings. A specific figure: **"視障優先椅" (priority seating for visually-impaired passengers) exists at 29 stations**, chosen for high visually-impaired ridership or longer branch-line headways. Train-car facilities: all priority seats network-wide are painted dark blue for visibility; wheelchair spaces are at the first and last cars on the four heavy lines (by the driver's end) but in every car on the Wenhu Line, where riders may board from any car (end cars recommended as less crowded). Outside-station facilities: accessible ramps wherever there's a level change, accessible parking near entrances at stations with park-and-ride, and tactile paving connecting to sidewalk ramps — with one claim (stations opened after 2008 discontinued part of this practice) sourced only to a WebFetch summary, not independently re-quoted from raw HTML — flagged as needing re-verification.
- **TRTC platforms are not fully level-boarding.** A documented minimum 75mm platform-train gap on straight sections, larger on curves (rigid train bodies cannot conform to curved platform edges), with TRTC stating it could not add further rubber edge strips. Source: news article reproduced at freeguider.com, attributed to Yahoo News, dated 2020-05-08, SECONDARY, Medium confidence (a secondary reproduction of a secondary source, original Yahoo article not independently located). Documents a specific 10 February 2020 incident at Dapinglin Station where a powered wheelchair's front wheel got stuck in the gap. This explains, and is consistent with, the staff-assistance boarding procedure (wheelchair users directed to the first-car first-door position).
- **Guide dogs are exempted from general animal restrictions**, per TRTC campaign material ("三不一要": don't interfere, don't feed, don't refuse) and corroborated by a dedicated 2025-04-12 TRTC news release on a guide-dog-friendly train event with Mayor Chiang Wan-an. Both PRIMARY, Medium-High confidence (read via WebFetch summarisation, not fully re-quoted verbatim). TRTC's formal legal passenger-regulations PDF, which almost certainly states the formal exemption clause, could not be text-extracted (see Checked and failed) — NTMC's equivalent formal clause was successfully read instead (below) and likely mirrors TRTC's, though this is not confirmed identical.
- **A Control Yuan corrective measure against TRTC, 20 February 2023 (case 112交正0002), over mishandling a fine against an autistic passenger.** Source: 監察院糾正案文, `https://www.cy.gov.tw/CyBsBoxContent.aspx?n=134&s=28212`, PRIMARY, High confidence, fetched in full. TRTC fined the passenger without reviewing CCTV footage, then made public statements the Control Yuan found factually wrong and harmful to public perception of disabled people. Corroborated by 自由時報, `https://news.ltn.com.tw/news/Taipei/breakingnews/4212207`, SECONDARY, High confidence, fetched in full — the incident occurred September 2022 on the Tamsui-Xinyi Line, TRTC reversed the fine 2 December 2022 after reviewing footage, and committed to staff training. This is a disability-discrimination/procedural-fairness case, not a physical-facilities case.
- **A stronger physical-facilities finding: a 2023-10-29 Taipei City building-inspection department (台北市建管處) inspection found ALL 81 checked TRTC accessibility facilities failed inspection**, most commonly stairway handrails (35 locations: not level, wrong height, unprotected ends, incorrectly mounted) plus 32 elevators missing lower-position call buttons and tactile/warning-sign non-compliance. Source: 自由時報, `https://news.ltn.com.tw/news/life/breakingnews/4473652`, SECONDARY, High confidence, fetched in full (not the inspection report itself, a secondary report of it). TRTC's then-general-manager committed to improvements from "next January"; the city's advisory panel set an end-of-2023 deadline. **No follow-up source confirming whether that deadline was met was found.**
- **A December 2025 multi-operator accessibility protest specifically implicating Taipei Metro.** Disability-rights groups held an "accessible-route experience action" at Taipei Main Station, 5 December 2025, with four demands (facility improvement in the station's "specific zone," continuous wayfinding, a queryable accessible-route map, staff training). Source: 中央廣播電臺 (RTI), `https://www.rti.org.tw/news?uid=3&pid=179328`, SECONDARY, High confidence, fetched in full. Named agencies: TRA, THSR, and Taipei Metro; the quoted official response came from a TRA regional director, not TRTC. Specific complaint: the ground-floor lobby elevator only reaches platform level; reaching the basement requires a single stair-mounted lift platform with a 6–10 minute wait.
- **NTMC accessibility, per its own hub page, PRIMARY, High confidence, fetched in full**: Danhai LRT — accessible elevators at all elevated stations, ramps at all stations, but accessible restrooms at only 3 of its stations (V01 紅樹林, V26 淡水漁人碼頭, K09 十四張); on-board wheelchair spaces in cars 2 and 4 of every train; priority seating **12 of 62 total seats per train (19.4%)**. Circular Line — accessible restrooms at every station; wheelchair space in the first and last cars, first door; priority seating **30 of 98 total seats per train (30.6%)**; wider accessible fare gates with lower sensors; both recessed and dot-pattern tactile warning tiles. This is a genuine, sourced design contrast between the light-rail and Circular Line vehicle/platform types — not a data error.
- **Ankeng LRT has no separately documented accessibility characteristics** distinct from the general "light rail" language covering Danhai — its one accessible-restroom station (K09) is described jointly with Circular Line station Y07 as the same physical interchange point.
- **Sanying Line accessibility facilities are entirely absent from NTMC's accessibility hub page** as of this fetch — a stated gap, not confirmed either as an oversight or a deliberate deferral; individual Sanying station-info pages (not yet opened) are the next lead.
- **Nursing rooms, free of charge, at nine station codes across NTMC's network** (8 distinct physical locations, since Y07 and K09 are the same interchange point): V01, V26, Y06, Y07/K09, Y08, Y10, Y16, Y17, Y19. Same NTMC hub page, PRIMARY, High confidence.
- **NTMC's formal passenger-guidelines documents (both light-rail and medium-capacity versions) give an identical formal guide-dog exemption clause**, alongside police dogs and trainers with puppies in training. Source: `https://www.ntmetro.com.tw/basic/?mode=detail&node=629` and `...node=630`, both PRIMARY, High confidence, fetched separately, identical text confirmed. Also state: mobility-device users may move at low speed with company permission; elderly/mobility-impaired passengers advised to use elevators over escalators; and a medical/disability-assistance procedure via staff.

## Conflicts

- **Priority-seat ratio differs between NTMC's own two systems, by design**: Danhai LRT 12/62 (19.4%) vs Circular Line 30/98 (30.6%). Both from the same primary source — a genuine published design difference, not a source disagreement, but worth stating as two values rather than one "NTMC priority seating" figure.
- **Accessible-restroom coverage differs by NTMC system**: Danhai LRT only 3 of its stations vs every Circular Line station — again a real design difference within the same source.
- **No numeric TRTC-wide elevator-coverage percentage or completion-year conflict exists to report — because no source found gives such a figure at all.** Recorded as an absence, not a resolved conflict.
