## Why depots deserve real pages

Depots are LTG's third rail category and Taipei's are unusually varied — one built
on a former leprosy sanatorium, one containing a passenger station inside its
perimeter, one physically in a different district from its name, and several with
air-rights development above the stabling roof. That last arrangement is how
Taipei part-funds metro construction and nothing explains it in English.

## Beitou Depot 北投機廠

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Line | Tamsui–Xinyi, plus the Xinbeitou branch |
| Grade | **Level 5 — the highest on the network** |
| Fleet | C301, C371 |
| Contract | **CT107**, earthworks |
| Contract signed | **19 January 1988** |
| Location | South of Fuxinggang station, Beitou District |
| Address | No. 88, Lane 527, Daye Rd |

**This was the first physical work on the entire Taipei Metro.** Not the first
line to open — the first construction contract signed and the first ground broken.
That makes Beitou Depot the origin point of the whole system, and it's a strong
opening fact for a depot page.

The largest and highest-grade depot on the network; once described as the largest
railway depot in Southeast Asia. **New rolling stock is generally tested here
first.**

*Source: task-supplied contract date; needs primary DORTS confirmation.*

## Muzha Depot 木柵機廠

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Line | Wenhu |
| Area | **6.2 hectares** |
| Stabling roads | **12** |
| Stabling capacity | **84 pairs** |
| Grade | Level 3 (was Level 5 before Neihu opened) |
| Junction | **BR01** Taipei Zoo |
| Opened | 28 March 1996, with the Muzha line |
| Address | No. 21, Sec. 2, Xinguang Rd, Wenshan District |
| Chinese name | 木柵機廠 |

Location: between Xinguang Road and the Jingmei River, northeast of Taipei Zoo.

Facilities: stabling yard, workshop, M&E building, control centre, traction
substation, main substation, emergency power and pumping station, test track.

**Notable:** houses the original Muzha line medium-capacity control centre, which
became the Wenhu line's **backup control centre** after Neihu opened. A depot that
demoted from Level 5 to Level 3 while gaining a backup-control role is an unusual
history.

**Incident:** on **22 August 2023** a lightning strike caused power loss at Muzha
Depot, triggering the fleet's fail-safe design and **stopping the entire line for
about 40 minutes**.

*Source: zh.wikipedia — secondary. Needs primary confirmation.*

## Neihu Depot 內湖機廠

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Line | Wenhu |
| Area | **7.11 hectares** |
| Grade | **Full-function maintenance depot** — the line's primary facility |
| Junction | **BR24** Nangang Exhibition Center |
| District | **Nangang, not Neihu** |
| Chinese name | 內湖機廠 |

Location: northeast of the Nangang Economic and Trade Park special district, on a
narrow northwest–southeast strip adjacent to the **Keelung River**.

Functions: stabling, washing, inspection, servicing, repair, testing, emergency
recovery, traffic control.

**Two corrections this project has already made and should state on the page:**
1. The depot connects to the line at **BR24, not BR19**. The site's strip map drew
   it at the wrong end of the railway for four builds — an inference from the
   shared name.
2. It is **in Nangang District**, despite being called Neihu Depot.

Both are exactly the kind of error an English reference should fix rather than
inherit.

*Source: DORTS 文山內湖線 project page — **primary**. Archive this URL; it's a CMS
node id.*

## Zhonghe Depot 中和機廠 — the network's smallest

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Line | Zhonghe–Xinlu |
| Location | **South of Heping Street, Zhonghe** |
| Site | **The former TRA Zhonghe station site** (原臺灣鐵路管理局中和車站基地) |
| Area | **≈1.47 hectares — including 南勢角 Nanshijiao station** |
| Facilities | Stabling yard, maintenance equipment building, main substation |
| Functions | **Stabling, general maintenance, cleaning only** |
| Opened | With the Zhonghe line, **24 December 1998** |

**DORTS states the limited facilities are a direct consequence of the site's size** —
"因用地面積之限制". At 1.47 hectares *including a passenger station*, this is a
fraction of Beitou's or Neihu's footprint, and by some margin the smallest depot on
the network.

**A metro depot built on a closed railway station's land, so constrained it can only
stable and clean trains.** That's a complete, self-contained story — and it explains
why the line needed Xinzhuang and Luzhou depots when it was extended north.

**Worth stating as a contrast:** Beitou is level 5 and can do everything; Zhonghe
cannot even do scheduled inspection. Same network, same fleet family, 1.47 ha versus
the largest depot in the region.

*Source: DORTS 中和新蘆線 — **primary**.*

## Xinzhuang Depot 新莊機廠 — and the Losheng Sanatorium

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Line | Zhonghe–Xinlu |
| Grade | Level 3 |
| Location | Adjacent to Huilong station, **straddling New Taipei and Taoyuan** |
| Original site area | **17.4 hectares expropriated** (of ~30 ha total) |
| Final site area | ≈**13.9 hectares** |
| Contract | **CK570F** (區段標) |
| Contractor | **工信工程** |

Functions: stabling, inspection, maintenance, washing, and storage of station and
main-line power and track maintenance equipment.

**DORTS confirms the depot layout was altered for preservation.** Its own project
page states the facility configuration was **adjusted to accommodate the Losheng
Sanatorium preservation plan** (因配合樂生療養院保存案計畫，調整機廠設施的配置).
That is the operator acknowledging the campaign changed the engineering — a
**primary-source** confirmation, not a campaigner's claim.

### The Losheng dispute — this deserves its own article

**Losheng Sanatorium 樂生療養院** was established in **1930** under Japanese rule
as Taiwan's first and only leprosy (漢生病) isolation hospital, at Huilong on the
Xinzhuang/Guishan border.

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **1995** | The provincial health department sells the site to the metro authority; DORTS confirms the depot location. **17.4 ha expropriated**, covering the most historically valuable building cluster |
| 21 May 2002 | CK570F contract signed to 工信工程 |
| 20 June 2002 | Groundbreaking |
| 2004 | **青年樂生聯盟** (Youth Losheng Alliance) forms |
| 2005 | **樂生保留自救會** forms among residents (院民) |
| **16 March 2007** | One-month demolition notice issued; clashes with police |
| **18 March 2007** | **NT$200,000 raised in 24 hours** via the 黑米 platform to run a half-page *Apple Daily* advertisement comparing the 90% and 41.6% preservation plans |
| **11 April 2007** | Premier **蘇貞昌** apologises and pledges "90% preservation as the maximum goal" |
| **15 April 2007** | **5,000–6,000 people march** |
| **30 May 2007** | The Executive Yuan's Public Construction Commission announces the **"530 plan"** |
| **13 August 2008** | The **漢生病病患人權保障及補償條例** (Hansen's disease patients' human rights and compensation act) is promulgated |
| **3 December 2008** | Second forced clearance |
| 2012 | The line opens fully despite the unresolved dispute |

**The preservation figures conflict and should be published as conflicting.** The
Ministry of Culture's potential-World-Heritage page records the 530 decision as
**"原地保留39棟建物，拆除6棟，9棟建物…重建"** — 39 buildings preserved in place, 6
demolished, 9 rebuilt. **Campaigners counted only 28 actually preserved**, calling
it the 41.6% plan against the promised 90%.

Actors: 青年樂生聯盟, 樂生保留自救會, 新莊文史工作會, 台灣人權促進會, and
professor **李乾朗**.

**Note a date tension in the sources:** one gives the CK570F award as 21 May 2002,
another as 19 April 2004. Resolve before publishing.

*Sources: 台灣人權促進會 (tahr.org.tw/news/602); e-info.org.tw/node/28638; 焦點事件
(eventsinfocus.org); 文化部 twh.boch.gov.tw — the last is primary-adjacent.*

## Xindian Depot 新店機廠

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Line | Songshan–Xindian |
| Area | **≈12 hectares** |
| Fleet | C371 |

**Its unique feature:** the **Xiaobitan branch (G03A) rises from underground to
grade, then climbs to an elevated passenger station inside the depot itself.**
There is no other arrangement like it on the network, and it exists because
residents campaigned during 1998 for a station at the depot; approved 17 September
1999.

**Worth a diagram** — a section drawing showing the branch climbing through the
depot would be genuinely illuminating.

*Source: DORTS 松山新店線 — primary.*
