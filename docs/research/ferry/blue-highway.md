## Why this belongs on the site

- **It's part of the TPASS fare zone.** The 基北北桃1200都會通 explicitly covers
  **藍色公路（含渡輪）**. Visitors with a TPASS are often paying separately for
  journeys already included.
- **The Tamsui–Bali crossing is genuine commuter transport**, not just tourism —
  weekday sailings every 10–15 minutes, and it's the only water mode integrated into
  the region's fare system.
- **EasyCard works at the piers**, with tap validators rather than ticket windows.
- Almost nothing about it exists in English beyond tourist blurbs.

## History

The Taipei-side Tamsui River Blue Highway opened on **7 February 2004** (民國93年
2月7日). By mid-July 2004 it had carried **over 16,000 passenger trips**.

Initially it ran **only from Dadaocheng to Guandu**, which passengers found
frustrating — they wanted to continue to Fisherman's Wharf, the Bali left bank, the
riverside cycle routes and Tamsui Old Street.

### The cross-boundary problem, and how it was solved

Extending the route meant crossing from Taipei City into what was then Taipei
County. A **joint review group** was formed specifically for it:

**臺北都會區藍色公路聯合審查小組** — the Taipei Metropolitan Blue Highway Joint
Review Panel, comprising:

- Taipei City Government
- Taipei County Government
- **MOTC 交通部**
- **Tourism Bureau 觀光局**
- **Keelung Port Bureau 基隆港務局**
- **Taipei Port Branch 臺北港分局**

**Six authorities across two municipalities and central government, to run a boat
between two riverbanks.** That's a good, concrete illustration of Taiwan's
jurisdictional layering and it's exactly the kind of thing an English reference can
usefully explain.

### The extension

**17 July 2004** — trial passenger operation begins on **Dadaocheng – Guandu –
Tamsui Second Fishing Port (Fisherman's Wharf)**.

The trial used **three small passenger boats**: **飛魚一號, 飛魚五號 and 飛魚六號**
(Flying Fish 1, 5 and 6), running **two sailings on weekdays and six at weekends**.

Full operation with larger vessels waited on maritime survey and licensing
procedures.

**A caveat published at the time and still true:** **not every sailing called at
Guandu.** Passengers were told to check the day's notice at the ticket office.

**Typhoon suspension:** operators suspend in line with Central Weather Bureau
warnings when the warning area includes northern Taiwan. The 2004 announcement
coincided with Typhoon Kompasu warnings, which is why it's stated explicitly.

*Source: **Taipei City Government Department of Transportation press release, 14
July 2004** — primary. Also mirrored on the Department of Urban Development site.*

## Operators — four, and they do different things

This is the key structural fact. The Blue Highway is not one service.

### 順風航業股份有限公司 Shun Feng Shipping

The main **commuter** operator. Routes serve **Fisherman's Wharf 漁人碼頭, Tamsui
Old Street 淡水老街 and Bali Left Bank 八里左岸**.

| Route type | Full | Concession | Frequency | Capacity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Main crossing | **NT$40** | NT$20 | **Weekday every 10–15 min; weekend every 3–5 min** | **99** |
| Secondary | **NT$100** | NT$50 | Weekday **charter by reservation**; weekend every 30–50 min, sea conditions permitting | **50** |
| 關渡 – 淡水 / 漁人碼頭 | | | **Charter by reservation only** | 50 (Fisherman's), 99 (Tamsui) |
| 淡水 – 紅樹林 – 淡江大橋 – 淡水 river cruise | | | Charter by reservation | ~60 min, **no disembarking** |

**Weekend headways of 3–5 minutes** are metro-grade frequency. That alone justifies
treating this as public transport rather than tourism.

**Journey times vary with the tide** and the Left Bank pier's operating hours are
adjusted daily to match. That's a genuine operational constraint no land mode has.

### 台北航運股份有限公司 Taipei Cruise

Operates the vessel **台航一號**, which from **17 June 2004** ran **淡水客船碼頭 –
八里左岸客船碼頭** on a **30-minute weekday headway**, with higher-capacity
scheduling at weekends.

**Original 2004 fares: full NT$18, half NT$10.** Useful for a fares-over-time
comparison against today's NT$40.

Operating hours were set "within the maximum permitted range for berthing at the
passenger piers, in accordance with the Tamsui River tides."

**Piers served:** 淡水漁人碼頭, 淡水客船碼頭, 八里左岸客船碼頭, 關渡碼頭,
大稻埕碼頭, 忠孝碼頭, 華江碼頭.

**That seven-pier list is the closest thing to a complete pier inventory found so
far** — note it includes 忠孝碼頭 and 華江碼頭, which don't appear in tourist
material.

Also ran a **river cruise**: 漁人碼頭 – 關渡大橋, 60 minutes, full **NT$180**, half
**NT$130**, **charter NT$6,000**, minimum 15 people.

### 好樂好股份有限公司 Haloha

Runs a **scheduled Dadaocheng – Tamsui** service.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Journey time | **≈90 minutes** |
| Minimum to sail | **10 passengers booked** |
| Schedule | **Saturday morning and afternoon**, tide permitting |
| Example times | Dadaocheng → Tamsui **10:00** and **13:30** |

The company describes years of experience with **the tidal interaction between the
Tamsui and Keelung rivers**, which is what governs whether a sailing can be
scheduled at all.

### 長弘航業股份有限公司 Chang Hong Shipping

Founded **2002**. Imported **Japanese aluminium-alloy passenger vessels**. Runs
charters, wedding and party cruises, on-board dining, and a **weekend Dadaocheng –
Tamsui** scheduled run in partnership with both city governments.

Route: **大稻埕碼頭 → 淡水老街**, **≈1.5 hours**, passing 忠孝橋, 臺北大橋, 重陽大橋,
社子島頭, 關渡碼頭, 關渡大橋, 紅樹林河畔, 淡水碼頭.

Marketed as **藍色公路微旅行** — the "Blue Waterway Mini Trip" — with ecology,
birdlife, fish species, history and scenery commentary on board, plus a Saturday
on-land heritage walking tour at Dadaocheng.

## Fares and ticketing

### EasyCard integration

**順風航業 routes accept EasyCard**, with **validators installed at the piers**.
Single full fares across its routes range **NT$20 to NT$50**.

Before this, passengers had to buy at a ticket window then queue separately to
board. The tap-to-board change removed that entirely.

**An important exception to document:** holders of **敬老 senior, 愛心
accessibility and 愛心陪伴 companion cards** should check their balance before
travelling, because **Blue Highway journeys do not qualify for the government's
monthly subsidy points**. So concession cardholders pay from stored value rather
than from their subsidised allowance.

**That's a real trap and no English source mentions it.**

### Concessions

| Category | Eligibility |
|---|---|
| Senior | **65 and over**, with national ID or senior card |
| Child | **3 to under 12** |
| Disability | Holders of a disability certificate, **plus one companion** |
| Free | **Under 3** |

### Bicycles

**NT$20 per bicycle**, stowed as directed by crew.

Worth stating prominently — combined with the riverside cycle routes and the Danhai
LRT's NT$50 bike fare, the region has unusually good bike-carriage provision and
nobody has written it up in English.
