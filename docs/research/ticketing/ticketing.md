## Contradicted the lead file

The earlier lead file (docs/research/ticketing/ticketing.md, version 1.0)
made several specific claims. Checked against primary sources this run:

1. **"Card fee ... refunded on real-name registration" — confirmed**, and
   now sourced properly to 交通部公路局's own FAQ (claim 20 above) rather
   than left as an assertion. The lead file was right; it just wasn't
   sourced.

2. **"You don't need a TPASS card ... loads onto any existing EasyCard" —
   confirmed and substantially narrowed.** The operative rule (claim 18) is
   that the pass loads onto a specific, named family of EasyCard products —
   ordinary cards, co-branded cards, Debit cards, Samsung Wallet cards,
   SuperCard, telecom cards, student cards and digital student IDs — which
   in practice covers nearly every EasyCard in circulation, but is not
   literally "any" card (co-branded and Debit cards need 60+ days of
   remaining validity, for instance). The lead file's framing was directionally
   right and is now sourced to the operator's own operating notice rather
   than asserted.

3. **"Unused 1280 passes were refunded pro-rata without a fee" —
   contradicted.** New Taipei City's own operating document for the 1,280
   pass (claim 22) gives the *general* voluntary-refund rule as a front-
   loaded daily deduction (NT$180 for day 1, NT$130 per day after) **plus a
   NT$20 handling fee** — not a flat pro-rata split, and not fee-free. A
   *different* clause in the same document, covering only a card that has
   physically failed through no fault of the holder, does use a simple
   pro-rata formula with no stated fee. The lead file's claim appears to
   describe that second, narrower clause as if it were the general rule.
   This page publishes the general rule; the narrower one is recorded here
   rather than dropped.

4. **EasyCard as "sole non-bank stored-value issuer" — needs the "at the
   time" qualifier the lead file dropped.** The 2009 law made EasyCard the
   only such issuer *at the point the law passed* (claim 6); iPass and
   icash are independently confirmed as being accepted TRA electronic
   tickets today (claim 14), which is not possible without their own
   issuer licences. Whether EasyCard's exclusivity lasted for any
   meaningful period afterward, and when iPass/icash were separately
   licensed, is not established by anything fetched this run — see Stated
   gaps.

5. **"Cards issued cumulatively: 110 million+" — not confirmed at that
   figure.** The most recent primary figure found and fetched in full is
   EasyCard Corporation's own **98.32 million circulating cards as of
   end-October 2022** (claim 9), alongside a separate, secondary report
   (信傳媒, not independently fetched in full this run) that put *cumulative
   lifetime issuance* over 100 million by the end of 2022 — a different
   measure (circulating vs. ever-issued) from a different, older date than
   the lead file's unsourced "110 million+". The content page uses the
   dated, primary 98.32-million circulating figure and does not carry
   "110 million+" forward.

6. **The i cash / iPass "almost entirely TBC" framing is now out of date.**
   Both card systems have primary-sourced founding histories in this file
   (claims 11–13) that were not available, or not found, when the lead
   file was written.

Nothing else specific and checkable in the lead file's Ticketing sections
was found to be wrong; most of the rest (predecessor company name, 2007
Keelung integration, 2009 Act, TPASS timeline shape) checked out once
sourced properly.
