# Research prompt templates

Paste one of these when you want a deep research pass. Each is written to be
used with the Research tool so it pulls from many sources rather than a handful.

Replace `[LINE]` where the template is generic.

---

## Standard line research prompt

> Deep research on the **[LINE NAME]** of the Taipei metro network, for an
> English-language transit reference site. Prioritise Mandarin sources —
> zh.wikipedia, PTT, Mobile01, official TRTC/NTMC/TYMC/DORTS pages, Taiwanese
> news archives, academic papers, and railfan sites. Cross-check every figure
> against at least two independent sources and report where they disagree.
>
> Cover:
>
> **Identity and basics** — official Chinese and English names, line code and
> colour, operator, line number, when the numbering was assigned, and any
> former names with the dates they changed.
>
> **Physical characteristics** — total length and what that figure measures
> (operating length vs construction project length vs alignment length, since
> these differ), station count, elevated/underground/at-grade breakdown by
> section, track gauge, electrification voltage and collection method,
> signalling system and its generations, maximum and operating speed.
>
> **Stations** — full list with codes, Chinese and official English names,
> interchanges, structure type, opening date per station where they differ.
> Flag any station renamed since opening, with dates.
>
> **Construction history** — original plan and how it changed, approval dates,
> budget, contract structure and contractors, groundbreaking, section-by-section
> opening dates including 初勘 and 履勘 inspection dates, and any delays with
> their causes.
>
> **Rolling stock** — every fleet that has run on the line, manufacturer,
> quantity, formation, delivery dates, depot allocation, propulsion system and
> any renewals, and withdrawals.
>
> **Depots** — name, area, grade, functions, connection point to the running
> line, which fleet each maintains.
>
> **Operations** — all service patterns including short workings and any
> unusual ones, headways by time of day and day type, end-to-end journey time,
> first and last trains, daily ridership with the date of the figure.
>
> **Incidents and controversies** — accidents, significant service failures,
> earthquake or typhoon damage, construction disputes, political controversies,
> cost overruns, legal cases. Give dates, causes, consequences and any official
> findings.
>
> **Current and future** — extensions under construction or planned with
> status and target dates, fleet renewal, ongoing works, recent policy changes.
>
> For every claim: state the source, whether it is primary (operator,
> government, manufacturer, court record) or secondary, and your confidence.
> Where sources conflict, report all values rather than picking one. Where you
> find nothing, say so — an empty finding is useful. Do not guess.

## Per-line variants — extra angles worth naming

Append the relevant block to the standard prompt.

**Wenhu (BR)**
> Also: the Matra contract dispute and arbitration, the 1993 test fire and the
> Control Yuan impeachments, the 2009 Neihu opening failures and the 詐胡
> nickname, the VAL256 to Innovia transition, the steel running surface on the
> Neihu section versus concrete on Muzha, the seat removal programme, the fleet
> renewal programme, and why the line survived the 403 earthquake undamaged.

**Tamsui–Xinyi (R)**
> Also: the conversion of the former TRA 北淡線 railway corridor, the
> three-way elevated/at-grade/underground transition, cross-colour operation to
> Xindian and why it ended in November 2014, the Xinbeitou branch and its
> closure and reopening, and the Xinyi East Extension to 廣慈/奉天宮 including
> the cancelled second station.

**Songshan–Xindian (G)**
> Also: the 1987 decision to redesign from part-elevated to fully underground
> and the objections that drove it, the Xiaobitan branch campaign and its
> elevated station inside Xindian Depot, and why NTU Hospital station belongs
> operationally to a different line than the one it was built with.

**Zhonghe–Xinlu (O)**
> Also: how three separately built lines became one Y-shaped operation, how the
> branch service pattern actually works, the two disjoint station number series
> and why, and the Losheng Sanatorium preservation campaign against Xinzhuang
> Depot in full — dates, actors, outcomes, and what was preserved.

**Bannan (BL)**
> Also: tunnelling beneath Beimen, the accelerated 1999 opening to complete the
> 雙十 network, the original Banqiao–Songshan plan and the 1988 extension to
> Nangang, the empty peak-direction workings from Jiangzicui, the 2009–2011
> Zhinan Bus shuttle to Nangang Exhibition Center, and Nangang Depot's
> air-rights development. Also the operational and security changes that
> followed the 21 May 2014 attack — the transport consequences, not the event.

**Circular (Y)**
> Also: the 3 April 2024 Hualien earthquake in full — the eleven girder
> displacements, why a line designed for 6-weak failed at 5-strong, the
> third-party investigation and its findings, repair method, cost, timeline,
> and restoration stages. Compare directly with Wenhu, which is also elevated
> and was undamaged: what structural differences explain it? Also the four-phase
> construction plan and the status of North, South and East Ring.

**Danhai LRT (V)**
> Also: the Jimmy Liao station artwork, the Green Mountain and Blue Sea branch
> structure, the V11 to V26 numbering gap, phase 2, and how a light rail line
> integrates with the heavy metro fare system.

**Ankeng LRT (K)**
> Also: why Ankeng was built as light rail rather than metro, ridership against
> projection, and the interchange with the Circular Line at Shisizhang.

**Sanying (LB)**
> Also: why New Taipei built this line itself rather than through Taipei's
> DORTS, the 26 years from proposal to opening, the LB03 挖子/長壽山 renaming,
> the station naming criteria, the Hitachi Rail Italy trainsets and which plant
> built them, the Hakka and Indigenous themed interiors, and the Bade extension.

**Airport MRT (A)**
> Also: the express versus commuter service split, in-town check-in at Taipei
> Main, the long delays before opening and their causes, TYMC as an operator
> distinct from TRTC and NTMC, and extension plans.

## Buses

> Deep research on Taipei's bus system for an English-language transit
> reference. Mandarin sources preferred. Cover:
>
> **The joint-operation system (聯營公車)** — how it works, its history, how
> fares and revenue are shared between operators, who regulates it, and how it
> differs from a franchised or contracted model.
>
> **Route numbering** — every convention and what it encodes. The 1xx/2xx/3xx
> bands, 幹線 trunk routes, 紅/綠/藍/棕 coloured routes, 小 minibus routes,
> 區間車 short workings, 副線 branches, directional suffixes like 0南 and 0東,
> and the 9xxx national highway coaches.
>
> **Operators** — every Taipei joint-operation operator with founding date,
> ownership, corporate history, mergers, privatisations, garages, and
> cross-holdings between them. The Capital Bus / Metropolitan Transport board
> takeover in December 2010 is one documented example; find the others.
>
> **Vehicle models** — chassis and body manufacturers used in Taipei
> historically and now, with procurement dates and which operators bought what.
> Cover the electrification programme in depth: the five domestic manufacturers,
> market shares, domestic content requirements, subsidy programmes, delivery
> figures, and the charging infrastructure constraint.
>
> **Garages and depots** — locations, operators, capacity.
>
> Cross-check enthusiast sources like 台灣巴士文化百科 against operator
> websites and MOTC Highway Bureau open data.

## YouBike

> Deep research on YouBike, focused on Taipei and New Taipei. Mandarin sources
> preferred. Cover: the 2009 Xinyi pilot, the full 1.0 to 2.0 transition
> timeline, why the two systems were incompatible and how the changeover was
> managed, the technical differences (dock power, onboard unit, QR hire, lock),
> 2.0E electric bikes, the operator relationship between Giant, Microprogram and
> the city governments, contract structure and renewals, pricing history and
> subsidies, station siting policy, ridership milestones, what happened to
> retired 1.0 bikes, and the separate Giant Kunshan variant deployed in Putian
> that uses 1.0 docks.

## Maokong Gondola

> Deep research on the Maokong Gondola. Mandarin sources preferred. Cover:
> planning and approval, POMA as manufacturer and the contractor structure,
> construction, the 2007 opening, the T16 tower foundation failure after Typhoon
> Jangmi in September 2008, the four engineering institutes' assessment, the
> 18-month closure, the 2010 reopening and early alarms, the crystal cabins and
> their construction, ridership, fares, maintenance regime, and its relationship
> to TRTC.

## Ticketing

> Deep research on Taipei-region public transport ticketing. Cover: EasyCard's
> history, ownership and technology; iPass; the TPASS programme including the
> 基北北桃1200都會通, its predecessor the 雙北1280, coverage differences,
> the card fee refund, the SuperCard mechanism, and uptake figures; the fare
> structure for metro, bus and the joint bus system; transfer discounts;
> concessionary fares; and how the ferries and light rail integrate.
